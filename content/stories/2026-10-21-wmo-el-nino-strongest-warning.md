The World Meteorological Organization warned on Wednesday that the current El Nino could be unprecedented in strength, with Secretary-General Celeste Saulo telling a Geneva press conference it could be literally off the monitoring charts used for the past four decades, as the agency's seasonal update forecasts sea surface temperatures in the tropical Pacific rising to 3.8 degrees Celsius above average during November to January, with the event peaking toward the end of 2026 and its impacts felt long after. UN aid chief Tom Fletcher, appearing alongside Saulo in a joint call for urgent anticipatory action, called El Nino a wrecking ball of extreme heat, droughts and floods and said he had released 50 million dollars to help 11 countries on the frontline, including Chad, El Salvador, Ethiopia, Somalia and South Sudan.
