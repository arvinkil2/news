Morocco has committed 4 billion dirhams, about 366 million euros, from the Hassan II Fund for Economic and Social Development to launch a national defence industry roadmap, the Royal Cabinet announced this week. King Mohammed VI allocated the funding to start the programme.

The roadmap sets three targets for 2035: creating about 50,000 direct and indirect jobs, meeting 40 percent of the equipment needs of the Royal Armed Forces and security services from domestic production, and earning more than 65 percent of defence industry revenues from exports. Priority areas include drones and autonomous systems, land and naval equipment, defence electronics, cybersecurity and artificial intelligence.

Delivery runs through new state machinery: a strategic agency and a holding company for the sector, and two new defence industrial zones at Benguerir and Benslimane, adding to existing zones at Nouaceur and Sidi Yahya.

The programme is the funding step behind Morocco's push to localize arms production and become a defence manufacturing base for African markets, reducing reliance on imported equipment while building an export industry around it.
