The warnings were delivered over the weekend by the State Department and the Pentagon, including direct communications from the US military to Ukrainian commanders, according to two European officials cited by CNN, with the Financial Times first reporting the threat to intelligence sharing and to PURL, the mechanism through which European allies buy US weapons for Ukraine. One US official framed the message to negotiators as a partner asking Kyiv not to take actions that hurt American consumers, a reference to the diesel price surge Washington blames in part on Ukraine's refinery strikes.

The pressure campaign sits behind the energy ceasefire President Trump announced on Sunday, which Kyiv said caught it by surprise and which Russia on Monday declined to confirm exists. The US agencies involved had not commented on the reported threats as of Monday.
