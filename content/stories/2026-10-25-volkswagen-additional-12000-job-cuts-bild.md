**Latest, October 25:** Audi, Volkswagen's premium brand, will spend $4 billion on its first factory in the United States, in Macon, Georgia, aimed at building 200,000 SUVs a year and creating 4,500 jobs, The Guardian reported. The plant will build the Q7, Q8 and Q9, shifting some production from Bratislava in Slovakia, and Audi chief executive Gernot Dollner announced it alongside Donald Trump at a campaign rally on Saturday night, days before the US midterm elections. The Guardian framed the move around the 15 percent US tariffs on imports from the EU, which Volkswagen has said cost it up to 5 billion euros a year before lost sales, and gave no opening date for the factory.

Volkswagen plans to cut an additional 12,000 jobs by 2030 as part of a cost saving programme, according to a report by the newspaper Bild carried by Antara, and the cuts would come on top of a previously approved workforce programme that already includes eliminating more than 35,000 positions. Management plans to carry out the layoffs in a socially responsible way, including retraining places supported by the German government, Antara reported. The IG Metall union is threatening strike action at the carmaker starting in 2027, formal negotiations on the collective bargaining agreement are scheduled for November 24, and the peace obligation that bars strikes expires on December 31.
