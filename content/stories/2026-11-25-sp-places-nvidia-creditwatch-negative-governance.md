## Latest

Vice President JD Vance said on Wednesday that Attorney General Todd Blanche had told him Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang was under no investigation, telling reporters in Ohio that the Justice Department and national security officials had assured him Huang was not under investigation and that the accusations against him rested on anonymous reporting rather than evidence. The account sits against President Trump's statement on Monday that an investigation had been done and had exonerated Huang; Reuters reported on Wednesday that the Justice Department conducted no formal investigation of Huang before his reinstatement, and that Blanche had concluded there was no evidence Huang broke any laws based on investigations into Nvidia clients Super Micro Computer and Megaspeed. Vance said Huang does not need a pardon. Nvidia did not immediately respond to CNBC's request for comment.

S&P Global Ratings on Wednesday placed Nvidia's credit rating on CreditWatch Negative, a step that signals a high likelihood of a downgrade, citing rising management and governance risks. The agency said the departure and then reinstatement of chief executive Jensen Huang does not sufficiently answer its questions about board oversight of the executive code of conduct.

The placement follows the board's reinstatement of Huang as chief executive on November 23, covered on this site, and lands while Nvidia shares are under pressure in the broader AI stock sell-off. S&P did not, in the reporting available, set a timetable for resolving the CreditWatch.
