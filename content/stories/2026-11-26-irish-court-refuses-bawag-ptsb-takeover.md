Ireland's High Court on Thursday declined to sanction the scheme of arrangement that would have implemented Bawag Group's agreed acquisition of Permanent TSB Group Holdings, PTSB's board announced. The scheme, brought under Chapter 1 of Part 9 of the Companies Act 2014, was the legal vehicle for Bawag, the parent of Austria's BAWAG PSK, to acquire the entire issued share capital of PTSB.

The court refused because PTSB had failed to hold a separate meeting for its minority shareholders to vote on the deal, Reuters reported. PTSB shareholders, including the Irish Minister for Finance, who holds about 57.5 percent of the shares, approved the sale at a meeting in July, but the court ruled the remaining shareholders were entitled to vote separately on the scheme. Judge Michael Quinn said in his written judgment that where that requirement is not met, the court has no discretion and must refuse to sanction the scheme.

Bawag and PTSB agreed the cash offer in April, with the support of the Minister for Finance and a recommendation from PTSB's board. Under its terms Bawag would pay 2.97 euros per share, a total consideration of about 1.619 billion euros, for the entire issued and to be issued share capital of PTSB. The transaction had already cleared European Central Bank supervisory approval, leaving the court's consent as the binding remaining condition.

PTSB said it is considering the judgment, and Bawag confirmed the refusal in a market announcement and said it was also considering the judgment, with a further announcement to follow. The refusal blocks the scheme in its current form. It does not by itself terminate the acquisition, and neither company has announced an appeal, a fresh minority vote or a revised structure. Four shareholders had challenged the deal at the sanction hearing in October, arguing the price was too low, and Axis Capital, a corporate advisory firm cofounded by former banker Mark Duffy, had been assembling a possible rival offer at 3.20 euros per share backed by Fortress Investment Group.
