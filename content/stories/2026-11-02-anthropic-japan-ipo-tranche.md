**Latest, November 2:** Anthropic, PBC filed Amendment No. 1 to its Form S-1 with the Securities and Exchange Commission on Monday, accepted at 9:24 a.m. Eastern, and the amendment adds the financial statements for the quarter and nine months ended September 30, 2026. Revenue was $16.956 billion in the third quarter and $33.242 billion in the nine months, against $2.545 billion in the same nine months of 2025, a thirteen-fold increase. The net loss was $23.291 billion in the quarter and $104.039 billion over the nine months, against $25.107 billion a year earlier. Of the nine month loss, $94.324 billion is a single non-cash line, the change in fair value of convertible notes, derivative liabilities and SAFEs, which grows as the company's own valuation rises. Compute and infrastructure expense was $18.808 billion in the nine months, up $14.427 billion, or 329 percent, from $4.381 billion a year earlier.

The amendment puts the commitment load in one aggregate. As of and subsequent to September 30, 2026, the company states that it has entered into purchase commitments and lease agreements, including short-term commitments and leases not yet commenced, with an aggregate committed spend of approximately $895.4 billion, substantially all of it related to compute and infrastructure. Approximately $447.5 billion is expected to be incurred before December 31, 2030, substantially all of the remainder between 2031 and 2045, and approximately $69.8 billion falls due in the twelve months following September 30, 2026. The filing also notes that approximately $213.8 billion of the total relates to arrangements carrying exercisable termination rights or contingent conditions before commencement.

The Japan tranche rests on a separate document. The US amendment confirms only that the offering includes public offerings in Japan and five other jurisdictions outside the United States (Australia, Canada, certain member states of the European Economic Area, Switzerland and the United Kingdom), that the company does not currently intend to list its shares in any of them, and that shares allocated to an international offering that is terminated would be reallocated to the others. It gives no size for the Japan offering, and the share count and price range for the offering as a whole remain blank in the preliminary prospectus. The figure of up to 552.9 billion yen, about $3.5 billion, of new shares to be sold to investors in Japan comes from the company's Japanese disclosure as reported by Reuters on Monday, which also reported the proceeds going to working capital, operating expenses and capital expenditure, and investor valuation talk of about $1.5 trillion that no filing states.
