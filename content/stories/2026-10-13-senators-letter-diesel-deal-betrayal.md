**Latest (Tuesday, October 13, night):** the criticism reached the campaign trail on Tuesday night, when Ashley Hinson, the Republican congresswoman running for the Senate in Iowa, used the second televised debate against Democrat Josh Turek to say she does not agree with the deal to bring in 300,000 tons of Russian diesel, arguing fuel prices should be cut through a temporary gas tax reduction, a diesel rebate for producers and investment in American biofuels instead of an arrangement with an adversary. Hinson had told the Associated Press earlier on Tuesday that relieving a sanction on Russia makes her nervous, and Turek said it was a rare point of agreement between them.

The senators, led by Thom Tillis for the Republicans and Richard Blumenthal for the Democrats, called the October 9 agreement a gift to an adversary, said Russian diesel would do little to cut the record $6.53 a gallon US diesel price, and warned the revenue would fund Moscow's war and deepen its security ties with China, Iran and North Korea. They also pointed to the sanctions law Congress passed last month, the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act, and asked Trump to use those pressure tools instead; John Curtis, who joined Sunday's statement, did not sign Tuesday's letter.
