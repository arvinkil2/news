Miner equities split cleanly along metal lines in the latest session. Gold miners fell 1.48 percent and silver miners fell 1.52 percent, tracking the underlying metals: gold futures fell 1.05 percent to $3,958.60 an ounce and silver futures fell 1.17 percent to $51.28 an ounce. Copper miners rose 0.75 percent even as copper itself fell 0.94 percent to $6.22 a pound, a small company-side divergence inside an otherwise metal-led tape.

The critical-mineral complex moved the other way. Rare earth miners rose 2.49 percent, lithium and battery miners rose 1.82 percent and uranium miners rose 1.48 percent. No matching single commodity print explains those gains in the tables, which points to stock and policy drivers instead: the session's mining news was dominated by project and supply stories in exactly those metals, including PMET's larger Quebec lithium study and Uzbekistan's plan to double uranium output by 2030.

The diversified majors held up better than the precious complex. BHP rose 1.16 percent to $82.06 on the morning it named a new chief financial officer and was reported to be planning an iron ore cost overhaul, and Rio Tinto rose 0.51 percent to $84.97. Over one week the picture stays mixed: rare earth miners are up 2.71 percent and uranium miners up 2.87 percent, while copper miners are down 1.53 percent and lithium and battery miners are down 1.31 percent despite the day's gain.

The session continues the whipsaw pattern of the past week, in which miners first rallied harder than their metals and then fell on a day metals rose. This time the precious segment at least moved with its metals. The critical-mineral segment did not need a metal move at all, which makes its gains the more fragile of the two if project news flow slows.
