Aliko Dangote has acquired a 24.76 percent stake in Kenya Pipeline Company, the state built network that distributes petroleum products across Kenya, in a single block trade on the Nairobi Securities Exchange. The trade moved 4.5 billion shares for 42.5 billion Kenyan shillings, about 327 million dollars, according to the exchange report cited by Bloomberg. Dangote disclosed the purchase at an event in London on Wednesday, saying his group had bought 25 percent of the pipeline network, but he did not identify the vehicle through which the stake was acquired.

The sellers were public entities. Business Daily reported that at least eight state firms sold shares they had bought in Kenya Pipeline's initial public offering earlier this year, when the government sold 65 percent of the company and it listed on March 9. President William Ruto confirmed the sale in his Mashujaa Day address on October 20, describing the buyers as private investors. The block was handled by Rock Investment Bank for the sellers and KCB Capital for the buyer.

The size is calibrated. At 24.76 percent the holding stays just below the 25 percent threshold at which Kenyan takeover rules treat a holder as having effective control and require an offer to remaining shareholders, unless the Capital Markets Authority grants an exemption. Business Daily reported the stake makes Dangote the company's second largest shareholder, a position that could carry board seats and influence over strategic investment without a full bid.

The purchase extends an energy position Dangote is building in Kenya alongside his Nigerian refining business. He is also planning a 700,000 barrel per day refinery in the Lamu archipelago, estimated at 16 billion dollars and linked to the Lamu Port South Sudan Ethiopia Transport corridor. Owning a quarter of the distribution network that would move refined product inland gives that refinery plan a route to market that does not depend on competitors' infrastructure.

For Kenya, the trade cuts both ways. The IPO was meant to broaden ownership of a strategic asset; within eight months a quarter of the company has consolidated into one foreign industrial holder, bought from the state firms that anchored the offer. The price discovery is real, but so is the concentration, and the undisclosed buying vehicle leaves the governance questions for the regulator and the company's next shareholder disclosures.
