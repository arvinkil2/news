**Latest, October 27:** Broadcom's own Form 8-K, filed on October 27 for the October 26 arrangement, puts a number on the support. The company is providing residual value support, which the filing calls the Backstop, to the newly formed special purpose vehicle, and its maximum potential liability once the AI racks are fully deployed is approximately $43 billion on an undiscounted basis. In a lease default by the customer, Broadcom's liability would equal the difference between 72.5 percent of the amounts outstanding on the lease and the value of the AI racks received on a sale of the assets. The filing describes the Backstop as a contingent liability the company believes has a low probability of occurring, citing the customer's expected profitability trajectory and the sustaining value of the hardware, and lists Broadcom's remedies in a default as assuming the lease, reselling the racks back to the seller at a fixed price under certain conditions, or arranging a sale of the racks. The amount of the Backstop rises as racks are delivered and deployed and falls as the customer pays down its lease obligations. The filing is signed by chief financial officer Amie Thuener.

Northstar Compute LLC, a new compute finance and lease platform, has completed an inaugural capital raise of $63 billion in debt and equity to finance AI compute capacity for Anthropic using Broadcom technology, Milbank, the law firm that represented North Star, said. The raise was arranged by Bank of America, Blackstone, Citi and Morgan Stanley, and the money will flow through a newly formed special purpose vehicle that buys Broadcom XPU AI accelerators and networking equipment and leases it to Anthropic, with deliveries expected to start in 2027.

The first lien portion is being arranged by Bank of America, Citi and Morgan Stanley, Blackstone is the sole provider of the committed second lien portion, and Broadcom is providing residual value support to the vehicle in an amount equivalent to the first lien. Datacenter Dynamics, reporting the Wall Street Journal, said Broadcom chief executive Hock Tan spent two months organising the deal and held talks with the banks over the weekend of October 24 and 25 to finalise their commitments, with the banks agreeing to higher interest rates and Broadcom offering guarantees against losses if Anthropic defaults. The transaction forms part of the strategic compute capacity agreement Broadcom had previously announced, and it lands the day Anthropic publicly filed its S-1, which put its aggregate compute commitments in the hundreds of billions of dollars.
