In the ALOFT-IPF trial, which enrolled about 1,255 people with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, admilparant at 60 milligrams twice daily significantly slowed the decline in forced vital capacity, a measure of lung function, over 52 weeks against placebo, while the 120 milligram dose slowed the decline without reaching statistical significance. The company said the oral drug, a potential first-in-class LPA1 antagonist, was well tolerated at both doses, with discontinuations due to adverse events low and similar to placebo.

An RBC Capital Markets analyst called the high-dose result confounding and said it requires further explanation, and Bristol Myers said it will complete a full assessment of the data, discuss planned regulatory submissions with health authorities, and present detailed results at a future medical meeting. A separate Phase 3 trial in progressive pulmonary fibrosis is expected to report topline results in early 2027.
