Epiroc and Hancock Iron Ore have reached a new milestone at the Roy Hill mine in Australia, with more than 500 million tonnes of material moved autonomously using LinkOA, the equipment maker says. AHS project director Marcel Stoelman said reaching half a billion tonnes is significant, but the rate at which the operation has scaled is equally important: moving the first 100 million tonnes took more than three years, and the most recent 100 million tonnes took just five months.

Today 78 haul trucks have been converted from manual to fully autonomous operation and deployed at Roy Hill, creating what Epiroc calls the world's largest autonomous mixed fleet. To move 500 million tonnes, the autonomous fleet travelled more than 12.1 million kilometres. LinkOA enables mines to introduce autonomous haulage across mixed fleets, allowing different truck makes and models to operate within the same autonomous ecosystem.

Epiroc and Hancock have progressively expanded the system while maintaining production as the fleet transitioned from manual to autonomous operation. Epiroc Surface division president Omar Allel said the results demonstrate that mixed fleet autonomy can operate at significant scale and provide mines with greater flexibility in how they approach automation.

The milestone matters beyond one mine. Autonomous haulage has been a large fleet, single vendor story for a decade; a mixed fleet system moving half a billion tonnes at an accelerating rate is the clearest public evidence that retrofit autonomy can run a major iron ore operation, which changes the capital case for mid-tier mines that cannot replace their fleets.
