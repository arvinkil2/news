Brent crude fell 1.5 percent to $96.91 a barrel by 0629 GMT on Monday, with West Texas Intermediate down 1 percent to $89.85, after President Donald Trump said on Sunday that he was unlikely to order fresh attacks on Iran as long as talks to end the conflict progressed, Reuters reported. Bloomberg later reported Brent down more than 3 percent to trade near $95 a barrel, extending its biggest weekly drop since April 2020, on an unconfirmed report that Iran had made concessions on its demands over the Strait of Hormuz; Bloomberg named no source for the claim in the portion read, and no Iranian or US official has confirmed it.

By midday in New York the fall had deepened further, with Brent at 93.84 dollars, down 4.56 percent on the day, and West Texas Intermediate at 87.70 dollars, down 3.45 percent, in the site’s market tables updated at 12:32 p.m. Eastern.

The fall extends a 16 percent drop last week, the steepest since the start of the Covid-19 pandemic, and it comes while both sides still report shipping hit in or near the strait and no agreement has been reached. JPMorgan estimates global supply ran above demand by 1.1 million barrels a day in September and 1.4 million barrels a day in October, a surplus that adds to the downward pressure if the strait reopens.
