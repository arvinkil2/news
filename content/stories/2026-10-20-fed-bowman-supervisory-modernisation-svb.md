The Federal Reserve's supervision of Silicon Valley Bank failed on speed and focus, not on information, according to the independent review of the 2023 failure that Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman commissioned and described in Bangkok. Frontline examiners, risk specialists and Reserve Bank and Board staff knew about the bank's exposure to interest rate and liquidity risk at least nine full months before the FDIC took it into receivership, the review found, and long standing uncertainty about decision making authority impeded timely action. The bank accumulated 54 matters requiring attention or immediate attention between 2019 and its failure, yet none required it to address its core financial risks; supervisory attention centred on process, governance and controls while the exposures that killed the bank went un compelled.

Bowman was explicit about what the review rejects. It corrects the earlier mischaracterisation that social media caused the run, and the claim that the failure to act was caused by statutory tailoring or regulatory changes, and it finds that the Basel Committee and the Financial Stability Board took language nearly verbatim from expedited United States reports that were not based on complete information, then used those findings to justify additional regulatory work. International bodies, she argued, must validate the data they build standards on and refocus on material risks to safety and soundness and financial stability.

The Federal Reserve's response is organisational. Bowman set out clearer allocation of responsibility, authority and accountability between Board and Reserve Bank staff, clear decision rights for examiners and supervisory leadership, and direct escalation channels from examination teams to senior leadership, building on the Board's fall 2025 Statement of Supervisory Operating Principles. Modernisation, in her framing, is not doing less: it is prioritising material risks, tailoring intensity to the institution, and distinguishing issues capable of threatening safety and soundness from matters that are merely easiest to document.

For rates readers the lesson of the episode is the one the review restores to the centre: SVB was an interest rate risk failure inside the supervisory perimeter during a tightening cycle, and the official record of why it happened is now being rewritten by the supervisor itself, three years on.
