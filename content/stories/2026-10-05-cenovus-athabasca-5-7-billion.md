Cenovus Energy has entered a definitive agreement to acquire Athabasca Oil Corporation in a cash-and-stock transaction with an implied enterprise value of $5.7 billion, the companies announced Monday. Athabasca shareholders can elect $12.00 in cash per share, 0.264 of a Cenovus share, or a mix — pro-rated so the total consideration lands at 65 to 75 percent cash, with a maximum of $4.3 billion in cash and 44.4 million Cenovus shares.

The strategic fit is geographic: Athabasca adds about 45,000 barrels of oil equivalent a day of thermal production proximal to Cenovus's Christina Lake, May River and Thornbury assets, with over 75 years of proved-plus-probable reserve life at Leismer and Corner and a pathway to accelerate thermal production to 115,000 barrels a day by 2032. The deal also consolidates Duvernay Energy Corporation, an oil-weighted Kaybob Duvernay position with an option to grow to a sustainable 20,000 barrels a day. Cenovus expects about $85 million a year in corporate and commercial synergies, most of it in the first full year after closing.

The cash portion will be funded from cash on hand and short-term borrowings; Cenovus's $4 billion net-debt target is unchanged, with pro forma year-end 2026 net debt of $5.0 to $5.5 billion — under half a turn of adjusted funds flow at strip pricing. Both boards approved unanimously, directors and officers signed 2.2 percent support agreements, and the deal is expected to close in December 2026 with no financing contingency. CIBC Capital Markets advised Cenovus; McCarthy Tétrault was legal counsel. Cenovus hosts a conference call today at 9:00 a.m. ET.
