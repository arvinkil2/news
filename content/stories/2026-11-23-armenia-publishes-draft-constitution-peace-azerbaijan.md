Armenia's Ministry of Justice published the first draft of a new constitution on Monday, and Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan said he stakes his entire political, personal and moral credibility on it leading to a signed and ratified peace agreement with Azerbaijan and to what he called institutional, lasting and irreversible peace. The draft's Article 8 states that the objective of Armenia's foreign policy is to ensure peace, a departure, in Pashinyan's words, from the logic of the 1990 Declaration of Independence, and Reuters reported that the proposed text rewrites the preamble and removes the reference Baku has objected to.

Calling a referendum needs votes his Civil Contract faction does not have: Pashinyan said the faction falls short by six mandates and asked the opposition to vote to put the draft to the people. He pledged to resign if voters reject the constitution, and said that if they adopt it he will not run for prime minister at the next elections and will spend the intervening period finalising peace.
