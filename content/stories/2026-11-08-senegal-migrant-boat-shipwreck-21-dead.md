Latest: The death toll has risen to at least 34, the Senegalese Press Agency said later on Sunday, a figure the Action for Human Rights and Friendship association confirmed in a statement to AFP. An indeterminate number of people were still reported missing as the search continued, and the nationalities of all the victims had not been confirmed. President Bassirou Diomaye Faye said all state authorities and resources had been mobilised to locate the missing, care for survivors and support families. Authorities also said they had intercepted 142 migrants on Friday aboard a similar boat bound for Spain.

At least 21 people died in a shipwreck off Kébémer department in northwestern Senegal, the country's interior ministry said. One person was reported to have survived, search operations were underway, and the state news agency APS quoted a source as saying at least 20 bodies had washed up on a beach. It is not known how many people were on board the boat.

Senegal is a common transit country for migrants seeking to reach the Spanish Canary Islands and Europe by sea. Arrivals have risen sharply in a short time, with more than 700 migrants reaching the islands on Saturday alone and more than 3,300 arriving on El Hierro in just over a week, where local authorities have declared a state of emergency.
