Banks are offering CVC Capital Partners up to 11 billion euros, about $12 billion, in debt financing to support a possible takeover of Verisure, the professionally monitored security group listed in Stockholm, Bloomberg reported on November 25. The package under discussion comprises leveraged loans split into two slices, for banks and for institutional investors, alongside high-yield bonds, with private credit firms separately seeking a role providing junior financing.

CVC is working with advisers as it evaluates a possible offer. Talks are ongoing and there is no assurance the firm will move forward; spokespeople for CVC, Hellman and Friedman and Verisure declined to comment. Verisure shares rose as much as 7.5 percent on Wednesday and were 5.5 percent higher by late afternoon in Stockholm, putting the company's equity value at around 11 billion euros, roughly the size of the debt package being pitched.

Hellman and Friedman floated Verisure in Stockholm in October 2025 at a valuation of 13.7 billion euros and remains the largest shareholder with about 44 percent. Reuters reported in October that CVC was in the very early stages of assessing a bid, less than a year after the listing, and that Hellman and Friedman would probably retain an interest in any deal with CVC. The shares have since fallen by more than a third from the listing valuation.

A take-private on these terms would return to sponsor ownership, barely a year after the IPO, a business that provides monitored security services across 18 countries in Europe and Latin America. Whether CVC proceeds, and at what premium, remains undecided.
