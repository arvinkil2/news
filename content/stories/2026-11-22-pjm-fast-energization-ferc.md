The biggest US electric grid operator, PJM Interconnection, is proposing a plan to federal energy regulators to speed up the connection of power plants and the energy-intensive data centers that need them, while requiring data center companies to pay a larger share of grid costs. PJM covers 13 Mid-Atlantic and Midwest states and about 67 million people.

In its proposal to the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission, PJM wants to combine its separate processes for studying the connection of power generation, transmission and large loads such as data centers into a single process. The study period, required before a power plant or large data center can hook up to the grid, would fall to 12 months from two years. PJM is asking FERC to begin a 60-day comment period and issue a ruling within 120 days.

The pressure behind the filing is a supply and demand crunch that has driven sharp rises in power bills across the region and a roughly 6.8 gigawatt electricity supply shortfall. "Neither generation nor transmission is coming online quickly enough to keep pace with new demand growth driven mainly by large loads like data centers," PJM spokesman Jeff Shields said. "The factor that PJM planning can influence is how quickly these new resources are studied for reliability."

Combining the studies would also let PJM identify exactly where the grid needs upgrades for new data centers, giving states and utilities clear cost information so they can bill the companies directly instead of spreading the costs across other users. That cost allocation is contested ground: the trade group Data Center Coalition told PJM's board in October that a combined process might complicate interconnection and ultimately produce longer delays, not shorter ones.
