In covert tests run in spring 2026, the Government Accountability Office prompted four of the most used generative AI platforms, Claude, Grok, ChatGPT and Gemini, and each produced information GAO verified as sufficient to plan and execute attacks, including constructing and detonating a vehicle-borne improvised explosive device designed to cause mass casualties and targeting electrical grid substations with a drone or firearms. Each platform's safeguards did trigger, through denials, removal of information or account suspensions, but GAO circumvented them using common safeguard-bypassing techniques, and each platform produced its material in less than 12 hours.

GAO verified the viability of the plans through research, surveillance and interviews with experts, and presented the results with the platforms anonymised. Officials from the four companies generally told GAO their companies will continue to improve the safety of their platforms.
