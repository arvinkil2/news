**Latest (08:50 ET):** September CPI came in hotter than expected where it matters. Headline prices rose 0.6 percent on the month, matching consensus and up from 0.4 percent in August, lifting the annual rate to 3.7 percent against a 3.6 percent forecast and 3.4 percent in August. Core prices, excluding food and energy, rose 0.3 percent on the month, matching August and beating the 0.2 percent forecast, and 2.6 percent on the year, up from 2.4 percent and above the 2.5 percent expected. Energy remained the biggest driver of the headline, with energy prices up 20.4 percent on the year against 16.3 percent in August and gasoline pump prices up 7.1 percent in the month on EIA data, while food inflation eased to 2.5 percent on the year from 2.7 percent. Market summaries of the release pointed to services as the driver of the firmer core reading, with recreation, used cars and trucks, education, airline fares, medical care and apparel all rising.

The market reaction was immediate. S&P 500 futures turned negative within minutes of the release, with Dow futures down 0.16 percent, S&P 500 futures down 0.24 percent and Nasdaq 100 futures down 0.49 percent at 08:33 ET, two-year Treasury yields spiked, and market odds on a rate hike at the October 27 to 28 meeting jumped to about 50 percent, from roughly 22 percent on CME FedWatch before the release. Natixis economist Christopher Hodge, quoted in market coverage of the print, said the numbers were far enough from consensus to make October a debate and to give the hawks more ammunition, while his base case remains that the Fed does not move this month, given how Williams and Jefferson have argued there is no rush to tighten again. Bowman made the same argument hours before the release, saying she sees no urgent need for further action.

Going in, the Wall Street Journal poll had expected headline inflation of 3.6 percent year on year, up from 3.4 percent in August, on a 0.6 percent monthly rise, and core inflation of 2.5 percent, up from 2.4 percent, on a slower 0.2 percent monthly rise. Markets had settled on a pause this month: LSEG put the probability of no change on October 28 at 78 percent on Wednesday morning, while a December hike remained the baseline in strategist notes and three hikes in total were priced over the next 12 months. The week's Fed commentary pointed both ways: New York's John Williams backed one more hike this year but saw none needed in 2027, Boston's Susan Collins expected rates to rise a bit further this year, and Cleveland's Beth Hammack has argued for a quicker move to restrictive policy.

What follows now decides whether the repricing holds. The Federal Reserve Beige Book is released at 14:00 ET today. Thursday brings September PPI, retail sales and jobless claims, and Chair Kevin Warsh speaks later this week at the IMF annual meetings, his last scheduled appearance before the October 17 blackout ahead of the October 27 to 28 FOMC. A core reading at 0.4 percent would have forced a fuller revisit of the terminal rate; at 0.3 percent, the argument is narrower and now runs through Warsh's remarks and the PPI composition.
