Lupin has launched sitagliptin tablets in the United States after the FDA approved its abbreviated new drug application, the company announced on November 27. The launch covers 25 mg, 50 mg and 100 mg strengths.

The tablets are bioequivalent to Januvia, the reference drug marketed by Merck Sharp and Dohme, and are indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes.

Lupin cited IQVIA data putting Januvia's estimated annual US sales at 2,328.8 million dollars for the period to September 2026. That scale makes sitagliptin one of the larger oral diabetes brands to face generic competition, and Lupin's entry adds a large Indian generics supplier to the US market for the drug.

The company did not disclose its launch price, the number of other generic suppliers already on the market, or expected sales. Lupin, headquartered in Mumbai, lists diabetes among its core therapeutic areas in both the US and India.
