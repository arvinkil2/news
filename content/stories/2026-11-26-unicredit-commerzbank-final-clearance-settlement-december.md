UniCredit announced on November 26 that it has received the outstanding approvals from Poland's financial supervision authority, the KNF, for its voluntary public takeover offer for Commerzbank, and that all regulatory clearances required for settlement have now been obtained and all offer conditions fulfilled. The KNF approval was the final step because Commerzbank owns 69 percent of Warsaw-listed mBank.

Shareholders who tendered will receive their new UniCredit shares at their custodian banks no later than the tenth business day after the announcement, which UniCredit expects to be by December 7, 2026. The exchange offer, launched on May 5, 2026, offered 0.485 of a UniCredit share for each Commerzbank share. Reuters reported the offer concluded in July at a value of about 39 billion euros, handing UniCredit a further 17.6 percent and lifting its overall stake to just below 50 percent, a threshold it cannot cross without triggering a mandatory buyout offer for mBank under Polish law.

In parallel, UniCredit has asked Commerzbank to convene an extraordinary general meeting to elect new shareholder representatives to the supervisory board. Commerzbank said UniCredit wants to replace 9 of the 10 shareholder representatives and name chief executive Andrea Orcel as chairman, according to Reuters. UniCredit said the request is consistent with the German government's publicly stated support for a swift process and an orderly transition of control, and follows last week's agreement with Berlin resolving key issues including keeping Commerzbank's headquarters and listing in Frankfurt.

UniCredit first built its stake in September 2024. The deal has now cleared the European Central Bank, the US Federal Reserve and the European Commission, which approved it unconditionally on November 16 after finding moderate combined market shares in German banking. UniCredit has previously said new governance arrangements and implementation of its strategy for Commerzbank are expected by the first quarter of 2027, and that it plans to keep Commerzbank separate from its German unit HypoVereinsbank until at least 2028.
