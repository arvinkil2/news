A magnitude 6.5 earthquake struck west of Japan's Ogasawara Islands, also known as the Bonin Islands, at 02:09 UTC on Sunday (11:09 local time), centred at 27.8 degrees north, 139.6 degrees east, the US Geological Survey said in a reviewed solution. The quake sat 492 kilometres below the surface, and the Japan Meteorological Agency, which put the magnitude at 7.0, recorded a maximum shaking intensity of just 3 in Ogasawara Village.

That depth is why a quake this size did so little at the surface: the Geological Survey issued a green PAGER alert, indicating a low likelihood of casualties and damage, and no casualty or damage reports had appeared in the sources read. Two smaller deep quakes, magnitudes 4.8 and 4.2, followed in the same area within 40 minutes at similar depths.
