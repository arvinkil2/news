**Latest (Sunday, October 11):** Saturday's toll came in worse than first reported. Al Jazeera reports at least eight Palestinians were killed in Israeli attacks across Gaza on the ceasefire's first anniversary: Shahd Abdul-Razzaq al-Najjar, 8, was killed by a quadcopter drone in western Gaza City, four more died in a bombing near Haider Abdel Shafi roundabout, and others were killed in Tuffah, northern Gaza and Khan Younis. Arab News, citing medics, had put Saturday's toll at five, a discrepancy that reflects the difficulty of independent casualty verification. Gaza's Health Ministry said at least 1,481 Palestinians have been killed and 5,254 injured since the ceasefire took effect on October 10, 2025, and put the war's total at 74,370 killed and 175,371 injured since October 7, 2023. In the occupied West Bank, the Colonization and Wall Resistance Commission recorded at least 12 settler attacks on Palestinian farmers on Saturday morning, according to Wafa.

Al Jazeera's year-one assessment of Trump's Board of Peace adds institutional detail to the failure. The board, first proposed in September 2025 and recognized by a UN Security Council resolution in November as a "transitional administration," charged member states a $1 billion annual fee. Its February Washington summit drew $7 billion in pledges, while aid agencies said reconstruction would cost closer to $70 billion; a 66-project, $2.4 billion recovery plan was unveiled at last month's UN General Assembly. The results: Hamas released all 168 living captives and announced the dissolution of its government, though Israel dismissed that as a stunt. The National Committee for the Administration of Gaza operates from Cairo because Israel has not granted it entry. About 227 aid trucks enter daily against a planned 600. The International Stabilization Force never deployed: six countries (Indonesia, Morocco, Kazakhstan, Albania, Kosovo and Uganda) committed personnel toward a 20,000-troop and 12,000-police force under US Central Command, but Indonesia put its 8,000-troop pledge on hold. Hamas has agreed to hand its weapons to the committee but rejects the "disarmament" clause while Israeli attacks continue; Israel says it will not advance the plan until Hamas disarms. Israel now occupies about 60 percent of the Strip, beyond the 50 percent the plan specified, and the "Yellow Line" buffer has been pushed deeper in. The board also told the Associated Press it will not work with UNRWA, effectively sidelining the agency, even as the UN General Assembly extended UNRWA's mandate to 2029 in June. Global pro-Palestinian protests marked the anniversary in multiple cities.

**Latest (Saturday afternoon):** Israeli strikes killed at least five Palestinians in the Gaza Strip on Saturday, health officials said: four died in a strike near Panda Mall in Gaza City and one in the Tuffah neighborhood. The Israeli military said the two strikes targeted militants, without giving further information. Israel has stepped up strikes against Hamas in recent weeks, saying the group has been rearming and attacking in violation of the ceasefire; Hamas denies rebuilding its arsenal and accuses Israel of undermining the US-brokered deal agreed a year ago.

**Latest (Saturday, October 10):** A year into the truce, the deal's grand promises have collapsed into stalemate. Arab News reported Saturday that Trump's 20-point agreement halted major military operations, freed all remaining hostages and increased humanitarian access, but has failed on a new Palestinian administration, the disarmament of Hamas, a full Israeli withdrawal and a postwar reconstruction process, with almost nothing rebuilt. Hamas officially dissolved its government in July but remains the dominant power in the roughly 40 percent of Gaza not controlled by the Israeli military, while an International Stabilization Force announced in February never materialized after Indonesia, which had pledged 8,000 troops, put its plans on hold. Netanyahu, facing elections on October 27, hardened his stance over the summer, openly defying Trump by declaring Israel would not withdraw from any part of Gaza until Hamas is completely disarmed. The Gaza Health Ministry counts over 1,400 Palestinians killed and five Israeli soldiers killed since the ceasefire took effect, and reconstruction is estimated at 71.4 billion dollars, barely begun.

The humanitarian picture is grim. The Palestinian Health Ministry said Friday that more than 18,500 patients, including at least 4,000 children, need medical evacuation, with more than 1,000 dying while waiting; none of Gaza's 36 hospitals operates at full capacity, and 80 percent of structures are damaged. A World Health Organization estimate puts roughly 24 percent of the 175,000 wounded with life-changing injuries, a quarter of them children. On hunger, AFP reporting from Khan Yunis Saturday noted that the UN-backed IPC declared famine in August 2025 and lifted the declaration in December after two months of improved supplies, but the World Food Programme's Shaun Hughes said the food security situation is still extremely serious, with 1.4 million of about 2.1 million people acutely food insecure and 200,000 one step below famine. Medecins du Monde found 93 percent of pregnant or breastfeeding women showed symptoms of anaemia.

More than 100 humanitarian, human rights and civil society organizations said Friday that the Gaza ceasefire exists in name only, one year after the US-backed deal was announced. "The ceasefire exists in name only, with Israeli forces continuing to kill and injure Palestinians daily, and restrictions on aid, equipment and rebuilding materials preventing families from recovering," the joint statement said.

Signatories include Amnesty International, the Norwegian Refugee Council, Save the Children, Oxfam and Greenpeace. They said Israeli attacks have killed more than 1,400 Palestinians, including at least 327 children, in the year since the ceasefire took effect under the Gaza Board of Peace framework, or nearly one child killed every day. Norwegian Refugee Council chief Jan Egeland called it "utterly shameful that a full year after the ceasefire was signed, so little has improved," and Amnesty's Agnes Callamard said the failure to hold Israel accountable "continues to fuel the atrocity crimes on a mass scale."

The statement also warned that 1.4 million people face crisis-level hunger or worse through the end of the year, and that Israeli forces have squeezed civilians into ever more crowded areas by moving the so-called Yellow Line buffer. The Palestinian Health Ministry puts the three-year toll at 74,254 killed, about 3 percent of Gaza's pre-war population.
