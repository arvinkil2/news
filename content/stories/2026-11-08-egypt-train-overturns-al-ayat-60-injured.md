Latest: Two trains collided at Al-Ayat in Giza province on Sunday, and Egypt's health ministry put the initial toll at four passengers killed and 88 others being treated for injuries in hospitals around Giza, Arab News reported, carrying AFP. State owned media had earlier reported 120 people wounded, without clarifying the degree of injury.

Egypt's railway authority said a train travelling from Giza to Fayoum crashed into the rear of another service heading south from Cairo to Aswan, derailing several carriages. Emergency crews used cranes to remove the wreckage, and authorities launched an investigation, with no cause given. President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi said he was following the accident with great concern and directed authorities to hold accountable anyone found to have been negligent.

Earlier reports described carriages overturning at Al-Ayat, injuring about 60 people, BBC Arabic reported, with the injured taken to hospital and more than 15 ambulances sent to the site. Train movement between Giza and Upper Egypt stopped in both directions after the accident while civil protection teams and the authorities worked to clear the site.

Al-Ayat was also the site of Egypt's worst rail disaster, in 2002, when a fire on a train carrying holiday travellers south of Cairo killed more than 370 people.
