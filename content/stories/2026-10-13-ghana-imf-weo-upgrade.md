Ghana received one of the larger upgrades in the IMF's October outlook. The Fund now expects the economy to grow 5.7% in 2026, up 1.1 percentage points from its April estimate of 4.6%, and to end the year with inflation at 4.8%, sharply below both the April forecast of 7.9% and the government's own budget assumptions. Growth is expected to moderate to 5.2% in 2027.

The revision rests on delivery rather than hope. The economy grew 6.0% in the second quarter for a first-half rate of 6.2% on Ghana Statistical Service figures, September inflation printed 5.2%, and the Fund credited fiscal discipline, targeted spending and strong commodity export earnings, particularly gold, which has supported the trade balance and helped build buffers. Adherence to targets under the Policy Coordination Instrument programme was cited as a further support.

The inflation number is the rates-relevant one. A 4.8% year-end print would sit below the Bank of Ghana's target band of 8% plus or minus 2 percentage points, and would mark a second consecutive year of single-digit year-end inflation after 2025. That trajectory, if it holds, gives the central bank room that most of the central banks in this edition do not have: easing from a position of undershooting, rather than tightening against an overshoot.

The Fund's caveat is concentration. Sources close to the Fund flagged Ghana's heavy reliance on gold export earnings and urged diversification, a vulnerability that matters more, not less, when the upgraded forecast itself leans on those same earnings.
