Hong Kong's government has raised its real GDP growth forecast for 2026 to 4.8 percent, from the 3.5 to 4.5 percent range set in the August round of review, in its Third Quarter Economic Report published on Friday. Third quarter growth was confirmed at 5.2 percent year on year, up from 4.2 percent in the second quarter, and growth over the first three quarters reached 5.1 percent, the fastest pace for the period in five years, underpinned by buoyant AI-related external trade and solid domestic demand; merchandise exports surged 64.9 percent year on year in value terms in September.

The same report cut the 2026 inflation forecasts, to 1.9 percent underlying and 2.0 percent headline, from 2.5 and 2.6 percent in August, as pass-through from higher international oil prices proved slower than expected. The government said fourth quarter momentum looked sustained, while flagging Middle East tensions, a possible resurgence of trade tensions, rising sovereign bond yields and a potential correction if AI expectations prove too optimistic as the risks to watch.
