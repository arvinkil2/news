UK power margins stayed thin for a third consecutive day on Thursday, with the grid's spare capacity short by more than 2.7 gigawatts between noon and 8 p.m., as cold weather drives strong withdrawals from European gas storage. The Dutch TTF front month stood at 87.28 euros per megawatt hour on November 26, German baseload power at 222.07 euros, and the Pacific LNG benchmark JKM at 28.07 dollars per million Btu, a spread that keeps Europe bidding for flexible cargoes. Engie's Chris Guth told Montel the cold does not help demand in a tight market that lacks LNG supply.

Lean hogs were the weak leg of the US livestock complex on Friday, with cash prices down about 2 percent near six year lows on continued pressure in pork markets, even as live cattle and feeder cattle closed higher. The divergence, strong beef prices against weak pork, is a consumer demand signal worth watching into the December data.

Congo's suspension of all cobalt exports takes effect on November 30, until further notice, under the ARECOMS notice covered in the mining beat's November 27 story. LME cobalt futures had already jumped about a third in the week before the ban, so the effective date itself is a checkpoint for shipments and paperwork rather than a new price event.

Airline fuel costs are becoming the demand side story of the oil rally. OilPrice.com reported that soaring fuel prices threaten to push more carriers into bankruptcy, after Norse Atlantic's Norwegian unit filed this week; the item is single sourced commentary rather than a verified industry count, and is carried here on that basis. Separately, January Brent expires on Monday, November 30, the day after the OPEC+ meeting, concentrating the rollover decision and the contract roll in the same 48 hours.
