The Tennessee Valley Authority has entered its first capacity commitment agreement under the new tariff it adopted for large data center loads, and the deal is worth $1.7 billion in aggregate payments, the federal utility disclosed in its fiscal 2026 annual report. TVA signed the agreement on November 10, about six weeks after the tariff took effect on October 1.

Under the agreement, an initial payment of $31 million was due on execution, with the balance payable in 59 subsequent installments. The charges are based on a rate of approximately $1.5 million per megawatt of requested capacity above 5 MW. TVA did not name the customer, the site or the requested capacity. Dividing the $1.7 billion by the stated rate implies roughly 1,100 MW of chargeable capacity, which is arithmetic from the disclosed figures rather than a TVA disclosure, though a TVA spokesperson confirmed to WVLT that the calculation was broadly correct without confirming the capacity.

The tariff, approved by TVA's board in August and phased in through 2029, is a rationing device as much as a price: TVA has said an additional 27.5 GW of prospective data center load has expressed interest in its territory, equal to about 85 percent of its current generating capacity, and it set a 2,000 MW first-year cohort for fiscal 2027 on a first-come basis. A single agreement implying about 1.1 GW would take more than half of that cohort.

The deal settles, for one utility at least, the argument over who pays for the grid buildout behind AI data centers. Rather than socialising the cost of new capacity across all ratepayers, TVA is collecting the reservation price upfront from the customer causing the load, and booking it as a new source of liquidity.
