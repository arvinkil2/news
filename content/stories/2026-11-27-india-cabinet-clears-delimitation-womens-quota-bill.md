India's Union Cabinet, chaired by Prime Minister Narendra Modi, on Friday cleared a fresh Constitution Amendment Bill that links the implementation of 33 percent reservation for women in the Lok Sabha and state assemblies to a delimitation exercise. Under the reported formula, every state's Lok Sabha seats would rise by 50 percent, while the existing proportions of representation between states would remain protected until 2051.

The design answers the central objection to delimitation: that states which controlled population growth, many of them in the south, would lose relative weight to faster growing northern states. A uniform increase preserves each state's share while expanding the House.

The clearance comes ahead of a special sitting of Parliament beginning November 30, with the Lok Sabha meeting first and the Rajya Sabha following on December 2. The government's announcement convening the Houses did not list the bill on the agenda, but the cabinet decision has raised expectations that the revised legislation will be introduced during the sitting.

An earlier version of the delimitation linked women's reservation bill, introduced in a special sitting in April, failed to secure the required support in the Lok Sabha. A constitutional amendment needs a two thirds majority in both Houses. The government is reported to be counting on regional parties including the DMK, the Sharad Pawar led Nationalist Congress Party and the Bharat Rashtra Samithi, while the opposition INDIA bloc remains against the legislation and meets on Saturday to settle its strategy.
