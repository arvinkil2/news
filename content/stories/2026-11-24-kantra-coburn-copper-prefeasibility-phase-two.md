Kantra Copper has approved the next phase of studies at its Coburn copper project after finding a commercially viable route to transport ore for processing. The decision follows a logistics assessment examining how material could be moved from Coburn to the company's Kanmantoo processing facility in South Australia, and combined with positive metallurgical results announced in October, prompted the board to approve phase two of the project's prefeasibility study.

Managing director Bob Fulker said the results had addressed two significant uncertainties identified during the company's initial assessment, and that while there is still 18 months of prefeasibility work before a financial investment decision, the two material risks identified during due diligence have been de-risked to a level where the board approved advancing the project. The proposed route includes an access road connecting Coburn to a potential rail siding near Cockburn, about 20 km from the project, with a second siding and rail loop at Kanmantoo.

Kantra estimates transport costs at approximately five to seven cents per tonne per kilometre, although those figures require further assessment. The company expects the full Coburn prefeasibility study to cost $11 million to $13 million, including $970,000 spent through October, and has increased its 2026 major capital expenditure guidance to $18.5 million to $19.5 million to accommodate the additional work.

Drilling is also underway at Coburn, with six holes totalling 1,318 metres completed as of November 20, intended to support further metallurgical testing, with assay results pending. Fulker said Coburn has the potential to become a meaningful source of future mill feed for Kanmantoo and to enhance the long term value of its processing infrastructure. The second study phase will examine mine design, processing arrangements, infrastructure, project economics and regulatory requirements.
