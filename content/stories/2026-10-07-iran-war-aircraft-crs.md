**Latest (Sunday, October 11):** fresh reporting on the same CRS numbers adds color and a new total-war cost figure. CNN's account, via Egypt Independent, names some of the Pentagon's most-prized assets among the 81: an F-35A fighter jet and an E-3 Sentry airborne-warning aircraft, with MQ-9 Reaper drones accounting for more than half of the losses. The total cost of the aircraft lost is between $1.9 billion and $3.3 billion depending on the Defense Department's replacement plans, per the report's citation of CBO figures, and the total cost of the war is "much higher," at least $45 billion, sources told CNN, not counting damage to infrastructure at US bases in the region. Some aircraft may have returned to service, including eight F-15 Strike Eagles that sustained light damage in the September Iranian strike on Muwaffaq Salti Air Base in Jordan. The report draws on Defense Department statements, its inspector general, US Central Command, and news reports, and poses questions for Congress on whether lawmakers get enough Pentagon information on losses, whether there is industrial capacity to replace them, and whether aging, limited platforms like the E-3 Sentry create capability gaps in other theaters. The $45 billion total is single-sourced to CNN's sources and has not been independently verified; treat it as reported.

An updated Congressional Research Service report lists 81 US military aircraft as lost or damaged since the United States launched Operation Epic Fury against Iran on February 28, nearly double the 42 the service counted in May, Military Times reported Wednesday. The October 5 revision adds jets damaged in a September Iranian strike on a base in Jordan, helicopter losses drawn from a Pentagon inspector general report, and a sharply higher count of lost MQ-9 Reapers, now 45. CRS cautions the total is not a count of destroyed aircraft, noting some damaged aircraft may have been returned to service.

The report also underscores the war's climbing cost: the Congressional Budget Office estimated the Pentagon had spent about $38 billion through August 1, including up to $3.3 billion to replace battle losses if fully funded. The White House asked Congress in June for $87.6 billion in emergency funding, $67.1 billion of it for the Pentagon, which Congress has not approved.
