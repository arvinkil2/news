Update: Cyclone Arnab has crossed the Andhra Pradesh and Yanam coasts and weakened from a very severe cyclonic storm into a severe cyclonic storm, the India Meteorological Department said in a release. The department said the system crossed between 2 a.m. and 8 a.m. local time, southwest of Yanam, about 20 kilometres east-northeast of Narsapur, bringing heavy rain, with some locations recording more than 200 millimetres, and fallen trees and power poles blocking roads across coastal districts. Chief Minister N Chandrababu Naidu reviewed the situation on Wednesday morning and ordered swift restoration of power and roads. One Indian outlet reported two electrocution deaths in separate incidents; the state government's stated position remains that no loss of life has been reported, and that conflict is flagged in this story's data note rather than resolved here.

Cyclone Arnab started making landfall in India's Andhra Pradesh state in the early hours of Wednesday, bringing high winds and heavy rain that uprooted trees and disrupted power supply. The India Meteorological Department forecast winds of 125 to 135 kilometres an hour, gusting to 150, over the landfall area, and a storm surge of 1.5 to 2.5 metres above normal tide likely to inundate low lying areas of the state. Andhra Pradesh officials put evacuations at more than 84,000 people, schools were closed across coastal districts, and some train and flight services were cancelled, and the Andhra Pradesh chief minister's office said no loss of life had been reported so far.
