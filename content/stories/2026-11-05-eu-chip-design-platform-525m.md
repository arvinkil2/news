The European Commission launched the European Chips Design Platform on November 4, an instrument that will mobilise 525 million euros to support a new generation of European chip design companies. Executive Vice-President Henna Virkkunen launched the platform as a key initiative under the EU Chips Act.

The money splits two ways. A total of 440 million euros of public investment from the European Union and member states is available as grants to help design companies meet the cost of developing chips for commercial use. The remaining 85 million euros funds the platform itself, which will give design companies access to design tools, design support services, intellectual property blocks and access to manufacturing capacity. Of the total, 285 million euros comes from the EU budget and 240 million euros from the participating states of the Chips Joint Undertaking.

The starting point is unusually weak for a bloc that subsidises fabrication at scale. According to the Commission, European chip design companies account for less than 1 percent of global revenue in the segment. Grants run from up to 100,000 euros for early stage proof of concept work to up to 16 million euros for more developed companies taking a design toward commercialisation, according to the programme details carried with the launch.

The instrument marks a shift in European semiconductor policy. The Chips Act and its proposed successor, Chips Act 2.0, proposed in June 2026 as part of a technological sovereignty package, have concentrated on manufacturing capacity and supply chain resilience. Design is where the value in the chip industry concentrates, and it is the layer where Europe, outside a handful of firms, barely participates. Whether 440 million euros of grants moves that share is an open question; the programme's own framing is that manufacturing investment alone would not secure Europe's position.
