The Reserve Bank of India announced Friday it will conduct a variable-rate reverse repo auction on Monday, October 12, for a notified amount of Rs 2,00,000 crore with a 3-day tenor, the window running from 9:30 to 10:00 AM with reversal on Thursday, October 15. The VRRR, which pays banks to park surplus cash with the RBI, is the central bank's main tool for absorbing system liquidity without permanent sales, and the Rs 2 lakh crore notified amount is a sizable drawdown for a three-day operation.

**Update (Monday, October 12):** the auction results are in, and they were light. The RBI accepted Rs 37,558 crore of the Rs 2,00,000 crore notified, at a cut-off and weighted average rate of 5.49%, just a basis point below the 5.50% repo rate. Banks offered less than a fifth of the notified amount, which suggests system liquidity is not as flush as the large notified size implied, and it takes some pressure off the absorption narrative for the week.

Tuesday brings the second leg: the Rs 25,000 crore open-market sale of government securities announced in the same Friday package and covered in yesterday's edition. Wednesday adds a third leg, a regular Rs 23,000 crore auction of 91-day, 182-day and 364-day Treasury bills (Rs 8,000 crore, Rs 8,000 crore and Rs 7,000 crore respectively) settling Thursday, October 15.

The stack of operations completes a week of tightening that began with Wednesday's 25 basis point repo-rate hike to 5.50% and Friday evening's emergency rupee package, including the special dollar window for oil marketing companies and the 20% Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve on large rupee derivatives. The pattern is deliberate: the RBI is draining liquidity through rates, bond sales and now short-term reverse repos at the same time it is rationing dollar demand. Whether the VRRR is a one-off calibration around the OMO sale or the start of structurally tighter system liquidity to support the currency will be read from how quickly the central bank returns to the window after Thursday's reversal.

**Update (Tuesday, October 13):** both of Tuesday's operations have now reported. The open-market sale was fully taken: the RBI accepted the entire Rs 25,000 crore notified across five securities, with the largest allotments in the 6.10% GS 2031 (Rs 8,430 crore at a 7.2236% cut-off) and the 7.18% GS 2033 (Rs 9,415 crore at 7.3947%), and nothing accepted in the 7.10% GS 2034. Cut-offs ran from 7.1428% on the 7.88% GS 2030 to 7.3947% at the long end. The overnight VRRR the same day drew Rs 71,580 crore of offers against Rs 1,50,000 crore notified, all accepted at a 5.49% cut-off, a fuller take-up than Monday's 3-day operation but still under half the notified size. The October 12 money-market statement shows why the overnight window stays light: banks parked Rs 2,06,081 crore in the standing deposit facility at 5.25% that day, and net liquidity absorbed from the day's operations was Rs 2,43,633 crore, with cash balances of Rs 8,01,723 crore against an average daily reserve requirement of Rs 8,17,219 crore for the fortnight ending October 15.

**Update (Wednesday, October 14):** the week's third leg has now reported, and it cleared in full. The RBI accepted the entire Rs 23,000 crore of Treasury bills notified: Rs 8,000 crore of 91-day bills at a cut-off yield of 5.7281%, Rs 8,000 crore of 182-day at 6.2896%, and Rs 7,000 crore of 364-day at 6.4891%. The one-year bill clearing almost a full percentage point above the repo rate is the cleanest measure yet of how far last week's hike and the accompanying drain have travelled along the short curve.
