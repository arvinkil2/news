Flavio Bolsonaro says he did not lose Brazil's presidential election to his opponent. "I did not lose to my opponent. I lost the election to the system," the senator told Fox News Digital in an interview published on Sunday, his first extended account since leaving Brazil after the October 25 runoff, in which President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva won a fourth term with 51.8 percent of the vote to Bolsonaro's 48.2 percent, nearly 60 million votes.

Bolsonaro alleged that Supreme Court Justice Alexandre de Moraes opened an investigation against him days before the election to damage his reputation, and that "just seven minutes after the official election results were announced," Moraes moved to seize his passports and bar him from leaving the country. He acknowledged he has no evidence of fraud in the vote itself, while arguing that Brazil's electoral system is flawed because a recount cannot be demanded. These timing and motive claims are Bolsonaro's own; the interview carries no response from the Supreme Court or from Moraes.

The documented record from October is narrower. After the runoff, Moraes ordered Bolsonaro to surrender his passports to the Federal Police within 24 hours as part of an investigation into alleged money laundering, which Bolsonaro denies, and the order also barred contact with a lawyer friend linked to betting companies under suspicion. When Bolsonaro did not surrender the documents, Brazilian authorities cancelled his passports, including his diplomatic passport as a congressman, according to reporting at the time citing Folha de S.Paulo.

Bolsonaro said he and his family crossed into Argentina and flew to Miami, and that President Donald Trump granted them political asylum. He said he left not to escape but to continue fighting Moraes and the Supreme Court from abroad. Right wing candidates performed strongly in Brazil's congressional and gubernatorial races in the same elections, and Bolsonaro is urging supporters to oppose the re-election of the lower house speaker. What would test his account is a formal statement from the Supreme Court on the timing and basis of the passport order, which has not appeared in the reporting.
