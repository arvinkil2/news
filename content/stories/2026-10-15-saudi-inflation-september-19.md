Saudi consumer price inflation remains the Gulf's exception. The General Authority for Statistics put annual inflation at 1.9 percent in September, up 0.1 points from August, with the overall index unchanged on the month. Housing, water, electricity, gas and other fuels rose 3.6 percent year on year, driven by a 3.5 percent rise in actual housing rents, and contributed 0.7 percentage points to the headline rate, the largest single contribution. Transport rose 2.3 percent and food and beverages 1.4 percent, each adding 0.3 points.

The rest of the basket was mixed in a way that keeps the print benign. Personal care and miscellaneous goods rose 3.3 percent, including a 12.9 percent rise in jewellery and watch prices, recreation rose 2.5 percent on a 4.5 percent rise in holiday packages, and insurance and financial services rose 2.0 percent. Furnishings and household equipment fell 0.5 percent and clothing and footwear fell 0.4 percent, offsetting part of the housing pressure. On the month, education rose 1.2 percent while recreation fell 0.6 percent and restaurants and accommodation fell 0.5 percent. The index is compiled from a 582 item basket based on the 2023 Household Income and Expenditure Survey.

The regional comparison in the same release round is the analytically useful part. Dubai's annual inflation eased to 5.03 percent in September from 5.54 percent in August, Oman's slowed to 3.2 percent from 3.4 percent, Bahrain's latest reading was 3.0 percent in August, Kuwait's 2.19 percent in July and Qatar's 2.21 percent in June. The Gulf is experiencing the same shipping and energy shock at very different inflation rates, which reflects subsidy regimes, housing weights and the degree of administered pricing more than monetary policy, since the region's currencies are pegged to the dollar and import the Federal Reserve's stance.

For Saudi funding, 1.9 percent inflation with a flat monthly index means real yields on riyal debt stay comfortably positive without any domestic tightening debate. The watch item is housing: rents rising 3.5 percent and contributing nearly two fifths of headline inflation is the one component with the persistence to matter if energy driven transport costs stay elevated into the winter.
