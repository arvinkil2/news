January natural gas futures, the front month since December expired on Wednesday, traded at 3.52 dollars per million Btu at 10:00 ET on Friday, down 13.9 cents or 3.8 percent from Wednesday's settlement of 3.659 dollars, and fell as much as 5 percent to 3.45 dollars earlier in the session. Updated forecasts showed milder temperatures across key consuming regions for early December after the Thanksgiving storm moved through, cutting expected heating demand and the size of coming storage withdrawals.

The contract roll explains part of the week's apparent jumpiness. December futures expired on November 25 at 3.269 dollars, while January settled that day at 3.659 dollars, a premium of about 39 cents that prices January as the coldest, heaviest demand month of the winter. Moves measured on continuous series across the roll overstate the underlying change in either direction.

Underneath the weather trade, the balances are two sided. LNG feedgas demand hit a record 21.8 billion cubic feet a day on November 14, roughly 19 percent of US production, and the EIA storage report on Wednesday was expected to show the first withdrawal of the heating season, with consensus at a 23 billion cubic foot draw for the week ended November 20. Against that, storage sits about 80 billion cubic feet above the five year average and production runs near record highs, which caps how far weather rallies can run without sustained cold.

The near term path is a forecast contest. A daily settle back above Wednesday's 3.659 dollars would reopen the 3.75 dollar area traders are watching; continued mild revisions into December would instead press the market back toward the low 3 dollar range where the expiring December contract finished.
