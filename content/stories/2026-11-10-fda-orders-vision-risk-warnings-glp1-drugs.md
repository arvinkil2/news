The Food and Drug Administration said on Monday that it is requiring application holders for all GLP-1 receptor agonist medications to add information to the Warnings and Precautions section of their labeling about a potential increased risk of nonarteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy, or NAION. The condition, caused by decreased blood flow to the front of the optic nerve, can produce acute, painless decreased vision in one eye, including permanent vision loss, and cases have been reported for every marketed GLP-1 product, CNBC reported.

The FDA said a causal relationship has not been established. An analysis of 126 trials with more than 134,000 patients, cited by CNBC from the agency's review, found 6.4 cases of NAION per 100,000 patient years among GLP-1 users against 3.8 among patients on other treatments or placebos. Citi analysts said the class wide action should have limited effect on GLP-1 uptake, given the condition's rarity and the drugs' established benefits.
