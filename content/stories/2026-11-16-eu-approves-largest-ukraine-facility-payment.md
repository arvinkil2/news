**Latest.** The European Commission said on Friday that it has released 7.9 billion euros to Ukraine, the exact disbursement being 7,859,441,144 euros, to support Kyiv's financing needs and keep its public administration functioning. It is the ninth disbursement under the Ukraine Facility and the largest single payment made under it, and it takes total support provided under the Ukraine Plan to 40 billion euros since mid-2024, including member state and partner contributions and funds channelled through the plan from the Ukraine Support Loan. Of Friday's payment, 5.1 billion euros comes from the Ukraine Support Loan, taking disbursements through the facility component of the loan to more than 5.9 billion euros. Commission president Ursula von der Leyen said Ukraine continues to deliver on reforms while defending itself, and that real progress on reforms brings real results.

The Commission said the payment follows its assessment of Ukraine's payment request on September 30, after which the Council concluded that Ukraine had fulfilled sixteen reform and investment steps linked to the ninth instalment, together with steps carried over from the fourth, seventh and eighth instalments and one brought forward from the tenth, with one ninth-instalment step assessed as partially fulfilled. The reforms span the judicial system, the fight against corruption and money laundering, financial markets, public asset management, energy, the agri-food market, digital transformation and the green transition. Ukraine also mobilised 948 million euros in public investments in education, healthcare, housing and the demining of agricultural land, of which about 451 million euros went to sub-national authorities. For the rest of 2026, 15.7 billion euros in EU support remains available to Ukraine, including just over 14 billion euros under the Ukraine Support Loan, split between 10.2 billion euros for defence industrial capacity and 3.9 billion euros in budget support, and almost 1.6 billion euros under the initial Ukraine Facility, all subject to the plan's conditions. The Commission put overall support from the EU and its member states since 2022 at 245.5 billion euros, including 3.8 billion euros from the proceeds of immobilised Russian assets, and restated its commitment to deliver the 90 billion euro Ukraine Support Loan foreseen for 2026 and 2027.

The Council of the European Union adopted a decision on Monday that will allow Kyiv to soon receive just under 8 billion euros under the Ukraine Facility, of which around 5 billion euros comes from resources made available from the Ukraine Support Loan in 2026. The Council called it the largest-ever single disbursement to Ukraine under the facility, which has more than 50 billion euros in grants and loans available through 2027.

The payment follows Ukraine's fulfilment of over 20 target steps covering the judicial system, the fight against corruption and money laundering, financial markets, public asset management, energy, digital transformation and other reform areas under the Ukraine Plan. Once this ninth disbursement is made, Ukraine will have received over 40 billion euros under the plan since mid-2024.
