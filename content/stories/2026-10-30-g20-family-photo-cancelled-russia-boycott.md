Latest: Rubio said on Saturday that he spoke briefly with Lavrov at the Atlanta meeting about an opportunity for Trump and Putin to meet in China at the APEC summit next month, Reuters reported. The brief contact produced no announced change on Ukraine: the meeting itself closed without a joint statement, and the next leaders' encounter already on the calendar is the G20 summit in Miami in mid-December, to which Putin is invited.

The meeting ended with no joint closing statement and no group photo. Tagesschau reported that the two days of talks in Atlanta closed on Saturday without a common text, with the rifts between the participants, above all over Russia, visible throughout. Germany's Johann Wadephul said the one commonality was that everyone in the room knew the world is in an extraordinarily critical situation, and pressed for grain to be transportable from Ukraine and from Russia to the Global South. Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov, who sat as a regular participant at the table, criticised Germany and the Europeans at his press conference and said he saw no progress in practice in relations with the United States either, despite Trump's attempts at rapprochement with Russia. Rubio stressed that the economic challenges facing the G20 countries are complex and interconnected and must be worked on together, and at his closing press conference, reported by RIA Novosti, he said Trump considers it important that Russia is part of the discussions within the G20. The leaders' summit follows in mid-December at Trump's golf resort in Miami; the Russian president is invited and whether he takes part is open.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio cancelled the family photo that traditionally opens the G20 foreign ministers' meeting after about a third of the countries at the Atlanta talks said they would not appear beside any Russian representatives, including Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov. Germany's Johann Wadephul had warned that European partners would not be photographed with Lavrov, the United Kingdom's Ed Miliband took the same position, and Australia's Penny Wong condemned Russia's invasion of Ukraine in front of Lavrov in the opening session, with her office saying Australia supported the cancellation and would not normalise the invasion. Rubio opened the talks with an appeal for unity, but participants said the disputes over Russia, Poland's bid for G20 membership and the absence of member country South Africa, which was not invited, will probably prevent a joint communique when the meeting wraps up on Saturday.
