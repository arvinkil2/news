Latest: the meeting ended without a joint closing statement. Tagesschau reported that the two days of talks in Atlanta closed on Saturday with no agreed text and no formal results presented, after the dispute over Russia's war against Ukraine blocked any common language. Germany's Johann Wadephul called the meeting relatively conflictual. Rubio said the absence of a joint statement was regrettable but not the end of the world, said the meeting was not a failure, and said Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov's participation had been valuable because it let positions be stated plainly in the same room. At his closing press conference, reported by RIA Novosti, Rubio said President Donald Trump considers it important that Russia is part of the discussions within the G20. The EU's foreign policy chief accused Lavrov of spreading lies at the meeting. Attention now moves to the G20 leaders' summit in Florida in December; whether Zelenskyy attends is still open.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio cancelled the family photo that traditionally opens the G20 foreign ministers' meeting after about a third of the countries at the Atlanta talks said they would not appear beside any Russian representatives, including Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov. Germany's Johann Wadephul had warned that European partners would not be photographed with Lavrov, the United Kingdom's Ed Miliband took the same position, and Australia's Penny Wong condemned Russia's invasion of Ukraine in front of Lavrov in the opening session, with her office saying Australia supported the cancellation and would not normalise the invasion. Rubio opened the talks with an appeal for unity, but participants said the disputes over Russia, Poland's bid for G20 membership and the absence of member country South Africa, which was not invited, will probably prevent a joint communique when the meeting wraps up on Saturday.
