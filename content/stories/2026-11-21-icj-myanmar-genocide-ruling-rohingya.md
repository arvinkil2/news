The International Court of Justice ruled on Thursday that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya during the military's 2017 clearance operations in northern Rakhine State, deciding the case brought by The Gambia by nine votes to six. It is the first time the court has found a state responsible for committing genocide under the 1948 Genocide Convention. The court found that Myanmar's security forces intentionally committed killing, the causing of serious bodily or mental harm, and measures intended to prevent births against the Rohingya, and that Myanmar also failed in its obligations to prevent genocide and to punish those responsible.

The court ordered Myanmar to cease its continuing violations, to investigate and, where warranted, prosecute and punish those responsible, and to provide reparations to Rohingya victims. The Associated Press, reporting evidence cited by the court, put the number of Rohingya killed at about 100,000; some 1.2 million Rohingya now live in camps in Bangladesh, and nearly all Rohingya have been denied citizenship since 1982. The judgment is final, with no appeal, but the court has no enforcement power of its own: if Myanmar does not comply, The Gambia can take the matter to the UN Security Council.

Rights groups moved immediately to convert the ruling into pressure. Amnesty International's secretary general, Agnes Callamard, called for a halt to all transfers of weapons, security and surveillance equipment and military assistance to Myanmar, and for the Security Council to impose a global arms embargo and refer the situation to the International Criminal Court. Justice for All, a US organisation, urged sanctions aimed at Myanmar's oil and gas sector and at the networks supplying weapons to the military, and support for a reparations fund. Human Rights Watch urged governments to press Myanmar's junta to comply and to ensure any refugee returns are safe and voluntary. The United Kingdom, which requested Security Council consultations on the ruling, expelled Myanmar's defence attache after the judgment; closed consultations are scheduled for next Wednesday, with a further private meeting on December 7.

Enforcement through the Council looks unlikely. Daniel Forti of the International Crisis Group said China has discouraged Council action on Myanmar since 2021 and that Beijing and Moscow, both veto holders, could not be expected to support a resolution enforcing the court's recommendations; a referral to the ICC would also face a near certain US veto, the AP noted, because the ICC is itself under US sanctions. Myanmar's government has denied committing genocide, saying the 2017 operations targeted insurgents. A spokeswoman for President Min Aung Hlaing, who commanded the military during the operations, said the nine to six split meant the ruling was not unanimous and called it unfair.
