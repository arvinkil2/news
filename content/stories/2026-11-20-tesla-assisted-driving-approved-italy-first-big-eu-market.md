Deputy Prime Minister and Transport Minister Matteo Salvini announced on Friday that the ministry had given the green light after what he called a thorough technical feasibility check, moving ahead of the EU recognition process rather than waiting for it, and Tesla Europe confirmed the approval, saying rollout begins soon, with no firm date given. The system remains a Level 2 driver-assist product that can steer, brake, accelerate and handle intersections and lane changes while the driver supervises at all times and stays fully responsible.

Italy follows the Netherlands, whose vehicle authority RDW first approved the system in April under the EU type-approval regulation, and ten smaller member states that recognised that approval nationally. The version Italy approved carries the stricter driver monitoring the RDW cleared on November 16, including hands on the wheel above 130 km/h, and follows Tesla's agreement to drop the Full Self-Driving name in Europe and cut the maximum speed margin over the limit.
