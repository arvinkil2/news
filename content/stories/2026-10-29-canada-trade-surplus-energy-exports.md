Canada's merchandise trade surplus widened in September. Exports rose 12.8 percent and imports 3.6 percent, taking the surplus to 9.9 billion dollars from a revised 6.2 billion in August, Statistics Canada said. Exports to the United States rose 11.4 percent and imports from the United States 2.7 percent, widening the bilateral surplus to 8.7 billion dollars.

Energy carried the increase. Energy product exports rose 12.7 percent, led by crude oil and bitumen, as export prices for crude rose 16.6 percent while volumes fell 3.0 percent. Metal and mineral product exports rose 11.2 percent, and exports rose 10.7 percent to countries other than the United States, reaching a record share of total exports.
