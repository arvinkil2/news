**Nigeria Mining Week opens in Abuja.** The 11th Nigeria Mining Week runs October 12-14 with the theme "Unlocking Investment and Growth through Partnerships," targeting investment and critical-minerals growth. The Miners Association of Nigeria expects more than 3,000 participants and 90 sponsors and exhibitors, with seven strategic forums including deal rooms and technical workshops. Agenda items include Nigeria's role in battery, magnet and EV supply chains, the gold value chain, integrated steel production and iron ore. The Women in Extractive Industries Forum will examine opportunities for women in the sector. (News Agency of Nigeria, October 10; single-sourced)

**Victoria to return royalties to regional areas.** The Victorian government will establish a Royalties for Regions program directing minerals royalties back into regional communities, a move welcomed by the Minerals Council of Australia's Victoria branch. The industry says about A$150 million in royalties is currently paid in Victoria each year, while mining companies invest A$570 million annually in community support, and argues royalty reinvestment will add to the economic case for developing the state's gold and critical-minerals resources. Consultation and co-design with councils and communities are promised. (Australian Mining, October 11; single-sourced to MCA Victoria)

**Astral secures Spargoville nickel rights from Estrella.** Astral Resources agreed to acquire 100 percent of the Spargoville nickel rights from Estrella Resources for 20 million shares at a deemed A$0.15, about A$3 million, removing third-party tenure obstacles over the Mandilla gold project development footprint. Completion was expected by October 12, subject to customary closing conditions, with half the consideration shares held in voluntary escrow for six months. Estrella said the divestment lets it focus on its Timor-Leste portfolio of manganese, limestone, copper and gold. (Grafa, October 6; single-sourced)

**EU-China dialogue to continue on rare earths and magnets.** The EU-China trade talks that produced the hybrid-car safeguard also committed both sides to continued dialogue on Beijing's restrictions on rare-earth and permanent-magnet exports, per ANSA's Brussels reporting on the October 9 deal. The rare-earth consultation sits alongside the agreement to discuss market-access issues around inverters, widening the agenda from autos into the clean-energy supply chain. No timeline or measures were specified. (ANSA via the 2026-10-11 EU-China hybrid import cap story file; single-sourced)
