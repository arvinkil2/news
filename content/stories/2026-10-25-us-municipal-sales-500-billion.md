The municipal market has passed a round number at a pace that makes the number the story. Bloomberg reported on Friday that state and local government bond sales crossed 500 billion dollars this week, a total the market reached only across the whole of 2025, itself a record year that broke the 498 billion dollar mark set in 2024. Issuance is now expected to pass 600 billion dollars for the first time in 2026, a third consecutive annual record, according to BAM Mutual, the municipal bond insurer, which said the vast majority of the financing is being provided by individual investors holding more than 40 percent of outstanding municipal bonds on Federal Reserve data.

The first half set the pace. Bond Buyer statistics put long-term issuance at 294.884 billion dollars across 4,644 issues in the six months to June, up 5.8 percent on the same period of 2025, with June alone producing 62.275 billion dollars, the heaviest June on record. California was the largest state issuer in the half at 45.029 billion dollars, followed by Texas at 33.618 billion and New York at 26.994 billion.

The drivers are structural rather than opportunistic. Pandemic-era federal aid is depleted, construction and labour costs have risen with inflation, and a backlog of projects that issuers deferred while waiting for cheaper money can no longer wait: the working conclusion among borrowers, as Bond Buyer's midyear review put it, is that delaying infrastructure now costs more than borrowing at today's rates. For the rates market, the significance is absorption. Record tax-exempt supply is clearing alongside record Treasury issuance and a corporate calendar heavy with AI financing, without the concessions that a saturated buyer base would demand, because the retail bid for tax-exempt income at yields above 4 percent is doing the clearing.
