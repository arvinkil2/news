**Latest, October 27:** A second wave of strikes shortly after 3 a.m. on Tuesday hit the South Bridge, one of the main road bridges over the Dnipro in Kyiv, and damaged the metro infrastructure on the bridge, Mayor Vitali Klitschko wrote on Telegram, with city officials adding that train services across the bridge had been suspended. President Volodymyr Zelensky said on Tuesday that the overnight attacks included 16 ballistic missiles and more than 280 drones, that there had been casualties and hits in Kyiv including on the South Bridge, and that strikes also hit the Kyiv, Vinnytsia, Zaporizhzhia, Dnipro, Kharkiv, Odesa, Chernihiv, Sumy, Donetsk, Kherson and Chernivtsi regions, Reuters reported. Zelensky called the bridge hit a serious escalation in Russia's operation against Ukrainian logistics. In Kharkiv, authorities said attacks late on Monday killed two people and injured four.

A Russian missile attack on Kyiv on Monday evening killed at least one person, who died near a supermarket according to preliminary information, and injured seven people across the city in the preliminary figures Mayor Vitali Klitschko gave, as reported by Reuters, whose witnesses in the capital said a series of explosions jolted the city and power was cut in districts on the east side of the Dnipro River. President Volodymyr Zelensky said a ballistic missile hit the grounds of one of Kyiv's universities and the area beside it, near Kyoto Park, a metro station and residential buildings, that the missiles hit crowded places where there are no military facilities, and that rescue work was continuing with people possibly still under the rubble, NV.ua reported in Russian. Klitschko's later figure, carried by NV, was one killed and ten injured, most of them students from the dormitory of one of the universities, while Reuters reported a strike next to an underground station, windows shattered at two medical facilities and a member of an emergency crew coming under attack after rushing to a strike scene. Moscow has long denied that it targets civilians.
