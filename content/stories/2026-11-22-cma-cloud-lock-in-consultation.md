The UK has extended its digital sovereignty drive with a call for evidence into cloud developers' use of Kubernetes and identity and access management (IAM) services. The questionnaire launched by the Competition and Markets Authority aims to probe vendor lock-in among the hyperscalers, and references the regulator's earlier work with Amazon Web Services and Microsoft to lower egress fees and improve interoperability between their cloud platforms.

IAM is the pointed part of the scope. It puts AWS's identity offerings under the spotlight alongside Microsoft's Entra ID, with Google and Oracle offering similar services inside their clouds. Independent IAM providers sit outside the hyperscaler group: Okta is the largest of them, and Palo Alto Networks now competes in the segment with its Idira platform, acquired with CyberArk.

The market data the CMA cites explains the focus. According to CMA figures, Microsoft commanded up to 70 percent of the British security software market in 2025, with AWS, Google and Palo Alto Networks each at 5 percent or less, and Okta, CrowdStrike and Ping Identity each between 5 and 10 percent.

The consultation runs in parallel with the bigger case: Microsoft is under examination for possible designation with strategic market status in respect of its business software ecosystem, a consultation on which ended last week. AWS was earlier given mostly the all-clear after satisfying the CMA that it was improving interoperability and reducing lock-in for British cloud users, including its AWS Interconnect links to Azure, Oracle Cloud Infrastructure and Google Cloud. The new consultation is open to stakeholders inside and outside the UK and closes on December 3.
