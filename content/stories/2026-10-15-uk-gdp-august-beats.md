The UK economy grew 0.1 percent in August, ahead of the 0.1 percent decline economists had expected and slower than July's 0.4 percent, with June also revised up to 0.4 percent and growth over the three months to August accelerating to 0.7 percent from 0.5 percent, the Office for National Statistics reported on Thursday. Manufacturing drove the month, with production output up 0.8 percent and manufacturing up 1.2 percent, led by a 5.1 percent rise in pharmaceuticals, while services output was flat after a 0.5 percent rise in July and consumer-facing services fell 0.3 percent. Construction fell 0.2 percent, a fourth consecutive monthly decline, leaving an uneven expansion in which services still carried the three-month picture, up 0.9 percent over the period.
