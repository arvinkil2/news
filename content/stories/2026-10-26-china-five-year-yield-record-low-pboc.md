The yield on China's five year government notes briefly touched 1.34 percent on Tuesday, heading for the lowest closing level on record, after the People's Bank of China increased its cash injection into the banking system, Bloomberg reported. The record is measured on Chinabond data going back to 2002.

The move extends the compression across Chinese sovereign yields that has accompanied repeated central bank liquidity support, and it lands the same morning the National Bureau of Statistics reported industrial profits up 14.4 percent from a year earlier in September. Bloomberg's report, in the portion that could be read, did not state the size or terms of the injection.
