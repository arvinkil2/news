Sean Cairncross, the national cyber director who oversaw cybersecurity policy and strategy across the federal government, has been let go from his post, a White House official confirmed to The Hill on Thursday. The official said President Trump supported the decision because he believed Cairncross did not have the proper cyber credentials for the job, and Reuters, via the Guardian, reported the same account from a person close to the White House, who said the president fully supported the firing. Cairncross declined to comment.

Defense Department chief information officer Kirsten Davies will assume the position in an acting capacity, the official said, and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth congratulated her in a post on X on Thursday night. The departure follows a week of technology outages, including a nationwide halt to air travel, a Microsoft outage that hit email services and a temporary disruption to the National Security Agency's network, which US intelligence officials have said were separate maintenance and technical failures and not a coordinated or state sponsored attack.
