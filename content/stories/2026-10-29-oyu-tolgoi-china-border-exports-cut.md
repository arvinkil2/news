**Latest, October 29:** Rio Tinto has suspended the underground operation at Oyu Tolgoi, Bloomberg reported, citing two people familiar with the matter. A rockfall triggered by a seismic event on Thursday injured 15 workers, the company said in an emailed statement reported by Bloomberg, without commenting on the status of operations. Whether the open pit continues and what the suspension does to production guidance were not in the accessible portion of the report.

Copper concentrate exports from Oyu Tolgoi, the Mongolian mine Rio Tinto operates and owns 66 percent of, have dropped about 70 percent over the past two weeks because of Chinese border restrictions, the Associated Press reported on Thursday. Daily truck convoys at the Gashuunsukhait to Ganqimaodu crossing have fallen to about four from 12 to 13, the mine operator Oyu Tolgoi LLC told AP.

Mongolian border officials attributed the slowdown to infrastructure and technology upgrades on the Chinese side, including installation and maintenance of an in-vehicle facial recognition system for truck drivers, work that began in August and could continue until the end of December, AP said. The mine produced 113,500 tonnes of copper in concentrate in the third quarter, about 56 percent of Rio's consolidated copper output, and Rio's group copper forecast of 800,000 to 870,000 tonnes for this year predates the slowdown.
