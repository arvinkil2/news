Mexico has filed a formal complaint with the United States over a Drug Enforcement Administration tip line seeking information on suspected drug traffickers and alleged corruption among Mexican officials. President Claudia Sheinbaum said on Friday that Foreign Minister Juan Ramon de la Fuente had contacted the US ambassador to lodge the complaint, and that the ambassador said he would look into it. She called the relationship one of coordination, not subordination.

The DEA announced the tip line this week, with Administrator Terry Cole inviting Mexicans to report members of criminal organisations, corrupt officials and money launderers linked to trafficking in Sinaloa, the northwestern state that is the stronghold of the Sinaloa Cartel, via Signal, WhatsApp and email, with identities kept confidential. Sheinbaum had already attacked the initiative on Thursday as interference, reading from an Associated Press investigation into the DEA allowing counterfeit fentanyl pills to go unseized during a tracking operation, and arguing the agency should investigate corruption inside the United States first.

Inside Sinaloa, the reaction was not uniform. Juan Manuel Partida Valdez, president of the Sinaloa Journalists Association, was among the first to write to the line on Thursday, sending information about alleged ties between Sinaloa politicians and criminal groups and saying he did so because authorities had failed to end the violence and corruption in the region.

The dispute sits beside working cooperation. Last week Mexican forces captured Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, a Sinaloa faction ally on the FBI's Ten Most Wanted list, in an operation Mexico requested US drone support for, though Mexico's attorney general says he will be tried in Mexico rather than extradited. Status: complaint filed, November 27, 2026; the tip line remains open.
