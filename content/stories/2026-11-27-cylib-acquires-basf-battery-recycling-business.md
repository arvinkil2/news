BASF and cylib signed an agreement on November 20 under which cylib will acquire BASF's battery recycling business. The transaction covers BASF's recycling activities in Europe and North America, the related intellectual property, and customer and partner contracts. It also includes the black mass production plant and the battery recycling prototype plant at Schwarzheide in Germany. The parties have agreed not to disclose the financial terms.

BASF is divesting as part of a decision to focus its battery materials business exclusively on cathode active materials. cylib, founded in Aachen in 2022, gains an operating industrial site, access to feedstock and an entry into the North American market with existing customer relationships. Operations at Schwarzheide will continue, employees at the affected sites transfer with the business, and BASF will continue to manage the business until closing.

The Schwarzheide black mass plant, commissioned in 2025, can process up to 15,000 tonnes of end-of-life lithium-ion batteries and production scrap a year. The transaction is expected to close in the second or third quarter of 2027, subject to approval by the relevant authorities and customary closing conditions. cylib's co-chief executive Lilian Schwich framed the goal as a closed European loop in which lithium, graphite and cobalt are processed and retained in Europe over the long term.
