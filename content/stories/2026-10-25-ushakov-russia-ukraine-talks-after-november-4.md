**Latest, October 28:** The trilateral meeting now has dates. Speaking at a news conference in the Netherlands on Wednesday after meeting Dutch Prime Minister Rob Jetten, Zelenskyy said the talks with Russia and the United States will take place from November 6 to November 10 in the United Arab Emirates, and that Kyiv wants Europe involved as well, citing the need for unity and stronger pressure on Russia. Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov gave the opposite account of the format: European countries will not be included in the Abu Dhabi round, he told Interfax, and their possible participation was not discussed during Tuesday's 90 minute call between Vladimir Putin, Finnish President Alexander Stubb and Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre. Stubb himself told a Nordic Council meeting in Helsinki that European countries would be involved in the November talks. The accounts cannot both be right, and no US official is quoted confirming either the dates or the format.

**Latest, October 25:** Volodymyr Zelensky told Ukrainian television on Sunday that a trilateral meeting between Ukraine, the United States and Russia will be held in November to restart the stalled peace negotiations, saying his side is willing to talk, that a territorial question must be addressed at the level of the leaders, and that the other matters are for the technical groups. He gave no date and no place. Kremlin spokesman Dmitri Peskov had said on Friday that a trilateral meeting was planned in the United Arab Emirates in November. Deutsche Welle's account places the announcements after a week of United States diplomacy in which special envoy Steve Witkoff met Zelensky and European leaders in Oslo and then President Vladimir Putin in St. Petersburg, and it quotes no direct United States confirmation of the meeting.


Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said Russia is ready to resume trilateral negotiations on Ukraine after November 4, Russia's National Unity Day, telling interviewer Pavel Zarubin of Vesti that the next deadline is the holiday and that after it Moscow will be ready to resume. He said Russia had earlier been ready to resume after the September 18 to 20 Duma elections, and blamed the Ukrainian side for breaking that arrangement, a claim Kyiv has not answered in the sources read. Other Russian wires carrying remarks from the same interview round on Sunday reported him describing the first resumed meeting as a sighting-in round at which no decisions should be expected, and saying that preparation for a summit of Vladimir Putin, Donald Trump and Xi Jinping has begun, without a date, a venue or an agenda.

The remarks are Moscow's account alone: no US, Ukrainian or Chinese official had confirmed the talks timetable or the summit preparation in the sources read.
