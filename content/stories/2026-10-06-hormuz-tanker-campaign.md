## Latest

The tanker campaign spread into the western Persian Gulf on Wednesday. A tanker 94 kilometres (58 miles) north of Madinat ash Shamal, off Qatar's north coast, was struck by multiple projectiles, the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations agency said, with a number of casualties reported. UKMTO did not specify how many were killed or injured, did not identify the vessel, and said authorities were investigating. The location falls within Qatar's exclusive economic zone; Qatar had not commented. The Maritime Executive, a commercial shipping publication, said it was the first reported attack on a tanker in the western half of the Gulf in weeks. UKMTO said Tuesday there had been nine attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz this month, half of September's total for the waterway and the Gulf combined. Despite the attacks, Middle East crude exports have held at or above pre-war levels: the seven-day moving average reached 18.3 million barrels per day on September 30, versus about 18 million bpd in the 12 months before the war, per Kpler.

## Earlier

The tanker campaign in the Strait of Hormuz intensified Monday as UKMTO logged five separate incidents, including a projectile strike that set a tanker's engine room on fire. The casualty toll escalated sharply: twelve seafarers were injured after the Panama-flagged Aframax tanker On Peace (IMO 9893204) was struck by a projectile while transiting the strait, according to India's Ministry of External Affairs, with eleven of the injured Indian nationals among a crew of 19. The injured were evacuated with Omani assistance to Khasab for medical treatment. India called for an immediate end to attacks on commercial shipping, without attributing the strike. Indian outlets, including indiandefensenews.in and INDIA New England News, report 11 injured rather than 12.

The On Peace appears on a blacklist maintained by Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority, which has accused listed vessels of violating Iranian transit protocols, and the incident likely corresponds to UKMTO's October 5 warning of an unidentified tanker struck by an unknown projectile at 1637 UTC, whose engine-room fire the crew was still fighting when the warning was issued.

Marine Insight's review of the UKMTO reports added texture: three of Monday's five reports were delayed accounts of October 3 and 4 strikes, an inbound LPG tanker hit at 1716 UTC on October 4, a crude tanker struck at 1907 UTC the same day, and a crude tanker struck above the waterline on October 3, with UKMTO not assigning responsibility. Separately, the IRGC contacted an inbound tanker about 11 nautical miles north of Khasab, Oman, ordered it to turn around, and warned it would be targeted if it continued; the master complied. The Joint Maritime Information Center's October 4 update had already confirmed four tanker attacks between October 2 and 3, including one in which a drone entered a tanker's funnel and dropped into the engine room.

The toll on crews is now the central issue. IMO Secretary-General Arsenio Dominguez issued another statement mourning merchant mariners, after five seafarers were killed September 29 in the rescue of the hijacked HONOUR 25 off Somalia. The IMO has verified 80 attacks on merchant vessels in and around the Strait of Hormuz since the conflict began February 28, killing at least 22 seafarers. Dominguez's message was unusually direct: do not use these conflicts as a pretext to attack merchant vessels and innocent seafarers.

The physical oil market is absorbing the campaign without panic. Brent closed Monday at $100.32, down 1.9 percent, and WTI at $89.43, down 1.8 percent. Loadings are holding: Middle East exports topped pre-war levels for part of September and Kuwait is back to about 75 percent of prewar output as Hormuz transits rise. US Central Command said it redirected its 130th commercial vessel on October 5, disabled three non-compliant vessels, and destroyed 13 vessels over the previous 12 weeks tied to the blockade or the IRGC shadow network, with the blockade resumed July 14.

The pressure is showing up where it usually does first: freight and insurance. War-risk premiums and rerouting are the marginal energy driver, not inventories. The US Strategic Petroleum Reserve sits at 283 million barrels, the lowest since October 1982 per EIA data, which constrains Washington's ability to answer any diesel tightness with releases.
