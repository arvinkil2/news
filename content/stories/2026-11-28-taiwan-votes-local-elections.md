Taiwan's ruling Democratic Progressive Party looked set to make gains in Saturday's nine-in-one local elections, while the opposition Kuomintang looked set to keep the capital, according to initial vote counts reported by Taiwanese media and Reuters. Polls across the island's 22 cities and counties closed at 4pm local time after choosing mayors, magistrates, council members and other local leaders for about 11,000 posts.

In the six largest cities, the DPP and the KMT looked set to win three each. The DPP was probably capturing Taichung, the island's second most populous city and currently KMT-held, where its candidate Ho Hsin-chun held a narrow lead, and it was holding its southern heartlands of Kaohsiung and Tainan. The KMT was retaining New Taipei, the most populous city, and in Taipei its mayor Chiang Wan-an led the count against the DPP's Puma Shen. Neither camp in the six cities had yet claimed victory or conceded when Reuters filed. The DPP also looked set to win back Yilan county in the northeast, Changhua in the centre and Hsinchu in the north.

The result would be a marked recovery from the last local elections four years ago, when the KMT won 14 cities and counties to the DPP's five. Campaigning focused on transport and the environment rather than relations with Beijing, but China framed the race throughout: Beijing, which claims Taiwan as its own territory, has repeatedly denounced Shen as a separatist, and Taiwan's defence ministry counted 33 Chinese warplanes around the island on Friday along with eight warships.

Voters also decided a referendum on ending the nuclear-free homeland policy, held alongside the local elections and covered in yesterday's referendum story; its count, which must clear a threshold of about five million yes votes, was expected later on Saturday evening. Manoeuvring for the presidential and parliamentary elections due early in 2028 begins in earnest after this vote. Status: counting, November 28, 2026; no certified results yet.
