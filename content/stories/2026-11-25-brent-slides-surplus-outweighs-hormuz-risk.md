Brent crude fell again on Tuesday, down 3.89 percent to 87.84 dollars a barrel on the estate front series and down 4.47 percent on the week, after Monday's settlement at the lowest close since August 31. WTI fell 1.16 percent to 81.10 dollars. Reuters reported the same direction in Wednesday's Asian and European trade, with Brent slipping more than 1 percent as the prospect of a market surplus offset concern about shipping disruption in the Middle East, including Iran's claim, carried in today's commodities coverage, that two tankers struck mines in the Strait of Hormuz.

The surplus case rests on the International Energy Agency's November report. The agency said on November 13 that the world oil market would return to a surplus in the fourth quarter as Middle East exports recover faster than demand, with output running 1.7 million barrels a day ahead of demand. Global supply reached 105.4 million barrels a day in October, up 2.3 million barrels a day on the month after a 3 million barrel a day rise in September, and observed global stocks grew by 52 million barrels in October, according to Deutsche Bank figures cited in the same reporting.

Analysts framed Tuesday's trade the same way. Supply surplus concerns going into 2027 are outweighing ongoing shipping risks, Zhenqiang Seto, a senior oil research specialist at LSEG, told Reuters, pointing to uncertainty over OPEC's 2027 policy, the UAE no longer being restricted by OPEC+ policy, and Iraq seeking higher output capacity. Tsuyoshi Ueno of NLI Research Institute said perceptions of a glut have kept prices in check since the IEA report, with attention on how next year's quotas are determined.

That decision lands on Sunday, November 29, when OPEC+ ministers hold a series of meetings, including the 42nd OPEC and non-OPEC Ministerial Meeting, to set the 2027 output framework and new baselines based on an independent review of each member's maximum sustainable capacity. The January Brent contract expires on Monday, November 30, the first session after the meeting, so the decision lands directly on a contract roll.
