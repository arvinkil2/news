The European Commission confirmed on Thursday that it was hit by an AI-driven cyberattack that took services offline, Infosecurity Magazine reported. Spokesperson Thomas Regnier told a press briefing that the intrusion was detected on October 22 and confirmed as a cyberattack on October 26, that several applications were taken down as a protective measure and most have since been restored, and that the European Data Protection Supervisor has been notified. He said he was not aware of any ransomware demand and that the investigation, which is ongoing, has not detected any evidence of a data breach affecting Commission staff.

Regnier confirmed the attack was AI generated and used a publicly available tool, saying AI assisted tools can be misused to detect vulnerabilities. Politico, which reported the incident earlier on Thursday, said the systems taken offline included Sysper, the HR platform staff use for payslips and time management, and a medical reimbursements system, and claimed the attack was launched using ARTEX, a Chinese AI penetration testing tool that CERT-EU warned on November 5 was being used by threat actors to automate campaigns. The Commission did not name the tool or any perpetrator.
