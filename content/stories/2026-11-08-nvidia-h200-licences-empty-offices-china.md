The US Department of Commerce approved export licences for two Chinese companies, New Matrix and Maginfra, to buy Nvidia H200 chips and assemble them into AI servers for unnamed final users, but a New York Times investigation found that addresses listed for both companies led to empty offices whose receptionists and neighbours did not know them. New Matrix was founded by Li Bin, who chairs the partly state-owned Tsinghua Unigroup and owns New H3C Semiconductor Technologies, which Commerce has placed on its Entity List for cooperating with the Chinese military, while Maginfra is suspected of links to Inspur, blacklisted since 2023 for supporting the People's Liberation Army, through shared personnel, patents and a similar government ownership structure.

Around two dozen firms have received H200 approvals in all, among them Baidu, Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance, BYD and ZTE, and a third recipient, Zhejiang Yindun Cloud Technology, has executives related to the founder of Range Intelligent Computing Technology Group, a data centre operator that has sold compute capacity to Chinese military contractors.
