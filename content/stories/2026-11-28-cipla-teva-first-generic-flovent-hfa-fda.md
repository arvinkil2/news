The US Food and Drug Administration has approved the first generic equivalent of Flovent HFA, Cipla announced on November 25. The approval, under an abbreviated new drug application, covers fluticasone propionate metered dose inhalers in 110 mcg and 220 mcg strengths, and the companies said the product has been launched.

The inhaler was jointly developed by Cipla and Teva Pharmaceuticals. Cipla is responsible for manufacturing and supply, while Teva holds the application and will commercialise the product in the United States.

Fluticasone HFA is an inhaled corticosteroid for the long term maintenance and prophylactic treatment of asthma in patients aged four years and older, and for patients who require oral corticosteroid therapy for asthma. The brand product, made by GSK, was discontinued in the United States at the end of 2023, leaving authorised generic arrangements as the main supply route.

Cipla cited IQVIA data putting the US market for fluticasone HFA in the two approved strengths at approximately 350 million dollars. The companies did not disclose launch pricing or revenue sharing terms.

First generic status for a complex inhaler is a manufacturing milestone as much as a regulatory one, since metered dose inhalers require the generic to match the brand's delivery performance. Cipla shares rose on the announcement in Mumbai trading.
