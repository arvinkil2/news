The UK government committed on Wednesday to bringing essential services back into public control, with First Secretary of State Louise Haigh setting out the case at the CBI annual conference. "A monopoly is not a market. For forty years, we pretended otherwise in the things that matter most," Haigh said, arguing that public control is about getting the foundations right so private enterprise can compete and grow.

On energy and grids, the government said it has already halted the Gas Shipper Obligation levy on bills, will introduce competition into network build-out through the Great British Grid, will impose tougher penalties on network companies that fail to deliver a grid slot on time, and will overhaul connections to prioritise demand projects of strategic national importance, against a backlog that has tripled to 125 gigawatts. On water, Haigh affirmed a commitment that the whole industry works in the public interest, with a Water Bill coming next year, and warned that without intervention consumers risk further steep increases on top of bills projected to rise 36 percent between 2025 and 2030. Ministers will also transfer powers from non-departmental public bodies to departments, and Juergen Maier has been appointed Northern Growth and Reindustrialisation Commissioner.
