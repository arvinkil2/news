Romania's intelligence service said on Monday that the September 14 blaze in Brasov, at an industrial site near a state-owned weapons factory it did not name, was part of a wider campaign of sabotage operations coordinated by Russia against arms manufacturers and other military and industrial targets. The Directorate for Investigating Organized Crime and Terrorism, DIICOT, said three people living in EU countries were involved, two Belarusians and a Ukrainian: two were remanded in custody in Germany, where they were allegedly preparing similar offences, and the third remains at large in Poland.

Foreign Minister Dan Neculaescu said the fire was part of a broader sabotage campaign across Europe targeting military production, and President Nicusor Dan has described Romania as a target of a Russian hybrid war. The intelligence service said recruits for such networks are typically found on social media and paid first for harmless tasks such as photographing targets, with the work escalating over time. Russia had given no immediate comment on the Brasov claim.
