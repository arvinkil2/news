Latest: the interior ministry has revised Saturday's detention count sharply upward. A new ministry count put detentions across France during the day and night of Saturday at 779, Le Parisien reported, up from the 565 in the ministry's late count, and put injured law enforcement officers at 159 nationwide, settling a total that city level reports had left in dispute. The ministry counted 320,000 participants across 289 protest actions, matching its turnout figure from Saturday.

Latest: the injury toll has a first count. At least 60 protesters were injured in Saturday's clashes across France, four of them in serious condition, Le Figaro reported, citing emergency services. The document the paper cited put law enforcement injuries at 18 wounded, a national total that sits below the Marseille police prefecture's own report of 19 officers wounded in that city alone, so the police injury count is not settled.

Latest: the interior ministry's late count put arrests across France at 565, above the 511 detentions reported as of 8 p.m., AFP reported. In Paris, four police officers and a gendarme were injured, three of them burned by Molotov cocktails, and Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez described the throwing of a Molotov cocktail at a riot police officer, who escaped with minor injuries, as an attempted murder, blaming far-left extremist groups for what he called an extraordinary level of violence. Youth organisations put turnout at nearly 500,000 nationwide, including 200,000 in Paris, against the ministry's 320,000 and 70,000, and students vowed new blockades of schools and universities in the coming week, including a union demonstration on November 5. A night curfew ran in the central Paris neighbourhoods of République and Châtelet-Les Halles from 11 p.m. to 6 a.m. on Saturday and Sunday nights.

Latest: detentions rose through the evening to 511 as of 8 p.m. local time, BFMTV reported citing the interior ministry, up from the 296 reported earlier in the day. The ministry's turnout figures were unchanged at 320,000 nationwide, including 70,000 in Paris.

Latest: police made more than 290 arrests across France on Saturday, Deutsche Welle reported with AP, AFP, Reuters and dpa. The interior ministry said radical elements in Bordeaux, Toulouse and Marseille tried to enter city centres to vandalise and loot, while the declared marches otherwise proceeded smoothly. Fires burned in the streets in Marseille, clashes with police were reported in Rennes and Toulouse, and police fired tear gas in Paris and Lyon in the late afternoon. At the end of the Paris march, on the city's southern outskirts, fireworks exploded and incendiary devices were thrown at officers in riot gear, with several officers caught in the flames; no injury count was reported. Paris police said they had carried out more than 1,200 checks at railway stations and nearby areas early on Saturday to keep fireworks, knives and other prohibited items out of the marches.

Latest: the interior ministry put Saturday's turnout at 320,000 nationwide, including 70,000 at the main Paris march, down from 410,000 the previous Saturday and 550,000 on October 17, when riots erupted across the country. Organisers put participation much higher. Tension flared at the end of the marches in Paris, Marseille and Lyon and other cities, with tear gas fired at some people on the sidelines of the demonstrations, Reuters reported. The Paris march ran from Porte de Bagnolet, on the eastern edge of the capital, along the route police imposed to keep protesters away from government buildings and tourist sites, with students leading the march hand in hand and stewards from the CGT union at the front.

France is preparing for a third weekend of protests on Saturday, with 70,000 security personnel deployed to prevent a repeat of the violence seen on October 17 and shops boarding up their premises in Paris, where dozens of metro stations are closed and curfews from 11:00 p.m. apply in certain areas. The protests were initially driven by high school students' anger over the cost of living and poor school conditions, and have broadened to include demands for greater civil liberties and the right to protest, along with anger at alleged heavy handed policing since the movement began in September. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez warned of a high risk of violence, demonstrations have been restricted in several cities, and in Paris only one gathering was authorised, on the outskirts of the city rather than the centre protesters wanted, due to start at 2:00 p.m.
