The Department of Energy has offered Orano Enrichment USA a conditional loan commitment of about 900 million dollars toward its planned enrichment facility at Oak Ridge, Tennessee. Orano, majority owned by the French state, is one of the world's largest enrichers, and the Oak Ridge project is designed to produce low enriched uranium for the existing reactor fleet and, in time, the higher assay fuel advanced reactors need.

The strategic logic is blunt. The United States banned Russian enriched uranium imports after the invasion of Ukraine, with waivers running to 2028, and domestic enrichment capacity cannot yet replace those volumes. Lending a French champion the money to build in Tennessee is the fastest available route to allied supply, and the Loan Programs Office is the instrument.

A conditional commitment is not a closed loan: Orano must meet technical, financial and permitting conditions before funds flow. But in the sequence of US nuclear fuel decisions this autumn, which has also seen federal support move toward other enrichment ventures, the direction is consistent, and it is being set in Washington rather than in the market.
