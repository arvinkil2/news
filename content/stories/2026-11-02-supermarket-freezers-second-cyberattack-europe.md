Dozens of supermarkets in Belgium, Luxembourg and Sweden found their refrigeration and freezer temperatures raised overnight in a suspected cyberattack, the BBC and The Guardian reported on Monday. Delhaize told Belgian media a computer incident had affected about 40 of its stores in Belgium and Luxembourg, Carrefour said 15 of its Belgian stores were affected but stayed open, and in Sweden several ICA stores and a small number of Coop stores were hit, with one ICA store in Sjobo reporting its freezers had been set to 50 degrees Celsius. Millions of euros of food had to be thrown away according to local media, including about 100,000 euros of produce at one Delhaize branch in Hoeilaart, and the Belgian and Swedish national cybersecurity agencies said they were monitoring the incident. No one has been identified as responsible, and the attack follows an October incident in France in which hackers took control of cooling units at up to 700 supermarkets, pushing temperatures from as low as minus 27 degrees to well over 50 degrees and causing millions of euros of food losses.
