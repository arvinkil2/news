## Latest: Category 3, 120 mph winds, landfall tonight or early Saturday

Isaias strengthened into a major Category 3 hurricane Friday morning, with maximum sustained winds of 120 mph and a minimum central pressure of 959 millibars, the National Hurricane Center said in a 7:20 a.m. CDT tropical cyclone update based on Air Force Reserve Hurricane Hunter aircraft data. The center was near 27.0 degrees north, 87.6 degrees west at that time, about 240 miles south of Pensacola, Florida, and about 170 miles south-southeast of the mouth of the Mississippi River, moving north-northeast at 15 mph.

Forecasters expect the storm to turn north and approach the coast Friday afternoon, with landfall somewhere inside the hurricane-warning area Friday night or early Saturday. The NHC said Isaias is likely to remain a dangerous hurricane at landfall, though increasing wind shear could weaken it somewhat before it reaches the coast; it cautioned that a small delay in that shear could bring a stronger hurricane ashore. A hurricane warning is in effect from Ocean Springs, Mississippi, to the Bay/Gulf County line in Florida, and a storm-surge warning covers the coast from the mouth of the Mississippi River to Florida's Suwannee River.

## Thursday: shutdowns jump to nearly two-thirds of Gulf output

Isaias is now a hurricane with maximum sustained winds of 85 mph, located about 380 miles south of the mouth of the Mississippi River and moving east-northeast at 10 mph, according to the National Hurricane Center's 10 a.m. CDT advisory Thursday. Forecasters expect further strengthening ahead of landfall late Friday or early Saturday, with hurricane and storm surge warnings in effect and dangerous coastal flooding and heavy rainfall expected.

The Marine Minerals Administration reported Thursday that about 62.9 percent of daily Gulf oil production, or 1.28 million barrels a day, has been shut in, up sharply from about 25 percent on Wednesday. Natural gas shut-ins reached 57.4 percent of daily output, about 1.13 billion cubic feet a day. Operators have evacuated 121 of the Gulf's 371 manned production platforms; five non-dynamically positioned drilling rigs were also evacuated and four dynamically positioned rigs moved off the storm's path. Shell has shut its Mars, Olympus, Ursa, Vito and Appomattox facilities, and Chevron has suspended operations at four offshore locations.

## Wednesday: storm strengthens toward northern Gulf Coast

Tropical Storm Isaias is forecast to strengthen into a Category 2 hurricane with the northern Gulf Coast in its path later this week, according to the National Hurricane Center advisory cited Wednesday. The storm sat about 285 miles west of Progreso, Mexico with 40 mph sustained winds and is expected to reach hurricane strength Thursday, with a landfall cone spanning Louisiana, Alabama, Mississippi and the Florida Panhandle.

Chevron is evacuating nonessential personnel from its Gulf platforms as dozens of offshore oil and gas rigs sit in the projected path. Chevron's Pascagoula refinery on Mississippi's Gulf Coast, which processes 369,000 barrels of crude a day into gasoline, diesel and jet fuel, is among the notable assets exposed. Isaias would be the first Atlantic hurricane of the season after September ended storm-free for the first time in 32 years.
