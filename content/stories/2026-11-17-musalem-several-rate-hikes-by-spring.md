St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem said on Tuesday that the Federal Reserve will likely need several rate hikes to bring inflation under control, and should act soon to keep the problem from getting worse. "The economy will likely require several interest rate increases before the spring of 2027 to achieve price stability in a timely manner," he said in remarks prepared for a Central Bank of Argentina conference in Buenos Aires, arguing that the current policy rate, in the 3.75% to 4% range, is as much as a percentage point below where it needs to be to return inflation to the Fed's 2% target.

Inflation is running above 3%, and Musalem said the most likely scenario is that it stays elevated unless the Fed acts, with risks skewed toward persistent above-target inflation and current policy exerting no significant restraint on above-trend growth. "Moving sooner, while inflation expectations are anchored, has a better chance of succeeding without adverse impact on the labor market or economic growth," he said. The Fed next meets on December 8-9 and is expected to approve a quarter percentage point rate hike; Musalem is not a voting member of the rate-setting committee this year.
