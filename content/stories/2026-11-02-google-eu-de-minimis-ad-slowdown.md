Google has put a name on one drag in its advertising business, and it is European customs policy. Speaking on Alphabet's third quarter earnings call, chief business officer Philipp Schindler said that in retail, some of the company's Asia Pacific advertisers were impacted by the EU eliminating its de minimis exemption. The advertisers were not named, but the Register notes they are almost certainly Chinese e-tailers of the Temu, Shein and Alibaba type.

The policy change is mechanical and large. From July 1, 2026 the EU abolished the duty exemption for low value imports and imposed a temporary flat duty of 3 euros per item on consignments valued at up to 150 euros, payable by the seller or importer, running until July 1, 2028 when normal tariffs and the EU Customs Data Hub take over. The Commission's own guidance records that almost 5.9 billion such items were shipped directly to EU consumers in 2025 alone, and product identifiers became mandatory for these consignments on November 1, 2026.

The revenue channel is what makes this a technology story rather than a trade footnote. The Register estimates that Chinese e-tail giants account for perhaps ten percent of advertising revenue at Google and Meta. A per item duty raises those sellers' delivered costs in Europe, and the first place a squeezed cross border seller cuts is the marketing budget that buys its traffic. Schindler's remark confirms the transmission is already visible in Alphabet's retail ad growth, in the same quarter the market was punishing Alphabet for the scale of its AI spending rather than for its advertising print.

No figure was put on the shortfall, and Alphabet did not break out the affected advertisers. The disclosure matters as a template: as low value import regimes tighten in Europe and elsewhere, the cost shows up not only in customs receipts but in the ad auctions of the platforms that intermediate the trade.
