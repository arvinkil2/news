Capita said chief executive Adolfo Hernandez had agreed with the board to leave with immediate effect, with chief financial officer Pablo Andres becoming acting chief executive, and its shares fell 70 percent on Monday. Hernandez said the company had not delivered the service that members of the Civil Service pension scheme deserve. Capita took over administration of the scheme, which covers 1.7 million public sector workers, on December 1 last year under a seven-year deal worth £239 million, and tens of thousands of retiring civil servants have faced delays to their payments since.

The company now expects underlying operating profit in 2027 to be reduced by £85 million to £95 million, against a previous expectation of up to £40 million, citing the cost of the pensions recovery plan and the loss of two other major contracts in defence and pension solutions, and it plans an equity raise of £190 million in the first half of next year. The National Audit Office is probing the contract, milestone payments have been withheld after government deadlines were missed, and the chair of the Public Accounts Committee, Sir Geoffrey Clifton-Brown, said the mishandling had caused severe hardship for some scheme members, particularly through delayed bereavement payments.
