Two Israeli opposition leaders denounced an "attempted coup" by outgoing Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu on Monday, following press reports that his office tried to frame members of an Arab Israeli party ahead of last month's election. Channel 12 reported on Sunday that Netanyahu's chief of staff, Ido Norden, phoned in a complaint to police about members of the Raam party on the eve of the October 27 election, pointing officers to a purported intelligence source whose information led to the arrest of three Raam members; the three were later released after police determined the information was unfounded, and Netanyahu went on to accuse the Arab parties of electoral fraud after they won a record number of seats.

Haaretz also reported that David Zini, head of the Shin Bet security service, sought to wiretap leaders of the other Arab slate, the Joint List, but the attorney general turned the request down for lack of evidence. "As more and more details come to light, it becomes clear: this wasn't an attempt to disrupt the elections. It was an attempted coup," Yair Lapid wrote on X, while Joint List leader Yousef Jabareen said that if confirmed the findings showed "a criminal mechanism that operated from the heart of government to rig the election results". Netanyahu's Likud party issued a statement denying the allegations, and his main rival Gadi Eisenkot, who has been tasked with forming a government after neither bloc secured the 61 seats needed for a majority, wrote on Sunday that the caretaker prime minister "cannot remain at the head of the government for a minute longer".
