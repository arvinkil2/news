Latest: Nvidia's board is preparing to oust chief executive Jensen Huang and could meet as soon as Wednesday to determine his future, The Wall Street Journal reported, citing people familiar with the matter, in reporting carried by Reuters and CNBC. The report landed minutes after The New York Times reported that Huang had an extramarital relationship with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice president of healthcare, an executive who reports to him, and whom the Times said was captured on camera being affectionate with Huang at a San Jose hotel during the company's GTC conference in March. No board decision, vote, named successor or removal date was reported, and Nvidia did not immediately respond to requests for comment from CNBC, Reuters or The Verge. Nvidia shares, which had closed at 173.73 dollars, up 2.87 percent on the day, fell about 8 percent in after hours trading to about 159.60 dollars. The Powell allegation remains unproven, and the board's independent investigation, led by outside counsel including Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen and Katz, into Huang's acknowledged relationship with Alice Huang has not reported.

Latest: A new report alleged on Wednesday that Jensen Huang maintained an extramarital relationship with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice president of healthcare and a senior executive who reports directly to him, Investing.com reported. The report, citing five people who worked at Nvidia, said the two were seen being affectionate at a San Jose hotel during the company's GTC conference in March and that a bystander filmed the interaction; the video has not been independently viewed by the outlet carrying the report. Nvidia declined to comment, Powell did not immediately respond, and Huang, reached by the outlet behind the report, is said to have ended the call without addressing the allegation. Nvidia shares fell about 3 percent in early after hours trading on the report, and quote snapshots later in the evening showed a fall of about 8 percent. The allegation is single sourced and unproven, and it lands on top of the board's existing investigation, led by outside counsel including Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen and Katz, into Huang's acknowledged relationship with Alice Huang.

Latest: In Washington the deadline in the three chairs' November 4 letter passed on Wednesday with no answer. Committee chairs Brian Mast, John Moolenaar and Rick Crawford had asked Huang to testify on November 18; Mast and Moolenaar said on Wednesday afternoon they had not heard from him and were giving him until the end of the day, and Mast said the committees would subpoena him if he did not respond, CNBC reported. The calls for Huang to resign widened beyond Senator Kevin Cramer: Representative Suhas Subramanyam said Huang should resign from Nvidia, and Representative Don Bacon, asked whether Huang should step down, said yes, on the ground that he allowed himself to be compromised. Senators Elizabeth Warren and Jim Banks urged colleagues to put the AI Overwatch Act, which would tighten controls on advanced chip exports to China, into this year's National Defense Authorization Act, alongside the MATCH Act and the Chip Security Act; Congress has not yet passed the defense bill. CNBC quoted no response from Nvidia.

Latest: The November 5 confrontation over Megaspeed went to the Oval Office the same day and produced a presidential instruction that still has not been executed, Axios reported on Wednesday. After the Situation Room meeting at which Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer and Secretary of State and national security adviser Marco Rubio lined up behind tighter controls on companies suspected of diverting Nvidia chips to China, the same officials reconvened with President Trump in the Oval Office by late afternoon. Trump called Elon Musk, his tech adviser, who sided with the four officials in favour of the controls; Lutnick warned that acting would ignite a trade war with China, and the others countered that Beijing was already taking similar steps against the United States. Trump told them, "Let's get it done." After the meeting, however, Lutnick suggested another delay, saying he would develop some options and return; officials pushed back that the decision had already been made, but the order still had not gone out afterward. Axios reported that Trump had already signed off on such steps at least three times this year, in May, in August and on November 5, and that Lutnick and Commerce Undersecretary Jeffrey Kessler repeatedly held back the effort. The Commerce Department, through spokesperson Kristen Eichamer, denied that Bessent confronted Lutnick and declined to address the other claims right away. No Entity List decision on Megaspeed has been announced, and the espionage allegations remain unproven reported allegations with no charges brought.

Latest: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent confronted Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick at a White House Situation Room meeting on November 5 over why Megaspeed, the Singapore company that bought Nvidia AI chips in bulk and is under US investigation for allegedly diverting them to China, has not been placed on the Commerce Department's Entity List, Yonhap reported on Wednesday, citing New York Times and Axios reporting of November 11. Axios reported Bessent asking, "How is Megaspeed not on the Entity List," and Lutnick answering that he did not know and would look into it; the Times reported Lutnick telling Bessent that Commerce could now move quickly to impose export restrictions on the company. White House and intelligence officials had pressed for action on Megaspeed since last fall, according to the reports, and at an earlier deputy-level meeting Commerce Undersecretary Jeffrey Kessler deferred the question to Lutnick; the Financial Times reported that several departments have demanded the listing while Commerce has slowed new listings over the past two months, saying some companies need further investigation. A Commerce Department spokesperson denied the exchange, saying Bessent and Lutnick never had such a conversation and that Entity List decisions are made at the administration level, not by Commerce alone. No decision to list Megaspeed has been announced. The espionage allegations against Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive, remain unproven reported allegations, and no charges have been brought against her, Megaspeed or Jensen Huang.

Brian Mast, who chairs the House Foreign Affairs Committee, said his committee is preparing a subpoena to send if Huang does not respond. Three Republican committee chairs in the US House of Representatives have asked Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang to appear for a joint closed door briefing on November 18. The letter, dated November 4, was signed by Brian Mast of Florida, who chairs the Foreign Affairs Committee, John Moolenaar of Michigan, who chairs the Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party, and Rick Crawford of Arkansas, who chairs the Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence. Huang has been asked to accept the date or propose an alternative by November 11.

The chairs want to question Huang on two subjects: Nvidia's security protocols against foreign adversary espionage, and his interactions with Alice Huang, a senior executive at Singapore based data center company Megaspeed International. The request follows an October 29 New York Times report that US intelligence agencies concluded Alice Huang was passing information to the Chinese government and had cultivated a close relationship with the Nvidia chief. The Times reporting does not assert that Jensen Huang knowingly took part in espionage, and neither Alice Huang nor Megaspeed has been charged with any crime. Megaspeed affiliates reportedly bought at least 4.8 billion dollars of Nvidia AI equipment between June 2024 and February 2026, a period when the company was already under investigation over suspected chip diversion to China.

Reporting on November 6 and 7 added a specific document to the case. The New York Times reported that a memorandum circulated earlier this year, based on US intelligence, claimed Alice Huang was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security to spy on Jensen Huang, and the Financial Times reported that the same memorandum claimed a romantic relationship between the two executives, who are not related. TechCrunch summarised both reports on November 7. The memorandum itself has not been published, and its claims have not been proven in public or tested in any proceeding.

Nvidia says Huang's interactions with the Megaspeed executive were ordinary business meetings of the kind a chief executive routinely holds with customers, and that he does not give customers national security information. An Nvidia spokesperson told the Times the interactions were "not unusual" and that the company does not share classified information with its customers. As of November 6 the company had not confirmed whether Huang would attend the briefing.

The timing concentrates the exposure. The requested briefing falls on November 18, one day after Nvidia's scheduled third quarter earnings on November 17, and the committees said Chinese infiltration and access to cutting edge American technology remain core investigative priorities for this Congress. For investors the issue is not the briefing itself but what follows it: tighter export controls, deeper end customer scrutiny, or restrictions affecting the data center customers through which Nvidia chips reach Asia.
