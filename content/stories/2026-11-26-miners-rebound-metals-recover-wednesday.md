Miners and metals reversed course on Wednesday, recovering most of Tuesday's losses in a second straight whipsaw session. Gold miners rose 3.53 percent to 73.89 and silver miners rose 4.84 percent to 65.45 at the close, while copper miners gained 1.88 percent to 58.66. BHP rose 1.67 percent to 70.77 and Rio Tinto rose 1.90 percent to 74.58.

The metals moved with the shares this time. Gold rose 1.43 percent to 3,586.2, silver rose 3.95 percent to 41.63 and copper rose 2.18 percent to 5.45 a pound in the market tables. SP Angel's Thursday morning view put gold at 3,565 dollars an ounce against 3,525 previously, silver at 41.4 dollars against 40.3, and copper at 11,885 dollars a tonne against 11,733, the same direction on a different pricing basis.

The recovery only partly repaired Tuesday's damage, when Comex copper fell 3.5 percent and copper miners dropped 4.27 percent. Lithium and battery stocks were the exception on Wednesday, falling 1.55 percent to 52.86 even as the rest of the complex rose, and uranium miners added only 0.40 percent to 27.71.

SP Angel linked the morning's firmer gold price to a rise in core inflation that it said raises the prospects for Federal Reserve tightening, an unusual driver for a precious metals rally and a reminder that the week's moves have been driven more by positioning and rate bets than by mine level news. No major producer reported results or a deal on Wednesday to explain the sector move.
