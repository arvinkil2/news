DoorDash has put a number on agentic commerce, and the number is small. Asked on the company's third quarter earnings call how much ordering is arriving through outside AI agents, chief executive Tony Xu said the company is seeing maybe several hundred orders in total from some of these third party agents, across the whole period they have been running. DoorDash handled 993 million orders in the third quarter alone, and about 3 million deliveries a day.

Xu framed it as a use case that is still forming rather than a channel to resist. He said customers still largely view third party assistants as unsuited to easier tasks such as ordering food, that the predominant route remains the DoorDash app, and that the company is learning with third parties which features actually drive incremental orders. He returned to the point on advertising: agents might eventually create advertising opportunities, but current volumes are too low for that to have value now.

The disclosure matters because DoorDash is not blocking agents. The company has shipped its own chatbot, a text to order agent and a Model Context Protocol connector that lets outside agents place orders, so the low count is a demand observation, not an access restriction. It is also consistent with the company's August position, when Xu described agentic partner volume as quite low so far.

Set against the rest of the week's evidence, a split is emerging in the agent economy. Enterprise and infrastructure agent demand is large and measurable, in cloud spending, CPU requirements and security incidents. Consumer agent transactions, at least in food delivery, the category most often cited as the first to fall, remain negligible. The platform risk that has weighed on DoorDash shares this year is, on its own chief executive's numbers, not yet a volume fact.
