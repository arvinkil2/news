**Latest.** The attacks have reached the kingdom's two holiest regions. Saudi air defences intercepted and destroyed two Houthi drones in the Makkah region on Sunday and two more in the Madinah region, coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki Al-Maliki said, Arab News reported. The Muslim World League condemned the Houthi attacks on Makkah, with Secretary-General Mohammed Al-Issa saying they violate Muslim sanctities and endanger civilian lives, according to the Saudi Press Agency. No casualties or damage were reported, and the interception claims are the coalition's own, uncorroborated as of this update.

**Latest.** Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saria claimed on Sunday that two ballistic missiles struck King Khalid International Airport and the financial centre in Riyadh, and that two drones attacked pumping stations 5 and 6 on Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline in the Riyadh area, RIA Novosti and TASS reported. RIA noted the Saudi side had earlier on Sunday reported intercepting the two ballistic missiles fired toward Riyadh, which matches the coalition interception already reported in this item. Neither report stated damage or casualties, and Saria's target claims are the Houthi side's own account, uncorroborated as of this update.

**Latest.** The Houthi-run Saba agency said a Saudi air raid struck a residential neighbourhood in Sanaa's Shu'ub district on Saturday night, injuring two women and two children, BBC News Arabic reported. A local authority in Lahj governorate said Saudi aircraft also struck a health centre, a school and a mosque in al-Maqatira district, injuring two people. No Saudi confirmation of either strike was reported, a linked BBC headline carried a different casualty count for Sanaa, and both claims are the Houthi side's own account, uncorroborated as of this update.

**Latest.** Arab News, in a fuller account of the same early Sunday statement, reported that the coalition also intercepted three drones launched toward the Eastern Province, the region holding most of Saudi Arabia's oil and gas reserves, while a Saudi Civil Defense alert sounded in Riyadh at 4:08 a.m., three loud explosions were heard minutes later, and an all-clear followed. Earlier reporting by TASS had put the eastern drone count at two, and Al Jazeera reported two missiles and two drones targeting Riyadh, so the drone counts differ between reports and are not resolved. No report carried any statement on damage or casualties from that night's interceptions. Arab News put the official tally of people killed in attacks on the kingdom since September at 26. The claims are the coalition's own, a party to the conflict, and no independent source had corroborated them as of this update.

The Coalition to Support Legitimacy in Yemen said it intercepted and destroyed two drones launched by the Iran-aligned Houthis toward Riyadh and Saudi Arabia's southern region on Saturday, according to its spokesman, Maj. Gen. Turki Al-Malki. The announcement reported no damage and no casualties, and gave no time for the interception.

The launch follows a heavier round earlier in the week, when Saudi air defences intercepted Houthi missiles and drones launched toward the capital, Makkah and other regions on Wednesday. Arab News reported that cross-border fighting between the coalition, which backs Yemen's internationally recognised government, and the Houthis, who control Sanaa, has intensified alongside the US and Israeli war with Iran.
