EU finance ministers formally agreed the Market Integration and Supervision Package (MISP) on Friday, moving significant trading venues, central custodians and crypto-asset service providers under direct supervision of Paris-based ESMA, but the Commission immediately said the compromise falls short after Germany secured a carve-out exempting Deutsche Boerse Group from ESMA oversight and Spain won similar exemptions. Finance Commissioner Maria Luis Albuquerque said she "deeply regret[s] that the compromise now on the table falls significantly short of the level of ambition needed," ECB President Christine Lagarde shared the concerns on scope and governance, and Belgian Finance Minister Jan Jambon said Prime Minister Bart De Wever would challenge the exemptions at next week's EU summit in Brussels. Ireland's Simon Harris, holding the rotating Council presidency, called the package a deal that "will fundamentally change ESMA," while France's Roland Lescure praised it as an important step toward a real Savings and Investment Union.
