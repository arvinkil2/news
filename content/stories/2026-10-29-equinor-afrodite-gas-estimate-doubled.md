Equinor has more than doubled its resource estimate for the Afrodite gas discovery near the Kvitebjørn field in the Norwegian North Sea. A branch appraisal well drilled this autumn puts recoverable gas and condensate at between 77 and 180 million barrels of oil equivalent, the company said on Thursday; the total volumes in the reservoir are about three times as large, but low reservoir permeability means only part can be recovered.

Afrodite was discovered in 2008. Equinor considers it commercially viable and will continue work on a possible subsea development tied back to the Kvitebjørn platform, with gas then sent via existing infrastructure to the Kollsnes processing plant in Øygarden and on to the European market. "To find such large gas volumes in a mature area on the Norwegian continental shelf is very good," said Kjetil Hove, Equinor's executive vice president for Exploration & Production Norway. The appraisal well was drilled by the Deepsea Yantai rig; Wellesley operated the well and Equinor operates the production licence.
