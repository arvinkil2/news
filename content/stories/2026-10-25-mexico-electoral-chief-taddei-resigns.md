Guadalupe Taddei, the president councillor of Mexico's National Electoral Institute (INE), announced on Sunday that she will file her resignation with the Chamber of Deputies on Monday, October 26, saying after deep reflection that protecting the institute, its stability, credibility and independence, comes above any circumstantial or personal interest. She said the recent allegations, carried in local press reports pointing to alleged irregularities in contracting and possible conflicts of interest involving former collaborators and supplier companies, which she denies, must each be examined with evidence through institutional channels. The federal auditor, the Auditoria Superior de la Federacion, announced on Friday an ex officio investigation into improper use of public resources at the INE, the local electoral bodies and linked companies, officials and suppliers, and President Claudia Sheinbaum said there must be no shadow of corruption in the INE, especially eight months before an election. Reforma first reported the resignation earlier on Sunday, and Bloomberg reported it on Sunday night.
