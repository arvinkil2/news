**Latest (Wednesday, October 14):** Kenya has a second confirmed Ebola case. The Health Ministry said a 39 year old man, a close family member of the first patient, developed fever, fatigue and joint pains on Tuesday while already in quarantine under daily monitoring, was isolated and tested positive at both the National Virology Reference Laboratory and the KEMRI-CDC laboratory, and has been moved to a designated treatment centre. Kenya now counts two confirmed cases and one death, the two cases are linked, and the ministry said there is no evidence of spread in the wider community. Of 128 contacts identified from the first case, 113 had been traced by October 13, with 108 in quarantine in Kenya for 21 days of monitoring and five outside the country, whose authorities have been notified; teams are also assessing anyone who contacted the second patient since his symptoms began, including quarantine staff and health workers.

**Latest (Tuesday, October 13):** Confirmed cases have passed 9,000 in the DRC outbreak. Health Ministry data reported by Al Jazeera on Tuesday put the tally at 9,066 confirmed cases and 4,381 deaths, up from the WHO tally of 8,728 cases and 4,205 deaths carried in the original story, making this the largest and deadliest Ebola outbreak in the country's history. The World Health Organization is preparing a clinical trial of vaccine efficacy against the Bundibugyo strain in the coming weeks, its Health Emergencies Programme executive director said on Tuesday; the strain still has no approved vaccine or treatment, and responders face community mistrust and the M23 conflict in the east.

The World Health Organization said Ebola has spread to Alimbongo health zone in North Kivu, with four confirmed cases including two deaths there as of October 6. Kenya confirmed its first imported case of Bundibugyo virus disease the same day: a Kenyan citizen who fell ill in the DRC, traveled through Uganda to Nairobi on October 3, and died on October 5, with Kenyan authorities identifying 28 contacts. The WHO has recorded 8,728 confirmed cases and 4,205 deaths in the DRC outbreak so far, and Doctors Without Borders warns treatment centers in North Kivu are at capacity, with roughly a third of confirmed patients treated in non-specialized facilities.
