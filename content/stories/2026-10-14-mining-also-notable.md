Lux Metals plans to seek about $7 million, about US$5 million, in flow through financing this fall for its La Grande gold project in northern Quebec, targeting a first resource by the end of 2027, Northern Miner reported. The Vancouver junior's raise is small even by junior standards and is flagged here as single sourced.

Sudan is probing deadly strikes on miners by Egyptian forces, with two Egyptians reported killed in Khartoum, allAfrica reported. The item follows the October 11 story here on the strike that killed Sudanese artisanal miners, and casualty claims from parties to the conflict remain uncorroborated, so this roundup treats them as claims, not confirmed counts.

South Africa's Public Investment Corporation chief executive faces questions over mining interests, the Mail and Guardian reported. No figures were verified from the item, and it is noted here as a governance watch item for holders of PIC managed mining exposure.

Lawrence Technological University has launched a $100,000 critical materials innovation challenge in the United States, Mining.com reported. The prize is small, but it sits inside a much larger US push, covered here on October 13, to fund domestic critical minerals capacity.

Almonty Industries chief executive Lewis Black discussed tungsten supply challenges in China and the West in a Northern Miner podcast. Tungsten has no deep Western supply chain outside a handful of producers, and Almonty's Sangdong mine in South Korea is the sector's main growth asset.

Hindalco has partnered with Vi Business to deploy a private network at its Baphlimali bauxite mine in India, International Mining reported, another step in the spread of private 5G style networks from large iron ore and copper operations into mid tier mines.

Equipment and software items filled much of the remaining feed: Epiroc revamped its 10 tonne Scooptram underground loader, EACON retrofitted autonomous haulage onto rigid trucks at a Chinese coal mine in four months, and Seequent and Bergbau both pushed AI tools for geological imagery and mine planning. None carried production or contract figures large enough for a full story.
