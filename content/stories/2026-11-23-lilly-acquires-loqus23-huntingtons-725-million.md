Eli Lilly has acquired LoQus23 Therapeutics, a preclinical company based in Cambridge, England, for up to $725 million in cash. LoQus23 announced the acquisition on November 23. The total includes an upfront payment, whose size was not disclosed, plus payments tied to development and regulatory milestones.

LoQus23 develops oral small molecules designed to slow the pathogenic expansion of triplet repeats. That expansion drives progression in Huntington's disease, myotonic dystrophy type 1 and other repeat expansion disorders. Its lead candidate, LQT-23, is described by the company as a potential first in class oral allosteric inhibitor of MSH3, a DNA mismatch repair protein implicated in the lengthening of repeats in the huntingtin gene.

The asset is early. LQT-23 has not entered clinical trials, and LoQus23 expects to submit a clinical trial application by the end of 2026. Lilly is therefore paying for a mechanism and a platform, not for clinical proof, and the milestone structure keeps most of the $725 million contingent on progress that has not yet occurred.

For Lilly, the purchase adds a genetically targeted neuroscience program at a preclinical price, well below the cost of a late stage acquisition. For the Huntington's field, it puts a major company's development resources behind repeat expansion as a therapeutic target, an approach that will stand or fall on the first human data.
