The Bank of Korea's Monetary Policy Board raised the base rate by 25 basis points, from 3.00 percent to 3.25 percent, at its final meeting of the year on November 26. Five board members backed the increase, while Hwang Kunil and Lee Soohyung voted to hold at 3.00 percent. The rate is now at its highest in two years, and the move was the third hike in the second half of the year, after increases in July and August and a pause in October.

The board said growth has further strengthened, inflation is projected to remain above the 2 percent target for a considerable time, and financial stability risks persist. Consumer price inflation and core inflation both stood at 2.8 percent in October. The bank kept its inflation forecast for this year at 2.7 percent, raised next year's to 2.4 percent from 2.3 percent, and put core inflation at 2.6 percent for both years, above its previous 2.5 percent forecast.

The forecasts carried the bigger news. The bank now expects growth of 3.9 percent this year and 4.1 percent next year, up sharply from the August forecasts of 3.3 percent and 2.9 percent, on strong exports and investment led by the semiconductor sector. Yonhap reported that cumulative exports passed 1 trillion dollars through November 19 and that third-quarter real gross domestic income rose 18.8 percent from a year earlier, the fastest pace since 1977.

On financial stability, the bank said the won stayed broadly stable against the dollar despite foreign stock fund outflows, helped by a large current account surplus, while household loan growth accelerated and expectations of further Seoul housing price rises remain high. The board said it will decide on the timing of any further increases as it assesses inflation, growth and financial stability. CNBC reported the hike was in line with economist expectations and that the KOSPI rose 1.88 percent after the decision; Capital Economics told CNBC the tightening cycle is likely drawing to a close, with macroprudential measures more likely than further hikes to address housing concerns.
