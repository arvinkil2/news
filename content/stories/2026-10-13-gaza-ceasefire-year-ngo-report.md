The Gaza ceasefire reached its first anniversary on October 10 with its central commitments largely unmet, according to a joint statement from more than 100 humanitarian and human rights organisations reported by Al Jazeera. Gaza's Health Ministry put the toll since the truce at 1,493 killed and 5,299 wounded as of October 12, with the cumulative toll since October 2023 at 74,382. Those are ministry figures reported by Al Jazeera, not independently verified counts, and Israel disputes the ministry's framing of individual incidents.

The implementation gap is structural. No international stabilisation force has deployed, aid trucks are entering at roughly a third of the agreed rate, and Israel now controls nearly two thirds of the Strip, above the roughly half it was to retain before further withdrawal, according to the NGO statement. The Health Ministry said 44 percent of essential medicines are out of stock and that no patient had been permitted to leave through Rafah in the past week. A Physicians for Human Rights Israel report found not one of Gaza's 36 hospitals operating at full capacity.

In the West Bank, the olive harvest has become the week's flashpoint, with the Palestinian Colonization and Wall Resistance Commission recording dozens of settler attacks across Friday, Saturday and Sunday, and the Israeli rights group Yesh Din documenting 109 harvest related incidents in ten days to October 6. Israeli forces and settlers dispute the characterisation of several incidents, and those competing claims are not resolved in the reporting.

Politically, Palestinian Authority President Mahmoud Abbas has again postponed legislative elections, this time to September, citing Israeli restrictions, while in Israel the Joint List leader said his alliance could support a coalition against Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu from outside government in exchange for policy commitments including a pathway to Palestinian statehood. Israel votes on October 27. Status: ceasefire in force on paper, implementation contested.
