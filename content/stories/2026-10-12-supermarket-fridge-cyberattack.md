**Latest (Tuesday, October 13, overnight):** the French attack is far larger than first reported. At least 300 Intermarche and Netto stores were targeted, along with more than 100 Cooperative U outlets, 80 Carrefour supermarkets and about 5 percent of Monoprix's roughly 490 stores, the retailers told AFP, in an attack the Paris prosecutor's office called large-scale. Fridge and freezer temperatures were raised as high as 50C, spoiling food and causing fires at several stores. Intermarche chief executive Thierry Cotillard told BFM TV the losses would run to tens of millions of euros nationwide in a single day, and Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu has ordered the national cybersecurity agency ANSSI to investigate, with France coordinating with European counterparts. Danfoss, whose refrigeration software was identified as the vulnerable system, said the affected units were running outdated firmware it had long advised customers to replace. A Sekoia analyst told Reuters that Spain, Portugal, Poland, Germany and Belgium had also been affected, a claim not yet confirmed by authorities in those countries. Some affected French stores are already operating again.

French broadcaster BFMTV reports attackers targeted outdated versions of Danish manufacturer Danfoss's refrigeration control software at numerous supermarkets, remotely forcing defrost cycles with temperatures programmed to reach as high as 50C. Around 150 Intermarche stores were affected along with Leclerc, Carrefour and Monoprix outlets, per retail trade coverage; two stores in Haguenau alone lost about €270,000 in merchandise. Cooperative U president Dominique Schelcher called the attack unprecedented and of the utmost gravity, given refrigeration's role in preserving fresh food.

Separately, Dutch market leader Albert Heijn halted online grocery deliveries and took its website, app and customer service offline after a cybersecurity incident, telling customers it cannot say when services will return. In stores, electronic shelf labels are showing last week's promotions while checkout charges current prices, and customers have been asked to keep receipts for compensation. The company has not disclosed the cause or whether data was compromised.
