The Reserve Bank of India's Monetary Policy Committee began its three-day meeting on Monday, and the question is no longer whether the first hike since February 2023 is coming — it is how far the cycle goes. Eleven of 12 bank chief economists polled by businessline expect +25 basis points on Wednesday, alongside majorities in the Moneycontrol, Reuters and Business Standard polls; IndusInd's Gaurav Kapur puts the October probability at 90 percent.

The cycle-length debate is where the new information sits. SBI's economists advocate a pre-emptive 25-basis-point increase now and another in December, arguing "the balance of risks has shifted towards tighter monetary policy." Union Bank expects +25bp in October plus one or two more in FY27, taking the repo to 5.75–6.00 percent. Morgan Stanley's Aastha Gudwani sees a 5.75 percent terminal rate with front-loaded Q4 hikes; Axis Capital's Prateek Ancha expects 50bp total in CY26 split across October and December. Nomura pushes back: it assigns 80 percent probability to a cycle of just 25–50bp total, warning markets may be pricing more tightening than the RBI will deliver.

The macro case has assembled on four fronts: August CPI at 4.82 percent — the third straight month above the 4 percent target, with WPI near 10 percent; the rupee at ~96.3, down about 6 percent in 2026; Brent above $100 against the RBI's own August assumption of ~$90 a barrel for 2026-27; and the Fed's September hike narrowing the rate differential. Overnight funding stress — INR MIFOR up 118 basis points on the week — shows conditions tightening ahead of the decision, and SBI chairman CS Setty told the Times of India a hike would not materially dent credit growth. Governor Sanjay Malhotra's tone matters as much as the 25 basis points: the August minutes showed the committee open to tightening if price pressures broadened, and they have.
