Latest: The injury count has risen to at least 22, the Associated Press reported, in an account carried by ERT News. Shop windows shattered in the affected area, goods were thrown from shop shelves, and some of the injured fell from stairs while leaving buildings. Meteorological Agency official Ayataka Ebita warned residents to be on alert for mudslides and rockfalls and for the possible effects of rainfall, and said no visible change had been detected in the Nankai Trough area.

A magnitude 5.7 earthquake struck western Shizuoka Prefecture at about 1:04 a.m. local time on Sunday, at a depth of about 40 kilometres, the Japan Meteorological Agency said. The strongest shaking reached upper 5 on the agency's intensity scale in Chita City, Aichi Prefecture, the first reading of that strength in Aichi since March 1997, with lower 5 recorded across parts of Aichi, Shizuoka, Gifu and Nagano. There was no tsunami.

An earlier tally put the injured at at least 15, at least 12 of them in Aichi, mostly in falls and head injuries, and three in Shizuoka, Yonhap reported, citing NHK and other Japanese reporting, and no deaths were reported. Water supplies were cut in parts of Higashiura in Aichi, the Tokaido Shinkansen ran at reduced speed between Shizuoka and Hashima with delays, and some local lines suspended service from the first trains of the day.

At an early morning press conference the agency said residents should expect possible shaking of up to upper 5 for about a week, and that the possibility of a large earthquake is high especially over the next two to three days. The agency said the quake appears unlikely to be related to a Nankai Trough mega-earthquake, while it keeps monitoring the activity.
