Foreign residents were heavy net buyers of US assets in September. The Treasury's International Capital release puts the month's total net inflow, across long-term securities, short-term securities and banking flows, at $261.0 billion, made up of $267.0 billion of net private inflows against $6.0 billion of net official outflows.

Long-term buying drove the total. Foreign net purchases of long-term US securities were $186.7 billion, of which private investors accounted for $148.3 billion and official institutions $38.4 billion, while US residents bought $31.4 billion of long-term foreign securities, leaving an adjusted overall net long-term inflow of $155.3 billion. Foreign holdings of Treasury bills rose $3.5 billion and banks' own net dollar liabilities to foreign residents increased $108.6 billion. The next release, covering October, is scheduled for December 15.
