China's State Council pledged a bigger role for state-backed investors in steadying the stock market on Friday, in Beijing's strongest gesture yet during a selloff that had taken mainland benchmarks to 16-month lows. Premier Li Qiang chaired the cabinet's executive meeting, which studied deepening the comprehensive reform of capital market investment and financing and said the capital market is crucial to the overall economy, according to the Xinhua readout.

The meeting called for the market stabilisation mechanism to be improved, existing policies implemented and new ones planned, and for listed companies to be encouraged to raise dividends and buybacks. It said the role of the "national team" in stabilising the market should be enhanced and Central Huijin's function as a quasi-stabilisation fund further optimised, with sufficient liquidity support. Policies guiding medium and long-term funds, including insurance funds, corporate annuities and social security funds, into the market should be improved, and defences against risks spilling over from overseas markets strengthened. The meeting gave no details on the size or timing of any new measures.

Stocks rallied sharply in afternoon trading after the rare midday statement. The Shanghai Composite and the blue-chip CSI 300 both jumped more than 3 percent, putting them on track for their best session in two years, and the yuan reversed early losses, Reuters reported. The tech-focused STAR 50 index surged about 12 percent, and chip indexes rose more than 16 percent, as the artificial intelligence and semiconductor names at the centre of the rout led the rebound.

The selloff had been driven by a sharp unwinding in local AI, chipmaking and industrial stocks and by heightened trade tension with Washington, which last week further tightened Chinese access to advanced American AI chips. A month of deep losses had wiped out all of mainland stocks' gains for 2026 before Friday's rebound, which bargain buying reinforced once state support was in prospect.
