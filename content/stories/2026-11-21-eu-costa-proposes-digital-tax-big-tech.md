European Council President Antonio Costa on Saturday proposed further cuts to the European Commission's draft EU budget for 2028 to 2034, reducing it by 40 billion euros to 1.582 trillion euros against the Irish presidency's October draft, and added new revenue sources including a digital tax to help finance EU policies. The tax would charge 3 percent of annual net turnover from online advertising, digital intermediation services and user-generated data at companies with at least 50 million euros of EU digital turnover and more than 750 million euros globally, a design that would fall mostly on US groups such as Google, Microsoft, Meta, Apple and Amazon. EU leaders will discuss the draft at a special summit on November 26 and 27, aiming for agreement in December.

**Update, November 22.** The European Parliament's lead negotiators rejected the size of Costa's negotiating box on Saturday. Co-rapporteurs Siegfried Muresan and Carla Tavares said the proposal, at 0.97 percent of EU gross national income excluding NextGenerationEU borrowing costs, falls well short of the 1.11 percent agreed for the current budget in 2020 and cannot finance new priorities such as competitiveness, defence and innovation without cutting cohesion and agriculture. They welcomed the work on own resources, including the digital levy Parliament proposed. The Parliament's Development Committee chair, Barry Andrews, said the box cuts development and humanitarian funding by 21 percent against the Commission's original proposal. Parliament debates the proposal on November 25, and its consent, by absolute majority, is required for the framework to take effect. Leaders discuss the box at the special European Council on November 26 and 27.
