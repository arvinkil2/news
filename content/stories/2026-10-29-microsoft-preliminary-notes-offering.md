**Latest, October 29:** Microsoft's bond sale is expected to raise about $25 billion, a source familiar with the matter told Reuters, across as many as 10 tranches with maturities ranging from two to 40 years. Reuters reported that Bloomberg News first reported the deal and that no final decision had been made on the size, which may change according to demand. The sale would be Microsoft's first United States investment-grade bond offering in more than nine years, and it follows chief financial officer Amy Hood telling investors on Wednesday's earnings call that the company would pursue additional sources of funding when attractive to preserve cash flows and financial flexibility. Wells Fargo, JPMorgan Chase and UBS are managing the sale, according to the reporting.

Microsoft is in the market with a new multi-tranche dollar bond offering. A preliminary prospectus supplement on Form 424B2, filed on Thursday morning, covers floating-rate notes and a series of fixed-rate notes across several maturities, under the company's December 12, 2024 base prospectus. Every economic term in the preliminary document is blank: no tranche sizes, coupons, spreads or maturity years are stated, and the supplement is marked subject to completion, dated October 29, 2026.

The filing landed the morning after Microsoft's quarterly report on Form 10-Q, filed on October 28, and final terms, when priced, will be set in a completed supplement.
