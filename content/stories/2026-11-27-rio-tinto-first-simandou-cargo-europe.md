Rio Tinto has shipped the first Europe-bound iron ore cargo from Guinea's Simandou project. A vessel carrying 79,081 tonnes of iron ore fines departed Simandou's Morebaya port on November 23 and is expected to reach Dunkirk, France, in early December, according to commodities data firm Kpler; Simfer, the Rio Tinto-led venture operating Simandou's two southern blocks, shipped the cargo, and ArcelorMittal operates a steelmaking plant in Dunkirk.

A second Europe-bound cargo is being loaded for delivery to Gijón, Spain, where ArcelorMittal also has steelmaking operations, Kpler reported. Simandou began shipping iron ore to China last year and is ramping toward a targeted annual production capacity of 120 million tonnes; Guinea's monthly iron ore exports surpassed 5 million tonnes for the first time in October, bringing shipments for 2026 to 22 million tonnes, according to BIMCO.
