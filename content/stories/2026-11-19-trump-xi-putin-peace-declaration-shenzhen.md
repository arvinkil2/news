China, the United States and Russia adopted a joint peace declaration at the trilateral summit of their leaders in Shenzhen on Thursday, covering nuclear risks, reform of the United Nations and oil supply, according to a Deutsche Welle report crediting EFE and AFP. The declaration was disseminated by Chinese state media after the unprecedented meeting between Xi Jinping, Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin, held on the sidelines of the APEC summit, and the Kremlin also reported it.

On nuclear weapons, the three leaders committed to enter into a dialogue on reducing nuclear risks and preventing nuclear proliferation, and reaffirmed that a nuclear war cannot be won and must not be fought. The document, as reported, contains no concrete disarmament measures, ceilings or timetable. The three also said they share a commitment to promote reform of the United Nations so that it gains effectiveness, faces global challenges more effectively and fulfils its fundamental mission of maintaining international peace and security; no specific reform measures, structures or timetable are reported.

On energy, the declaration announces an agreement to increase the production and supply of petroleum products, and the leaders stressed the importance of guaranteeing a stable flow of energy amid tensions over international energy supply linked to the war in Iran and the situation in the Strait of Hormuz, a key oil transit route for Asian countries in particular. No volumes, percentages, prices or dates are given in the account read, so the oil commitment is a statement of intent rather than a supply schedule markets can price.

The three heads of state said they had held a profound exchange of views on the current international situation and committed themselves to defending strategic stability and international security. The summit itself was the scheduled catalyst flagged on this site's calendar: the first trilateral meeting of the three leaders, watched going in for tariff and trade language, Ukraine war language, and energy and sanctions signals. The declaration as reported addresses energy directly, is silent in the account read on tariffs and on Ukraine, and its practical effect now rests on follow-through by three governments that remain in dispute on much of the agenda it names.
