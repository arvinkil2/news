**Latest, October 26:** Machado told the Spanish news agency EFE on Monday that the time had come for serious and constructive dialogue, and that any dialogue has to lead to recognition of popular sovereignty and respect for the constitution. Her spokesperson Claudia Macero told Union Radio that Machado has expressed her willingness to sit down not only with interim president Delcy Rodriguez but with whoever she has to, before the country, in total transparency and with an agenda. She reached Caracas on Sunday in a convoy met by crowds of supporters, hours after attending mass at the church in Santa Rosa, Barquisimeto. Rodriguez responded by calling the return a show, the TT news agency reported, after saying on Sunday that Venezuela is a country of peace where people can express themselves and protest. No meeting has been scheduled, and Rodriguez has promised elections without announcing a date, while Machado's Vente Venezuela movement is pressing for a vote in 2027.

**Latest, October 25:** US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said on X on Sunday that the conditions for Machado's safe return are the direct result of the US supported reconciliation talks between the Chavista government and the delegated commission of the 2015 National Assembly, El Pitazo reported. Rubio said the same talks had already secured the release of hundreds of political prisoners and the return from exile of hundreds of opposition figures, and called them the best opportunity Venezuela has had in thirty years to reach a free and fair election and a lasting democracy by peaceful means. Dinorah Figuera, who heads the opposition delegation at the dialogue table, said the return in freedom was the result of the talks and of progress toward full guarantees for Venezuelans. Asked by reporters in Washington before Rubio's statement, President Donald Trump said he knew nothing about the return and hoped she would be safe. Minutes after Machado's own message, her party Vente Venezuela released images of her entering a church in Santa Rosa, in Lara state, surrounded by supporters.

Maria Corina Machado announced her return on social media on Sunday morning, writing that she is here, with Venezuelans, and Edmundo Gonzalez confirmed shortly after that she is on Venezuelan territory and said he himself would return very soon. She had been outside the country for almost ten months, since leaving in December 2025 to collect the Nobel Peace Prize, and El Pais reported at least seven attempts to return in the last three months by boat, commercial and private flights and overland were blocked or cancelled at the last moment. El Pais reported, citing Newsmax, that she entered via Colombia and planned a public appearance on Sunday.
