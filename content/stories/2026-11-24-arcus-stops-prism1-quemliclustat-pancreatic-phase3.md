Arcus Biosciences said on November 23 that it is discontinuing PRISM-1, its Phase 3 trial of quemliclustat, after a pre specified, event driven interim analysis showed the pre specified efficacy boundary for overall survival was not reached. The decision followed a recommendation from the trial's independent data monitoring committee.

PRISM-1 enrolled approximately 650 patients with previously untreated metastatic pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma and tested quemliclustat, a small molecule inhibitor of CD73, plus gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel chemotherapy against placebo plus the same chemotherapy. At the interim analysis, the combination showed similar efficacy on the primary and secondary endpoints to chemotherapy alone, the company said. Arcus said patients in both arms reached the longest overall survival observed to date in a global first line metastatic pancreatic cancer study, a point its chief medical officer cited while calling the result deeply disappointing.

Quemliclustat has no regulatory approval and its future is now uncertain. Fierce Biotech reported that Arcus has not clarified whether development will continue in other settings.

For Arcus, the pipeline weight shifts to casdatifan, its HIF-2 alpha inhibitor in Phase 3 for clear cell renal cell carcinoma. For the CD73 field, PRISM-1 is the randomized proof the mechanism needed and did not get.
