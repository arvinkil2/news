China's industrial producer prices rose 5.7 percent from a year earlier in October and 0.7 percent from September, the National Bureau of Statistics said, with producer prices for the first ten months of the year up 2.7 percent on average. The increase was concentrated upstream: mining prices rose 27.9 percent from a year earlier, raw material prices 11.6 percent and processing prices 3.5 percent, while prices for consumer goods leaving factories fell 0.1 percent. Coal mining prices rose 36.6 percent from a year earlier, oil and gas extraction 35.0 percent and fuel processing 29.9 percent, and the prices producers pay for inputs rose faster still, up 7.9 percent from a year earlier, led by fuel and power at 18.6 percent.

Consumer prices told a different story. The consumer price index rose 0.7 percent from a year earlier and was unchanged on the month, as food prices fell 2.9 percent from a year earlier, with fresh vegetables down 16.9 percent and pork down 8.6 percent. Prices excluding food and energy rose 0.8 percent from a year earlier, and the main upward pressure came from transport fuel, up 20.2 percent from a year earlier, inside a transport and communication category up 5.5 percent.
