Australia's parliament has passed the centre-left Labor government's gas reservation scheme, which will require liquefied natural gas exporters to set aside up to 20 percent of their exports for the domestic market starting in 2028, Reuters reported. The opposition Coalition voted with the government in the Senate on Thursday night, giving the scheme the numbers it needed, and a Greens bid for a 25 percent tax on gas exports was rejected.

Western Australia is exempt from the national scheme: it has run its own reservation policy for about two decades, under which 15 percent of gas exports are withheld for the domestic market. The scheme sets no price for the reserved gas, and the detailed obligations for each exporter are still to be set before the requirements begin in 2028.
