France's sovereign risk premium keeps climbing. The 10-year OAT-Bund spread reached 149.17 basis points on Friday, per LSEG, the highest since 2012 and up from around 84 basis points in early September. The 10-year OAT yield fell only 1.0bp to 4.908 percent on Friday while Bunds rallied 8.2bp to 3.451 percent as investors fled to safety; a Monday note put the 10Y at 4.92 percent, its highest since July 2002, and the spread at 152bp.

The €43 billion 2027 budget unveiled October 1, savings built on freezing public-sector wages and pensions, did not land. "A clear indication that the budget details announced in parliament did not go down well with investors," MUFG's Derek Halpenny wrote. France's spread is now wider than Italy's and Greece's, and 5-year French CDS hit 81bp, their highest in several years.

The mechanics are unforgiving. Public debt stands near €3.6 trillion, roughly 122 percent of GDP, with annual interest costs approaching €91 billion. Paris is refinancing sub-1 percent legacy coupons near 4.9 percent, with roughly €340 billion in gross issuance planned for next year. Every extra basis point costs the state about €31 million a year in interest. And the budget that is supposed to fix this must still survive a parliament that has ousted prime ministers over cuts twice in two years, while 400 to 500 schools closed Monday over protests and the April 2027 presidential election looms.

Correction to the calendar record: the draft 2027 budget was presented on October 1 by Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu, not October 8. The package totals a 54-billion-euro adjustment, with 43 billion euros in new measures, and targets a deficit of 5.0 percent of GDP in 2027 versus 5.4 percent expected for 2026. Public debt stands near 119-122 percent of GDP, with debt service at 79.2 billion euros in 2026 and 91.2 billion in 2027. The National Assembly debate begins October 13.

The political fallout is widening. Marine Le Pen unveiled a rival plan on October 6 pledging 140 billion euros in net savings by 2032 compared with 2026, up from the 125 billion she had previously planned, with most cuts front-loaded in the first three years of a potential presidency and a deficit target of 3 percent of GDP by 2030, according to Reuters. The 10-year OAT-Bund spread blew past 130bp on budget day to roughly 141-152bp, the widest since 2012, and France now pays more to borrow than Italy or Greece.

The mechanics that decide whether France can hold the line are now on a two-week clock: the October 13 Assembly debate will show whether Lecornu's package survives contact with a fragmented chamber, and the spread's trajectory will show whether markets give him the benefit of the doubt.
