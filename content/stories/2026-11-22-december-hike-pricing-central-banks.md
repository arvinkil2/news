Interest rate markets ended the week still pricing increases at every major central bank's December meeting, after what investingLive described as a dovish repricing almost across the board as growth concerns intensified amid the stock market rout. The Federal Reserve was priced for a full 25 basis point increase at its December meeting, at 99 percent probability in the compilation, with 70 basis points of tightening priced by the end of 2027. The ECB was priced for 23 basis points, a 93 percent probability of a hike, and the Bank of England for 20 basis points at 79 percent.

Further east and south the picture was similar. The Bank of Japan was priced for 16 basis points, a 66 percent probability of a hike at its December 17 to 18 meeting, the Reserve Bank of New Zealand for 21 basis points at 83 percent, and the Bank of Canada for 15 basis points at 62 percent. The Swiss National Bank was the exception, priced for 4 basis points with an 82 percent probability of no change, and the Reserve Bank of Australia was priced for no move at its next meeting with cuts expected across 2027.

The pricing sits against a bond market that rallied hard on Friday even as the hike path stayed intact. In this site's market tables, the US 5 year yield fell 16.5 basis points on the day to 4.70 percent, the 10 year fell 14.0 basis points to 4.91 percent and the 30 year fell 9.8 basis points to 5.30 percent, the bond side of the same risk off session that cut equities and metals.

That combination, front end pricing for hikes and long yields falling on growth fear, is the week's central tension. It says investors expect central banks to tighten into a weakening tape, and it leaves the December meetings, the Fed on December 8 to 9, the Bank of Canada on December 9, the SNB on December 10 and the ECB, Bank of England and Bank of Japan in the following week, carrying more weight than usual.
