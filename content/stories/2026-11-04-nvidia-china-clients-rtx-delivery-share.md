Chinese AI and cloud majors including Alibaba, Tencent and ByteDance ordered about 6 million of Nvidia's RTX Pro 5500 chips, worth about $70 billion at more than $11,000 a card, but expect to receive only some tens of thousands of chips, roughly 1 percent of the order, within the next four months, Bloomberg reported. Seoul Economic Daily put the near-term delivery figure at about 60,000 chips and reported that buyers had previously expected about 700,000 chips by the end of 2027.

The order book itself is not new. Reuters reported on October 20 that ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent alone had ordered more than 3 million of the cards, worth more than $35 billion, and that Nvidia had told partners it could fulfil only 5 to 10 percent of requested volume, against global production capacity of no more than about 3 million cards in 2027. Bloomberg's wider count doubles the Chinese order book to about 6 million cards while cutting the near-term delivery share to about 1 percent, with the first several thousand cards expected by the end of this year and a further batch around February.

The RTX Pro 5500 matters because it sits below the thresholds of the United States export controls, making it one of the most powerful Nvidia parts Chinese firms can still buy directly. The reported rationing is a capacity problem rather than a legal one, and Nvidia is reported to have told customers that supply will stay limited because of production constraints and scheduling conflicts. Nvidia has not commented on the figures, and none of the buyers has confirmed them.

The squeeze lands in the same week as two other closures of China's indirect routes to advanced compute. Oracle has scrapped a $7 billion chip leasing contract with Tencent under United States pressure, and smugglers are reported to be moving restricted high-bandwidth memory into China through a packaging loophole in the December 2024 export rules. Both stories are covered separately in today's edition.
