BHP said copper production fell 19 percent year on year to 400,700 tonnes in the September quarter, as lower planned concentrator feed grades and scheduled maintenance at Escondida and Spence in Chile combined with severe weather, safety stops after a worker death at Escondida on September 23, and a conveyor failure at Carrapateena in South Australia. Iron ore production rose 4 percent to 66.5 million tonnes, with mine and port records in Western Australia despite intensive maintenance, and BHP kept fiscal 2027 guidance unchanged at 1.65 million to 1.8 million tonnes of copper and 260 million to 272 million tonnes of iron ore. The company said an average realised copper price of 7.01 US dollars a pound, up 53 percent year on year, cushioned earnings, and its Sydney listed shares fell 2.1 percent to 59.22 Australian dollars in early trading.

Update, October 20: the full operational review confirms the shape of the quarter. Copper South Australia production fell 16 percent year on year to 60,700 tonnes, primarily on the underground conveyor failure at Carrapateena; BHP installed a replacement belt within seven weeks, ramped the conveyor through September back to full capacity, and held Copper South Australia guidance at 290,000 to 320,000 tonnes. Chief executive Brandon Craig attributed the lower group copper output to planned grade declines and scheduled maintenance alongside severe weather in Chile, safety stops and the Carrapateena failure. The same review carried the company's first Olympic Dam block cave resource, covered in a separate story today. Mining Weekly's report that BHP missed quarterly copper output estimates on Chile mine disruptions is the same release and adds no new figures.
