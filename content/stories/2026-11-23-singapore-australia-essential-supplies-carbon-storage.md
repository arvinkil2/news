Singapore and Australia signed two agreements in Canberra on November 24 at the 11th Singapore-Australia Annual Leaders' Meeting, in a ceremony witnessed by Prime Minister Lawrence Wong and Prime Minister Anthony Albanese. A new memorandum of understanding expands the two countries' essential supplies arrangements beyond petroleum oils and liquefied natural gas to 10 categories, including food, semiconductors, pharmaceuticals and medical products, water treatment chemicals and telecommunications equipment, supplementing a legally binding protocol signed in July under their free trade agreement. Singapore supplied 55 percent of Australia's petrol, 15 percent of its diesel and 23 percent of its aviation fuel, according to Australian government figures cited in July.

The second agreement sets a framework for transporting carbon dioxide captured in Singapore to Australia for offshore geological storage, formalises the transfer of responsibility for accounting for the transported carbon, and lays groundwork for measuring and reporting it under Paris Agreement guidelines. Wong called the arrangements practical steps that would matter when the next disruption comes. No volumes, timelines or storage locations were announced for the carbon framework.
