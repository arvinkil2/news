Japan's wholesale prices rose 6.8 percent in the year to September, the Bank of Japan said, below the 7.6 percent median forecast and August's 7.6 percent gain. On the month, prices fell 0.3 percent, against expectations for a 0.5 percent rise, as the headline came in softer than feared.

The details were less comforting. Companies kept passing higher fuel and chemical costs to each other, and the yen-based import price index jumped 24.5 percent year on year, showing the weak currency is still inflating raw material costs. The reading feeds into the BOJ's policy meeting later this month, where the board publishes fresh growth and inflation forecasts.
