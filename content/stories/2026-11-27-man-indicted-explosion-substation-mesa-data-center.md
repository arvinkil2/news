Prosecutors in Arizona have charged a 23-year-old man over an explosion at a power substation that supplies data center load in Mesa. The Maricopa County Attorney's Office announced on November 24 that a grand jury indicted Xander McLendon on five felony counts: one count of arson of a structure or property, one count of burglary, two counts of misconduct involving weapons, and one count of criminal damage.

Prosecutors allege that on June 21, 2026, McLendon entered the fenced Salt River Project substation near Elliot and Signal Butte roads and attempted to place an improvised explosive device on a breaker. The device exploded and started a fire, causing nearly 148,000 dollars in damage. McLendon was burned in the explosion and called 911 himself. SRP confirmed to investigators that he was not an employee.

Datacenter Dynamics reported that the substation was built by SRP to serve Apple's data center in Mesa and has been operating since 2021. Apple acquired the Mesa site after its supplier GT Advanced Technologies went out of business, and pledged 2 billion dollars to develop it in 2015.

McLendon has pleaded not guilty, is being held on a 1 million dollar secured bond, and is scheduled to go to trial on March 10, 2027. No motive has been announced, and no outage at the data center was reported in connection with the June incident. The charges are allegations, and he is presumed innocent unless convicted.
