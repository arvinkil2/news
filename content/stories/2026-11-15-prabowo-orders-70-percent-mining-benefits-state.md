Indonesian President Prabowo Subianto said on Saturday that the state should secure a greater share of the benefits from all mining permits and, if necessary, hold controlling stakes in mining assets. "From now on, all mining permits, at least 70% must benefit the state. If necessary, the shares should be state-controlled," he said in a speech in Palu, Central Sulawesi, to members of Muhammadiyah, one of Indonesia's largest Islamic groups, adding that Indonesia remained open to mining investors but that profits must predominantly go to the Indonesian people.

A presidential palace statement narrowed the figure, saying the 70 percent referred to the proceeds from management of mining permits, and no formula, regulation or treatment of existing permits has been published. The directive revives a number the government had set aside in June, when Energy Minister Bahlil Lahadalia said a proposed gross split scheme, under which 70 percent of mineral and coal output would go to the government and 30 percent to the operator, had been dropped after consultations with Prabowo.
