**Latest.** The producer price release published alongside the consumer figures showed the pipeline pressure building faster than the headline rate. Producer input prices rose 10.8 percent in the year to October, up from 8.3 percent in September and the largest annual rise since February 2023, with input prices up 2.2 percent on the month as crude oil input prices rose 22.8 percent in October and 88.4 percent on the year. Factory gate output prices rose 5.1 percent on the year, up from a revised 4.7 percent in September and the largest annual rise since April 2023, led by coke and refined petroleum products, up 72.4 percent on the year. The ONS said the increases in both input and output inflation were mainly caused by oil-related products, as the data continue to be affected by the conflict in the Middle East.

UK consumer prices rose 3.6 percent in the 12 months to October 2026, up from 3.5 percent in September, and rose 0.5 percent on the month, the Office for National Statistics said. The broader CPIH measure, which includes owner occupiers' housing costs, rose 3.7 percent on the year, up from 3.6 percent.

Transport made the largest upward contribution, as motor fuel prices rose 33.2 percent on the year, the largest annual rise since July 2022; average petrol prices reached 174.7 pence per litre, the highest since August 2022, and diesel reached 199.5 pence per litre, the highest since ONS records began in January 1996. Core CPI, excluding energy, food, alcohol and tobacco, was little changed at 2.8 percent, services inflation was 3.7 percent, and restaurants and hotels made the largest downward contribution.
