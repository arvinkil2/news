The European Commission has sent Temu preliminary findings that its measures to tackle illegal products on its platform are ineffective, including what the Commission called a weak content moderation system and ineffective sanctions against traders. The findings open a further strand of the Digital Services Act investigation the Commission began in 2024, after it fined Temu 200 million euros in May over a deficient 2024 risk assessment.

According to the reporting, sellers must submit test reports for only a small share of products, Temu often does not check the reports it does receive, inspections have not kept pace with the growth in listings, and the results of spot checks are frequently not followed up. Cosmetics, toys and electronics, classed as particularly high risk, are not prioritised for physical checks.

Tech Commissioner Henna Virkkunen said shopping online should not mean taking the risk of acquiring illegal and unsafe products. Temu, owned by PDD Holdings, now has the chance to respond before the Commission concludes its investigation; a confirmed breach can draw a fine of up to 6 percent of worldwide annual turnover.
