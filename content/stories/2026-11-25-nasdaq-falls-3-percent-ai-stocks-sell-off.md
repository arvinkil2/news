US stocks fell sharply on Wednesday morning as anxiety over artificial intelligence investment, unextinguished by Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang's return, drove a second consecutive day of selling in the AI names that had rebounded on his comeback. At 10:05 a.m. ET the Nasdaq Composite was down 720.32 points, or 3.04 percent, at 23,013.22, the S&P 500 was down 146.20 points, or 2.03 percent, at 7,054.89, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average was down 634.30 points, or 1.27 percent, at 49,384.40, Yonhap reported from New York.

Nvidia opened at $144.13, down 7.24 percent from the previous close, and slid as low as $141.17 intraday, a fall of 9.15 percent. AI-related stocks that had surged on Huang's return fell sharply for a second day, surrendering a considerable share of those gains.

Yonhap, citing Wall Street analysis, said the prospect of further benchmark rate increases and concern that AI data centre investment rests on massive borrowing are eroding the appeal of highly valued technology stocks. Two of this morning's own releases fed that pressure: the Bureau of Economic Analysis put October PCE inflation at 3.8 percent from a year earlier, with the core index at 3.3 percent, readings that strengthen the Federal Reserve's tightening case ahead of the December 8 to 9 FOMC, and the US Trade Representative determined against 14 economies in its Section 301 excess-capacity investigations and proposed new tariffs on all of them, including up to 20 percent on China and 15 percent on the European Union.
