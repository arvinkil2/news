Latest: Tokyo closed with the losses largely intact. The Nikkei 225 ended down 6.04 percent at 57,389.00 on Friday, with falling stocks outnumbering advancing ones on the Tokyo Stock Exchange by 3,589 to 118 and the Nikkei Volatility index up 34.35 percent to 33.56, according to Investing.com's close report. Japan Tobacco was the index's worst performer, down 19.31 percent, followed by Rohm, down 15.00 percent, and Ajinomoto, down 12.40 percent; the report attributed the fall to losses in the paper and pulp, transport and communication sectors.

Latest: The rout spread across the region on Friday, and the worst of the early falls partly retraced. The Kospi was last traded about 4.1 percent lower after plunging as much as 8.8 percent to 4,911.28, its lowest since early February, when the Korea Exchange triggered a 20 minute circuit breaker after the 8 percent drop, Investing.com reported. The Nikkei 225 was last about 5.5 percent lower after falling as much as 7.1 percent, with the broader Topix down nearly 6 percent, Taiwan's benchmark down 4.1 percent, Singapore's Straits Times down 2.4 percent, China's Shanghai Composite down 1 percent and the CSI 300 down 1.3 percent, Hong Kong's Hang Seng down 0.3 percent and Australia's ASX 200 down 1 percent. Samsung Electronics was down 4.1 percent, SK hynix down 7 percent and TSMC down more than 4 percent at the report's latest readings, and US stock futures were trading higher in the Asian session.

Latest: Tokyo's cash session confirmed the fall the futures had pointed to. The Nikkei 225 fell 6.2 percent to 57,320.01 by 1:50 p.m. Tokyo time on Friday, more than 20 percent below its record peak of 72,831.73 on June 22 and so into bear market territory, after a 1.8 percent fall on Thursday took it to its lowest close in more than five months. Semiconductors led again in Japan, with Advantest down 9 percent, SoftBank down 11 percent and Kioxia down 10 percent, according to Investing.com.

Latest: The selloff intensified on Friday in Seoul. Samsung Electronics and SK hynix each plunged more than 10 percent in early Friday trading, dragging the Kospi down 8.8 percent and below the 5,000 milestone, and the Korea Exchange halted trading for 20 minutes shortly after the open, Bloomberg reported. That followed Thursday's global rout, in which the Nasdaq 100 fell 6.4 percent. In Japan, Nikkei 225 futures traded at 57,255.00 at 8:02 a.m. Tokyo time, against Thursday's close of 61,077.90 for the underlying index, pointing to further falls when Tokyo opened.

Latest: The selloff deepened further in the afternoon, with the Kospi down 5.20 percent at 5,388.51 at 2:37 p.m. and the Kosdaq triggering its own sell sidecar at 2:27 p.m., the first time both markets have triggered sell sidecars on the same day since October 15, according to MoneyToday and Seoul Economic Daily. Samsung Electronics was down 7.57 percent and SK hynix down 9.38 percent at 2:37 p.m. Earlier in the session the rest of the region split along the same AI line, with Tokyo's Nikkei down about 1.5 percent while Hong Kong's Hang Seng rose about 3 percent, according to investingLive's Asia-Pacific wrap.

South Korea's Kospi fell sharply on Thursday morning in Seoul, down 4.73 percent at 5,414.82 at 10:24 a.m. after opening down 1.82 percent at 5,580.09, while the Kosdaq was down 4.57 percent at 682.30. At 10:16 a.m. the Kospi 200 futures index, down 5.21 percent at 843.96, triggered a sell sidecar that suspended program sell orders for five minutes, the first such halt in about a week.

Samsung Electronics was down 6.65 percent and SK Hynix down 8.88 percent at the 10:24 a.m. snapshot, widening losses from the open. Yonhap attributed the fall to growing concern about tighter regulation of AI data centre construction after the Democratic Party's sweep of the US midterm elections, alongside a fall in the US semiconductor index.
