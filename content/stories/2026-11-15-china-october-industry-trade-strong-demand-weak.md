**Latest.** The release missed economists' estimates on each of its main series. Retail sales rose 0.4 percent on the year against a forecast of 0.8 percent, slowing from 1.1 percent in September, industrial output rose 4.8 percent against 5.3 percent expected, down from 5.5 percent in September, and fixed asset investment fell 8.1 percent over the first ten months against an expected fall of 7 percent, CNBC reported, a deterioration it described as unprecedented outside the Covid-19 period.

China's October data split between a strong supply side and a weak demand side. Industrial value added rose 4.8 percent from a year earlier, with manufacturing up 5.4 percent, equipment manufacturing up 10.6 percent and high technology manufacturing up 17.6 percent, and goods trade rose 31.2 percent on the year, exports up 28.7 percent and imports up 34.7 percent.

Demand lagged well behind. Retail sales rose 0.4 percent on the year in October and 1.0 percent over the first ten months, fixed asset investment fell 8.1 percent over the first ten months, and property development investment fell 21.6 percent, with new home sales down 13.7 percent by floor area and 14.3 percent by value.

Prices and jobs were steady. Consumer prices rose 0.7 percent on the year, core consumer prices 0.8 percent, producer prices rose 5.7 percent with the increase widening 0.7 percentage point from September, and the surveyed urban unemployment rate was 5.1 percent, down 0.1 percentage point on the month, while the manufacturing purchasing managers index stood at 49.3 percent.
