South Korea's Kospi fell sharply from the open on Wednesday, sliding into the 5 percent range in the first minutes of trading and triggering the Korea Exchange's sell sidecar, the mechanism that suspends program sell orders when futures fall steeply.

The index opened at 4,809.00, down 197.03 points or 3.94 percent from the previous close, giving up the 5,000 level at the open. By 9:12 a.m. in Seoul it stood at 4,775.37, down 230.66 points or 4.61 percent, and Yonhap reported the decline then widening into the 5 percent range.

The sidecar was triggered at 9:08 a.m. after the Kospi 200 futures index fell to 738.20, down 39.90 points or 5.12 percent from the previous close. A sell sidecar takes effect when the futures index falls 5 percent or more and holds there for a minute, and it suspends the effectiveness of program sell quotes for five minutes.

The selling was heaviest in the two chipmakers that carry the index. Samsung Electronics fell 6.65 percent and SK Hynix fell 6.56 percent in early trading. The Kosdaq index fell 3.35 percent to 643.57.

The fall is the Kospi's second drop of about 5 percent in a week. On November 20 the index closed 5.21 percent lower in the AI-linked selloff that swept Japan and Taiwan as well, and Korean exchanges had to suspend program sell orders then too.
