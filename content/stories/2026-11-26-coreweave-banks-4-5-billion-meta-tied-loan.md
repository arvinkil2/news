Bankers at Morgan Stanley and Mitsubishi UFJ Financial Group are preparing a loan sale of up to $4.5 billion for CoreWeave, the AI cloud provider, potentially as soon as next week, Bloomberg reported on November 25, citing people familiar with the matter. CoreWeave is seeking at least $4 billion, with the total possibly reaching $4.5 billion, in a delayed-draw term loan structure.

The proceeds would sit in an escrow account and be drawn to fund graphics processing units for a data center in Dalton, Georgia, serving Meta Platforms. The financing is secured by the GPUs and underpinned by CoreWeave's contract with Meta, which Bloomberg and subsequent reporting put at $14.2 billion through 2031.

Because the credit rests on Meta's contract rather than on CoreWeave itself, the loan is expected to receive investment-grade ratings even though CoreWeave carries a speculative-grade rating. Initial pricing discussions centre on a yield of about 7 to 7.5 percent. By contrast, CoreWeave's 9 percent bonds due 2031 traded at record lows on Wednesday to yield about 18 percent, according to Trace data cited by Bloomberg.

Prospective buyers had already signalled indications of interest roughly in line with the proposed size, the reporting said, but the schedule and terms are still being finalised and could change. Neither the final size, at the $4 billion floor or the $4.5 billion top, nor the pricing is settled.
