Kraft Heinz announced on November 4 that it has entered a definitive agreement to sell its North America coffee business to an affiliate of One Rock Capital Partners at an enterprise value of 650 million dollars. Kraft Heinz will retain a 30 percent non controlling equity stake in the business.

The perimeter is brands, plants and people: Maxwell House, Gevalia, Nabob, Yuban, Tassimo, Ethical Bean and Sanka, the coffee manufacturing facilities in Jacksonville, Florida and Vancouver, British Columbia, and about 250 employees expected to transfer with the business. Net cash proceeds after taxes are expected to be about 500 million dollars, which Kraft Heinz plans to use to pay down debt.

The transaction is expected to close in mid 2027, subject to regulatory approval and other customary closing conditions. That is a long tail for a carve out of this size, and it leaves Kraft Heinz operating the business for another year and a half under its new chief executive, Steve Cahillane, who is refocusing the group on its core packaged food and condiments.

Analysts quoted by Reuters noted the disclosure gap: with no profit figures published for the coffee unit, the implied valuation multiple cannot be judged from the announcement, and preliminary estimates did not look attractive for the seller. What is clear is the direction of travel: another consumer staples group is converting a heritage brand portfolio into deleveraging cash and handing the operations to a private equity owner whose model is cost and focus rather than portfolio breadth.
