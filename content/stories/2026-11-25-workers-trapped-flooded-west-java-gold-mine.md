Rescue teams have recovered three unidentified bodies from a depth of 30 to 40 metres, the search and rescue agency said on Wednesday, the first confirmed deaths in the flooding. Authorities have been pumping water out of the shaft to aid the search, and the agency said access was narrow and the terrain slippery and dark. State miner Antam, which runs gold operations in Pongkor, said the incident occurred at an unauthorised gold mine and was not part of its operations.

Rescuers were searching on Wednesday for 26 workers still to be evacuated from an unauthorised open-pit gold mine in the Pongkor area of Bogor district, West Java, after the mine flooded when a nearby river overflowed following torrential rain on Monday. West Java police spokesman Hendra Rochmawan said 26 people had yet to be evacuated from the workings, about 100 metres deep in mountainous terrain 60 kilometres south of Jakarta, near gold operations run by state miner Antam.

The Jakarta Search and Rescue Office said the depth of the shaft, narrow access and limited lighting were shaping the rescue strategy, and that teams were monitoring water conditions before committing personnel. No deaths had been reported. Authorities closed about 30 gold mine pits in a separate part of Bogor last week.
