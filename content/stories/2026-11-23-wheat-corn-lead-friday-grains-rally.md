Corn rose 3.74 percent and wheat rose 3.22 percent at Friday's close in the estate market tables, the strongest daily gains in the grain complex and a contrast with falling energy and only stabilising metals. The rally extends the move that began with the November WASDE report, in which USDA cut the national corn yield to 179.6 bushels an acre from 181.2 in October and trimmed 2026-27 corn ending stocks to 1.758 billion bushels from 1.849 billion, while the soybean yield of 53.3 bushels an acre badly missed trade estimates near 55.

Wheat has its own support. Funds remain heavily short the wheat complex, so part of Friday's gain is covering, but the physical trade is tightening as well: EU wheat exports are running at a record pace as Black Sea disruption redraws trade flows, the development covered in yesterday's commodities edition, and French availability is the next constraint to watch if the export pace holds.

Reuters trade expectations ahead of Friday's session already had wheat and soybeans called higher, and the close confirmed the direction with a larger move than expected. With no new WASDE until December, the complex now trades weather in South America and the pace of export sales against a fund position that is still short enough to amplify any bullish surprise.
