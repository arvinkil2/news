**Latest (Friday morning, October 9):** Reuters confirmed the FT's reporting: OpenAI told investors its annualized revenue for September was almost $50 billion, down from the roughly $70 billion it had indicated at a separate event, according to a person familiar with the matter. The discrepancy arose from an attempt to produce a direct comparison with rival Anthropic, which counts revenue booked by its cloud partners as part of its own total while OpenAI does not. Anthropic pays cloud partners about 16 percent of every dollar earned through them, which accounted for half of its revenue last year. Dow Jones Newswires put Thursday's damage in detail: Oracle deepened its losses to 4.9 percent at $136.50 midday, Microsoft slipped 1.2 percent to $523.29, Micron fell 4.3 percent, AMD 3.3 percent and Broadcom, which is collaborating with OpenAI on a custom chip, 3.2 percent. Overnight, chip and cloud names edged up slightly and some analysts called the reaction overblown.

The broader numbers frame the stakes. OpenAI started this year with $20 billion in annualized revenue versus $6 billion in 2024, and its quarterly revenue was eclipsed by Anthropic for the first time in the second quarter, $6.7 billion against Anthropic's $11.5 billion. Anthropic's annualized revenue crossed $65 billion in July and is set to reach $100 billion by year end, sources previously told Reuters. Both companies are preparing to go public: Anthropic is expected around November, OpenAI early next year, after which Wall Street gets a clearer view of the finances. Analysts caution that annualized revenue run rate is a sometimes misleading metric, one month's revenue multiplied by twelve, but it remains the lingua franca of fast-growing AI startups.

**Earlier (overnight, Friday, October 9):** The full session's damage was sharper than the early read: the Nasdaq Composite fell 1.25 percent to 27,193.34, its worst day since mid-August, the Nasdaq 100 dropped 1.4 percent and the S&P 500 fell 0.5 percent, while the Dow edged up 0.1 percent. Intel fell more than 5 percent, Oracle 5.5 percent, Nvidia 2.9 percent and CoreWeave 7.8 percent; the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped 3.4 percent, its worst slide since September 14.

The Financial Times reported that OpenAI told investors its annualized revenue was reaching $50 billion toward the end of September, $20 billion short of the $70 billion it had previously projected, according to MarketWatch. Shares of Micron, Nvidia and other artificial-intelligence chip makers fell hard on Thursday on the report; MarketWatch noted analysts attribute the gap to differences in how the figure is reported rather than weakness in AI demand.
