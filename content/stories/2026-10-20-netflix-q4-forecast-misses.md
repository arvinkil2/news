Netflix issued a weaker than expected forecast for fourth-quarter profit on Tuesday, days after the streaming company laid off 6 percent of its workforce and replaced two of its top programming executives. The company forecast fourth-quarter net income of $2.79 billion and earnings of 67 cents a share, below the average analyst forecast of $3.07 billion and 73 cents, and cut its 2026 advertising revenue forecast to roughly $2.7 billion from about $3 billion, saying some advertising product capabilities had rolled out more slowly than expected. Shares fell more than 5 percent in after-hours trading.

Third-quarter revenue rose 11.8 percent to $12.87 billion, in line with Wall Street forecasts but the lowest rate of increase in nearly three years, while earnings per share of 87 cents beat the 82 cent average forecast. In its shareholder letter Netflix said it would license more third-party content and focus on its core streaming service, as it competes for viewing time with YouTube and TikTok.
