Working natural gas in storage in the Lower 48 states ended the injection season at 3,810 billion cubic feet, almost 2 percent above the five-year average, the Energy Information Administration said on Wednesday, even though net injections over the April to October season ran 2 billion cubic feet below the five-year average. Stocks started the season 3 percent above average, at 1,878 billion cubic feet, and most of the 3 percent rise in dry gas production during the refill season went to liquefied natural gas export terminals. In its November Short-Term Energy Outlook, the EIA forecasts heating season withdrawals of 1,900 billion cubic feet and inventories about 4 percent above the five-year average at the end of March 2027.
