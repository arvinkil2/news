## Monday, November 16

No scheduled catalysts on the calendar. Markets reopen carrying Friday's close, with the US 10 year Treasury yield at 5.15 percent, up 29.5 basis points on the week, and December Federal Reserve pricing treating a rate increase as certain. Nothing on Monday's calendar settles that pricing, the French budget question, or the summit week that starts on Tuesday.

## Tuesday, November 17

**Quad leaders meeting, Manila (politics).** Leaders of the United States, Japan, India and Australia meet in Manila on the sidelines of the ASEAN summit, in what this site's November 14 reporting described as the first Quad leaders summit in two years, since Delaware in September 2024, and the first since President Trump returned to office. Going in: two people familiar with the planning told Reuters the meeting would follow the Quad foreign ministers' October 31 talks in Atlanta, where the four agreed to respond jointly to China's control of critical minerals supply chains. Critical minerals supply, free and unimpeded commercial shipping lanes for energy supplies, and attacks on commercial ships are expected on the agenda. No Quad government had formally announced the meeting in the reporting this site carried on November 14. A surprise counts as a named critical minerals or supply chain commitment with dates and funding, or joint language on China and maritime security that goes beyond the Atlanta ministers' wording. A short meeting with only a general statement would confirm the revival at leaders level without adding a programme. Most exposed: defence and critical minerals names, Asia-Pacific currencies and regional equities.

**France 2027 budget, full bill vote, National Assembly (politics, with rates exposure).** The calendar schedules the National Assembly vote on the full 2027 finance bill. Going in: the position has already moved once. The finance committee rejected the revenue section 31 to 3 on October 9, and on November 4 the Assembly rejected the revenue part 458 to 0 in first reading, a result that voided the whole text in the lower house and sent the government's original draft to the Senate, with Senate examination due to begin on November 20. The government of Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu governs without a majority. Its plan rests on a fiscal effort of about 54 billion euros and a deficit near 5 percent of GDP in 2027. France's 10 year spread over Germany closed at 170 basis points in late October, after touching 177 basis points, and Moody's kept France at Aa3 with a negative outlook on October 23 on the condition of a deficit reducing 2027 budget. A surprise counts as a revived Assembly path that restores a deficit reducing text, or the opposite, a motion of censure tabled or use of Article 49.3 that turns a budget vote into a government survival vote. Passage in a form that widens the deficit would also count, because it would fail the test Moody's set out. Most exposed: French OAT spreads, the euro, and French bank and utility equities.

## Wednesday, November 18

**FOMC October meeting minutes (rates).** The Federal Reserve publishes the minutes of its October 27 to 28 meeting at 2:00 p.m. Eastern. Going in: the October decision held the funds rate at 3.75 to 4.00 percent, with two dissents in favour of a hike, from Cook and Hammack. Friday pricing treated a December increase as certain, at about 86 percent for 25 basis points and 14 percent for 50 basis points, after the US yield repricing that took the 10 year to 5.15 percent. The next meeting is December 8 to 9. The minutes are the fullest account before that meeting of how the committee weighed inflation against growth. A surprise counts as explicit December signalling, in either direction: language showing a majority ready to tighten in December would lift the front end further, while language showing the two hike dissents were isolated and the majority focused on growth risks would reopen the hold case that Friday pricing had closed. Most exposed: the front end of the Treasury curve and December meeting pricing, then the dollar.

## Thursday, November 19

**ICJ judgment, The Gambia v. Myanmar (politics).** The International Court of Justice delivers its judgment at 10 a.m. in The Hague, 4 a.m. Eastern, on whether Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya, in the case The Gambia brought in 2019 after the 2017 military campaign that drove hundreds of thousands of Rohingya into Bangladesh. Going in: the court has never found a state responsible for genocide, so any finding sets the benchmark under the 1948 Genocide Convention. The judgment is final, with no appeal, and its reasoning is expected to bear on the separate genocide case against Israel. Myanmar has denied carrying out a genocide and says its actions targeted a military uprising. On November 15 ASEAN kept Myanmar's summit bar in place, with a phased path back. A surprise counts as a finding of state responsibility for genocide itself, rather than a narrower breach such as a failure to prevent or punish, or reparations and compliance orders with dates attached. A dismissal on the merits would also count, because it would set the evidentiary bar at a level no case has yet cleared in the other direction. Most exposed: Myanmar and ASEAN diplomacy, with read across to other pending genocide cases. There is no direct market channel.

**Trump, Xi and Putin trilateral talks, China (politics).** Presidents Trump, Xi and Putin meet in Shenzhen on the sidelines of the APEC leaders meeting of November 18 to 19. Going in: the White House confirmed the meeting on November 13 and the Kremlin confirmed it on November 14. The stated agenda is nuclear weapons reduction and the US war with Iran. A senior US official said the meeting is expected to start three way nuclear talks, with no agreements expected to be announced, and that President Trump does not plan to discuss Ukraine. The last US Russia nuclear pact, New START, expired in February. On the economic side, US officials said the separate Xi meeting will focus on implementation of the trade agreement, following the first US China trade committee meeting reported on November 14, and that Trump does not plan to raise the Nvidia matter or US export controls. A surprise counts as an announced nuclear framework with a follow up date, any Ukraine language agreed without Kyiv at the table, or tariff and trade commitments that go beyond implementation of the existing agreement. Energy and sanctions signals would also count, given the Iran item on the stated agenda. Most exposed: crude and tanker freight, defence names, the yuan, rouble linked assets and broad risk sentiment.

**Banxico meeting minutes published (rates).** Banco de Mexico publishes the minutes of its November 5 meeting. Going in: that meeting cut the overnight rate by 25 basis points to 6.25 percent, by 3 votes to 2, a move only six of 36 analysts in the Citi survey had expected. The board called the cut a one off adjustment, and Governor Victoria Rodriguez Ceja has since signalled an extended hold at 6.25 percent. The cut left the US Mexico rate differential at a record low near 225 basis points on Banamex's measure. Asset managers hold their largest peso short in 20 years, on CFTC data through November 4. The peso traded near 18.60 per dollar on November 13, down about 9 percent from its September peak. A surprise counts as minutes showing the majority open to further cuts, which would validate the record short and press the peso toward 19 per dollar, the level Bank of America expects next year, or minutes showing the majority uneasy about currency weakness and pass through to prices, which would make the record short crowded and support a squeeze. The board split and any language on currency weakness are the lines to read first. Most exposed: the peso, Mexican local rates and emerging market currencies more broadly.

## Friday, November 20

**UK-EU summit, Brussels (politics).** Prime Minister Andy Burnham meets European Council President Antonio Costa and Commission President Ursula von der Leyen in Brussels for the second UK EU summit, announced by the UK Government on October 12. The first summit, in London on May 19, 2025, agreed a new strategic partnership. Going in: no agreement has been announced in advance. Burnham framed the meeting in cost terms in October, saying he wanted practical steps that cut costs for farmers, food producers and families, reduce paperwork for business and ease pressure on supermarket prices, with food standards, agriculture and trade friction the decision points, alongside security, illegal migration and crime. The summit lands three weeks after the October 28 UK Autumn Budget, so the UK fiscal position arrives freshly tested in gilt markets. A surprise counts as a signed sanitary and phytosanitary or food agreement text with dates, or farm and food cost commitments with figures attached. A joint statement that stays at the level of intent, with only a date for a future summit, would be the expected outcome, not a surprise. Most exposed: sterling, gilts, UK food and farm trade, and UK exporters.
