Dakota Gold (NYSE-A: DC) expects its Richmond Hill project in South Dakota to produce 12 percent more gold annually than projected last year, even as lower recoveries and a shorter mine life cut the metal the company plans to sell by 40 percent. Initial capital rose 40 percent to $538 million for an open pit, heap leach operation that would produce 171,000 ounces of gold annually and run for nine years instead of 17, Dakota said Monday in a prefeasibility study. The company raised its base case after tax net present value to $2 billion, compared with $1.6 billion in a 2025 assessment, using a 5 percent discount rate in both cases.

The higher valuation rests on a higher assumed gold price of $3,500 an ounce, at which Dakota projects a 71 percent internal rate of return. Using $2,750 gold, the new valuation is $1.25 billion against last year's $2.15 billion. The new plan assumes a 72.4 percent gold recovery, down from 85.1 percent, and cuts lifetime payable production to 1.56 million ounces from 2.6 million ounces. BMO Capital Markets analyst Andrew Mikitchook said reclassifying material previously considered suitable for heap leaching took about 1 million ounces out of the plan, and called the new plan significantly more conservative.

Proven and probable reserves as of November 23 totalled 119.6 million tonnes at 0.56 gram gold and 6.61 grams silver per tonne, for 2.16 million ounces of gold and 25.4 million ounces of silver, calculated at $2,600 gold. All in sustaining costs are estimated at $1,289 an ounce, including South Dakota's severance tax.

Dakota is drilling higher recovery breccia to try to add at least 10 percent to reserves for a feasibility study due in mid 2027, and aims to start production in 2029, subject to permits and a construction decision. Shares of Dakota fell 8 percent to $4.02 on Tuesday morning in New York, valuing the company at about $539 million.
