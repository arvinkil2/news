**European Commission recovery disbursements.** The Commission announced on Friday that it has disbursed almost 6.5 billion euros to France, Austria and Luxembourg under NextGenerationEU, and on Thursday that it has greenlit Czechia's seventh and final payment request, for 951 million euros. Both are the Commission's own announcements, single sourced at this stage, and the deal and funding angles belong to the infra deals edition; the politics point is the programme's endgame, with final payment requests now closing out for member states as the recovery fund's timetable runs down.

**US sanctions designations notice.** The State Department published a notice in Friday's Federal Register recording designations onto the Treasury's Specially Designated Nationals list under four executive orders covering weapons of mass destruction proliferation, Iran's conventional arms activities, harmful foreign activities of the Russian government, and repression in Cuba. The notice, read in full in this run, is the standard legal instrument rather than a policy announcement: the designations themselves were made through OFAC, and the notice adds no new country programme.

**Trump after the midterms.** Republican and industry officials do not expect President Trump to slow his agenda if his party does poorly on Tuesday, Politico reported, via Political Wire: if anything, he may speed it up. A separate strand of reporting, based on interviews with 15 Republican lawmakers, has members of his own party weighing whether a heavy loss would loosen his hold on Congress, with leadership contests in the week after the election as the first test. Both are expectations, not decisions, and the December 11 government funding deadline is where they will first be priced.

**Zambia court nullifies an MP's election.** Zambia's High Court has nullified the election of the Mapatizya member of parliament, Lusaka Times reported on Friday. The item is single sourced in the pool and is carried at headline level only; a by election and any appeal are the developments that would move it into a full story.

**Election Day weather.** Severe weather is expected on Election Day in several key states, The Hill reported, a turnout variable in a midterm where several Senate races are inside the margin that weather can plausibly move. It is a forecast, not an outcome, and it is noted here because both parties' final weekend plans are being built around it.

**Armenia's parliament brawls again.** Al Jazeera reported that Armenia's parliament descended into a physical fight for the third time in a month. No policy consequence has been reported, and the item is carried as a marker of institutional strain rather than a development.
