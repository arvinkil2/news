**Latest (Sunday 14:56 ET):** President Emmanuel Macron addressed the nation on Sunday night, opening by saying he was speaking at a moment of extreme gravity, and blamed rioters from far left groups, joined by numerous criminals, for looting shops and destroying public buildings, asking why anyone would burn the Limoges town hall and saying nothing explains or justifies it, El Pais reported. He called the violence unprecedented, said he had asked the government to draw every necessary lesson on intelligence, with full transparency, and demanded explanations for the intelligence failures, and said those who destroyed must pay and those who tried to bring down the Republic must answer for their acts. He announced no concrete new measure and did not declare a state of emergency, instead calling for calm and pointing to the presidential election in six months, and he distinguished protesters with legitimate demands from those who destroyed, saying young people's anguish about their future must be heard and that the responsibility is double: listen to the indignation and firmly uphold republican order. El Pais also reported renewed Sunday afternoon clashes near Place de la Republique, where groups scattered after the gathering was dispersed with tear gas, a pile of bicycles was set on fire on the Quai de Valmy by the Canal Saint Martin, and firefighters intervened.

**Latest (Sunday 12:53 ET):** Hundreds of people answered social media calls for an unauthorized gathering at Place de la Republique in central Paris on Sunday afternoon, and police moved in at the first signs of tension, using tear gas and stun grenades to disperse the crowd and making several detentions as a water cannon advanced, the Paris Police Prefecture told Agence France-Presse. Television images showed the situation under control in the early afternoon before tensions resumed, with some protesters throwing objects at officers, and no injuries were reported. In Italy, Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni condemned the rioting on Sunday and expressed solidarity with police after Saturday's clashes in Milan, Turin, Bologna and Bergamo; in Milan at least four police officers and 20 demonstrators were injured and about 50 young people were arrested, around half of them minors, Deutsche Welle reported. In Bologna, four people were arrested, all of them minors, and seven people were reported to prosecutors. The CGIL union confederation used a demonstration in Rome to announce a general strike for November 20.

**Latest (Sunday 11:52 ET):** The political fight over Saturday's policing opened on Sunday, with politicians from the left and the right calling for Interior Minister Laurent Nunez to resign after the government appeared to be caught by surprise as public buildings were stormed and burned, the Guardian reported. The government was in crisis talks ahead of President Macron's address to the nation, and Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu replied on social media that blaming police chiefs for the violence is dangerous. In Marseille, where masked groups stormed the city hall and a police station, Mayor Benoit Payan said questions must be asked about policing tactics, and Lyon's mayor Gregory Doucet said he had never seen such violence in his city.

**Latest (Sunday 10:46 ET):** Interior Minister Laurent Nunez raised Saturday's toll again on Sunday, posting on X that more than 2,000 people had been detained and more than 600 police officers, gendarmes and firefighters injured, figures he said were still preliminary, with material damage significant, especially for several hundred business owners, and a promise to bring those responsible for the rioting to account. Tagesschau reported that the mayors of Paris, Marseille and Lyon had written jointly to Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu demanding a meeting and an inquiry into how the situation arose, ahead of President Macron's crisis meeting with the prime minister and the ministers concerned on Sunday evening and his 8 p.m. address to the nation. From Washington, President Donald Trump reacted on Truth Social, writing that France was on fire and that immigration had crushed a once great country, and urging Paris to close its borders and take control of the situation before it was too late.

Latest (Sunday 07:44 ET): President Emmanuel Macron will address the nation at 8 p.m. local time on Sunday (2 p.m. ET), his office said, after first meeting the prime minister and the ministers concerned at the Elysee at 5:30 p.m., as France cleaned up after one of its most violent days of unrest in years. The French commerce and distribution federation estimated that 1,500 traders were directly affected by the violence, with the damage bill likely to run to several hundred million euros. Reuters reported that one protester died after suffering a cardiac arrest during the demonstrations in Paris. A clean-up operation began in cities from Lille to Marseille, Strasbourg and Bordeaux, where a masked mob invaded the residence of the local prefect and Mayor Thomas Cazenave said up to 100 stores had been looted; Marseille Mayor Benoit Payan announced emergency financial aid for businesses hit by the violence, and Economy Minister Roland Lescure pledged support for owners who suffered losses, saying those who break things will pay and repair the damage. In Limoges, workers assessed the damage to the 143-year-old city hall, partially destroyed in the arson attack. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu said the government would not be intimidated by violence and blamed the unrest on the ultra-left and criminals.

Latest (Sunday 05:42 ET): Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez raised the toll from Saturday's protests on Sunday morning, posting on X that 1,800 people had been arrested and that more than 500 police officers, gendarmes and firefighters had been injured, a casualty count he said was only provisional. The figures replace the 1,200 arrests and 150 police injured that Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu gave on Saturday night. In Paris, police arrested 855 people during Saturday's protests, which Agence France-Presse reported was the highest number detained for a single event. Nuñez said the Republic will not yield, blamed thousands of far-left protesters and criminals who looted and ransacked businesses in cities including Paris, Marseille, Lyon, Bordeaux, Rennes, Strasbourg, Grenoble, Nice and Limoges, and said investigations were under way to identify those responsible. Paris mayor Emmanuel Grégoire said whole streets in the capital had been devastated, and his deputy Lamia El Aaraje called it a level of violence Paris had never seen before. In Limoges, the prefect said on Sunday that the fire in the town hall, set alight on Saturday, had been contained. No new demonstrations had been reported on Sunday as of the latest coverage, with authorities waiting to see whether the protests would run into another day.

Latest (Saturday 18:52 ET): Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said about 1,200 people had been detained after the day's violent incidents, most of them adults, up from the 272 arrests the ministry reported by mid-afternoon, and that at least 150 police officers were injured. The ministry put nationwide turnout at about 550,000 people; the CGT union estimated 800,000. Related cost of living protests were held the same day in Belgium, Italy and Portugal. In Brussels, police used tear gas and water cannon against protesters they accused of throwing missiles and setting fires, and water cannon was also used in Liège. In Lisbon, tens of thousands marched over low wages, housing costs and rents, and demonstrators set up tents in Rossio square. In Milan and Bologna, police reported stone throwing and clashes alongside peaceful rallies, and Italian media reported several injuries and arrests. Alberto Alemanno, a professor of EU law and policy at HEC Paris, told Al Jazeera that France is the epicentre of the movement, that there are signs it is spreading beyond France, and that early mobilisation in Greece, Germany and Austria could follow in the coming week.

Latest (Saturday 14:56 ET): The protest day escalated into direct attacks on public buildings. In Marseille, protesters rammed the town hall door with security barriers taken from the street and briefly entered the building, where one spray-painted an anarchist symbol on a wall before police removed them; a municipal police station in the city was also vandalised, and the mayor said additional police were being sent after talks with the government. In Limoges, the 19th century town hall was set alight and the blaze spread to the roof. In Lille, protesters smashed their way into the town hall and vandalised the premises before leaving, and in Lyon the city's tourism office was set on fire. In Paris, rioters at Place de la République attacked a bank, looted a sports shop and set a lorry ablaze, with flames spreading to a nearby building. The interior ministry said 272 people had been arrested by mid-afternoon and blamed the violence on the ultra-left. President Macron posted that those who had attacked prefectures and businesses and looted town halls would be prosecuted and punished.

Latest (Saturday 12:42 ET): Police used tear gas and water cannon in Paris, where hooded protesters threw fireworks and other objects at officers at Place de la République, and the interior ministry reported assaults on police, looting and damage to public buildings, with at least 272 arrests nationwide, while many demonstrations remained peaceful. The Guardian reported police sources putting national turnout at around 350,000, a figure organisers disputed, and reported solidarity protests in Italy turning violent, with stones thrown and police charges in Milan and clashes in Turin and Bologna. President Emmanuel Macron, in his weekly cabinet meeting, said young people have legitimate concerns that are being exploited for political purposes and turned into urban violence, and asked ministers for concrete responses within weeks, government spokeswoman Maud Bregeon told AFP. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu told Parliament that 937 police officers and 149 teachers and school staff have been injured since the protests began, and the justice ministry puts arrests over the whole movement at nearly 8,000; the interior ministry has opened internal investigations into 36 allegations of police violence. Several hundred mostly young protesters also clashed with police in Brussels in demonstrations at least partly inspired by the French movement, Reuters reported.

Demonstrations began across France on Saturday in what is expected to become a nationwide day of protest, as yellow vest activists joined a movement that began in mid-September at a high school in the Paris suburb of Créteil, over dilapidated buildings, overcrowded classrooms and teacher shortages. The mobilisation has since spread to thousands of high schools, and teachers, trade unions and university students have joined it; a tracking site listed more than 1,750 planned protest locations across the country. In Paris a heavy police presence guarded the Champs-Élysées and the Arc de Triomphe, which were excluded from the two official protest routes, protesters in yellow vests blocked streets, and garbage bins were set on fire in Marseille, Arab News reported.

The authorities have described the unrest as among the most violent in decades, with more than 900 police injured since the protests began, and reports published this week put the number of children taken into police custody since mid-September at more than 6,700. France suspended the use of stun grenades for the student demonstrations, but Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said on Friday that he had authorized police to use them in the event of urban violence, and that he expected a fairly significant turnout on Saturday. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu said on Thursday that the country was in a moment of fatigue and an atmosphere of permanent crisis, and the government has accused the far-left France Unbowed party of inflaming the protests, which the party rejects.
