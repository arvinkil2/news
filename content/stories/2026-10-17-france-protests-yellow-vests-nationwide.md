**Latest (Tuesday 06:38 ET):** President Emmanuel Macron has obtained commitments to deliver an additional 200,000 tonnes of diesel a month from the United Arab Emirates and Bahrain during his Gulf tour, his office said on Tuesday. He obtained commitments for around 100,000 tonnes a month from the Emirates, while an additional 100,000 tonnes a month will be secured during a stop in Bahrain later in the day, the Elysee Palace said. Including deliveries from Saudi Arabia, the presidency put total commitments at 295,000 tonnes a month, just over 10 percent of France’s consumption. Kuwait has also given what the presidency called very clear commitments to deliver diesel and kerosene to the French overseas territories of Mayotte and Réunion, with those shipments to be sent via Oman, bypassing the Strait of Hormuz, and talks are under way with countries in the region on bringing down maritime insurance prices so companies can ship greater quantities through the strait at lower cost. The presidency presented the tour as a supply response to the cost-of-living crisis that has fuelled the unrest in France, where fuel prices are among the grievances behind the protests.

**Latest (Tuesday 05:58 ET):** The extra yield investors demand to hold French 10-year bonds over German Bunds rose through 150 basis points to 153 on Tuesday, the widest since October 2, Bloomberg reported, against a peak of 159 basis points earlier this month. Bloomberg attributed the move to the government’s struggle to tame the budget deficit while it grapples with the cost-of-living protests, and said political and fiscal pressures have prompted many investors to liquidate positions in French debt. The move lands on the day the National Assembly votes on Part I of the 2027 budget, with Moody’s due to review France’s Aa3 rating, on negative outlook, on Friday.

**Latest (Tuesday 04:13 ET):** The first night of targeted curfews passed calmly in French cities, Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu said on Tuesday, saying the curfews “have contributed to calm overnight” and thanking police officers and gendarmes for enforcing them. The curfews covered parts of central Paris, including Place de la Republique and the Les Halles shopping area, with movement and gathering restrictions also in force in Marseille, Bordeaux, Nice and Lyon and in Lille and other northern towns; a helicopter swept a searchlight over Place de la Republique during the night, Agence France-Presse reported. Paris’s restrictions applied for the Monday to Tuesday night only, while those in Bordeaux and Nice run for several days, and Lecornu said in a post on X that any curfews for Tuesday night would be decided on the ground by prefects and local mayors. Neither the BBC nor the Guardian reported an overnight arrest total. In the judicial follow-up, French media reported that a 40 year old man was arrested on Tuesday in connection with the arson attack on Limoges town hall, with Le Figaro describing him as a far-left activist previously placed on the security services’ watchlist.

**Latest (Monday 21:37 ET):** Italy's government will dispatch 500 army troops to Milan after two days of unrest in the city, Deputy Prime Minister Matteo Salvini said on Monday, announcing the deployment ahead of a package of security measures expected to be approved at a cabinet meeting on Tuesday. Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni chaired a meeting with ministers and security chiefs on Monday, and Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani said the government would respond firmly, including through measures aimed at curbing violence by minors. In Milan's Via Montenapoleone luxury district, most stores had removed merchandise from their windows and shut early as a precaution, with police patrolling the street, after several hundred hooded protesters, many believed to be minors, looted shops and set bins ablaze in the city centre over the weekend.

**Latest (Monday 18:24 ET):** French officials put the number arrested over the weekend unrest at more than 2,500, with about 1,000 police officers injured, the BBC reported on Monday, and Interior Minister Laurent Nunez admitted police had lost control in some cities. The government also moved on the resale of looted goods: ministers said looted items had been listed on selling platforms, the government met online resale companies on Monday night, and France 24 reported that eBay, Vinted, Amazon and others were asked to crack down on suspicious listings of items such as handbags, cosmetics and trading cards. The French Alliance of Marketplaces said platforms were using detection and moderation systems to identify and remove listings of suspected looted items, and Vinted said its teams had already removed a number of them. The government put the number of businesses looted or attacked at nearly 3,000, and the Casino group said 50 of its outlets were attacked. Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu's support package added a special fund for affected businesses and deferrals of tax and social security contributions, with partial unemployment benefits raised to 100 percent of pay from 70 percent from Tuesday. Markets priced the crisis in: France 24 reported the French 10-year yield above 4.9 percent, the spread over Germany at its widest since 2012, and the CAC 40 down 1.1 percent on the day.

**Latest (Monday 4:02 ET):** French authorities imposed the first curfews on Monday night. Paris police ordered a curfew around Place de la Republique and Les Halles from 11 p.m. to 6 a.m., with protests banned in certain other areas from 8 p.m., Deutsche Welle reported in its live coverage, and police said a deployment was in place to ensure strict compliance. The mayor of Nice signed a curfew for minors in the city centre overnight, and curfews for minors from 8 p.m. to 6 a.m. over the next two days were set in Lille, Douai, Wattignies and Denain with the agreement of local mayors, sources in Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu's office told Reuters. Lecornu ruled out blanket curfews in Paris but not a state of emergency, saying his determination to restore order is absolute. In Italy, Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni was considering curfews and other measures after the weekend rioting in Milan, Turin and Bologna and convened an emergency cabinet meeting; more than 270 people were detained across Italy, most of them minors, and at least 14 security personnel were injured in Milan.

**Latest (Monday 07:46 ET):** Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu said on Monday he had asked prefects to impose partial curfews in cities affected by the weekend's rioting, Reuters reported, describing the measures as very limited and localised and to be put in place from Monday evening in discussion with mayors and local chambers of commerce. Lecornu attributed the disorder to the ultra-left and criminal elements, announced an operation of about 200 investigators in Paris to analyse footage of the weekend's devastation, said furlough measures for businesses hit by the violence open from Tuesday, and said 80 percent of those arrested during the weekend rioting now face criminal charges. Marseille separately banned public gatherings and demonstrations for most of Monday.

**Latest (Monday 01:45 ET):** President Emmanuel Macron refused the resignation Interior Minister Laurent Nunez submitted on Sunday, the Elysee said in a statement on Monday, citing the necessity of continued leadership of law enforcement at this time, Agence France-Presse reported. Macron asked Nunez to ensure transparency over the failures in planning and intelligence that led to the violence and to put new operational methods for security forces against rioters in place as fast as possible. Looting and attempted looting by small groups were reported again in Paris, Lyon and Marseille in the night from Sunday to Monday, and Paris police counted 92 arrests around midnight. In Mulhouse, a 17 year old woman lost an eye, AFP reported, alongside the Saint-Etienne case already reported below. Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu chairs an interministerial crisis cell from 10 a.m. on Monday covering the economy and trade, insurance and judicial follow-up, according to Matignon, traders, distributors, hoteliers, restaurateurs, banks and insurers meet at Bercy from 9 a.m. to assess the material damage, Nunez is expected in Marseille, and the Association of Mayors of France called for rallies from midday outside town halls against the attacks on democratic institutions.

**Latest (Sunday 15:46 ET):** French authorities raised the toll from Saturday's unrest again on Sunday. Deutsche Welle, drawing on AFP and dpa, reported that almost 2,400 people were arrested across France and that the Interior Ministry put injured officers at about 1,000, above the more than 600 police, gendarmes and firefighters Interior Minister Laurent Nunez had reported earlier in the day. Paris police reported 855 arrests in the capital, a record, and prosecutors said 517 people remained in custody, including 171 minors, on suspicion of handling stolen goods, joining a group intending violence, or vandalism. No total for injured demonstrators and no deaths were reported. Le Figaro reported that a 17 year old lost a hand to a grenade fired by police in Saint-Etienne. Deutsche Welle also reported attacks on public buildings and shops in Avignon alongside Marseille, Lyon and Grenoble.

**Latest (Sunday 14:56 ET):** President Emmanuel Macron addressed the nation on Sunday night, opening by saying he was speaking at a moment of extreme gravity, and blamed rioters from far left groups, joined by numerous criminals, for looting shops and destroying public buildings, asking why anyone would burn the Limoges town hall and saying nothing explains or justifies it, El Pais reported. He called the violence unprecedented, said he had asked the government to draw every necessary lesson on intelligence, with full transparency, and demanded explanations for the intelligence failures, and said those who destroyed must pay and those who tried to bring down the Republic must answer for their acts.
