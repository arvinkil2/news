United Fidelity Bank, fsb, of Evansville, Indiana was closed on November 27 by the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, which appointed the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation as receiver. The FDIC said it entered into an agreement with First-Citizens Bank & Trust Company of Raleigh, North Carolina to assume substantially all deposits and purchase substantially all assets of the failed bank.

As of September 30, 2026, United Fidelity had approximately $5.9 billion in total assets and $5.4 billion in total deposits. The FDIC estimates the cost to the Deposit Insurance Fund from the failure will be $1.83 billion, about 31 percent of the bank's assets.

Depositors of United Fidelity will become depositors of First Citizens and will retain all of their funds and deposit insurance coverage, the FDIC said. United Fidelity's 22 branches in Indiana, Illinois, Florida, Colorado and the U.S. Virgin Islands will reopen as branches of First Citizens during normal business hours on Saturday at locations that normally operate on Saturdays and on Monday at all other locations. This evening and over the weekend, depositors can access their money by writing checks or using ATM or debit cards, and checks drawn on United Fidelity will continue to be processed.

At $5.9 billion in assets, the failure is the largest in the United States since Republic First Bank of Philadelphia, with about $6 billion in assets, was closed in April 2024, according to reporting citing FDIC data. The size of the estimated insurance fund loss, relative to the bank's balance sheet, is the sharpest signal in the release: resolutions of healthy but illiquid banks usually cost the fund far less as a share of assets.
