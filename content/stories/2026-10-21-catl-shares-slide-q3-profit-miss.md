CATL's Hong Kong shares fell 10.64 percent to 368 Hong Kong dollars on Wednesday, their lowest since July 2025, and its Shenzhen shares fell about 12 percent, after the battery maker reported third quarter net profit of 21.25 billion yuan, up 14.54 percent year on year but down 5.77 percent from the second quarter, on revenue up 58.58 percent to 165.22 billion yuan.

Gross margin fell to 20.69 percent, a second consecutive quarterly decline. The company attributed the drop in profit per unit to product mix changes, a consumption tax on lithium batteries and a reduced battery export tax rebate, and said it is negotiating how to share the tax costs with customers. Morningstar, carrying Dow Jones Newswires, reported the result as an earnings miss, and analyst target cuts followed the print.

The tax backdrop is dated and specific. China resumed a consumption tax on lithium ion batteries on September 1 at a transitional 2 percent rate, rising to 4 percent in September 2027, and cut the value added tax export rebate for battery products from 9 percent to 6 percent in April, with the rebate eliminated entirely from January 1, 2027.

For the sector, the print separates demand from margin. Revenue growth of 58.58 percent year on year shows volumes are not the problem. The binding constraint is policy cost landing on the world's largest battery maker faster than it can be passed through, which is why the negotiation with customers, not the sales line, is the number to watch into the fourth quarter.
