## Latest

Pacific Coast Energy Company, a California independent, has begun operating in Venezuela with a plan to revive production at the Delta and Cabimas blocks and bring combined output to 40,000 barrels a day within six months, Bloomberg reported, via El Nacional. The company, which started activities in the country in the third quarter, has already put more than 50 heavy-crude wells back into service; the two assets currently produce about 19,300 barrels a day. PCEC has four platforms deployed in Cabimas working roughly 900 wells, and the company estimates the blocks could eventually reach 160,000 barrels a day over a decade.

The operations run under 25-year agreements with PDVSA. The Delta block carries a recent history: it was previously tied to a joint venture linked to the family of the late Venezuelan businessman Oswaldo Cisneros, an arrangement PDVSA revoked in May. PCEC is the first independent American operator to join the reopening alongside the majors already expanding in the country.

## Earlier

Nine months after the US operation that captured Maduro, Venezuela is being rebuilt as a Western oil play. Acting president Delcy Rodríguez signed what Trump called the largest oil deal in history: 65 billion barrels across 17 fields under a new Hydrocarbons Law passed by the National Assembly in January, with 25-year terms, a 16% royalty, 34% income tax, and roughly $100 billion of pledged investment.

The majors are moving. Repsol regained operating control of Petroquiriquire in April; output is back above 1.1 million barrels a day; Chevron, Shell, ENI, and BP are all expanding. The commercial logic is straightforward, long-life barrels under fiscal terms designed to attract capital.

The political deficit is the risk. Rodríguez promised "inclusive elections" at the UN but set no date, while Nobel laureate María Corina Machado remains exiled and blocked from returning. Contract terms come from Venezuelan-side reporting; the full text has not been independently seen. These barrels will flow or stop on political decisions, not market ones.
