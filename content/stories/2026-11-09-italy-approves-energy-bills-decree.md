Italy's Council of Ministers on Monday approved the Bollette decree-law, containing urgent provisions to reduce the cost of electricity and gas. Its central mechanism temporarily reimburses a share of the cost of natural gas used in thermoelectric generation, a design the energy ministry said was defined in dialogue with the European Commission, with the Arera regulator and the GME market operator in supporting roles. Environment and Energy Security Minister Gilberto Pichetto Fratin said the decree's measures together would allow an average reduction of about 15 euros per megawatt-hour from January.

Business group Confcommercio welcomed the move, noting that business energy bills reached 3.8 billion euros in the third quarter, up 46.2 percent on a year earlier, while urging that the financing method not add new burdens to bills. The opposition Greens and Left Alliance deputy Angelo Bonelli countered that the household benefit amounts to about 1.4 euros a month and that the government is again using temporary measures rather than addressing dependence on gas.
