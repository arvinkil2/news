Copper eased in London on Monday for a second weekly decline. Benchmark three month copper on the London Metal Exchange fell 0.5 percent to 14,247.50 dollars a tonne in Shanghai hours, Bloomberg reported, while Reuters put the contract down 0.56 percent at 14,242.50 dollars a tonne by 0300 GMT. The most traded contract on the Shanghai Futures Exchange rose 0.55 percent, tracking Friday's overnight gains in London, which left the two benchmarks pointing in opposite directions on the day.

The London softness is a stock story. Copper stocks in LME warehouses surged last week to their highest since July after deposits into registered warehouses, mostly in North America, Reuters reported, easing the supply worries that had carried the metal to a record 14,875 dollars a tonne on September 11. Expectations of a United States tariff on refined copper imports have pulled metal into the United States and thinned stocks elsewhere, and Marex senior metals strategist Alastair Munro described macro fears and expectations of a shift in that draw as a bearish backdrop.

China is the counterweight. Chinese smelters are in maintenance and stocks in Shanghai monitored warehouses fell more than a quarter last week to 54,356 tonnes, Reuters reported, while the Yangshan premium, the gauge of demand for imported copper, stood at 168 dollars a tonne on Friday. Bloomberg reported the premium extending gains to its highest in its data going back to 2017, and Minmetals Futures head of base metals research Wu Kunjin warned that doubts about the United States tariff and weakening Chinese consumption into winter could ease the tightness from here.

Other metals were flat to lower in London, with aluminium down about 0.4 percent and nickel down between 0.9 and 1.19 percent across the two reports. The site market tables put United States copper at 6.56 dollars a pound, up 1.24 percent, a different contract and snapshot from the London session, so the day's honest read is divergence: London easing on visible stocks, Chinese import demand still paying a high premium, and the tariff draw the hinge between them.

Update, October 27: Copper turned up on Tuesday. LME copper closed at 14,338 dollars a tonne, up 0.28 percent, with open interest at 262,000 lots, up 641 lots, and the most traded SHFE contract closed at 108,570 yuan a tonne, up 0.43 percent, Shanghai Metals Market reported. Reuters put benchmark three month copper up 0.22 percent at 14,359.50 dollars a tonne by 0301 GMT, supported by a stock market rally and demand strength in China, while Dow Jones market talk cited tight mine supply and declining Chinese inventories against rising LME stocks and rate expectations. SMM reported that typhoon delayed import arrivals and an inventory drawdown were supporting high premiums. The site market tables put United States copper at 6.61 dollars a pound, up 1.55 percent. Monday's divergence remains the story, with the direction now up on both sides of the Pacific.

Update, October 27: The site market tables updated October 27 at 06:00 ET put United States copper at 6.61 dollars a pound, up 1.55 percent on the day and 0.65 percent on the week, a firmer snapshot than Monday's easing London session. The divergence the story describes remains the market's structure: visible LME stocks building in the West, a high Yangshan import premium in China, and the United States tariff decision still the hinge between the two.

Update, October 28: Copper rose on Tuesday as the Chinese import premium surged. Benchmark three month copper on the LME was up 1.1 percent at 14,487.50 dollars a tonne as of 1700 GMT, having hit 14,490.50 dollars, its strongest since October 16, Reuters reported via Kitco. The Yangshan premium hit a nearly 13 year high of 188 dollars a tonne on Tuesday, SMM put the average warrant price at 188 dollars, up 10 dollars on the day, and the LME cash contract traded at a 199.50 dollar premium over three month metal, having earlier hit 206 dollars, the most since August 27. Shanghai Futures Exchange copper stocks fell more than 25 percent in the latest weekly data cited by Reuters, while LME stocks rose last week and remain concentrated in North America. The site tables put United States copper at 6.64 dollars a pound, up 0.21 percent. ING described the Yangshan premium in its October 28 feed as a record high on its series; SMM and Reuters describe it as a nearly 13 year high, and the story carries both descriptions with their sources.

Update, October 29: The site market tables updated October 29 at 06:00 ET put copper at 6.59 dollars a pound, down 0.77 percent on the day and up 3.58 percent on the week, a pause in price that does not loosen the prompt market: the Yangshan premium at 188 dollars a tonne and the LME cash premium at 199.50 dollars reported Tuesday stand as the latest physical prints. The supply side added a duration story today, with Codelco's Andes Norte expansion at El Teniente still halted and 643 of its affected workers not yet relocated, covered in today's Codelco story. The Financial Times reported that copper's AI fuelled boom is getting a reality check; its article sits behind a paywall and was not read in this run, so no claim from it is carried here.

Update, October 30: Copper fell again as the technology selloff and the oil rally dented risk appetite, Bloomberg reported on Friday, and the site market tables updated October 30 at 06:00 ET put US copper at 6.52 dollars a pound, down 0.86 percent on the day and up 0.75 percent on the week. The physical market did not follow the price down: session reporting put the Yangshan import premium at a record 200 dollars a tonne as Chinese buyers sought to secure supply. Two new structural items frame the move, both covered in full stories today: a GEM Mining Consulting study finds 69 percent of visible exchange stocks now sit in COMEX warehouses in the United States, leaving about 190,000 tonnes of visible exchange copper outside the US excluding US located LME stocks, and BMI warns a strong El Nino threatens copper output in Chile, Peru, Zambia and the Democratic Republic of Congo through early 2027. The price is easing on flows while the deliverable market tightens, which is the divergence this story has tracked all week.
