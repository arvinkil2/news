Venezuela expects nearly 76 billion dollars of investment in its oil and gas sector over the next five years under recently signed agreements, acting President Delcy Rodriguez said on national television on Saturday, Reuters reported. A bilateral accord with the United States covers 17 oil fields and includes plans to drill 250 rigs, and it forms part of a broader package of 61 agreements signed by the two sides.

Rodriguez said production in western Zulia state had risen from 70,000 barrels per day to 370,000 barrels per day and was expected to reach 400,000 barrels per day by the end of the year. She did not state the period over which the Zulia increase occurred, and those output figures are her claims, reported as such.

The context is a reopening. Since the January ouster of President Nicolas Maduro, Venezuela has been readmitting foreign energy investment with the backing of the Trump administration, after years in which underinvestment, operational failures at state oil company PDVSA and US sanctions cut output far below the levels of a decade ago, despite the country holding the world's largest proven crude reserves.

The distinction that matters is between expectation and commitment. The 76 billion dollars is what the government says signed agreements should bring over five years. No operator by operator capital schedule accompanied the announcement, and delivery depends on sanctions terms, contract security and companies willing to fund drilling at that pace. This story reports the official figure and its stated components, 61 agreements, 17 fields and 250 rigs, without treating the headline as money already raised.

The announcement sits alongside the World Bank's assessment, reported on October 16, of up to 5 billion dollars in possible lending to Venezuela over two years. Together they describe a financing reopening that is still at the term sheet stage: large official numbers, few funded projects yet.
