With a little more than five weeks to go before Britain tightens its sanctions on the transport of Russian LNG, Dynagas LNG Partners is still seeking replacement insurance for the two vessels it charters to the Yamal LNG project. The Athens-based, New York-listed partnership said in its third-quarter results on November 20 that it had not yet found replacement protection and indemnity or hull and machinery cover for the Yenisei River and the Lena River, and warned in an SEC filing that failure to secure alternative cover could ultimately result in a default under its debt agreements.

The two ships are 2013-built, 155,000 cubic metre ice-class LNG carriers on long-term charter to Yamal Trade, running to 2033 and 2034 respectively. Both are currently insured in the United Kingdom. From January 1, 2027, those insurers will no longer be allowed to cover voyages carrying Russian-origin LNG unless a licence or exception applies, under the UK maritime services ban introduced on May 20, 2026, which bars UK businesses from taking part in the seaborne transport of Russian LNG to and between third countries, including the insurance that keeps those voyages moving. Older contracts received a transitional reprieve; it ends on January 1, and the government said in October that it is standing by full implementation.

The exposure is concentrated. The two Yamal charters accounted for approximately 34 percent of Dynagas's revenues in the first nine months of this year and represent about 56 percent of its estimated 700 million dollar contracted revenue backlog, roughly 390 million dollars. The partnership warned that failure to secure replacement insurance could result in off-hire claims, litigation or premature termination of the charters. Its third-quarter results were otherwise firm: adjusted net income rose 29.3 percent year on year to 18.4 million dollars, voyage revenue rose 13.1 percent to 43.979 million dollars, and fleet utilisation reached 100 percent.

The case also exposes a split between sanctions regimes. The European Union is allowing certain longstanding contracts covering Russian LNG shipments to third countries to continue until at least July next year, while London's deadline is January 1. Dynagas, meanwhile, has been growing its exposure to American LNG exports, with two of its six vessels now on long-term charters with Rio Grande LNG in Texas.
