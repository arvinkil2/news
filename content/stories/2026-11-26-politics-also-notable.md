ASEAN+3 finance and central bank deputies met in Kyoto on November 25 and 26, with Japan and the Philippines as co-chairs of the 2026 process and Vice Minister Atsushi Mimura attending for Japan alongside the Bank of Japan. The deputies welcomed the entry into force of the amended Chiang Mai Initiative Multilateralisation agreement, including its new Rapid Financing Facility, endorsed the ASEAN+3 Macroeconomic Research Office's medium-term implementation plan for 2027 to 2029 and the Asian Bond Markets Initiative roadmap for 2027 to 2030, reached consensus on the governance principle for the paid-in capital structure, and agreed to establish a new AMRO programme on cross-border digital payments with a dedicated working group. A trilateral China, Japan and Korea deputies' meeting, chaired by Korea, was held on the margins.

Government representatives and housing experts opened the third session of the UN's open-ended intergovernmental expert working group on adequate housing in Nairobi on November 26, a process set up in 2023 to produce recommendations for the UN-Habitat Assembly in 2029. UN-Habitat's executive director Anacláudia Rossbach put the number of people living without a roof over their heads at about 300 million, with no agreed global definition of homelessness, and the agency's World Cities Report estimates up to 3.4 billion people lack adequate housing, more than 1.1 billion of them in informal settlements and slums. The session, running to November 27, also launches the first version of UN-Habitat's Housing Knowledge Platform.

In Spain, three days before Sunday's snap election, the leader of the new left coalition called for a "rental revolution," pitching housing, the issue on which the last legislature fell, directly against the right bloc that leads the published polls. The call is campaign rhetoric, not a government measure; polling publication is banned from November 24 to 28, and the vote itself is on Sunday, November 29.
