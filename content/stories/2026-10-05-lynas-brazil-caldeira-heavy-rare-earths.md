Lynas Rare Earths has agreed to acquire 100 percent of Meteoric Resources via a scheme of arrangement, an all-scrip transaction announced October 1 and valued at approximately A$968 million — about US$673 million — on a fully diluted basis. Meteoric shareholders would receive 0.0207 new Lynas shares per Meteoric share, a 68.4 percent premium to the last close, and the Meteoric board has recommended the deal unanimously. On completion, Lynas shareholders would hold about 94.1 percent of the combined entity.

The prize is Caldeira, in Brazil's Minas Gerais state: the largest known ionic-clay rare earth resource outside China under the JORC code, with a definitive feasibility study targeting average annual production of 3,862 tonnes of neodymium-praseodymium and 127 tonnes of dysprosium-terbium over the project life, at an estimated capital cost of about US$500 million. Alongside the deal, Lynas is providing up to A$110 million in interim funding — A$35 million on signing, up to A$75 million more — at 10 percent, capitalized. The scheme booklet lands in mid-December, the shareholder meeting is set for late January 2027, and implementation is targeted for early March 2027.

The premium is the market's verdict on the calendar: China's one-year suspension of its rare-earth export controls expires November 10, and the January 1, 2027 U.S. defense procurement ban on Chinese-origin magnets lands 52 days later. Rival interest is already being priced — mining analyst Rick Squire says U.S. and South Korean firms could emerge as challengers. Korea is moving regardless: LS Cable & System is wiring the Brazilian mine into a non-Chinese permanent-magnet chain, and its LS Eco Energy unit is investing 11.6 billion won in a Vietnam processing facility with trial production set for December.

Lynas entered the deal from strength: fiscal 2026 revenue reached A$977.95 million, up 76 percent, with net income of A$222.35 million, and the shares closed Friday at A$12.95, up 2.45 percent on the week. The company also holds a U.S. government-linked offtake-style agreement with floor prices on neodymium-praseodymium volumes, complementing its long-standing Japan arrangement. The combination would lift Lynas's measured and indicated rare earth resources by 79 percent on a pro forma basis.
