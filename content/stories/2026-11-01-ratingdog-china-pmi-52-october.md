China's factory activity expanded at a marginally slower pace in October, with the RatingDog general manufacturing PMI, compiled by S&P Global, easing to 52.0 from 52.1 in September and beating a Reuters poll forecast of 51.6. New orders edged down to 53.3 from 53.4 and export order growth slowed to a three-month low, while output rose at its fastest rate in six months and purchasing activity grew at its fastest pace since February. Output prices fell after rising in September as firms cut prices to support sales, and the reading contrasted with the official PMI released on Saturday, which showed factory activity unexpectedly falling.
