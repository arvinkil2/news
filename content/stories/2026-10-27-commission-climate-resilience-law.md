The European Commission has put forward a European climate resilience law to equip and empower public authorities, businesses and communities, alongside a framework that takes what it called a more proactive and systemic risk management approach after a summer of wildfires and droughts in 2026. A common approach to assessing and responding to climate risks is meant to better protect people, essential services and infrastructure when climate related disasters occur, supported by tools including the European Climate Viewer, a Navigator of Climate Resilience Tools and a Mission Solutions Explorer of proven adaptation solutions.

Looking ahead, the Commission said it will add a European heatwave plan, a climate insurance alliance and a European water initiative, launch a PreparEU Advisory Group on disaster preparedness, and identify Europe's 100 most climate vulnerable territories. It restated the EU commitment to become climate neutral by 2050, saying the framework prepares Europe for the climate impacts that are already unavoidable.
