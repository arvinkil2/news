Iran's Revolutionary Guards claimed Tuesday that a supertanker caught fire after striking a naval mine south of the Strait of Hormuz, and in a second statement carried by Iranian media said two tankers had been set ablaze and disabled by mines along a route south of the strait that Tehran considers unauthorized. Neither statement identified the vessels or provided independently verifiable evidence, and the Guards did not make clear when the tankers were allegedly hit, leaving open whether the announcements describe new incidents or repeat earlier claims.

Maritime assessors have answered the pattern directly. In its latest advisory, issued October 18, the Joint Maritime Information Center addressed Iranian claims that two supertankers struck mines on October 17: "On 17 Oct the IRGC Navy claimed that two supertankers had struck mines in the Strait of Hormuz; there is no substantiation to these claims." The clearest contradiction came last week, when Iran claimed on October 14 that a containership had struck a mine; JMIC's October 15 assessment identified the vessel, which appeared to correspond to the Singapore flagged GFS Pride, attacked on October 13, as struck by an unknown projectile, and CENTCOM identified an IRGC projectile as the cause. All 21 crew were safely evacuated and the ship sank while being towed toward shore.

The underlying danger is not in dispute. JMIC maintains a severe threat level for Hormuz and warns mariners to remain vigilant for attempted mining within and near the traffic separation scheme. Its October 18 advisory documented 153 confirmed maritime security incidents across the wider region since March 1, including attacks with unidentified projectiles, drones and small boats, and UKMTO recorded 12 attacks in the week covered by its October 16 overview, including a tug struck by two unknown projectiles near Khor Fakkan on October 18. The same advisory reported traffic through Hormuz about 70 percent below prewar levels, with 254 vessel transits in the week ending October 14. A verified mine strike would indicate newly laid mines in waters US forces previously declared clear; for now, no such verification exists.
