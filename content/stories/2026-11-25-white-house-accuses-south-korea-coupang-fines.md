The White House accused South Korea of breaking a promise to President Donald Trump to treat US technology companies fairly, telling Fox News that Korean regulators have hit Coupang, the Delaware-incorporated, New York-listed e-commerce company whose business is almost entirely in South Korea, with roughly $688 million in fines, including a provisional $224 million penalty against its food delivery service announced this month by the Korea Fair Trade Commission. A State Department official said the commission's continued action against American companies threatens the US-South Korea alliance and that Washington is actively evaluating a range of policy options in response.

South Korea's embassy in Washington rejected the charge of targeting, saying all investigations and decisions, including the food delivery fine, were conducted lawfully under Korean law, that legal remedies remain available, and that Seoul abides by its agreement with the Trump administration and is committed to a fair and nondiscriminatory business environment. The dispute follows reporting on this site on November 23 that Washington is reviewing a Section 301 investigation into digital-sector policies in South Korea, the European Union and Australia, with the treatment of Coupang among the measures in scope.
