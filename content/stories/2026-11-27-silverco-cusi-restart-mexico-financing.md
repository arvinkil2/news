Silverco Mining has secured up to $15 million in financing tied to concentrate sales from its newly restarted Cusi mine in Mexico, giving the company more room to fund the ramp up of two underground operations through the first half of 2027. The agreement was disclosed late on Wednesday alongside third quarter results, and follows Cusi's first concentrate shipment on November 16. Silverco shares rose 6 percent to C$7.30 on Thursday afternoon in Toronto, valuing the company at about C$408 million.

Cusi, in Chihuahua state, had been on care and maintenance since September 2023. Silverco says the $19.2 million restart remains on budget: the company plans to hand the refurbished Mal Paso mill to operations by the end of November and start extracting ore from its first stope at Promontorio late this quarter, working toward the mill's design rate of 1,200 tonnes per day. The prepayment facility can be drawn at Silverco's discretion, with each draw repayable over 24 months.

At La Negra in Queretaro state, acquired in May through the all share purchase of Nuevo Silver, the mine processed 125,000 tonnes in its first full quarter under Silverco and produced 161,741 ounces of silver, or 348,578 silver equivalent ounces including copper, lead and zinc. It generated C$24.6 million in revenue and C$3.4 million in mine operating earnings, at an average of about 1,360 tonnes per day, just over half its 2,500 tonne design capacity. A new underground fleet and a tailings filtration plant, targeted for commissioning by the end of June, are central to lifting throughput.

Silverco aims to declare commercial production in the first half of next year at both mines, and says the two could deliver more than 4 million silver equivalent ounces in 2027, based on their preliminary economic assessments. Its quarterly loss of 2 cents per share was narrower than the 8 cent average analyst forecast, largely because costs came in below estimates.
