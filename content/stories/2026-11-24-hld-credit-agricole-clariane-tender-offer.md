HLD Europe and Crédit Agricole Assurances announced on November 24 a proposed concerted action over Clariane, the French care home and healthcare group, after which HLD, as sole initiator, would file a mandatory simplified tender offer for the company's shares at €4.60 per share. The price is a premium of 38.3 percent over the last closing price before the announcement, 38.7 percent over the one-month volume-weighted average price and 28 percent over the three-month average. The offer is not intended to delist the company and carries no squeeze-out.

HLD currently holds 27.2 percent of Clariane's share capital and voting rights, and Crédit Agricole Assurances, through Predica, holds 25.9 percent. Acting in concert would put the two above the threshold that triggers a mandatory offer under French rules. The plan is conditional on regulatory approvals, on the board adopting a reasoned favourable opinion, and on the definitive lifting of an existing standstill commitment limiting HLD's ability to increase its holding.

Clariane's board met on November 23 and gave the proposal a preliminary positive reception, after an ad hoc committee of four independent directors, formed on November 16, reviewed it. The board noted that the two shareholders intend to continue the company's operations under its 2025 to 2028 business plan, support debt reduction, and ensure no dividends are paid for the 2026, 2027 and 2028 financial years. HLD has negotiated a bridge loan of up to €500 million in connection with the plan.

Clariane shares jumped 39 percent to a more than one-year high on the announcement. The transaction, if completed, is a control consolidation rather than a change of ownership in substance: the two institutions that already anchor the register would formalise joint control of a heavily indebted care operator whose equity story now depends on deleveraging rather than distributions.
