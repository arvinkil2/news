King Philippe refused the resignation of Prime Minister Bart De Wever on Friday and told him to resume Belgium's budget negotiations immediately, NOS reported. De Wever, who offered his resignation on Monday after months of failed coalition talks on the budget, was sent away after a meeting of about two hours. The King had consulted the other governing party leaders in recent days; they told Flemish broadcaster VRT they still want a budget agreement, and the five party chairpersons pledged they are willing to compromise. De Wever is to report back to the King on November 16, and it is not clear whether that date is a final deadline. Had the King accepted the resignation, early elections would have followed. The dispute is over about 10 billion euros of savings over the next three years needed to stay within the European spending norm: De Wever's N-VA wants cuts in health care, while other parties argue for tax increases. Barbara Pas of the opposition Vlaams Belang said the voter, not the King, should have the last word.

Belgium's Prime Minister Bart De Wever tendered his resignation to King Philippe on Monday after overnight budget talks with the five coalition parties broke down again, and the King held the resignation under consideration, the Royal House said. The talks had been deadlocked for months over a €10 billion round of savings, and De Wever had set Tuesday as his deadline for a deal. The King said Belgium could not afford a prolonged political crisis and will begin consultations with the leaders of the majority parties on Tuesday.
