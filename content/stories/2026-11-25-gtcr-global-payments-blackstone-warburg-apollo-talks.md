Blackstone, Warburg Pincus and Apollo Global Management are in talks to help GTCR finance its roughly $50 billion take-private acquisition of Global Payments, Reuters reported on November 24, citing three people familiar with the matter. The firms are discussing equity or preferred equity investments that would reduce the deal's reliance on new borrowing. The discussions are ongoing and no agreement has been finalized, and all three firms declined to comment.

GTCR disclosed its intention to pursue a deal in a regulatory filing on November 20, days after Reuters reported that an earlier consortium effort with Clayton, Dubilier and Rice had been dropped. GTCR already holds 16.35 percent of Global Payments, making it the company's largest shareholder, and Bloomberg has reported the transaction would be worth about $50 billion including debt and a premium.

The structure is the story. A payments processor of this size would once have been financed mainly with syndicated leveraged loans and high-yield bonds. Instead, the sponsor is assembling equity and preferred equity from rival private capital firms, a sign that lenders remain selective on multibillion-dollar tickets and that the largest buyout firms now routinely fund each other's deals.

Global Payments is one of the largest US merchant acquirers and payment processors. A take-private at about $50 billion would rank among the largest leveraged buyouts attempted in the payments sector, and its financing mix will be read as a market signal for every other sponsor holding a mega-deal that needs a club to close.
