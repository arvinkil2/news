**Latest, October 23:** President Zelenskyy said on Friday that the restrictions on Starlink and Starshield are still in place, writing that "the speed has not been restored", and his defence technology adviser Serhii Beskrestnov confirmed the limits were in force, saying isolated cases of service beyond them most likely reflect SpaceX's algorithms rather than a removal, CNBC reported. President Trump, at the White House the same day, insisted he had not spoken to Elon Musk and repeated that the service had been restored, saying he had heard it was fixed and that if it was not, he would speak to Mr Musk. Ukrainian officials say the Starshield speed limits, in place since October 14, cut the connection of drones travelling above 100 miles per hour; lawmakers from both parties have called for an investigation into the throttling, and SpaceX and Mr Musk did not respond to CNBC's request for comment.

President Trump told reporters on Thursday that Elon Musk had restored Ukraine's Starlink access. Asked whether he had spoken to Mr Musk, he declined to comment, saying it did not make any difference whether he called or somebody else did, and that the bottom line was that Mr Musk had put it back, Reuters reported. The claim goes further than the accounts from Ukraine in the same reporting: some Ukrainian officials said the system was working at higher speeds than under the cap but had not been restored to full functionality, and President Zelenskyy said earlier on Thursday that speed had not been restored.

President Trump called Elon Musk and asked him to lift the restrictions on Ukraine's use of Starlink, the Wall Street Journal reported on Thursday, citing US officials. US and Ukrainian officials told the Journal the system was working more normally shortly after the conversation, though some Ukrainian officials said speeds had risen without returning to full capability, and the Journal said Mr Trump told Mr Musk he was frustrated that speeds remained capped at around 100 miles per hour. President Zelenskyy, speaking earlier on Thursday, said Starlink speed had not been restored for Ukraine and that he hoped for talks with Secretary of State Marco Rubio in the coming days.

President Trump denied any part in the slowdown on Wednesday as he left the White House for a campaign rally in South Carolina. Asked about the decision, he said he was not involved, and pressed on whether Musk ordered it, he said he did not know who did it. Senator John Cornyn told CNBC the same morning that he did not believe Musk would throttle Starlink without at least White House acquiescence. President Zelenskyy said on Monday that his military still faces speed restrictions on Starlink and Starshield in different parts of the front and that the American side has given Ukraine no explanation, and on Wednesday he said he had discussed launching new satellite communication capabilities with Norwegian Defence Minister Tore Sandvik.

Two incompatible accounts of who slowed Ukraine's military satellite service are now on the record at the same time. The Wall Street Journal reported on Tuesday, citing US officials and people familiar with the matter, that Elon Musk last week ordered the slowdown of Starshield, the military grade version of SpaceX's Starlink service that Ukraine relies on for battlefield communications and drone operations. Restricting the service hampered Ukraine's attack and air defence drone missions against Russia, the Journal reported.

Reuters, carrying the Journal's report, added two qualifications that define the story's evidentiary status. It could not immediately verify the report, and it had itself reported exclusively that the order to limit the satellite system came from inside the White House. Musk, SpaceX and the White House did not immediately respond to Reuters requests for comment.

The denials are also on the record, and they do not line up with each other. President Trump said last week he did not think either he or Musk was responsible for Ukraine losing Starlink access. Musk has said any limits on speed or Starship use were decided by the US military or the president. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said on Wednesday that Washington had no prior knowledge of the slowdown and that the president has nothing to do with decisions made by Starlink, Starshield or Mr Musk. Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth said on Tuesday that Washington had not pulled back Ukraine's ability to access the network and signalled the problem lay in terms of service Kyiv needed to comply with.

What is not disputed is the operational fact underneath the argument, which this site has covered since October 14: speed caps on the service inside Ukraine degraded missions, and Kyiv wants full function restored. President Zelenskyy, in Oslo on Tuesday in a bid to repair relations with Washington, said he had discussed the situation with Starlink and Starshield, which needs to be restored, with Rubio. The Journal also reported that Trump and Musk discussed the Starlink decision in a call soon after the slowdown became public, and had discussed the matter before the throttling decision as well. Until one of the principals produces a document, the order's origin remains a reported and contested claim, and it is presented as such.
