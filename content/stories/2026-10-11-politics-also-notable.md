**Washington installs a China currency hawk at Treasury.** Secretary Scott Bessent appointed economist Judy Shelton as a counselor in the Office of the Secretary, where she will advise on currency policy "with a particular focus on evaluating financial conditions in China," per the October 9 Treasury release. Shelton, a former US director at the EBRD and chairman of the National Endowment for Democracy, is a longtime voice for sound-money and dollar-focused policy; her remit lands as US-China trade and FX tensions run hot ahead of the midterms.

**Nigeria's 2027 race keeps building.** Governors of the north-central states endorsed President Tinubu's re-election, while SGF Akume argued the 1999 North-South power rotation agreement favors Tinubu's bid, setting up a regional-alignment push weeks before the Kaduna campaign flag-off on October 24. Atiku countered that the 2027 election is "a rescue mission for Nigerians." Separately, a foundation launched an initiative to prevent election violence in the Niger Delta, and SERAP sued the Central Bank of Nigeria over $6.23 million in allegedly missing election funds plus 1.63 trillion naira in other unaccounted public money.

**Zambia takes stock of the 2026 vote.** The Lusaka Times began a post-election autopsy series, with opposition voices rejecting President Hichilema's "free and fair" characterization, days after he opened the 280-seat parliament where his UPND holds 169 seats, short of a two-thirds majority.

**Bank of America prices a blue wave.** BofA analysts, per MarketWatch, mapped how far stocks could fall if Democrats sweep Congress on November 3, adding a Wall Street pricing layer to the Senate forecast movement that already has Kansas at toss-up and New Hampshire tightening.

**US midterms: a quiet Sunday.** After Saturday's cascade of state-by-state deltas, no genuinely new poll or development moved overnight; the map stands where yesterday's update left it: Kansas 45-45, Iowa split, Maine Jackson slightly ahead, Ohio Brown ahead, New Hampshire tightening, with 23 days to Election Day.

**Analytical, not news:** The Diplomat makes the case that Kim Jong Un should seek a fourth Trump summit, an argument about diplomatic incentives rather than an imminent meeting.
