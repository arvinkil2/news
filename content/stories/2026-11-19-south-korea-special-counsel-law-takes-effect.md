South Korea's special counsel law on alleged fabricated investigations and indictments was promulgated on Thursday after President Lee Jae-myung approved it while travelling overseas, and it took effect immediately on publication in the official gazette. The law provides for a special prosecutor to examine allegations that, under the Yoon Suk Yeol administration, the prosecution, the National Intelligence Service and the Board of Audit and Inspection fabricated investigations and indictments against the then-opposition Democratic Party and other political opponents.

The investigation reaches the handling of cases tied to Lee himself, including the Daejang-dong, Wirye and Baekhyeon-dong development cases, the Ssangbangwool remittance to North Korea case and Seongnam FC sponsorship bribery allegations. The special prosecutor's office may field up to 309 personnel, including seconded prosecutors and investigators, and may investigate for up to 200 days; the cabinet approved the law on November 17, and the recommendation of a special prosecutor is the next step.
