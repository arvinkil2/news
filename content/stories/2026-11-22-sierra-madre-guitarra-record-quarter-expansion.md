Sierra Madre Gold and Silver reported a record third quarter at its La Guitarra silver-gold complex in Estado de México. The company milled 50,795 tonnes and produced 89,145 ounces of silver, up 22 percent and 39 percent respectively from the second quarter, on net revenues of $8.58 million and gross profit of $1.54 million.

The expansion behind those numbers is running ahead of its own schedule. After quarter end, mill throughput averaged 751 tonnes per day in October, with daily rates as high as 792 tonnes, against a previous nameplate capacity of 500 tonnes per day. That meets the Phase 1 expansion target of 750 to 800 tonnes per day. A new 11 by 12 ball mill has been installed, with drive motor, cyclones and instrumentation being fitted and commissioning expected in late November; combined with the existing mills it is expected to deliver the milling capacity for Phase 2, a target of 1,200 to 1,500 tonnes per day by the third quarter of 2027, more than eight months ahead of the original schedule on the company's account.

Underground development has been accelerated at all three mines in the complex, Guitarra, Nazareno and Coloso, to feed the bigger plant, with more than 800 metres of access and production drives completed since July 2026. Groundworks have started for the permitted dry stack tailings facility expansion that Phase 2 depends on. The company has also begun a 30,000 metre drill programme in the East District, with first assay results expected in the first quarter of 2027.

The operating story is a volume offset to price. Silver has fallen hard this month, and Sierra Madre's answer is throughput: management expects head grades to improve and unit costs to trend lower through the rest of 2026 and into 2027 as a growing share of fresh resource mineralisation reaches the mill.
