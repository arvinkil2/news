Vice President J.D. Vance, appearing in Nebraska, and Secretary of Agriculture Brooke Rollins announced the selections on Thursday under the Fertilizer Investment and Expansion for Long-Term Domestic Supply (FIELDS) program, pairing about $573 million in proposed USDA funding, partly recovered from failed projects under the previous administration's fertilizer program, with private capital. The largest selections were Intrepid Potash, chosen for about $139 million toward a $265 million sulfate of potash plant near Carlsbad, New Mexico, and Agriculture Project Holdco, chosen for about $116.9 million toward two manure-to-fertilizer plants in Kansas and Indiana, with PCI Nitrogen and TalusAg each selected for about $100 million. USDA said the eight projects would support about 1.08 million tons of annual production across nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium and sulfur fertilizers.
