**Latest, October 25:** Prime Minister Ahmadou Al Aminou Mohamed Lo could use constitutional means to resolve the standoff over Senegal's 2026 budget if talks with lawmakers fail, Bloomberg reported. Parliament is due to vote on the revised budget on Tuesday, after lawmakers from Pastef, Ousmane Sonko's party, which holds a majority in parliament, rejected the budget this week. Bloomberg said the dispute threatens to complicate efforts to secure a new International Monetary Fund program. The Bloomberg article is paywalled and only its headline and its own takeaways summary were readable, so no provision, figure or market reaction is carried from it.

Senegal's Eurobonds weakened on Tuesday and ranked among the worst performers in emerging markets, with the dollar notes due in 2033 and 2048 falling below 51 cents on the dollar, Bloomberg reported. The selloff followed a threat by Ousmane Sonko to block the revised budget for this year and the 2027 spending plan. Bloomberg said the standoff raised concern that Senegal's debt restructuring could be complicated and a new International Monetary Fund program delayed, as investors weighed the risks to the government's fiscal plans.
