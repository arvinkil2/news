**Latest, October 25:** Premier Ousmane Sonko told supporters in Ziguinchor, his southern stronghold, that he would invoke Senegal's constitution to adopt the budget without a parliamentary vote if lawmakers have not approved a spending plan in line with the government's vision by mid-November, Bloomberg reported. Sonko framed the budget as an instrument for carrying out the government's program and said it could not be compromised under pressure. The constitution allows a budget to be adopted without a vote where parliament has not decided within the time allotted, Bloomberg said. The standoff, between Sonko and President Bassirou Diomaye Faye, became public in June when Sonko called on the National Assembly to reject Faye's spending plans; the assembly is dominated by Sonko's own party, but the presidential coalition does not hold the two thirds majority a constitutional change would need, and the dispute has delayed the revised 2026 budget and the 2027 plan. An IMF mission visited Senegal earlier in October to discuss a new program, with no timeline set. Bloomberg put the country's next Eurobond principal payment at $460.3 million, due in March 2027 and equal to about 22 percent of foreign reserves, against defaulted loans from the previous administration that are still being restructured.

Senegal's Eurobonds weakened on Tuesday and ranked among the worst performers in emerging markets, with the dollar notes due in 2033 and 2048 falling below 51 cents on the dollar, Bloomberg reported. The selloff followed a threat by Ousmane Sonko to block the revised budget for this year and the 2027 spending plan. Bloomberg said the standoff raised concern that Senegal's debt restructuring could be complicated and a new International Monetary Fund program delayed, as investors weighed the risks to the government's fiscal plans.
