Trafigura, one of the world's largest commodities traders, has acquired full ownership of the $260 million La Farola copper gold project in Chile, expanding beyond concentrate trading into mine development. The company completed on Monday its purchase of the remaining 50 percent of Minera Altair, the project's owner, after holding an equal stake since 2021. Trafigura had disclosed the planned acquisition in its half year financial report for the six months ended March 2026. The purchase price was not disclosed.

La Farola, near Tierra Amarilla in the Atacama region, is a previously mined deposit that Altair has sought to restart since 2023. The environmentally approved project is designed to produce 90,000 tonnes of gold bearing copper concentrate annually over a 15 year mine life. Detailed engineering began in May 2025, with construction scheduled for June 2027.

That timetable faces uncertainty from a legal challenge to the project's environmental approval. In April 2026, Chile's First Environmental Court admitted a claim filed by the Colla Tata Inti Indigenous Community of Los Loros against the Atacama Environmental Evaluation Commission's approval decision, arguing the open pit project would affect local communities and air quality. The court heard arguments in July and has yet to issue a ruling.

The acquisition also deepens Trafigura's position in Chile, where it has added a 273 MW power plant stake and a long term lithium purchase agreement with EnergyX covering 100,000 tonnes of lithium products over 10 years. Trafigura reported revenue of $141.9 billion and net profit of $4.1 billion in the first half of fiscal 2026.
