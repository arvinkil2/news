The European Commission proposed its 22nd package of sanctions against Russia on Monday, targeting financial institutions and crypto platforms, President Ursula von der Leyen said in a video address, Reuters reported. On top of new sanctions on financial institutions, the Commission intends to create a task force to work on sanctions on crypto asset platforms.

According to Ukrinform, reporting von der Leyen's statement, the transaction ban would extend to about 60 more Russian banks, which would be cut off from the SWIFT messaging system, and controls on crypto and payment networks would tighten, including against third countries that help Russia evade sanctions. High Representative Kaja Kallas said dozens of Russian energy-sector actors, including shadow-fleet enablers, would be listed, and that the package adds more than 100 designations against the Russian and Belarusian military-industrial complex. The two accounts differ on the vessel count, with Kallas citing over 60 vessels and Ukrinform quoting von der Leyen at more than 70.

The package would also target electricity companies, bar European companies from helping Russia build, operate or repair energy infrastructure such as refineries, and prohibit European citizens from occupying posts in companies in Russia, Reuters and Ukrinform reported. The Commission is also proposing a way to implement a travel visa ban on Russian ex-combatants, a measure agreed pending feasibility in the previous package, and Ukrinform reported proposals to keep Russian teams and athletes out of European sporting and cultural events.

The measures are a proposal, not adopted law. Sanctions packages require unanimous approval from the 27 member states, and the final text may differ from what the Commission presented, including the number of banks and vessels listed.
