**Latest, October 29:** The talks are now a signed deal. DoorDash and Instacart announced a definitive agreement on Thursday under which DoorDash will acquire Instacart, formally Maplebear Inc., for a total value of $65.00 for each Instacart share, or an equity value of approximately $17 billion, DoorDash said in its third quarter release furnished to the SEC. Instacart stockholders may elect, for each share, $65.00 in cash, 0.3372 shares of DoorDash common stock, or a mix of $48.75 in cash and 0.0843 DoorDash shares, subject to a cap on total stock consideration of 25 percent of the aggregate consideration and to proration if the stock election is oversubscribed. The $65.00 value is a 26 percent premium to Instacart's closing price on October 22, 2026, the last trading day before media reports of a possible transaction, and Instacart's board has unanimously approved the agreement. Stockholders holding about 19 percent of Instacart's outstanding shares have agreed to vote in favour.

The closing timetable is the tell on regulatory risk. DoorDash expects the transaction to close at the end of 2027 or in the first half of 2028, subject to approval by Instacart stockholders, expiry or termination of the waiting period under the Hart-Scott-Rodino Act, other regulatory approvals including in Canada and Israel, and customary closing conditions. Financing is not a condition: DoorDash expects to fund the cash portion with cash on hand and debt financing, has a commitment letter from JPMorgan Chase Bank for a 364-day senior unsecured bridge loan facility, and said it is targeting a capital structure that maintains investment grade ratings. In its companion announcement, DoorDash said it expects to keep Instacart's brand, enterprise technology products and app after closing, that local grocers will face no grocery exclusivity and can work with any delivery platform, and that the two companies will operate separately until closing.

The deal arrived inside a record quarter. DoorDash reported third quarter total orders up 28 percent year over year to 993 million, Marketplace GOV up 36 percent to $33.9 billion, revenue up 34 percent to $4.6 billion, GAAP net income up 91 percent to $466 million and adjusted EBITDA up 41 percent to $1,060 million. Those figures matter to the deal's arithmetic: the buyer is funding a mostly cash purchase, at least 75 percent cash under the election structure, from a business now generating record order volume, rather than from a stretched balance sheet story.

**Latest, October 28:** The timing has narrowed. CNBC reported on Wednesday afternoon, citing sources familiar with the matter, that DoorDash's deal to buy Instacart could be announced as early as Thursday morning. The report states no terms: no price per share, no cash or stock mix, no closing conditions and no signed agreement, and the roughly 17 billion dollar valuation remains the figure several outlets reported on Monday rather than a number either company has confirmed. Neither DoorDash nor Instacart commented in the article. The market traded the new timing in extended hours, with Instacart shares up about 4 percent and DoorDash shares down less than 1 percent, a calmer reaction than Monday's, when Instacart jumped about 8 percent and DoorDash fell around 6 percent on the first reports. CNBC noted the acquisition would extend DoorDash's push beyond restaurant delivery into grocery and general retail, where it has been adding chains such as Sephora, Macy's, Staples and Anthropologie to its marketplace.

DoorDash is in advanced talks to acquire Instacart in a deal that could value the grocery delivery platform at about $17 billion, Reuters reported on Monday, citing a source familiar with the matter. A deal may be finalized as soon as Wednesday, although there are no guarantees it will come together, the source said. The Wall Street Journal first reported the talks on Monday, and The Information reported the same day, citing its own sources, that an announcement could come within days.

The market reaction priced the report before any agreement exists. Instacart shares rose about 8 percent in regular trading on Monday, from a market value of about $13 billion at Friday's close, while DoorDash shares fell around 6 percent. DoorDash has a market value of around $73 billion. Instacart's separate talks with Uber are no longer active, according to the Reuters source.

The strategic logic is complementarity. DoorDash began in restaurant delivery and has expanded into retail categories including Macy's, CVS and Target, while Instacart's business is grocery, built on relationships with supermarket chains and its own advertising and software lines. A combined company would sell delivery, advertising and store software to the same retailers from one platform, and would follow DoorDash's purchase of Deliveroo and Uber's earlier purchases of Postmates and Delivery Hero in a sector consolidating around scale.

Neither company has confirmed anything. Instacart declined to comment to Reuters and to PYMNTS, and DoorDash did not respond. Instacart separately announced on Monday that it will wind down Caper Carts, its AI shopping cart business, saying retail adoption had not scaled quickly enough, and will focus its in-store work on software. Until a merger agreement is filed, this remains a reported negotiation, and the $17 billion figure is a reported valuation, not terms.
