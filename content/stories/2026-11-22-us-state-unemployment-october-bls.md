Unemployment rates were lower in October in three states and the District of Columbia, higher in one state, and stable in 46 states, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on Friday. The national unemployment rate, 4.1 percent, changed little over the month. The District of Columbia's rate fell 0.2 percentage point, Florida, New Hampshire and Pennsylvania each fell 0.1 point, and Colorado had the only increase, up 0.3 point.

South Dakota had the lowest jobless rate in October at 2.0 percent, followed by North Dakota at 2.2 percent. The District of Columbia had the highest rate at 5.4 percent. Nineteen states had rates lower than the national figure and three states plus the District had higher rates.

Nonfarm payroll employment increased in four states and was essentially unchanged in 46 states and the District. The gains were California, up 47,100 jobs or 0.3 percent, Indiana up 15,900, Kansas up 7,100 and Iowa up 7,000. Over the year, payroll employment increased in 12 states, led by Texas with 203,300 jobs, California with 187,200 and Florida with 100,200, with the largest percentage increase in Louisiana at 2.0 percent.

One caveat travels with the release: household survey data were not collected for October 2025 because of a lapse in appropriations and were not collected retroactively, so the release carries no over the year unemployment rate comparison. The next state release, for November, is scheduled for December 18.
