Century Aluminum shares leapt about 45 percent after Goldman Sachs raised its price target on the stock to 71 dollars, MarketBeat reported, one of the largest single name moves in the sector this year.

The move is analyst led and is flagged as such. Target changes of this kind compress a view on aluminium prices, US smelting economics and tariff protection into a single number, and in a thinly traded mid cap they move the market before the underlying thesis can be tested. Aluminium's fundamentals, Chinese output caps, Western smelter restarts and the tariff wall around US primary metal, are genuinely tighter than a year ago, which is the substance beneath the call.

The caution is the base rate: target driven spikes in commodity mid caps have a habit of retracing half the move within weeks unless earnings confirm them, and Century's next print is the first real test.
