**Latest (Saturday night, October 10):** Western officials familiar with intelligence assessments told Bloomberg on Friday that Iran has retained the ability to manufacture ballistic missiles and drones despite eight months of US and Israeli airstrikes, with parts of its weapons-manufacturing infrastructure surviving and substantial ballistic-missile and anti-ship-weapon stockpiles intact. One official said Russia is supplying Tehran with missiles, consistent with an earlier intelligence assessment from June; Russia's Defense Ministry did not respond to Bloomberg's request for comment. The White House rejected the assessment: spokeswoman Anna Kelly said Operation Epic Fury had destroyed Iran's ballistic missiles, production facilities and navy, while Trump has claimed Iran's military was "totally destroyed" and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth said on September 30 that US operations destroyed Iran's "military industrial capacity" and its "entire defense base."

The report landed alongside a separate Financial Times investigation into a covert program, reportedly coded C430L and running since 2023, in which Russian specialists are cooperating with Iran to develop supersonic cruise missiles designed to threaten US Navy warships and aircraft carriers in the Middle East. Iran has flight-tested a ramjet propulsion system, though there is no evidence a Russian-assisted missile has entered operational service. Tehran separately claims its domestic weapons-production capacity has risen two and a half times since the war began.

The dispute over Iran's remaining capabilities comes as Trump has pledged no new strike on Iran before the November midterms and as the Hormuz tanker war continues, with the IRGC still targeting vessels in the strait. If Western intelligence is right, the war's stated objective is further away than the administration claims, and Russian missile deliveries give Tehran a resupply channel that raises the stakes for US naval forces and Gulf air defenses.
