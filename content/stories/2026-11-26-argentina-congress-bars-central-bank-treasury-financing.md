Argentina's Chamber of Deputies gave final approval on Thursday to a reform of the central bank's charter that bars the bank from printing money to pay the government's bills, completing one of President Javier Milei's main legislative projects. The vote was 141 to zero, with 99 abstentions, the Peronist opposition abstaining rather than voting against.

The new charter makes preserving the value of the peso the bank's primary and fundamental mission, ends the transitory advances through which the Treasury drew on the bank, and forbids primary-market purchases of government debt. It also removes the employment and development goals added to the charter in 2012. The Senate had passed the bill earlier after stripping out a Milei provision that would have required a two-thirds majority in both chambers to remove the bank's governors. Milei's office said the law ends what it called 91 years of plunder and institutional decay at the bank, founded in 1935.
