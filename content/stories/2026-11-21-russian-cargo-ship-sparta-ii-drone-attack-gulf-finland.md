**Update, November 25.** President Volodymyr Zelenskiy has confirmed that Ukraine carried out Saturday's drone strike on Sparta II, ending the period in which Kyiv did not claim the attack. Zelenskiy told Bloomberg News in an interview in Kyiv late on Monday that the vessel was a legitimate military target and, in his account, was carrying weapons; Bloomberg said it could not independently verify the claim that the ship was carrying weapons, and that cargo claim remains Ukraine's own account. Reuters reported that Norway, a strong supporter of Kyiv, urged Ukraine to exercise caution in such operations. Sparta II is owned by Oboronlogistika, which provides logistics services for the Russian defence ministry, according to the company's website. The claim of responsibility is confirmed by Zelenskiy's own statements; what the ship carried is not independently established.

**Update, November 24.** Sparta II arrived at the port of St Petersburg under tow on the evening of Monday, November 23, Leningrad Oblast governor Alexander Drozdenko said, and Russia's transport ministry confirmed the tow on Tuesday. All 16 crew had been evacuated onto a Russian ship, one slightly injured. Russia raised the attack at the UN Security Council on November 23, with deputy permanent representative Anna Evstigneeva calling it a spread of terrorism to the Baltic, a characterisation that is Russia's own claim; Ukraine has not claimed the attack.

**Latest.** Sparta II has left Finnish territorial waters under tow, President Alexander Stubb confirmed on Sunday, saying the damaged ship in its current state posed a serious danger in the Gulf of Finland. Finland's National Enforcement Authority said on Monday that it had determined the vessel belonged to a company on the EU sanctions list and imposed a temporary measure on Saturday evening prohibiting the ship's release, with exceptions for maritime rescue, safety and reducing environmental risk; the ship is operated by Oboronlogistika, a Russian company linked to the defence ministry. The Russian rescue vessel Murman began towing Sparta II on Sunday, assisted by the Finnish patrol vessel Uisko, the Estonian Navy's Kindral Kurvits and commercial rescue tugs, after Finnish, Estonian and Russian vessels fought the fire together; Finnish authorities reported no leakage of fuel or hazardous substances. The Kremlin on Monday accused Ukraine of terrorist activity in the Baltic Sea, a characterisation that is Russia's own claim; Ukraine has not claimed the attack, which Finnish defence officials assessed was likely Ukrainian.

The fire on board Sparta II continued to burn through Saturday night even as Finnish vessels sprayed the ship with water cannons to douse the flames and cool the containers stacked on its deck, Finland's coast guard said on Sunday. Its 16 crew members were evacuated onto a nearby Russian ship. Finland's military has said the strike on Saturday was likely carried out by Ukraine; Ukraine has not claimed it. Estonian Navy Commander Ivo Vark told public broadcaster ERR on Sunday that the risk of further attacks in the Gulf of Finland cannot be ruled out.

Finland's Defence Forces said they detected a drone attack on Saturday morning against a Russian cargo vessel in international waters west of Gogland Island, in the eastern Gulf of Finland, and the Finnish Border Guard said the damaged ship caught fire and drifted into Finnish territorial waters, where authorities were coordinating a rescue operation and preparing pollution control measures while stressing the situation posed no danger to other maritime traffic. Estonian authorities, who received a fire report with initial indications of explosions at 9:35 a.m., identified the vessel as Sparta II, a Russian-flagged cargo ship sailing from St Petersburg to Kaliningrad, about 11 nautical miles north of Estonia's Vaindloo Island.

Leningrad region governor Alexander Drozdenko said two unmanned aerial vehicles struck the vessel about three nautical miles from the Russian border, that the crew was being evacuated and that damage was being assessed, with no immediate confirmation of casualties; he also claimed Russian air defences intercepted 80 drones over the region overnight, a figure not independently verified. No organisation or country has claimed responsibility and the drones' origin is unknown. Finland said it had informed its allies, Prime Minister Petteri Orpo said the war was spilling into Finland's immediate vicinity, and Finnish authorities temporarily restricted aviation and maritime traffic in the eastern Gulf, including a closed maritime area off Kotka. Sparta II appears on the US sanctions list and, according to Ukrainian military intelligence cited by Helsinki Times, has previously transported Russian weapons and military equipment; its cargo on Saturday is unknown.
