Thursday brings the data the Federal Reserve just told markets it is waiting for. The advance estimate of third quarter GDP and the September personal consumption expenditures price index, including the core measure the Fed targets, are due this morning, alongside weekly jobless claims, one day after the FOMC held at 3.75 to 4.00 percent by 10 to 2.

The bar is set by the press conference. Chair Kevin Warsh described third quarter growth as expanding at over 3 percent, led by high technology, and said first half estimates had been revised higher. A GDP print in that zone confirms the statement's language of activity expanding at a strong pace and removes growth as an argument against the two dissenters, Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack, who wanted a hike on Wednesday.

Core PCE is the number December pricing turns on. The statement said inflation remains elevated and that more information will help the committee determine the stance appropriate to return it to 2 percent in a timely manner. A core reading that shows price pressure broadening beyond energy would validate the dissenters' case in the market's eyes; a benign one is the only evidence that could soften it before the December meeting.

Treasury yields go into the releases already repriced, with the 10 year at 5.05 percent after a 5.2 basis point rise. That positioning cuts both ways: the prints need only confirm the hawkish read to extend the selloff, while a genuine downside surprise would meet a market positioned for the opposite.
