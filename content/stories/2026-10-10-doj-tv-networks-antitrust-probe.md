**Latest (late Saturday night, October 10):** The Justice Department publicly confirmed the investigation on Saturday. Department spokeswoman Emily Covington said the television press pool "proudly boycotted covering White House events" and that "group boycotts among commercial competitors can violate the Sherman Act," adding: "The Antitrust Division is simply investigating to determine whether these news organizations have violated the antitrust laws." The Committee to Protect Journalists criticized the probe on Saturday. The boycott, which lasted about four days in late September, coincided with Chinese President Xi Jinping's White House visit, leaving a gap in pool video coverage of the visit. CNN, MS NOW and Politico sued over the credential ban; a judge lifted the ban, with the reprieve in place until Tuesday. Pool coverage has since resumed.

The Justice Department has opened an investigation into ABC, CBS, CNN, NBC and Fox News over pool coverage of President Trump, the New York Times reported Saturday, citing six people with knowledge of the department's action. A department spokesperson told the Times that "members of the television press pool have proudly boycotted covering White House events" and that "group boycotts among commercial competitors can violate the Sherman Act," adding that the Antitrust Division is investigating whether the news organizations violated antitrust laws. The networks had briefly stopped providing pool coverage after Trump banned CNN, Politico and MS Now from the White House, then reinstated it. Neither the department nor the networks immediately responded to Reuters' requests for comment.
