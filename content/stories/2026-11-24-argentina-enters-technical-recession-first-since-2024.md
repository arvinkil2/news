Argentina's economy entered a technical recession in the third quarter, the first since the first half of 2024. INDEC's monthly activity estimator, the EMAE, rose 1.1 percent in September from August in seasonally adjusted terms but fell 1.2 percent from a year earlier, and the monthly series puts the third quarter about 1.8 percent below the second. Gross domestic product had already fallen 0.6 percent in the second quarter, so the two consecutive quarterly contractions meet the standard definition, subject to the official third-quarter GDP figure due on December 16.

Ten of the fifteen sectors INDEC tracks fell year on year in September. Mining rose 11.1 percent, electricity, gas and water 4.0 percent and construction 3.5 percent, while fishing fell 48.6 percent, financial intermediation 11.5 percent, commerce 2.2 percent and manufacturing 1.8 percent. Activity over the first nine months of 2026 was up just 1.0 percent from the same period of 2025, and INDEC revised July to a fall of 2.5 percent and August to a rise of 0.4 percent.

The government has not accepted the label. Before the release, presidential spokesman Adrian Ravier said the conditions to confirm a recession were not in place and that, on the two-quarters-of-falling-GDP definition, the call must wait for the December GDP print. Analysts cited by MercoPress expect activity to hold near September's level and the fourth quarter to return to growth, with full-year 2026 forecasts clustered between about 0.6 and 1.0 percent.
