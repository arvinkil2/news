The South African Reserve Bank and the Prudential Authority have published a recommended fallback methodology for contracts linked to Jibar, the Johannesburg Interbank Average Rate, that name the Prime Lending Rate as their fallback reference rate. The paper, dated November 23, 2026, was prepared under the Reserve Bank's Market Practitioners Group, which is coordinating the transition away from Jibar to ZARONIA, the South African Rand Overnight Index Average.

The recommendation sets out how to calculate a credit adjustment spread between Jibar and Prime, so that a contract falling back to Prime does not silently transfer value between borrower and lender. It also covers the treatment of compounding and rounding, the operational details that determine the interest actually charged once the fallback is triggered.

A companion Market Practitioners Group publication addresses the transition of Jibar-linked payment-in-advance derivative instruments, a corner of the market where the timing convention of the legacy rate differs from its successor and needs its own conversion treatment.

The publications matter because benchmark reform is now in its contract mechanics phase. The successor rate has been chosen and the cessation path set; what remains is thousands of individual contracts whose fallback language decides who bears the difference between a term interbank rate and the rate that replaces it. A published, standard methodology for the Prime fallback reduces the scope for disputes when Jibar stops.
