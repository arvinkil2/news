Senegalese lawmakers have again rejected the government's revised 2026 finance law. Parliament's Finance and Budgetary Control Committee remained divided after talks late on Friday, October 30, over the treatment of special funds and assistance for vulnerable households, according to Bloomberg, citing unnamed lawmakers and a government official involved in the discussions.

The rejection prolongs the standoff between the government and parliament and threatens to delay approval of an International Monetary Fund program. It lands a day after Moody's cut Senegal's rating for the fifth time and warned of bigger losses for creditors in the country's debt revamp, so the budget impasse and the missing IMF anchor are now the two open questions over Senegalese bonds.
