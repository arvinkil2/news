The International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea declined on Saturday to prescribe provisional measures against oil development off the Falkland Islands, voting 18 to 3 that a sovereignty dispute was not a question for the law of the sea court. Argentina had asked the tribunal to block the venture, arguing that nothing should change the situation in disputed waters, including drilling wells, while its sovereignty claim over the islands stands.

The project at issue is the Sea Lion oilfield, about 130 miles from the islands and estimated at 1.7 billion barrels, where Britain's Rockhopper Exploration and Israel's Navitas Petroleum are due to begin extracting within two years. Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband and Attorney General Ellie Reeves welcomed the decision as a vindication of the UK's legal defence, said Argentina had sought to stop the islanders making their own sovereign decisions, and called on Buenos Aires to end its wider economic pressure against the islands.
