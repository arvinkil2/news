**Latest:** London Metal Exchange cobalt futures recently hit $43,855 a tonne, up from $33,080 on Monday, a rise of about a third this week, BMO Capital Markets analysts Helen Amos and George Heppel said in a note on Thursday. The ban takes effect on November 30 and includes previously authorized shipments that have not left the country by then, according to ARECOMS. Glencore, which produced 13,700 tonnes of cobalt in the first nine months of 2026, down 52 percent from a year earlier, has increasingly retained cobalt in solution rather than processing it into saleable hydroxide while focusing on copper production, the company said in October.

China's cobalt futures surged on Tuesday to their highest level in more than three months after Congo imposed the fresh export ban, Reuters reported. Contracts for November, December and May delivery on the Wuxi Stainless Steel Exchange hit their daily upper trading limits in morning trade, and the December contract rose 10.97 percent by 02:57 GMT after touching its limit at 316 yuan ($47.42) per kilogram, the highest since August 18. Shares of Ganzhou Tengyuan Cobalt rose 12.5 percent and Nanjing Hanrui Cobalt rose 11 percent. The figures are intraday levels, not closes.

The Democratic Republic of Congo will suspend all exports of cobalt from November 30 until further notice, the regulator ARECOMS said in a statement on Monday, in a decision it said was intended "to address the oversupply in the international market and to promote a sustainable rebalancing of the market."

ARECOMS said it reached the decision after considering the "significant and sustained decline in cobalt prices" since June, in an environment marked by a slowdown in global cobalt consumption, rising inventories and speculative practices. The agency approved the halt at a meeting on November 9, in a statement signed by its president, Patrick Luabeya. It plans to review the ban in four months against market developments, inventory levels and the balance between global supply and demand, and will use the suspension to review and adjust its quota allocation mechanism.

It is the second time Congo has suspended cobalt exports to support prices. A first full ban, introduced in February 2025, was replaced eight months later by strict quotas that remain in place and that capped exports at 96,600 tonnes a year for 2026 and 2027, less than half of the country's 2024 output. Prices spiked after each intervention, then fell back: cobalt hydroxide, the main product shipped from Congo, initially more than quadrupled before almost halving from an April peak, according to Fastmarkets data, as weak consumer electronics demand and recycled supply weighed on the market.

Cobalt is produced in Congo as a byproduct of copper mining, and CMOC Group, Glencore and Eurasian Resources Group hold the largest export quotas. Congo supplied about three quarters of the world's mined cobalt before the restrictions were imposed. A renewed ban could benefit Indonesia, the second producer, which makes a cobalt-bearing intermediate product at plants processing its nickel ores.
