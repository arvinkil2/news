Ukraine's Military Intelligence Directorate (HUR) said on November 26 that North Korea has temporarily suspended its military support for Russia, in what it described as a decision taken directly by Kim Jong Un and conveyed by Foreign Minister Choe Son Hui. HUR said further supplies of KN-23 ballistic missiles and artillery shells have stopped, Russian military transport aircraft have been barred from North Korean airspace, and Pyongyang is holding off on a Russian request to raise its troop deployment in Russia from 8,500 to 50,000. HUR attributed the move to October remarks by Russian Deputy Foreign Minister Andrei Rudenko on relations with South Korea, which it said Pyongyang judged inconsistent with the two countries' alliance agreement, alongside heavy North Korean troop losses.

The claim is uncorroborated and cuts against the public record. President Vladimir Putin met senior North Korean official Jo Yong Won about ten days ago and praised the two countries' military brotherhood, and President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said on November 16 and 17 that North Korea is supplying Russia with dozens of ballistic missiles every month. Neither North Korea nor Russia has confirmed any suspension.
