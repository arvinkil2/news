European benchmark gas rose for a fourth consecutive session on Tuesday, extending a Monday jump of as much as 8.6 percent to 96.075 euros a megawatt-hour, after weather models shifted sharply colder over the weekend. The front-month TTF contract topped 90.50 euros in early Amsterdam trade on Tuesday, up about 1 percent on the day.

The forecast change is the catalyst. Before the weekend, models pointed to a brief cold spell followed by warmer than normal conditions; the latest runs instead keep northwest Europe cold into early December, with temperatures 3 to 5 degrees Celsius below the 30-year average this week and 5 to 8 degrees below normal at the start of December, according to Atmospheric G2 data cited by Bloomberg. The United Kingdom, Germany and France, the largest gas consumers in the northwest, face freezing temperatures this week, with an unusually bitter snap also across central and eastern Europe.

Storage leaves little room for that weather. Gas Infrastructure Europe data put EU storage at 68.9 percent full on November 23, against a five-year average of 89 percent and about 82 percent a year earlier, after a summer refill season depressed by the loss of Qatari LNG cargoes during the Hormuz disruption. Withdrawals are now expected to run rapidly for a week or two, leaving less in store for the coldest winter months.

The move is global, not only European. Colder weather in Japan, South Korea and parts of China this week intensifies competition for spot LNG, and Japanese utilities are separately returning to the spot market as power prices restore gas-fired margins, a development carried in this site's Japan spark spread story today. In the United States, Henry Hub rose 12.66 percent to 3.59 dollars per million British thermal units at Monday's close in the estate market tables, up 11.81 percent on the week, as a pre-Thanksgiving cold shot lifted Northeast cash prices by more than a dollar at New England hubs, according to Natural Gas Intelligence reporting.
