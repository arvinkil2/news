The Copler gold mine in eastern Turkey has resumed processing stockpiled ore more than two years after a heap leach failure killed nine workers, Bloomberg reported on Wednesday. Operator Anagold Madencilik began treating the ore at its pressure oxidation plant in the third quarter under a temporary operating licence, a company spokesperson told the news agency.

Anagold aims to produce about 1.3 tonnes of gold by year end, equivalent to roughly 41,800 ounces. The mine produced 6.9 tonnes in 2023, about a fifth of Turkey's total output that year. The restart comes as Turkey seeks to expand domestic gold production and reduce its dependence on imports: Energy and Natural Resources Minister Alparslan Bayraktar told a parliamentary committee on November 23 that the government's goal is annual production of 100 tonnes, though he gave no deadline for the target.

The resumption follows the exit of SSR Mining, which completed the sale of its 80 percent stake in Copler and related properties to Turkish conglomerate Cengiz Holding and affiliates on June 24 for about $1.5 billion in cash after adjustments. Turkey's Lidya Madencilik holds the remaining 20 percent.

The February 2024 heap leach failure sent material into the nearby Sabirli Valley and Manganese Pit, burying nine workers, all of whose bodies had been recovered by June that year, according to SSR. Turkish authorities revoked the mine's environmental permit after the disaster, and SSR spent $149.3 million on reclamation and remediation at the site through the end of 2025. The landslide also raised concerns about potential cyanide contamination of the Euphrates River, though Turkey's deputy environment minister told parliament in May 2024 that surface and groundwater analyses had found no adverse effects from cyanide. It was the mine's second major disruption in under two years, after a June 2022 pipeline leak of diluted cyanide solution prompted a production halt.

Legal proceedings over the fatal landslide continue. A trial involving 43 defendants accused of causing deaths and injuries through negligence last convened on October 20, and the court has scheduled the next hearing for January 26, 2027, while awaiting a new expert report, Turkish news outlet Bianet reported.
