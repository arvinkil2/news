Thirty-one days before the November 3 midterms, the numbers point to a Democratic House and a coin-flip Senate. Democrats lead the generic ballot by 7 to 8 points; Trump's approval sits between 32 and 39%; prediction markets price a Democratic House near 86% while the Senate — Democrats need a net gain of four from 47 seats, with Maine, Michigan, Texas, a Nebraska independent, and Ohio as the margin — remains a toss-up.

Policy is already bending around the outcome. Trump expects renewed Iran bombing after the midterms. The lame duck is loaded: Congress funded the government only through December 11, and the Senate's October 2 compromise — passed 71 to 29, attaching two weeks of DHS funding to five full-year bills — still needs House action, with a fast-track vote scheduled Monday requiring two-thirds. Trump is pushing a $1.5 trillion military budget against Democratic resistance to the current $1 trillion.

A Democratic House win on November 3 changes the leverage on everything from Ukraine aid to tariff authority before the December 11 funding cliff.
