China is importing less liquefied natural gas despite the start of peak demand season, because an abundant domestic gas supply has brought prices down at home. The Chinese national average for LNG stood at $21.97 per million British thermal units, almost $10 below the Japan Korea Marker benchmark, and state energy majors have finished their stockpiling drive and begun releasing gas from inventory, leaving spot cargoes unattractive to Chinese buyers.

The pullback matters beyond China because Asian spot LNG prices topped $30 per million British thermal units in late October for the first time since December 2022, amid continued disruption to Qatar's exports, and the latest close was $31.49. Chinese buyers had already curbed imports earlier this year when prices rose above $20, and reduced Chinese demand now frees cargoes for other Asian importers and for Europe, where buyers are still stocking up for winter.
