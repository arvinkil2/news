Latest: North Korea said on November 5, Korea time, that Wednesday's launches included a test firing of a new anti-aircraft missile toward the East Sea and a separate long range strategic cruise missile training exercise, KCNA reported via Yonhap. KCNA said the anti-aircraft test was run to confirm the design of a new missile complex, including a new guidance method and target tracking, and that the cruise missiles struck a virtual airfield target. South Korea's military assessed that the missiles flew less than 300 kilometres and left the type open, listing a close range ballistic missile, a surface to air missile and a cruise missile as possibilities. Yonhap noted the launches fell on the day of the US midterm elections and on the day Kim Yo-jong mentioned a dialogue option with the United States in a statement. Pyongyang's account of what the tests proved is its own claim and is uncorroborated.

South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff detected the launch of several missiles from the Sondok area on North Korea's eastern coast toward the Sea of Japan, Al Jazeera reported on Wednesday, citing the Joint Chiefs. The report gives no missile type, exact count beyond several, or flight range, and says South Korean and US authorities are analysing the details. Al Jazeera counts the test as Pyongyang's fourth weapons launch in a month.

North Korea launched an unidentified projectile toward the East Sea, South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff said, in a Yonhap breaking flash transmitted at 9:53 a.m. Korea time on Wednesday. The flash gave no further detail: it did not identify the projectile type, say how many were fired, or state where the projectile landed. The launch follows the October 29 firing, when Seoul said North Korea fired more than ten short-range ballistic missiles toward the East Sea.
