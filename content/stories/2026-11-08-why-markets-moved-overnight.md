The Nikkei 225 was the day's largest mover, down 5.96 percent on Friday and 11.17 percent on the week. This morning's reporting carries the central Japan earthquake that injured at least 22 people, but no single driver in the briefs accounts for a fall of this size; it reads as the sharp end of the post-election global equity selloff that also produced record US options volumes on Thursday (see today's rates story on the options record).

Uranium miners rose 4.39 percent, the strongest group move in the mining table. Today's mining market story ties the bid to the sector's policy tailwinds, including the US rare earth and uranium supply chain buildout and UR-Energy's Wyoming restart, even as metal futures themselves fell.

Tesla rose 4.23 percent and the semiconductor group rose 3.51 percent, with Nvidia up 2.78 percent to 186.64 dollars. All three remain deep below their levels of a week earlier (semiconductors down 13.13 percent on the week, Nvidia down 8.8 percent), so Friday's gains read as a partial rebound from oversold levels rather than a new trend; no single company story in this morning's reporting explains the bounce, though Groq's 8 billion dollar valuation round points to continued private appetite for AI silicon (see today's Groq story).

Silver miners rose 2.63 percent and gold miners rose 2.16 percent, extending a strong week (gold miners up 13.63 percent, silver miners up 11.38 percent) even though gold futures fell on the day. Today's mining story flags exactly that divergence: equities are being bought as leveraged exposure while the underlying futures consolidate.

Natural gas rose 1.73 percent to 3.00 dollars per mmBtu, a third consecutive weekly gain, against an oil tape where Brent was flat on the day at 98.32 dollars but down 16.42 percent on the week as hedge funds cut their combined Brent and WTI net length by 78,492 lots (see today's commodities positioning story).

The synthesis is a barbell tape. Asia sold off hard and US yields backed up (the 10-year at 4.86 percent, up 2.2 basis points on the day) with December rate hike pricing near four in five, yet the assets bought on Friday were the week's most beaten-down growth names and the miners. That is short covering and bargain hunting into a binary week, not a settled risk-on turn: Tuesday's October CPI print is the test that either validates the rebound or resumes the selloff.
