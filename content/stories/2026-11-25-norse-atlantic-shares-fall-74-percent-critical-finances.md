Latest: The BBC reported on Thursday that Norse lost 94.6 million dollars in the first half of the year and, unlike many European carriers, does not hedge its fuel purchases, leaving it fully exposed when fuel costs roughly doubled after the Iran war began; the airline has been looking for a buyer since July. Consumer group Which? advised passengers not to cancel their own bookings, since cancelling removes the right to a refund while the airline has not yet collapsed, and said refunds for direct bookings would depend on card-provider claims if it does fail.

Norse Atlantic suspended all flights on Thursday morning. In a statement to the Oslo stock exchange, the company said that if a short-term financing solution is not secured urgently, it is the board's intention to file for bankruptcy, The Guardian reported. Norse said it was still looking at options for short-term financing and was in discussion with investors about a possible takeover, but that there could be no assurance a viable solution would be found. The airline apologised to passengers for the inconvenience; no refund or rebooking arrangements were announced. Affected routes include London Gatwick to Orlando, Bangkok and Cape Town, and a winter service from Manchester to Thailand had been due to start next month. The carrier's Project Falcon cost-cutting programme aimed to save 50 million dollars, including about 75 jobs.

Some Norse Atlantic flights were cancelled on Wednesday and Thursday, CNBC reported. Two flights Norse was to operate for Pakistan International Airlines under a wet-lease agreement, PK786 from London to Islamabad and PK710 from Manchester to Lahore, were cancelled due to what PIA called an unexpected operational suspension, and PIA said it would operate the routes with its own Boeing 777 aircraft. Norse flight Z0 783 from London Gatwick to Orlando was also shown as cancelled on Wednesday. Norse has not made an official statement on its operational status, and CNBC checks showed the airline was still selling tickets early on Thursday. Its shares closed 53.3 percent lower on Wednesday after falling as much as 86 percent during the session.

Norse Atlantic shares were last trading 74 percent lower on Wednesday after the Norwegian low-cost carrier disclosed what it called a critical financial situation, with soaring fuel costs amid the Iran war straining budget carriers in the winter season. The company said its situation depends on securing financing for its short-term liquidity needs.

Norse said it has held discussions about a potential strategic takeover and other options including ACMI contracts, which are short-term capacity leasing arrangements, and that it is in discussions with selected parties on short-term financing, while warning there can be no assurances those discussions will result in a viable solution. In October the carrier had already flown with reduced capacity because of prolonged high fuel prices.
