Silver fell 5.71 percent to $55.78 an ounce, the largest single move in the midday tables, extending a 6.87 percent weekly decline. This morning's commodities roundup (see "Also notable: gold steady, US gasoline, diesel pardons, crop progress") had silver easing only modestly ahead of the US inflation data, and no story in today's reporting explains the midday acceleration.

Silver miners fell 5.65 percent and gold miners fell 4.79 percent, amplifying the underlying metals, with gold itself down 1.91 percent to $4,097.70 an ounce. Today's mining reporting, including Evolution Mining holding its full year guidance after the September quarter, carries no company specific driver for the sector fall, which tracked the metals lower.

Uranium miners fell 4.99 percent, down 11.06 percent on the week. No story in today's published reporting explains the move.

Dry bulk freight fell 4.57 percent to $12.52, extending a 13.23 percent weekly decline, while tanker freight rose 0.44 percent. The fall was already the largest move in the overnight tables and, as this morning, no clear single driver appears in today's reporting.

Copper miners fell 4.35 percent even though copper itself eased only 0.28 percent to $6.66 a pound. Today's copper reporting ran in opposite directions, with the Escondida supervisors strike tightening near term supply (see today's Escondida strike story) and the International Copper Study Group forecasting the refined surplus to more than triple in 2027 (see "Global copper surplus forecast to more than triple in 2027"), and no story directly attributes the miner fall.

Semiconductors fell 4.30 percent, extending a 10.69 percent weekly decline, with Nvidia down 3.08 percent and Broadcom down 2.96 percent. The fall extends the repricing of the AI hardware chain flagged in today's IMF warning that a correction in AI linked equities could cut global output (see "IMF warns AI correction could cut global output"), and ASML's profit beat this morning did not hold the wider complex.

Meta fell 3.36 percent to $736.85, reversing its 2.63 percent rise in the overnight tables. Today's technology reporting carried no Meta specific catalyst, and no story in today's reporting explains the reversal.

The cross market read is risk off in the United States and split elsewhere. The S&P 500 fell 1.08 percent, the Nasdaq 100 fell 1.85 percent and the Russell 2000 fell 1.88 percent, while the dollar index rose 0.71 percent to 102.90 and front end yields rose, the five year up 4.3 basis points to 5.05 percent and the 10 year up 2.7 basis points to 5.24 percent. Europe held up, with the STOXX 600 up 0.43 percent and the FTSE 100 up 0.70 percent, and energy stayed bid on the Red Sea escalation, with Brent up 0.58 percent to $106.30 and WTI up 1.43 percent to $95.49 (see today's oil and Aramco stories), even as natural gas fell 3.17 percent to $3.18.
