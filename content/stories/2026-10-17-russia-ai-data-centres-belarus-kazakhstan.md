Russia could deploy AI data centers in Belarus and Kazakhstan as its local capacity struggles, Datacenter Dynamics reported, a move that would place Russian compute in allied states beyond the immediate reach of the strikes that have hit domestic infrastructure, including the Yandex facilities this site covered earlier in the week.

In parallel, Rostelecom's leadership has argued Russia needs a hardened, doomsday style data center to withstand Ukrainian strikes, an admission from the state telecom operator that ordinary commercial resilience standards no longer match the threat model for Russian digital infrastructure.

Neither item is a confirmed build, and both are single sourced trade reporting, graded C accordingly. Together they describe a capacity strategy splitting in two directions: geographic dispersal into partner states for new AI capacity, and hardening for the capacity that stays. For Western operators the read across is about sanctions era compute: Russia's AI ambitions now depend on sites it can defend or sites outside its own borders.
