**Latest (October 19):** EE Times reports the one decision that still carries money: Judge Maryellen Noreika has yet to rule on whether the material breach finding lets Qualcomm suspend royalty payments to Arm for up to five years under the licence's material breach clause. Qualcomm accounted for about a tenth of Arm's global revenue in fiscal 2024, so a five year suspension would be material even though the damages were nominal.

The five-day trial was the pair's second in two years, and Reuters tied the breakdown between them to SoftBank's 2016 takeover of Arm and Arm's shift from pure licensing toward selling chips of its own, which puts it in competition with customers like Qualcomm. Arm shares fell about 1% after the verdict and Qualcomm's rose about 1%, a market verdict that matches the split legal one: Qualcomm gets the finding that its license was breached, Arm keeps the billions Qualcomm had sought out of reach.
