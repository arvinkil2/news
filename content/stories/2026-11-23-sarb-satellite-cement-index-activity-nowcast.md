The South African Reserve Bank is turning satellite pictures of cement plants into an economic indicator. A working paper published by the bank constructs high frequency, spatially granular cement production activity indices from satellite imagery, using observed activity at production sites as a proxy for output.

Cement is a classic coincident indicator with an official data problem: production and construction statistics arrive with lags and revisions, while monetary policy needs to know whether rate changes are biting in interest sensitive sectors now. A satellite based index can in principle be produced at higher frequency and at plant or regional level, which is the spatial granularity the title promises.

The paper is a methods contribution, not a policy signal, and it arrives the same week South Africa's policy rate was held at 7.25 percent in a split decision, as regional reporting noted. Tools like this are how central banks in data thin environments shorten the gap between a decision and evidence of its effect.

Its limits are the usual ones for proxy measurement: imagery shows activity, not tonnage, and calibration against official production data decides whether the index tracks the cycle or just the weather and plant maintenance schedules. The bank publishes it as research, and markets should treat the first vintages accordingly.
