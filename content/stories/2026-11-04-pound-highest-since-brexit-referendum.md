The pound has risen to its highest overall level since the Brexit referendum more than a decade ago, with sterling up notably in recent weeks against both the euro and the dollar. The Bank of England's trade weighted measure, against a basket of 18 main currencies, reached 86.4 this week, its joint highest since June 2016.

Traders cited relative political stability, solid growth and closer ties with the European Union, alongside the expected rise in interest rates, as the reasons the UK is being viewed differently. UK growth so far in 2026 has outperformed expectations, although higher energy and fuel prices have begun to weigh on consumer confidence.
