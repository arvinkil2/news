Latest: President Donald Trump told reporters on Friday that the United States had carried out the strike, saying Iran had built a small anti-aircraft device and US forces knocked it out in minutes. The Guardian, reporting with the Associated Press, said Iranian state media reported the sound of two explosions in the Suza area of Qeshm Island, and described the action as the first US strike on Iran since Tuesday's midterm elections. The article reported no casualties and no damage beyond the device Trump described.

Trump also said the United States is talking to Iran and that mediators' discussions continue. Late on Friday, US Central Command said on X that the United States is offering a reward of up to $20 million and possible relocation for information about Iran's nuclear programme, and Qatar's government said Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth spoke with his Qatari counterpart, Sheikh Saoud bin Abdulrahman Al Thani, after Trump and Qatar's emir, Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad al Thani, discussed efforts to end the tensions. Qatar has been mediating between the United States and Iran.

Explosions were reported on Iran's Qeshm Island on Friday, according to Iran's Press TV, cited by TASS. The Tasnim agency had earlier reported that two missiles fell on the island, flying in from the Persian Gulf side, TASS said, and there was no official information about casualties.

Anadolu Agency, citing the Iranian press, reported explosion sounds near Qeshm and near the city of Bandar Khamir, and said residents of Qeshm heard the sound of a warplane and that two missile like objects hit the island. Anadolu said the Iranian reporting raised the possibility that the sounds were Iranian warning shots at ships using the alternative southern route through the Strait of Hormuz, and that no official statement had yet been made. Neither report names who fired anything, and no independent source had confirmed a strike at publication.
