The Basel Committee on Banking Supervision met in Basel on November 18 and exchanged views on financial risks and vulnerabilities facing the global banking system, noting that macroeconomic and geopolitical risks remain elevated, including through energy and commodity prices, trade developments and the prospect of interest rates remaining higher for longer. Particular attention was given to interest rate risk in the banking book, unrealised losses, refinancing risks in commercial real estate and growing links between banks and non-bank financial institutions, including private credit and financing related to data centres and artificial intelligence. The Committee said banks' capital and liquidity positions remain broadly strong and asset quality has not deteriorated materially overall.

The Committee also agreed to consult on targeted revisions to its 2008 Principles for Sound Liquidity Risk Management and Supervision, with the consultation to be published next month, and on technical amendments to the counterparty credit risk and Liquidity Coverage Ratio standards, to be published later this month. It is continuing its review of targeted elements of its prudential standard for banks' exposures to cryptoassets.
