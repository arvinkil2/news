A federal jury in Delaware found on October 13 that Arm breached its Architectural License Agreement with Qualcomm and intentionally interfered with the commercialisation of Qualcomm's custom Oryon CPUs, including by leaking the 2024 licence termination warning letter and withholding software patches and chip testing tools, but it awarded Qualcomm only two dollars in damages, one dollar on each of its two successful claims. Arm said the jury found for Arm on four other claims and confirmed it operated in good faith under its technology licence agreement. The larger question remains open: Judge Maryellen Noreika must still decide whether the material breach clause allows Qualcomm to suspend royalty payments to Arm for up to five years.
