An oil tanker hit a naval mine and caught fire in a restricted zone in the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday, the semi-official Fars news agency said, citing a statement by the Iranian Army. Reuters reported the claim on Thursday alongside state media reports that two oil tankers struck mines and exploded in the strait on Wednesday. The claim is Iran's account and is not independently confirmed; it follows the two tanker incidents covered in yesterday's Hormuz story and adds to the route risk that tanker freight has been pricing all week. The estate tables put tanker freight at 827.15 dollars, up 1.07 percent at the previous close, and dry bulk freight at 12.52 dollars, down 1.03 percent.

In Asia, Reuters noted on Thursday that Thailand's Bangchak Corp had shut all production units at its Phra Khanong refinery in Bangkok following Wednesday's fire, confirming the shutdown reported in today's developing Bangchak story. The 120,000 barrel a day plant is Bangkok's only in-city refinery, and no restart timing has been given.

Soybean oil fell to a nine-month low as strong US crush keeps adding supply, Grain Central reported, dragging ICE canola, rapeseed and palm oil lower with it, while soybean meal rose 6.57 percent on the week and is carrying crush margins. Palm oil fell 3.58 percent on the week on the soyoil weakness and fading Indian demand, although the Malaysian Palm Oil Council expects crude palm oil to hold above 4,100 ringgit in December.

In Black Sea wheat logistics, Grain Central reported that Baltic ports handled 61 percent of Russian wheat exports in October, up from 52 percent in September, as the Baltic route takes up the slack from the blocked Black Sea ports, and Vyborg volumes rose 2.3-fold to 1.5 million tonnes over January to October. Russia put its 2026 grain crop at 140 million tonnes net and 2026-27 grain exports at 40 million tonnes, subject to the Black Sea situation. Ukraine's winter wheat area is 4.19 million hectares, against 4.69 million a year ago.
