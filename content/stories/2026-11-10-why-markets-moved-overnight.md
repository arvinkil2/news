Tanker freight fell 13.82 percent to 583.03, the largest move in the tables. The fall extends the shipping side of the Hormuz normalization in today's commodities reporting: LNG traffic through the strait reached its highest since February, with 31 tankers in October, and Brent crude closed at 90.84 dollars, down 1.87 percent on the day and 12.32 percent on the week, before slipping to 89.70 dollars in early Tuesday trade (see today's Brent story and the Hormuz LNG traffic story).

Broadcom rose 8.78 percent to 319.48 dollars and the semiconductor basket rose 7.49 percent. No story in this morning's reporting explains the Broadcom move on its own. The sector context is in today's technology stories: Infineon printed a record quarter and guided fiscal 2027 revenue to about 20 billion euros on data center power demand, and Akamai disclosed an 11.6 billion dollar Anthropic commitment behind a 2028 revenue target above 7 billion dollars. Nvidia fell 4.27 percent against the sector, a divergence today's technology brief traces to the single-name overhang carried in the November 7 Nvidia story rather than to any new sector news.

Uranium miners rose 6.18 percent, copper miners rose 5.61 percent and silver miners rose 5.14 percent. Today's mining reporting is explicit that the rally outran the metals themselves: gold futures fell 0.77 percent, silver fell 0.37 percent and copper fell 0.29 percent on the same close, so the move was equity-led (see today's miner equities story). The physical copper backdrop remains tight, with the Yangshan premium at a record 230 dollars a tonne in today's commodities reporting.

Tesla rose 5.54 percent to 378.05 dollars and Meta rose 5.12 percent to 641.37 dollars. No clear single driver for either name appears in this morning's reporting.

The cross-market read is risk-on everywhere except energy. Equities rallied broadly on Monday's close (S and P 500 up 2.35 percent, Nasdaq 100 up 3.4 percent, Nikkei 225 up 2.87 percent), miners and chip names led, the dollar index was flat at 101.7, and Treasury yields barely moved, the 10 year at 4.86 percent, with the long end waiting rather than worrying. Against that calm, oil and freight are still pricing the supply fear out. The whole configuration now meets one number: October CPI at 8:30 a.m., where Governor Waller has said a core reading around 0.3 percent or higher should put a 50 basis point December hike on the table.
