JPMorgan Chase, Visa and Mastercard have all confirmed they are investigating breaches at third party service providers after a data theft and extortion group claimed to have compromised them. Visa said the incident involved a provider in Asia Pacific, that its own systems were not involved and that no payment data such as card numbers was affected. Mastercard said a limited amount of email addresses, not payment information, from a small subset of cardholders in a specific benefit programme was exposed, and that the exposure has been contained. Chase said attackers accessed certain marketing data held by its vendors, which may include some customer names and email addresses, and that the incident is unconnected to this week's service disruptions. None of the three named the providers involved.

CISA added four vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on evidence of active exploitation: a SAP NetWeaver query logic flaw, hard coded credentials in Cisco Expressway, an authentication bypass in VMware vCenter and a deserialization flaw in Apache Roller. Federal agencies must prioritise remediation of catalogued flaws on exposed assets under Binding Operational Directive 26-04.

NASA's 2001 Mars Odyssey orbiter dropped into safe mode between November 4 and 6 after one of its remaining reaction wheels stopped spinning; the wheel moved when commanded, but if the spacecraft must fall back on its hydrazine thrusters only a few months of fuel remain, putting a quarter century mission and a key Mars relay link at risk. The Perseverance rover is also in a protective mode after both its primary and backup radios were deemed inoperable, and is communicating at reduced rates over its high gain antenna while science operations are on hold.
