Hackers have breached Cloud One, the US Air Force's flagship cloud computing platform, three people familiar with the matter told Reuters, in an intrusion discovered within the past month during work to upgrade the system. The platform, built since 2017 on services from Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle and Alphabet, runs scores of applications including personnel and pay systems, logistics and the Theater Integrated Combat Munitions System, which tracks bombs and missiles across more than 200 Air Force locations. The Air Force declined to comment on the security posture of specific networks, and Reuters could not establish the hackers' identity, the full scope of the intrusion or whether any data was stolen.

The discovery has disrupted the platform, the sources said: migrations of back-office applications to Cloud One have been paused, updates to applications already running on it were blocked, and some users had administrator rights revoked. Investigators have called in Cyber Command to assess the damage, and one source said the intruders reached the platform's control plane by exploiting a software vulnerability.
