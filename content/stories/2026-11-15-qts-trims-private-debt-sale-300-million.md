QTS, the data center operator Blackstone bought in 2021 for about $10 billion including debt, has scaled back a private debt placement by roughly 60 percent, Bloomberg reported on November 13. The company will raise about $300 million rather than the $800 million it had targeted, after failing to attract the demand it expected at the yields it was willing to pay.

What did price was small and relatively short. The package includes a $100 million seven-year privately placed note priced on Friday at a yield 2.45 percentage points above market benchmarks, plus a $200 million five-year bank loan that has yet to be priced and is expected to be set in the coming weeks. The original placement, first reported in late October, contemplated maturities of seven to 15 years, issued through a special purpose entity, with Mizuho, Natixis, Societe Generale and Wells Fargo arranging.

The trim has a balance sheet consequence. Part of the proceeds was meant to refinance existing debt, and the five-year bank debt QTS had intended to repay with the bond proceeds will now remain outstanding, according to a person familiar with the matter cited in the report. A larger QTS borrowing, including a reported $4.6 billion green bond, is a separate transaction.

The signal is about price discovery, not solvency. QTS raised a smaller amount rather than pay up, which means the constraint was the issuer's price limit meeting a thinner bid, in a private market that has absorbed very large data center financings all year. If the pattern repeats at other sponsor platforms, the marginal AI data center project will be financed with more equity, shorter debt, or not at all.
