Australia's centre-left Labor government said on Tuesday it will fine internet companies including Google and Meta up to 5 percent of their global revenue if they fail to remove harmful content and addictive features, after striking a deal with the Greens in the Senate that puts its Digital Duty of Care bill on track to pass parliament this week. The revised penalty replaces the A$109 million (about $72 million) cap in the original bill, and the package also gives individuals a right to sue platforms for enabling wrongdoing.

The law would replace industry codes of conduct with a single duty requiring platforms, from messaging apps to search engines to AI chatbots, to proactively check for and stop harmful user experiences, including child sexual abuse material, non-consensual fake nude material, gender-based violence, self-harm promotion and terrorism. Communications Minister Anika Wells called it "seismic reform" that the government will deliver this week; tech industry groups have opposed the bill, saying it gives officials discretion over what counts as harmful and could push platforms toward heavy-handed takedowns.
