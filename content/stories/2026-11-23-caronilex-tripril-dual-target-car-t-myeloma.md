A first in human Phase 1 trial of TriPRIL, a CAR T cell therapy that targets two proteins on myeloma cells, found that the treatment reduced or eliminated signs of cancer in 12 of 15 patients with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma. Mass General Brigham investigators published the results in The Lancet, and STAT reported on November 23 that a new company, Caronilex, aims to develop the approach.

TriPRIL is short for trimeric APRIL. It was engineered so the modified T cells recognize both BCMA, the standard myeloma target, and TACI. Adding the second target is meant to catch cancer cells that stop expressing BCMA, a known route by which myeloma escapes existing CAR T therapies such as Carvykti.

In the Mass General Brigham account of the study, 12 of the 15 patients, or 80 percent, responded, meaning cancer signs fell by at least half. In eight patients, or 53 percent, tests found no signs of active myeloma. Nine of the 15 had already received a BCMA directed CAR T therapy and then progressed. The account reported no severe cases of cytokine release syndrome or neurotoxicity, and said TriPRIL cells remained detectable in some patients beyond six months.

The limits are plain. Fifteen patients, a single program, and short follow up cannot establish durability or uncommon side effects. A Phase 2 trial with more patients and longer follow up is planned, and that study, not this one, will decide whether dual targeting is a real advance or an early signal.
