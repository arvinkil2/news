A new round of negotiations between Lebanon and the International Monetary Fund began at the Finance Ministry in Beirut on Tuesday, and Finance Minister Yassine Jaber said the government hopes to reach a preliminary agreement by Friday that would pave the way to an IMF programme. Jaber said passing the draft financial gap law, still before Parliament, is essential to unlocking a programme, and that the state and the central bank would shoulder losses first, then commercial banks, with depositors bearing losses only as a last resort.

The IMF has said a new agreement requires continued banking and economic reforms, a 2027 budget and a medium term fiscal framework consistent with debt sustainability. The talks are the latest attempt to resolve a crisis that began in 2019 and has left depositors unable to reach their savings; Jaber said the legislative process should be completed no later than March 2027, with the final decision resting with Parliament.
