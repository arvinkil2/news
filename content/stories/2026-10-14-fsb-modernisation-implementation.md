The Financial Stability Board published two reports on Wednesday, ahead of the G20 finance ministers' meeting on October 15. The first is a consultation on eight high level principles intended to help authorities make regulatory and supervisory frameworks more effective, efficient, risk focused, coherent and forward looking. The principles rest on four shared premises, including that financial stability underpins sustainable growth, that members remain committed to internationally agreed standards, and that modernisation should preserve or enhance existing resilience. Comments are due by January 8, 2027, with a final report in the first half of 2027.

The second report is the 2026 G20 Implementation Monitoring Review, the follow up to 2025 findings of a worrisome slowdown in implementing agreed reforms. It finds that national circumstances, including legal frameworks, market structure and political capacity, are the main reason implementation has been uneven, while in a number of cases the FSB's own policy development process, including insufficient attention to implementation difficulty, contributed.

The review is led by Randal Quarles, the former FSB chair, at the request of the South African G20 presidency, which asked the board to examine how it promotes and monitors implementation of G20 financial sector reforms.

Read together with Monday's stablecoin monitoring note and Tuesday's Global Financial Stability Report from the IMF, the FSB package completes the official sector's Bangkok week message: the standards are largely written, the vulnerability is in uneven application, and sovereign and funding market stress would expose the gaps first.
