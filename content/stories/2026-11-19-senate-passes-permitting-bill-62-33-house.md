The Senate passed the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act on Thursday night by 62 votes to 33, The Hill reported, a bipartisan permitting overhaul spearheaded by Senators Shelley Moore Capito and Mike Lee with Martin Heinrich and Sheldon Whitehouse, and backed by the White House. Only 15 Democrats and caucus members voted in favour, and the only Republican opponents named were Senators Mike Rounds and Josh Hawley. The bill would let federal regulators approve power lines when states do not act, protect existing energy permits from presidential revocation, exempt some activities including natural gas pipeline expansions from environmental review, require project lawsuits to be filed within 150 days of approval, and make large data centres pay for costs stemming from their high electricity demand. It now heads to the House, where significant bipartisan support is expected but Democrats are split and the lame duck calendar is crowded.
