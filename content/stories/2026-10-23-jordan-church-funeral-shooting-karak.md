**Latest:** The Royal Jordanian Air Force struck what Amman called the hideouts of terrorist organisations in southern Syria on Monday, in an operation the Jordan Armed Forces named Jordanian Deterrence, Hmoud, the Jordan News Agency (Petra) reported. The armed forces said the operation was conducted in coordination with the Syrian government and in retaliation for the martyrs of Friday's Hmoud church attack, that the terrorists behind that attack have ties to the organisations targeted, and that fighter jets destroyed a number of locations, killing and wounding several of their members and destroying their equipment; it named no organisation and gave no locations or counts. Syrian state media said five civilians were killed in the strikes, Arab News reported with Reuters, while Syria's state agency SANA reported explosions near Nasib and Al Muzayrib in the southern province of Deraa and Syrian state television reported civilian victims south of the village of Al Musaymiyah, El Pais reported in Spanish. The local outlet Suwayda24, cited by El Pais, said Jordanian jets also bombed positions at Tal Dhalfa, northeast of Suwayda province, an area it described as a former Islamic State bastion. The strikes came hours after Jordanian authorities identified the three men arrested at dawn over the church attack and, in the Public Security director's account carried by El Pais, described that attack as unplanned, carried out spontaneously by three men who share an extremist ideology when they found themselves near the funeral procession.

**Latest:** Jordanian authorities on Monday identified the three men arrested at dawn over Friday's church attack in Karak as members of the same family, two brothers and a third man who is their relative by marriage, AFP reported via Arab News. The Director of Public Security, at a press conference, presented the three as the perpetrators and named them as Hazza Zaidan Mohammed, Meshal Zaidan Mohammed and Ahmad Sami Hussein. The report did not assign the family roles to individual names, and it stated no charges, no motive and no affiliation with any group.

**Latest:** Jordanian security forces arrested three suspects in Friday's church attack on Monday after locating them at an abandoned house in the village of Al-Jadeeda in Karak, the Public Security Directorate said in a statement published on state media and reported by Al Jazeera. The suspects refused to surrender and attacked the security forces, who returned fire and arrested all three after wounding them, in fighting that lasted about three hours, a government source told Al Jazeera; one suspect was in critical condition, no security personnel were hurt, and a search of the site yielded firearms and live ammunition, the Petra news agency reported. Government Communication Minister Mohammad Momani commended what he called a successful joint operation, the directorate said terrorism has no place in Jordan and named no affiliated group, and King Abdullah II said those behind the attack would face the harshest penalties and ordered a review of the country's security procedures for responding to attacks. No names or charges were given.

**Latest:** The toll from Friday's attack on the Greek Orthodox church in Hammoud, south of Karak, rose to nine, Jordan's Ministry of Health said in updated figures reported by TASS. The ministry gave no further detail in that report, and the wounded and manhunt figures it carried, 27 still in hospital with two in critical condition and the attackers not yet detained, match those already reported here.

**Latest:** The toll from Friday's attack on the Greek Orthodox church in Hammoud, south of Karak, rose to eight after a woman wounded in the shooting died in hospital, TASS reported, citing Jordan's Ministry of Health. Health Minister Ibrahim Al-Budour said 27 of the wounded remain in hospital, eight of them transferred to the Prince Ali bin Hussein Military Hospital, with two in critical condition, and Al-Mamlaka reported that the attackers had not yet been detained, with the search continuing and security measures strengthened in Amman and other cities. Jordan has decreed three days of national mourning, Prensa Latina reported, with Prime Minister Jaafar Hassan ordering flags at half-mast at ministries, government departments and public institutions.

Health Minister Ibrahim Al-Budour put the wounded at 40, with two in critical condition, Reuters reported via Arab News on Saturday, up from the 34 injured officials reported on Friday, and security has been stepped up at malls, foreign embassies and other locations across Jordan. The US Embassy in Amman said it was closely monitoring what it called the October 23 terror attack on the Greek Orthodox church in Hammoud, reminded US citizens of the continued need for caution, and urged them to stay alert at locations publicly associated with the United States.

At a joint press conference on Friday evening at the Karak Governorate headquarters, Public Security Directorate Director Maj. Gen. Obaidullah Al-Maaytah said seven people had been killed and 34 injured in the attack on the Greek Orthodox Church in Humud village, with 27 of the wounded still receiving treatment in hospital, and pledged that those responsible would be arrested soon and brought to justice. Interior Minister Mazen Al-Faraya, who briefed alongside him, said the country was facing a terrorist operation and that the cowardly attack would only strengthen Jordanians' resolve to reject violence and extremism in all its forms. King Abdullah II had earlier posted on X that the blood of Jordanians is precious and that Jordan would strike with an iron fist against anyone who overreaches against the country. No group had claimed responsibility and officials named no group.

Earlier in the day, Jordan's Public Security Directorate had put the toll at six people killed and 28 injured, Deutsche Welle reported, after state television Al-Mamlaka had initially reported seven killed and 10 injured. Between 150 and 200 mourners were at the Greek Orthodox Church of Saint George in the village of Hmoud, south of Karak city, for a funeral that had begun about 30 minutes earlier when the two gunmen arrived by car and opened fire, one with a rifle and one with a pistol, and the attackers fled as security forces deployed and began a manhunt. Government spokesman Mohammad al-Momani called the shooting a heinous attack, King Abdullah II issued a statement on the attack, and Prime Minister Jafar Hassan was to chair a Security Council meeting with all ministers, while no group had claimed responsibility.

At least seven people were killed and 10 others injured on Friday when gunmen opened fire during a funeral at a church in Jordan's Karak governorate, the state-owned television station Al-Mamlaka reported, via Reuters. Two gunmen got out of a vehicle and opened fire on a funeral procession at the Hamoud church in Karak, one carrying a rifle and the other a pistol, a man attending the funeral told the station, and security forces deployed at the church and were pursuing the suspects. There was no immediate claim of responsibility and Jordanian authorities did not say who was responsible, in a country where such attacks are extremely rare.
