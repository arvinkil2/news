**Latest:** Jordan's Public Security Directorate revised the toll to six people killed and 28 injured, Deutsche Welle reported, after state television Al-Mamlaka had initially reported seven killed and 10 injured. Between 150 and 200 mourners were at the Greek Orthodox Church of Saint George in the village of Hmoud, south of Karak city, for a funeral that had begun about 30 minutes earlier when the two gunmen arrived by car and opened fire, one with a rifle and one with a pistol, and the attackers fled as security forces deployed and began a manhunt. Government spokesman Mohammad al-Momani called the shooting a heinous attack, King Abdullah II issued a statement on the attack, and Prime Minister Jafar Hassan was to chair a Security Council meeting with all ministers, while no group had claimed responsibility.

At least seven people were killed and 10 others injured on Friday when gunmen opened fire during a funeral at a church in Jordan's Karak governorate, the state-owned television station Al-Mamlaka reported, via Reuters. Two gunmen got out of a vehicle and opened fire on a funeral procession at the Hamoud church in Karak, one carrying a rifle and the other a pistol, a man attending the funeral told the station, and security forces deployed at the church and were pursuing the suspects. There was no immediate claim of responsibility and Jordanian authorities did not say who was responsible, in a country where such attacks are extremely rare.
