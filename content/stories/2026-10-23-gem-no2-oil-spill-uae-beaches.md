## Latest

European Union Copernicus satellite images dated October 24 show the spill has widened over the past few days and is reaching close to the shores of Umm Al Quwain, with dark layers of oil spreading at sea and along its coastline, Reuters reported on Monday. The Ministry of Climate Change and Environment said on Friday that containment work was under way and an environmental assessment was being conducted. Authorities have still not named the tanker or said what caused the spill. Experts told Reuters the oil is coming from the Gem No.2, a tanker shipping sources said was struck by an unidentified projectile last month, causing a fire that was later put out. Reuters also reported data compiled by the Washington Institute for Near East Policy showing October has been the worst month for attacks on commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and nearby waterways since the start of the US-Israeli war with Iran.

Ras Al-Khaimah's Public Services Department said on Saturday that all beaches in the emirate would be closed until further notice, in a statement shared by Ras Al-Khaimah Police General Headquarters, Reuters reported. No reason was given for the closure, which came a day after the offshore oil leak prompted beach closures in neighbouring Umm Al-Quwain and Sharjah.

UAE authorities said on Thursday that the spill is affecting beaches near Umm Al-Quwain, and the Ministry of Climate Change and Environment said containment work began immediately, with all beaches and coastal areas around the emirate closed until technical teams complete their assessments, Reuters reported; Sharjah also closed Al Hamriyah Beach as a precaution. The UAE has not named the tanker or the cause, but BBC Verify matched satellite imagery of the leaking ship to the Panama-flagged crude tanker Gem No.2, which maritime firm Vanguard reported was struck by an unknown projectile on October 9 just north of Al Jazeera Al Hamra, and analysts cited by Reuters put the slick at about 160 square kilometres. A smaller leak was first seen on October 19 and had spread more than 35 kilometres from the tanker by Wednesday, BBC Verify said.
