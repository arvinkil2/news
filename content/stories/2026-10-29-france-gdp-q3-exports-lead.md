France's economy grew 1.2 percent in volume in the third quarter of 2026, after 0.4 percent in the second quarter and 0.1 percent in the first, INSEE said in its first estimate. Foreign trade did the work: exports rose 3.5 percent after 0.9 percent in the second quarter, imports fell 0.8 percent, and net foreign trade contributed 1.2 points to quarterly growth, with inventories adding a further 0.8 points.

Domestic demand was quieter. Household consumption rose 0.3 percent, total gross fixed capital formation was flat (up 0.1 percent excluding EU emissions allowance sales), household investment fell 1.3 percent and public administration investment fell 1.9 percent. INSEE puts growth acquired for 2026 at 1.3 percent on the third quarter figures.
