US Treasury yields steadied on Monday after Friday's sharp rally, with the front and belly of the curve giving back a little ground and the long end holding its gains. The 5 year yield rose 2.7 basis points to 4.73 percent and the 10 year rose 0.6 basis points to 4.92 percent, while the 30 year eased 0.9 basis points to 5.29 percent.

The weekly picture remains a large move lower. The 5 year is down 20.7 basis points on the week, the 10 year down 20.3 and the 30 year down 17.3, a rally yesterday's edition attributed to growth fears driving a bid for duration even as markets price rate hikes at several December central bank meetings.

The dollar softened slightly alongside the steady yields, with the dollar index down 0.21 percent at 104.1. A flat dollar and a flat long end together suggest Monday was consolidation rather than a reversal: investors kept Friday's positioning and waited.

What comes next is the calendar. The Central Bank of Nigeria decides today, the Bank of Korea on November 26, and the December run of the Federal Reserve, the Bank of Canada, the SNB, the ECB, the Bank of England and the Bank of Japan follows. Whether yields hold near these levels depends less on Monday's price action than on whether those meetings validate the hike pricing now embedded in the curve.
