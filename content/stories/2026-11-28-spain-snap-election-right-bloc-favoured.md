Spain votes on Sunday, November 29, in a snap general election called by Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez after Congress voted down his housing plans on October 2, when the Catalan party Junts withdrew its support. The vote was originally due in 2027. Sanchez, in office since 2018 and governing without a budget of his own since 2023, closed his campaign saying he can beat the polls, as he did in 2023.

The final polls, published before the five-day blackout on new surveys, put the right ahead in nearly every case. Poll averages place the People's Party of Alberto Nunez Feijoo at about 31.5 percent, the Socialists at about 29 to 30 percent and Vox at about 18 percent. Seat projections for the 350-seat Congress mostly give the PP and Vox together between about 190 and 200 seats, comfortably past the 176 needed to govern, though the 40dB tracking poll for El Pais and Cadena SER, the closest of the major surveys, still produced about 192 seats for the right on November 16.

The race has narrowed. When the election was called on October 6, the PP led by about six points; by the final polls the gap was under two and a half points, and the Socialists passed 30 percent in the 40dB poll for the first time since 2023. Two pollsters, SW Demoscopia and Cluster17, put the Socialists first, as did the state-run CIS, which most averages exclude and which projected 134 to 143 Socialist seats against 155 to 171 for the PP and Vox combined.

The campaign was dominated by housing affordability, immigration and corruption, and by the train crash near Urda on October 28 that killed 69 people and forced Transport Minister Oscar Puente to resign. The decisive question on Sunday night is not only which bloc wins but the margin: at the lower end of the projections the PP majority is thin and Vox, set to roughly double its 33 seats from 2023, holds the balance. Sanchez remains acting prime minister until Congress confirms a successor. Status: election scheduled November 29, 2026; campaigning closed, no voting yet.
