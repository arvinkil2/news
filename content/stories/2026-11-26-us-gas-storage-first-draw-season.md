US natural gas storage recorded its first withdrawal of the heating season in the week ended November 20. Working gas in storage fell by 24 billion cubic feet to 3,858 Bcf, according to the Energy Information Administration's weekly report, released on Wednesday because of the Thanksgiving schedule. Stocks were 79 Bcf below last year at this time and 80 Bcf above the five-year average of 3,778 Bcf, and total working gas remains within the five-year historical range.

The draw was in line with expectations. It matched the Bloomberg survey estimate of 24 Bcf and the five-year average draw for the week, leaving the surplus to the average unchanged at 80 Bcf, after three straight weeks of smaller than expected builds. Natural Gas Intelligence described the withdrawal as steeper than the 19 Bcf draw analysts it cited had expected, and said near-record LNG demand caught buyers' attention on Wednesday, offsetting high production and a milder weather outlook.

Supply remains ample. US output was revised to 112.3 Bcf a day and printed at 113 Bcf a day in the latest estimates, while LNG feedgas nominations stood at 21.7 Bcf a day, with Golden Pass back up to 0.9 Bcf a day and Sabine Pass above 5.3 Bcf a day. Exports to Mexico were 6.8 Bcf a day, against a 6.5 Bcf a day average over the past week.

The storage report landed on the December contract's expiry day. The December NYMEX contract closed at 3.323 dollars per mmBtu on Tuesday, up 13.9 cents from Monday, and January is now the front month. The estate market table puts natural gas at 3.62 dollars, up 10.68 percent at the previous close, a jump that largely reflects the roll from the expiring December contract to the higher priced January contract rather than a single session's spot move. Weather models took some demand out of the forecast overnight, with cooler than normal temperatures in the East and South Central set to give way to a warmer than normal pattern over the next couple of weeks, and Bank of America raised its end of March storage estimate to 2.128 trillion cubic feet.
