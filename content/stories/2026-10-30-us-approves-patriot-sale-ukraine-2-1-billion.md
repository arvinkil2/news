**Latest, October 30, evening:** President Volodymyr Zelenskyy welcomed the State Department decision as a strong one and said the 500 PAC-3 interceptors could form an effective defensive package this winter, but that it has been determined deliveries under the sale may begin only in a few years. He said Kyiv had asked the US side for a winter support package calculated at 100 Patriot interceptors a month, and that Russian forces carried out more than 30 strikes on key elements of Ukraine’s energy network in the last two weeks of October alone, against electricity, heat and water facilities and bridges. His proposal was that partners hand Ukraine interceptors from their own warehouse stocks now, in winter 2026 to 2027, and replenish their arsenals in later years from the production that Ukraine and its partners have contracted and paid for. He thanked President Donald Trump and Secretary of State Marco Rubio, with whom he said he had recently discussed PAC-3 interceptors.

The US State Department said on Friday it had approved the sale of 500 Patriot PAC-3 missiles to Ukraine for an estimated $2.1 billion. Ukraine will pay for the interceptors with contributions from Canada, Germany and Norway routed through the US JUMPSTART program, along with national funds financed by the European Union's Ukraine Support Loan, and Lockheed Martin is the principal contractor. The approval follows President Volodymyr Zelenskyy's statement earlier this week that Ukraine had been waiting for almost a month for the US to sign off on critical missile supplies as Russia stepped up attacks on energy and civilian infrastructure ahead of winter.
