**Latest (Monday 05:43 ET):** In Irpin, northwest of Kyiv, the number injured in the overnight strike rose to 17, including eight children, and 84 objects were damaged, including 70 private houses, four apartment blocks, six cars and production and warehouse premises, Kyiv region governor Timur Tkachenko said, NV reported; he had earlier reported six injured and three people missing. In Kyiv itself one person was killed and eight were injured, with destruction recorded in three districts, NV reported. President Zelenskyy said Russia struck Kyiv and the region with 16 ballistic missiles in several waves and that partners again gave no warning of the strike; the Air Force said Russia attacked with Iskander-M and S-400 ballistic missiles, Iskander-K cruise missiles and 269 drones, and that air defences shot down four Iskander-K missiles and 243 drones. Air Force spokesman Yuriy Ihnat said no partner warning arrived for the first strike at about 23:00 on Sunday, while a warning for the repeat launches at about 03:00 left about a minute. Ukrenergo said new blackouts were recorded in ten regions, Volyn, Zhytomyr, Rivne, Ternopil, Khmelnytsky, Vinnytsia, Kirovohrad, Kharkiv, Odesa and Mykolaiv, with hourly cut schedules and power limits of up to two queues in some regions to the end of the day, Espreso reported.

**Latest (Monday 00:48 ET):** Kyiv Metro said the surface stations Chernihivska and Lisova are temporarily closed to entry and exit and that morning trains on the red line will run only between Akademmistechko and Darnytsia while repair crews work on the damaged tracks and networks, Ukrainska Pravda reported. The city administration added buses on routes 1-M and 118 to cover the closed stations, TSN reported. Mayor Klitschko later reported that three people were injured where debris hit the 25 storey residential building in the Solomianskyi district and were treated on site, NV's live blog reported.

Russia attacked Kyiv with missiles in the night of October 19, damaging a metro station on the left bank of the capital, where debris ruptured the running and contact rails beside the station, Mayor Vitaliy Klitschko said, Ukrainska Pravda reported. Debris also hit the first floor of a 25 storey residential building and a non-residential building at another address in the Solomianskyi district, and a hit was recorded on non-residential grounds in the Dniprovskyi district. The Air Force had first announced a threat of ballistic weapons from the east and then warned of missiles moving toward the capital.
