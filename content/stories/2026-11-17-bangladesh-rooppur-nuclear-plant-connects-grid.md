Bangladesh connected the Rooppur nuclear power plant to the national grid for the first time on Tuesday, in a trial run supplying 300 to 350 megawatts while engineers monitored turbine vibration and grid frequency. The 2.4 gigawatt plant, built by Russia's Rosatom on the banks of the Padma River about 160 kilometres from Dhaka, has two 1.2 gigawatt reactors, the first scheduled to be fully operational in 2027 and the second in 2028.

Project director Dr. Kabir Hossain called the connection a major breakthrough and said regular transmission is expected early next year, with no date fixed yet. At full output the plant is projected to meet about 11 percent of Bangladesh's electricity demand, in a country of 170 million people that draws about half its power from gas and has faced chronic shortages through the Middle East fuel crisis.
