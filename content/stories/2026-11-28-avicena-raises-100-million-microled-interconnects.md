Avicena, a Sunnyvale, California startup, announced on November 24 that it has raised more than 100 million dollars in Series C funding to accelerate commercialization of its LightBundle platform for AI infrastructure. InterVest led the round, with participation from Tiger Global, Cerberus Capital Management, SK hynix Ventures, Samsung Catalyst Fund, AMD Ventures, Prosperity7 Ventures, Diamond Edge Ventures and Hitachi Ventures.

LightBundle replaces the lasers used in conventional optical links with dense arrays of microLED transmitters paired with silicon photodetectors and multicore fiber, spreading bandwidth across many slower channels to cut power and complexity on the short links between chips inside AI systems. Avicena began shipping 1 terabit per second LightBundle evaluation kits in August 2026 and showed a connectorized version at the ECOC 2026 conference in September. It expects its first product to reach the market in 2027, and Kevin Pope will succeed Marco Chisari as chief executive for the commercialization phase.

The investor list is the story as much as the amount. SK hynix Ventures' Rene Do said energy-efficient optical interconnects are becoming important to next-generation memory architectures and called LightBundle a promising approach for future memory fabrics, which places the technology in the contest to connect HBM stacks to accelerators as copper reaches its limits.

The round more than doubles Avicena's 65 million dollar Series B of May 2025 and lands in a crowded field: Ayar Labs raised 500 million dollars in March 2026 for optical chip-to-chip links. Avicena has evaluation kits in customers' hands but has not yet reported production orders, so the Series C buys time to convert trials into design wins before the larger rivals set the standard.
