India's headline consumer inflation rose to 5.54% year on year in September from 4.82% in August, according to CNBC reporting Monday, beating the 5.40% median forecast in a Reuters poll of economists and extending the run of monthly increases to eleven, the highest reading in 22 months. The print keeps inflation above the Reserve Bank of India's 4% medium-term target for a fourth consecutive month and comes just five days after the central bank raised its repo rate to 5.50% on October 7, its first increase since February 2023, with a shift in stance from neutral to calibrated tightening.

The drivers are the ones the RBI has been warning about. Energy costs are spilling beyond transport into the rest of the consumer basket, ANZ economist Dhiraj Nim told Reuters, with India's crude import basket averaging $116.09 a barrel in September and above $120 so far in October. Food prices stayed elevated after the weakest June-to-September monsoon rainfall since 2001, and the RBI itself has flagged that headline inflation will average close to 5.8% over the next three quarters, with core at 4.4% for the fiscal year ending March 2027.

Policy expectations hardened immediately. HSBC said in an October 7 report that it expects another RBI rate hike in December, and the central bank has said further cuts are off the table in the near term, leaving a hike or a pause as the only options. The September print arrives in the same week the RBI is draining liquidity through a Rs 2,00,000 crore reverse repo and a Rs 25,000 crore bond sale, and one day after it activated the special dollar window for oil marketing companies that helped the rupee open about 1% stronger on Monday.

The combination is the classic emerging-market bind: domestic inflation rising, the currency under pressure, and global yields at two-decade highs compressing the room for maneuver. With the RBI's own inflation forecast for the fiscal year at 5.2%, well above target, the question for the December meeting is no longer whether tightening continues but how fast.
