**Latest.** On the earnings call, Intrator addressed the bond market directly, Datacenter Dynamics reported. He put the company's average cost of debt at 7.9 percent, against the yields above 15 percent that some of its bonds have traded at, and said: "Looking ahead, we will not use the unsecured debt market to fund our business at current yields, nor do we need to." Remaining capital spending is to be financed mostly through delayed-draw term loans matched to specific customer contracts, which can fund 70 to 100 percent of the associated spending; more than 75 percent of committed capital spending through 2027 is tied to investment-grade and enterprise customers, and the company says it has paid down more than $4 billion of debt from contract cash flows. Third-quarter capital spending was $14.5 billion, above the high end of the guided range because deliveries were pulled forward from the fourth quarter, while construction in progress fell from $11.9 billion to $9.3 billion and more than 850 megawatts of power came online in the first three quarters. Adjusted operating income of $379 million beat the $200 million to $260 million guidance range. Agrawal noted that more than half of the $132.5 billion backlog is attached to contracts where delivery is expected to commence by the end of this year. Older Ampere and Hopper generation GPUs are being repriced 30 to 50 percent higher quarter over quarter on strong demand, and more than 130,000 GPUs come off contract in 2027 and about 170,000 in 2028, with a pipeline of customers competing for the capacity. The company also said its Omni product, which runs the CoreWeave stack inside customers' own data centres, has signed its first customer.

**Latest.** Chief executive Michael Intrator told CNBC on Wednesday that he has sidelined his 10b5-1 share-sale plan until there is a material change in the company's equity pricing, with any further selling limited to tax purposes. Under the plan, adopted in November 2025, he had sold 8.26 million shares at an average of $96.14, for $794.2 million, sales that had drawn criticism as the stock fell; the shares rallied sharply on Wednesday after the results. Intrator also said CoreWeave does not anticipate an impact from the regulatory shift that followed the November 3 midterm elections, in which voters backed greater restrictions on data centres in 40 of 41 ballot measures across 10 states, and that the company remains comfortable with its pipeline, still expects 1.85 GW of active power by the end of 2026, and is targeting $37 billion to $39 billion of full-year capital spending.

CoreWeave reported revenue of $3,661 million for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026, against $1,365 million a year earlier, with operating income of $207 million and a net loss of $709 million after $846 million of net interest expense. Adjusted EBITDA was $2,256 million, a 62 percent margin, and the company put its revenue backlog at approximately $132.5 billion as of September 30.

The release pointed to about $9 billion in customer prepayments and deposits expected in the next three months, a $4.2 billion upsized convertible senior notes offering, and the close of the $2.6 billion DDTL 5.5 facility, its first financing designed for shorter-duration customer contracts. Active power passed 1.7 GW after more than 200 MW was added in the quarter, and contracted power rose above 4.6 GW.

CoreWeave raised full-year 2026 revenue guidance to $13.0 billion to $13.3 billion and adjusted operating income guidance to $1.275 billion to $1.375 billion, a 26 percent increase at the midpoint, while lifting expected 2026 capital expenditures to $37 billion to $39 billion.
