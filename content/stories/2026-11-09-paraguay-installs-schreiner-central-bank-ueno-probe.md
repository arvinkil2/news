## Latest

Fitch placed Paraguay's BB+ sovereign rating on negative watch on Wednesday, citing fallout from the collapse of Ueno and the subsequent political upheaval, and flagging potential spillovers to the banking system, government finances, economic growth and policy continuity given the public and private deposits held with the bank. Ueno, the country's sixth-largest lender, held roughly $850 million in public funds as of June, including state pension money. Fitch said it does not presently anticipate broader financial stability risks, given the sound solvency and liquidity positions of other systemically important banks, while warning of downside risks to its 4.5 percent growth forecast for 2026.

---

Paraguay's Congress on Monday approved President Pedro Alliana's nomination of economist Jorge Schreiner to head the central bank, replacing Carlos Carvallo, and named Fernando Rivarola, the former banking regulator whose July report on Ueno helped trigger the crisis, to the bank's board. Schreiner previously served on the central bank's board and led the country's securities commission for a decade until 2013.

The same day, prosecutors raided Ueno's brokerage arm and released images of ripped-up documents recovered in the search, including fragments of apparent due diligence reports. Former president Santiago Peña and Carvallo, who both had business ties to Ueno, have been charged over alleged breach of trust and placed in pretrial detention, and both have denied wrongdoing.
