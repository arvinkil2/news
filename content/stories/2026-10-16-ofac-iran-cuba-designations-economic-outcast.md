The Treasury Department moved against Iran's money networks on Thursday, publishing designations that target religious charity structures the US says the regime abuses to move funds, and the shadow banking architecture that settles Iranian commerce outside the formal system. A Cuba designation and a set of new and amended general licences and FAQs were published in the same OFAC release.

The targeting choice is the strategy. Rather than adding tanker names to a list the market has learned to route around, Operation Economic Outcast goes after the intermediaries: front charities, exchange houses and the bankers who give Iranian trade its financial rails. Each designation forces every bank and insurer touching the region to re-screen its book, which is how sanctions raise costs beyond the named parties.

The general licences matter as much as the designations: they define what remains permitted, including humanitarian channels, and they are where compliance teams will spend the weekend. For energy markets, the package is a slow tightening story, not a cargo story, but it lands in the same week that tanker rates and shadow fleet policy are already moving, as today's commodities edition records.
