Ramon.Space, which builds computing systems designed to operate on board satellites, has raised 124 million dollars in a Series B round led by growth equity firm PSG. New investors Micron Technology, Senvest and Phoenix Financial joined the round, alongside existing investors StageOne, Deep Insight and Grove Ventures. CTech and Globes put the company's total raised to date at about 180 million dollars.

The company says the money will scale production and accelerate deployment of its onboard computing systems, which are built to withstand radiation, extreme temperatures and tight power budgets in orbit. Its systems already support commercial and government Earth observation and communications customers, including Eutelsat OneWeb and SES Space & Defence, according to Datacenter Dynamics.

Chief executive Avi Shabtai said the company has proven that Earth-grade computing works in space and is delivering it today, and that customer demand is growing quickly. PSG managing director Ronen Nir said the ability to process and manage data in orbit is becoming essential to the growth of the space economy.

The round lands in the middle of a wider push to move computing off the ground, including SpaceX's proposed orbital data center constellation authorized by the FCC earlier this month. Ramon.Space's position is narrower and nearer term: it sells the processing hardware that flies on other operators' satellites, rather than operating a constellation of its own, so its revenue test is satellite makers' order books rather than the economics of orbital data centers themselves.
