NexGen (TSX: NXE) has expanded the high grade zone at its PCE uranium discovery, with new drilling extending mineralization in both directions. The campaign has increased the vertical extent of the high grade subdomain by 15 percent and the strike length by 13 percent, with new intersections to both the southwest and northeast.

Highlights included hole RK-26-300, which intersected 10.0 metres of more than 10,000 counts per second, including 1.8 metres of more than 61,000 counts per second. Counts per second are scintillometer readings indicating radioactivity, not assayed uranium grades. To date, the 2026 drilling campaign has increased the overall mineralized footprint vertically by 205 metres to 905 metres, a 29 percent increase, and along strike by 58 metres to 678 metres, a 9 percent increase.

The PCE discovery is located about 3.5 kilometres from the company's Arrow deposit, which hosts NexGen's wholly owned Rook I project, one of the world's largest high grade uranium deposits. Founder and CEO Leigh Curyer said the high grades at PCE continue to grow and show strong continuity, both characteristics of the Arrow deposit during its development. NexGen shares rose 0.62 percent to $9.81 on Tuesday afternoon in Toronto.
