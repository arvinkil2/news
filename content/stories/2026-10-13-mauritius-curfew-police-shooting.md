**Latest (Wednesday, October 14):** Police said the situation was under control after the curfew lifted at 6 a.m. local time, while reporting some incidents overnight in several areas and arrests already made. By shortly before 11 a.m. local time, police had reported 93 arrests in connection with the unrest, including 15 minors, with 43 people remaining in custody and due to appear in district courts on Wednesday; the curfew had covered 15 areas, including Port Louis, Beau-Bassin and Rose-Hill, Quatre-Bornes and Curepipe. Schools remained closed for a second day, except for students sitting international examinations, and Business Mauritius advised members to resume normal activities after checking local security conditions. The cabinet was meeting later on Wednesday to determine whether further action is needed.

Prime Minister Navin Ramgoolam announced the curfew in an address to the nation on Tuesday, covering Port Louis and major population centres, after a night of unrest that closed some schools. The protests followed the shooting of Elodie Thomas, 27, who was wounded while travelling home from work in a van in the south of the capital and underwent successful surgery to remove a bullet.

Local media reported that a police sergeant has been charged in connection with the shooting, and police said an investigation is under way, according to Reuters. The prime minister's office said the situation had returned to normal and the public sector would operate as usual, with the cabinet set to meet on Wednesday.
