**Latest, November 2:** Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei said on Monday that Iran was considering reopening the Strait of Hormuz within two days if its demands are met, Reuters reported, presenting the shorter timeline, down from a previously proposed one week, as a good-faith signal and a way to restore security to the waterway and resume negotiations. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said on Sunday that Iranian and Qatari negotiators had developed a proposal to reopen the strait within two days of a potential agreement with Washington, with no final conclusion reached, and Iranian negotiators left Doha after three days of consultations with Qatari mediators; an official briefed on the talks told Reuters the departure was not because of difficulties in the talks. The report did not specify Iran's demands, and no agreement between Washington and Tehran was announced.

**Latest, November 1 (evening):** Speaking at a campaign rally in Dubuque, Iowa, Trump renewed his threat of strikes on Iran, saying he had held back to try to save lives but that at some point the United States would have to give Iran "the hammer", AFP reported. He also said Washington still had one or two remaining targets in Iran, among them the suspected Pickaxe Mountain underground nuclear complex, that could be struck within minutes. Iranian army spokesman Mohammad Akraminia warned that another war could last longer than the previous one and that Washington would not dictate its timing, and Revolutionary Guards spokesman Hossein Mohebbi said a confrontation would have no geographical limitations; UAE presidential adviser Anwar Gargash told a conference in Manama the Emirates hoped diplomacy would break through but was braced for the worst.

**Earlier, November 1:** Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Trump has not made a decision on resuming strikes, has great confidence the United States could act militarily and succeed if needed, and would prefer to end the war peacefully, CNBC reported. Trump told Right Side Broadcasting Network on Sunday that the United States would settle the matter very soon one way or the other, saying either Iran makes a deal or the United States ends it. CNBC also reported that Iran's foreign ministry on Sunday designated the United Kingdom's military intelligence services a hostile group, in response to Britain's designation of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, and that reports in the Wall Street Journal and the New York Times say the United States is preparing for possible renewed military action after the November 3 midterm elections.

President Donald Trump held a candid phone call with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu on Saturday about US negotiations with Iran and how quickly to return to combat, Axios reported, citing one Israeli and one US source. The sources said Netanyahu is pressing Trump to strike Iran immediately after the midterm elections on Tuesday, while Trump for now wants to let negotiations play out; the Israeli source called the call “tough” and the US source called it “direct”. The White House and Netanyahu’s office declined to comment.

The call sits against live diplomacy. Qatari mediators met Iranian officials on Sunday for the third consecutive day, and Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said there is no agreement yet. Last week Vice President Vance sent a letter to Iran’s national security chief Mohsen Rezaei about restarting negotiations, the most senior US outreach to Tehran in several months, and Trump told reporters on Saturday that negotiations with Iran are “going fine” and that he is in no rush.
