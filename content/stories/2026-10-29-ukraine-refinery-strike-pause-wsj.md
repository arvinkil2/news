Ukraine's military has paused its strikes on Russian oil refineries after pressure from President Donald Trump, the Wall Street Journal reported on Thursday, citing people familiar with the operations. Ukrainian units carrying out long-range strikes were told in mid-October that the campaign against refineries was effectively on hold once missions already in motion were completed, and the unofficial pause is expected to last until after the United States midterm elections and potentially deeper into November, the Journal reported. Reuters said it could not immediately verify the report.

The reported pause came on a day when Russian drone and missile attacks killed at least 20 people across Ukraine, according to Ukrainian officials. Fourteen were killed at Dnipro railway station, two teenage girls were killed in Chernihiv with 23 people wounded, three people were killed in Kremenchuk with 32 injured, and a late evening missile attack on Kyiv killed one person. President Volodymyr Zelenskiy described the attacks as particularly cruel and deliberate and warned that Ukrainian intelligence had detected preparations for further Russian ballistic missile strikes.
