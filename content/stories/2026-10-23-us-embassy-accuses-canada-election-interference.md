The United States embassy in Canada has accused Canadian politicians of seeking to interfere in the American midterm elections, in a fact sheet released on Wednesday that recasts Canada's pushback against US tariffs as an attempt to sway the November 3 vote. The Canadian government brushes off concerns about potential interference in US elections, the fact sheet said, even as its ministers and premiers openly describe plans to influence American politics in official documents and public appearances. It concluded that all Canadians should refrain from attempting to participate in or influence US elections in any way, adding that Americans decide America's elections.

The document singles out Industry Minister Melanie Joly for August comments on retaliatory tariffs, when she said Canada was targeting products that would hit US states and was being wise and strategic to put political pressure, and for her September meetings with Democratic officials in Boston and Chicago, where she said that was exactly why she was meeting so many governors and potential presidential candidates. It also cites Ontario Premier Doug Ford's calls for retaliation and his government's advertising campaign featuring Ronald Reagan criticising tariffs, the campaign that prompted President Trump to call off trade talks in October 2025. The embassy also cited 11 Canadians charged with illegally voting in US elections between 2012 and 2026, without linking the cases to Canada's government.

Ottawa rejected the framing. Joly said Canada would never meddle in US elections and that her US meetings, with Democrats and Republicans, were about attracting investment and building Canada's economy. Carney said Canada was responding to US tariffs, which have cut Canadian auto exports to the US by more than 10 percent while US exports to Canada have fallen by more than 20 percent, and Ford denied any interest in interfering and said the bilateral relationship has changed permanently.

The analytical caution in the Canadian coverage is worth carrying. McGill political scientist Daniel Beland said the items in the fact sheet are not genuine examples of foreign interference, and warned that accusing Canada ahead of the midterms could pave the way to blame Canada rhetoric after the results are in. Public economic pressure can have electoral consequences; the fact sheet does not present evidence of a covert electoral operation, campaign coordination or an election-law violation, and this story reports the allegation at that weight.
