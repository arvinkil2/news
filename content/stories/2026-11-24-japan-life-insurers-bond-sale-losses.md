Japan's four biggest life insurers booked more than 1.2 trillion yen ($7.5 billion) in losses from selling government and other domestic bonds in the April to September half, Bloomberg reported. The insurers sold debt bought when rates were low in order to reinvest at the higher yields now available.

The rotation matters beyond the headline loss: life insurers are among the largest private holders of Japanese government bonds, and sustained selling of older low-coupon stock to rebuild portfolios at higher yields is one of the forces setting the pace of adjustment at the long end of the curve as yields stay elevated.
