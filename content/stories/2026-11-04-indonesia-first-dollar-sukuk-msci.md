Indonesia's government kicked off a three part dollar sukuk offering on Thursday, the country's first public dollar bond sale since MSCI warned in October that Indonesia's emerging market status was in jeopardy. The three year notes were offered at around 5.7 percent, according to a person familiar with the matter cited by Bloomberg; no total size has been announced.

The MSCI warning has kept Indonesian issuers out of the dollar market for weeks: Bank KB Indonesia postponed a planned dollar sale in late October, citing market volatility, in what would have been the first corporate deal since the warning. A well bid sovereign sukuk would reopen the market for the country's borrowers.
