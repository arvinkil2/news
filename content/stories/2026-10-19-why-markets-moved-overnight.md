Amazon rose 5.10 percent to 262.74 dollars, the largest move in the midday tables. No story in today's reporting explains the rise: the only Amazon mention in today's files is the Linux KVM story on the technology beat, which reported that Amazon Web Services says its cloud is not at risk from the hypervisor flaw, and that is not presented there as a share price catalyst.

Tesla rose 4.00 percent to 368.92 dollars. No story in today's reporting explains that move either: today's only Tesla reference is in the China solar export controls story on the commodities beat, which named Tesla among United States firms whose solar expansion plans the proposed controls could affect, a potential headwind rather than a reason to buy.

The semiconductor index rose 2.89 percent to 507.67 dollars, Broadcom rose 2.73 percent to 337.72 dollars and Nvidia rose 1.84 percent to 218.07 dollars, reversing part of Friday's falls of 4.14 percent for the index and 4.52 percent for Broadcom. Today's Kospi story is the linked reporting: Seoul opened down 3.17 percent but pared the loss to 1.36 percent in early trade as foreign and institutional buyers stepped in, the first Asian read that Friday's selloff was stabilising, and the midday New York figures follow that pattern rather than breaking it. No story in today's files gives a company specific cause for the rebound. The counterweight in today's reporting is the Nscale story on the infra-deals beat, where investors are pressing the Nvidia backed developer to cut its IPO valuation target from about 30 billion dollars to between 16 billion and 19 billion dollars, evidence that private AI infrastructure valuations are still being marked down even as listed chips recover.

WTI crude fell 2.67 percent to 98.70 dollars a barrel, while Brent was nearly flat, down 0.36 percent at 109.46 dollars. Today's oil story on the commodities beat explains the direction: oil slipped from a five month high after President Trump waived Russia sanctions, raising hopes of more Russian fuel supply, and traders took profits, even though Gulf crude flows through Hormuz averaged about 8.5 million barrels a day last week against more than 20 million the week before. The midday split between the two benchmarks, WTI down sharply and Brent steady, is in the tables but is not explained by any story in today's reporting.

Dry bulk freight rose 3.19 percent to 12.95 dollars, the third largest move in the tables, while tanker freight fell 1.79 percent. No story in today's reporting explains either freight move.

The cross-market read is a selective risk-on, confined to the United States technology complex. The Nasdaq 100 rose 1.58 percent and the S and P 500 rose 0.61 percent, but European benchmarks fell, the FTSE 100 down 1.08 percent and the DAX down 1.18 percent, Treasury yields backed up, with the United States 10 year up 4.8 basis points to 5.15 percent, and gold fell 0.45 percent to 4,162.20 dollars with the dollar index nearly flat. A broad relief rally would have lifted Europe and pushed yields and gold the other way; instead, Monday midday looks like buyers returning to the specific names sold hardest on Friday, at yields that remain above 5 percent.
