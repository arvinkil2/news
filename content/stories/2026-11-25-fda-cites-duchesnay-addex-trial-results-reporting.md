The FDA has sent formal Notices of Noncompliance to two drugmakers, Duchesnay Inc. of Canada and Addex Pharma of Switzerland, for failing to submit required summary results to ClinicalTrials.gov. They are the agency's first such notices since August 2025 and, by the count of its public list, only the 10th and 11th it has ever issued.

Duchesnay's notice, dated October 19, covers a trial of Diclegis, its nausea and vomiting of pregnancy drug, in pregnant adolescents (NCT02045901). Addex received two notices dated October 7, covering two Parkinson's disease trials of dipraglurant: a Phase 2b/3 study in levodopa-induced dyskinesia (NCT04857359) and an open-label safety study (NCT05116813). Federal law requires sponsors to submit results within one year of a trial's primary completion date. The FDA said it had sent pre-notices first, to Addex in July 2025 and to Duchesnay in March 2026, and that both companies began entering results in the reporting system without completing the submission, which the agency said does not count.

Each company has 30 days from receipt to submit the results and respond in writing. If a sponsor does not correct the failure, the FDA can pursue enforcement that includes civil money penalties of up to $10,000 per day. Neither company has a response letter on the FDA's list yet.

The amounts at stake for two small companies are modest, but the signal is wider. Results reporting to ClinicalTrials.gov is a legal obligation that much of the industry has historically treated loosely, and the FDA has enforced it only a handful of times. Two notices in one month, following reminder letters sent earlier this year to more than 2,200 companies and researchers, suggest the agency is moving from reminders toward named enforcement, and sponsors with long-finished, unreported trials are the exposed group.
