**Latest, October 24, evening:** US Central Command has plans ready for follow-up strikes on Iran if the White House authorizes them, and its options have been presented to Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, The Hill reported on Saturday, citing two sources aware of the planning who spoke on condition of anonymity. The options are framed for possible use after the November 3 midterm elections. A Pentagon official told The Hill that the department's job is to develop and present military options to the president and that it does not speculate on future operations, and Central Command did not respond to its request for comment.

The timing in the same reporting runs in two directions. Tom Barrack, the US ambassador to Turkiye and President Trump's special envoy for Syria and Iraq, said in an interview published on Friday that a strike this weekend was possible. Asked about those comments on Friday, Mr Trump said he did not know anything about it. On Wednesday he had said the United States is not negotiating with Iran, that something will happen fairly soon one way or the other, and that most people seem to think it is after the election. At a rally in Fort Myers, Florida on Friday he said the United States may do another little Epic Fury, the codename of the war that began on February 28, or Iran would agree to a deal.

In Tehran, President Masoud Pezeshkian blamed Washington for the unravelling of an interim deal aimed at ending the war, in comments broadcast by Press TV on Saturday, and said both sides should return to the deal and conclude it, Al Jazeera reported. The Revolutionary Guards warned that even limited strikes would bring a response several times greater, the semi-official Fars News Agency reported, and government spokesperson Fatemeh Mohajerani said Iran would not wait for permission to defend itself.

Iran's chief of general staff, Ali Abdollahi, said in a statement carried by Iranian state media on Saturday that the United States Navy is incapable of re-entering Iran's territorial waters or their periphery because Iranian forces can target any vessel in the region, Al Jazeera reported. Abdollahi argued that Washington's refusal to bring its aircraft carriers and support vessels closer to Iranian territory undercuts US claims that Iran's military capabilities have mostly been destroyed, and said Iranian forces were determined to mire the United States and Israel in a quagmire in retaliation for the surprise attack on Iran on February 28 that started the war.

The statement followed a meeting in Istanbul on Friday where the foreign ministers of Turkiye, Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Egypt met counterparts from the United Kingdom, France, Germany and Italy, with neither the United States nor Iran present, Al Jazeera reported. Turkiye's Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan said the ministers agreed a new attack on Iran would widen the war and spread conflict across the region, that military intervention in Iran is not a solution and that no one at the meeting was endorsing it. The four regional ministers issued a joint statement supporting a negotiated settlement, demanding unrestricted passage through the Strait of Hormuz, opposing attacks on civilians and commercial vessels, and condemning Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia.
