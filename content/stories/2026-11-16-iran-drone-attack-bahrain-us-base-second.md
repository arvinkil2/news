Iran attacked a US military base in Bahrain with drones early on Sunday, the second Iranian attack on the kingdom in three days, ending a lull in strikes on Gulf states that had held since September. Explosions were heard over Bahrain around 4:00 a.m. local time, with a second series of blasts about two and a half hours later, and authorities issued siren and shelter alerts for the first time since September, AFP reported.

The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said its Aerospace Force targeted the US air defence radar system at the Al-Riffa helicopter base with six drones, in a statement carried by Iranian state broadcasting, and said the attack answered hostile US actions. Bahrain's military said its air defences intercepted several Iranian aerial attacks before they reached their targets, and its foreign ministry called the attack heinous aggression and a flagrant violation of international law, rejecting the use of Bahraini territory to settle scores and holding Iran fully responsible. No damage or casualties from the Sunday attack have been reported by any side, and the US military had not publicly commented on it as of Monday morning.

Iran's foreign ministry spokesperson, Esmaeil Baghaei, defended the strikes as purely defensive under Article 51 of the UN Charter, said they targeted only US bases and assets used to attack Iran, and accused Gulf states of aiding US strikes by hosting the bases and logistics behind them. Jordan, Egypt, Saudi Arabia and Qatar condemned the attacks and expressed solidarity with Bahrain. Sunday's attack followed a first round on Friday, when the Guard claimed it destroyed a US early warning radar in Bahrain and US Central Command said it shot down two drones with no casualties or damage, a claim this site covered on November 12.

The exchange is widening at sea and in Washington's posture. A third US aircraft carrier, USS Theodore Roosevelt, is heading to the Middle East to join two carriers already in the region, according to a US military official cited by the New York Times, and a tanker leaving the Strait of Hormuz was hit by an unidentified projectile on Monday morning, starting an engine room fire, in an incident covered separately in this edition. President Donald Trump said last week that the United States no longer needs a deal with Iran. Iran's parliament speaker, Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, said on Sunday that the path to ending the conflict remains open if Washington accepts Iran's logical conditions, and Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi told a regional ministers' meeting in Islamabad on Monday that Iran remains committed to diplomacy while accusing the United States of choosing aggression and economic pressure instead.
