Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller renewed his call on Wednesday for the Federal Reserve to consider raising its benchmark rate by 50 basis points in December, telling CNBC he is very worried about inflation, which he put at about 3.5 percent annualized over the past three months, well above the 2 percent target, moving in the wrong direction and broadening. Bringing inflation down, he said, will take more aggressive action and may, in his words, require shocking people, and he argued that last year's three insurance rate cuts should above all be reversed.

On the choice before the December meeting, Waller said splitting the move into two 25 basis point hikes would work only if the Fed could credibly signal a further hike in January; if that signal cannot be sent clearly, it should raise by 50 basis points in December and be done with it. Yonhap noted Waller first floated a 50 basis point December hike on November 10, and that New York Fed President John Williams, speaking on Tuesday, said work remains to return inflation to target but drew a line against an extreme measure of that kind, saying one is not needed.
