South Korea's government and the ruling Democratic Party announced a plan to improve financial company governance at a consultation on November 12, under which reappointing a bank holding company chief gets harder with each term. A first reappointment would need at least two thirds of attending voting rights at the shareholders' meeting and at least one third of all issued shares, and a second or further reappointment at least three quarters of attending votes and at least half of all issued shares, while the committee that recommends chief executive candidates would be composed entirely of independent directors and a reappointment recommendation would need three quarters of all its members.

The plan also introduces a clawback of performance pay where intentional or gross negligence causes a loss or the accounts behind the pay are corrected, raises minimum pay deferral to 50 percent over five years, and bars an incumbent chief from attending the committee that recommends independent directors. The Financial Services Commission said it will prepare an amendment bill to the governance law within the year.
