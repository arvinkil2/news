Treasury yields fell across the curve on Wednesday, extending the rally to an eighth consecutive daily decline, even though the morning's data pointed the other way. The 5-year yield fell 5.2 basis points to 4.66 percent, the 10-year fell 5.6 basis points to 4.85 percent, and the 30-year fell 5.7 basis points to 5.24 percent. Tradeweb closing levels carried by Dow Jones put the 10-year at 4.863 percent, its lowest since early September, and the 30-year earlier touched its lowest since the end of August.

The data released at 08:30 gave no support to the rally. The Bureau of Economic Analysis revised third-quarter real GDP growth up to a 4.3 percent annual rate, from a 3.4 percent advance estimate and against consensus near 3.5 percent, and October core PCE inflation rose to 3.3 percent from a year earlier, above the 3.2 percent expected and up from 3.1 percent in September. Headline PCE inflation was 3.8 percent. Futures pricing reported after the releases still put the probability of a 25 basis point rate increase at the December 8 to 9 FOMC meeting at roughly 95 percent or higher.

The bid for bonds came instead from equities. An AI-led selloff pushed the Nasdaq sharply lower intraday on Wednesday before stocks recovered part of the loss, and money moved into government debt as a haven. European yields followed the same pattern: the 10-year Bund yield fell 2.7 basis points to 3.235 percent and the 10-year gilt yield fell 3.1 basis points to 5.157 percent, according to Tradeweb data reported by Dow Jones.

That leaves the market in an unusual position going into the Thanksgiving holiday, with US markets closed on Thursday and an early close on Friday. The front end is priced for a central bank that is still tightening into above-target inflation, while the long end keeps rallying on risk aversion and positioning. If the equity stress fades, the long end is the exposed part of the curve: yields have fallen roughly 25 basis points over the past week on a haven bid that the underlying data do not support.
