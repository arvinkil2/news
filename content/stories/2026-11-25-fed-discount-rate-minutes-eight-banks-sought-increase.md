Directors at eight of the Federal Reserve's 12 regional banks voted in October to recommend raising the primary credit rate to 4.25 percent from 4.00 percent, according to minutes of the Board of Governors' discount rate meetings released on Tuesday. The Board declined to act on the requests and kept the rate at 4.00 percent at both its October 19 and October 28 meetings.

The support for an increase built within the month. At the October 19 meeting, five banks, Boston, Cleveland, Chicago, St. Louis and Minneapolis, had asked for 4.25 percent, while seven backed the existing rate. By October 28, Richmond, Kansas City and Dallas had switched to the higher rate in votes taken on October 22, leaving Atlanta, New York, Philadelphia and San Francisco on record for 4.00 percent.

The discount rate is set by the Board of Governors, not by the regional boards, whose votes are recommendations. The Board's own process is separate from the Federal Open Market Committee's, which held the federal funds target range at 3.75 to 4.00 percent on October 28 by a 10 to 2 vote. At 4.00 percent, the primary credit rate sits at the top of that target range, and the secondary credit rate remains 50 basis points above the primary rate.

The minutes add a regional dimension to the hawkish evidence already in the October FOMC record, where almost all participants judged that further rate increases would be appropriate if the economy followed the baseline. They land two weeks before the Fed's final meeting of the year on December 8 to 9, and they show the constituency for tighter administered rates broadening beyond the five banks that first requested a move.
