The Pan American Health Organization said on Tuesday that measles is no longer considered eliminated in the United States. PAHO Director Jarbas Barbosa told a media briefing that the organisation's verification commission had concluded endemic measles transmission has been re-established in Bolivia, Mexico and the United States, and recommended their elimination status be withdrawn, a recommendation he said he had accepted. The United States had held the designation since 2000; a country loses it when a chain of transmission is not stopped within a year.

The ruling formalises a year of spread that has already happened on the ground. The United States has reported more than 5,000 measles cases this year, the first time since 1991, and 96 percent of infections are in unvaccinated people or people whose vaccination status is not known, according to CDC data cited by STAT. States have reported 10 measles deaths this year, though the CDC's website currently lists six, and kindergarten vaccination rates have fallen below 90 percent in nearly 20 states. CDC Director Erica Schwartz said in a statement that the elimination designation is not what protects Americans and that vaccination does, and pointed to $100 million the agency has distributed to state and local health departments in outbreak areas.
