The dollar rose to 159.94 yen early on Thursday, its strongest level in over two months, as expectations grew that the Federal Reserve will raise interest rates after hot United States inflation data and hawkish policymaker remarks. Japan's top currency diplomat Atsushi Mimura, in his first foreign-exchange remarks since late September, said recent moves had been excessively rapid and warned that it is "still too early to forget what happened this summer," when Japan bought yen in late July and early August, including one operation conducted jointly with the United States. The weak yen is lifting import costs: Japan's yen-based import price index jumped 24.8 percent in October from a year earlier.
