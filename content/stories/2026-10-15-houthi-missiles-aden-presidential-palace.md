Latest: The Houthis claimed responsibility on Saturday for Friday night's missile strike on Aden's airport, saying the two missiles targeted a weapons depot and a Saudi military command headquarters at the Badr camp in Aden, France 24 reported, with AFP. The Aden Security Department confirmed two missiles struck the airport grounds as a Yemenia Airways flight bound for Cairo prepared for takeoff, and no casualties were reported; whether the stated military targets were hit was not independently confirmed.

Two missiles fired by the Houthis struck Aden's airport on Friday night as a Yemenia Airways passenger plane prepared to take off, Yemen's information minister Moammar Al-Eryani said in a post on X, reported by Arab News. The missiles landed about 500 meters from the aircraft, which was operating as Flight IY 612 bound for Cairo, and about 100 meters from the midpoint of the runway; the passengers and crew were safe and no casualties were reported. Al-Eryani condemned the strike as a direct threat to passengers, flight crews and civilian aviation, and to Yemenis travelling for medical treatment and education.

The toll rose on Thursday evening. Five people were killed and 16 wounded in the Houthi attack on the Maashiq palace, Al Jazeera reported, citing Yemen's health ministry, in a report carried by Interfax. The prime minister and all members of the government were unharmed, information minister Muammar al-Eryani wrote on X, saying the Houthis fired five missiles at the palace. At least two explosions struck the hilltop beside the palace and two more hit at the security service gates, and the Saba agency said the targets were an operations headquarters led by Saudi officers inside the palace and its weapons stores. Saudi Arabia called the Houthi attacks full-fledged war crimes, and the United States warned of possible attacks on Saudi airports, according to Al Jazeera's live coverage.

Missiles struck the Maashiq presidential palace in Aden on Thursday while the Presidential Leadership Council was holding a session inside, according to a Yemeni government statement carried by the state news agency Saba and reported by the TT and dpa wire services. The government said two presidential guards were killed and three wounded, that the attack did not disrupt the council's work, and that council chairman Rashad al-Alimi and the other participants were not in immediate danger.

Al-Alimi had recently arrived in the southern port city and was due to deliver a speech at a University of Aden graduation ceremony. The government accused the Houthis of carrying out the attack and of targeting the state and its institutions, and Russian state media separately reported that the Houthis had fired five missiles at the presidential palace in Aden.
