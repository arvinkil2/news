## Latest

A convoy of Russian ships carrying military vehicles bound for Libya turned around in the Mediterranean after Ukrainian sea drones sank the corvette Merkury at Benghazi on November 22, Reuters reported on Friday, citing three people from military and security services. The convoy, the Russian flagged cargo and vehicle carriers Maia 1, Lady R and Lady D, escorted by the frigate Golovko, turned near the Strait of Sicily on the day of the attack, a European military official tracking it said; a Ukrainian military intelligence official and a Middle East security official confirmed the turn. The ships left Kaliningrad on November 5, last broadcast positions near the Bay of Biscay on November 12, and were last tracked near Algeria on November 25, with their onward destination unclear. The European and Middle Eastern sources said the vehicles were possibly bound for West Africa and Russia's Africa Corps, although some could have been for the Libyan National Army. Russia's defence ministry did not respond to a request for comment.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said on Saturday that the Libya operation was carried out without prior coordination with European partners, confirming it in a social media post after a phone call with Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni, Yonhap reported from Rome. The admission walked back his statement in a BBC interview released on Thursday that Ukraine never attacks without consulting partner countries. Italy's foreign minister, Antonio Tajani, had rebutted that claim, saying none of them was notified of the attack and that the statement was not true. Zelenskyy said the two countries' authorities would share all necessary information and communicate directly and effectively.

Two Libyan soldiers have died of injuries sustained in Sunday's attack, the head of Libya's eastern administration, Oussama Hamad, confirmed, describing the strike as Ukrainian aggression against the port of Benghazi, Deutsche Welle reported with AP, AFP and Reuters. Ukraine's foreign minister, Andrii Sybiha, defended the operation on Tuesday, saying Ukraine respects Libya's sovereignty, is not dragging anyone into the war, and that Russia endangers third countries by using their civilian infrastructure for military purposes. Libya's foreign ministry had denounced the attacks on Monday and said it authorized no foreign military operations in its territory or waters, and the Libyan National Army's general command said a response would come at the appropriate time and place.


President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said on Monday that Ukrainian forces sank the Russian corvette Merkury off Libya, hailing what he called an exemplary Ukrainian sea drone operation in response to Russia's war. Al Jazeera reported that seven Ukrainian-made naval drones were launched in the attack, that a KA-27 combat helicopter was also destroyed, and that Zelenskyy named a second target, the Russian dry cargo ship Angara, which he said Russia used to transport weapons. Libya's eastern health ministry said all crew members had been rescued and were receiving treatment, while local sources told Al Jazeera the number of Libyan and Russian nationals killed was not yet known. Russia had not responded in the reporting read.

The UN Support Mission in Libya called for an immediate, independent and transparent investigation into the attacks at Benghazi seaport and off the city’s coast, expressed serious concern over Sunday’s incidents, and said Libya must not become a battleground for foreign conflicts. The mission urged all parties to exercise restraint and avoid escalatory rhetoric while investigations are under way, and said it stood ready to assist Libyan authorities. The eastern-based government’s prime minister, Osama Hamad, also requested an international investigation, including into suspicions that the drone boats originated from western Libya.

Two Libyan military officials told the Associated Press that the vessel struck at the Benghazi naval base on Sunday was the Merkury, a Russian Navy corvette, and that it was severely damaged after two sea drones hit it and at least four more were intercepted and destroyed. The officials, affiliated with the self-styled Libyan National Army that controls eastern and southern Libya and speaking on condition of anonymity, said the drones were launched from a base run by Ukrainian forces in western Libya, according to an initial investigation. The eastern-based government said a joint investigation with Russia had been launched to uncover the source of the attack and the party behind it. No group has claimed responsibility, and there was no immediate comment from Russia or Ukraine.

Reuters reported that ten people, including Russians and Libyans, were slightly injured in the explosion, and that later on Sunday a commercial vessel flying the Maltese flag was targeted by a drone boat off the coast of Benghazi. Libyan naval forces rescued that vessel's crew, the health ministry said some crew members were in stable condition after minor injuries, and Libyan naval and air forces intercepted more than 20 drone boats off the coast of Benghazi on Sunday evening, two Libyan military sources told Reuters.

The Tripoli-based government in western Libya said it rejected dragging Libyan ports and civilian facilities into external conflicts and would not allow Libya to become an arena for international conflicts. The Libyan officials told AP that Ukrainian forces, most of them drone experts, operate mainly at an air base in Misrata and at other facilities in Tripoli and Zawiya, and that in March Ukrainian forces operating in western Libya struck a Russian tanker in the Mediterranean in a suspected sea drone attack near Maltese waters.

## Earlier

An explosion struck a Russian ship docked at the Benghazi naval base on Sunday, and Libya's eastern-based government said a preliminary investigation indicated a suspected drone attack. The vessel had stopped at the port to refuel and resupply on its way back to Russia, under maritime cooperation agreements between the two countries. The government said port and naval base facilities, including warehouses, were safe, and that normal operations had resumed after the fire was brought under control.

The eastern-based government condemned the suspected attack as a violation of Libya's sovereignty and a threat to maritime safety, said Libya was not a party to the war between Russia and Ukraine, and said it would take legal and diplomatic measures against anyone found responsible.
