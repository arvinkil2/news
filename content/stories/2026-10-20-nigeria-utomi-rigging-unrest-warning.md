Professor Pat Utomi, a political economist and supporter of Peter Obi's presidential candidacy, has warned that attempts to manipulate Nigeria's 2027 elections could trigger serious unrest. Posting from an informal town hall in Manchester, where Obi was a fellow guest, Utomi said participants were preoccupied by what they saw as the confidence of some public officials in their ability to manipulate the 2027 vote, with results spoken of as already in the pocket.

They were energised to ask how they could fight rigging, he wrote, warning that the determination to prevent electoral malpractice suggests we may see a civil war if power tries to muscle the will of the people this time. BusinessDay carried the same warning under a civil war headline a day earlier.

The intervention should be read at its proper weight. Utomi is not a neutral observer: he backs Obi for the presidency and has said he would withdraw that support if Obi accepted a vice presidential slot on another ticket. His account of official boasts about rigging is his report of a diaspora discussion, not documented evidence of a rigging plan. What the warning does establish is the frame the opposition intends to campaign in: the 2027 election will be fought as much over whether the count is credible as over policy, and the cost of a disputed result is being priced in public, early.
