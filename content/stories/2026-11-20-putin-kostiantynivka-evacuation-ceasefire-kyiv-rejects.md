Putin proposed a humanitarian mission in Kostiantynivka modelled on the recent operation in Oleshky, saying Russia controls the city, that five days would be needed to clear the access roads, and that Ukrainian and Russian human rights organisations and foreign press delegates should take part. Around 340 civilians remain in the city, which had about 70,000 people before the full-scale invasion, has no central water, electricity or gas supply, and since the spring of 2026 has been reachable for evacuation only on foot, according to local authorities cited by Deutsche Welle. Russia announced in July that it had taken Kostiantynivka but provided no proof; Ukraine says fighting is still ongoing, and the Ukrainian open-source mapping project DeepState says the Ukrainian army still controls the city in an extremely difficult situation.

Zelenskyy said Putin simply wants Ukraine to leave Kostiantynivka so Russian forces can move in and occupy the entire city, and that Ukraine has always given civilians there opportunities to leave. The Oleshky ceasefire ran from October 27 to November 1, and after it ended the Ukrainian General Staff said Russian forces had used it to rotate troops, entrench their positions and bring in ammunition, a claim Zelenskyy repeated in rejecting the new proposal. Ukrainian analysts quoted by Deutsche Welle were split on the response, with some warning a pause would let Russia consolidate forces around the city and others arguing the remaining civilians cannot be evacuated without a ceasefire and that Kyiv should insist on fixed positions and neutral supervision as conditions.
