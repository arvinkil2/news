Chile has cleared Marimaca Copper to begin early construction work at its namesake copper project near Antofagasta. The National Geology and Mining Service granted the mining method permit to Marimaca's subsidiary, Compania Minera Cielo Azul Limitada, allowing site preparation and bulk earthmoving at the Marimaca Oxide Deposit. Full scale construction still requires board approval, the company said in its release on Thursday.

The permit builds on environmental approval granted in November 2025, allowing the start of fully funded early works, said Jose Antonio Merino, Marimaca's managing director in Chile. BMO Capital Markets analyst Rene Cartier called the receipt of the permit another derisking milestone, adding that further sectoral permits are expected in due course but are not viewed as critical path items.

The open pit, about 45 kilometres from Antofagasta, would produce an average of 48,000 tonnes of refined copper cathode a year over its first decade, at an upfront cost of $587 million and a mine life of 13 years, according to the company's 2025 feasibility study. The oxide deposit holds measured and indicated resources of 213.5 million tonnes grading 0.40 percent copper, including proven and probable reserves of 178.6 million tonnes at 0.42 percent.

The clearance follows Sprott Resource Lending's investment committee backing on Tuesday for a proposed funding package of up to $450 million, comprising up to $425 million in senior secured debt and up to $25 million in equity under a non binding term sheet. Marimaca shares rose 2.4 percent to C$6.30 on Thursday morning in Toronto, valuing the company at about C$854 million.
