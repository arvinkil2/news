Tanker freight fell 7.59 percent to 843.31 dollars, the largest move in the midday tables. No story in today's reporting explains the fall, and it is recorded here without a cause.

Semiconductors rose 3.08 percent and Nvidia rose 3.08 percent to 208.29 dollars, partly reversing the selloff that dominated the morning edition. Today's reporting explains the earlier leg, not this rebound: the copper and tin story tied the morning weakness to a renewed selloff in Asian technology stocks and doubts about AI spending (see today's copper and tin story). No story published today explains the midday reversal itself.

Broadcom rose 3.25 percent to 332.70 dollars. The only Broadcom story published today is the EU General Court ruling that CISPE can keep confidential the names behind its VMware merger challenge, which does not explain the share move (see today's Broadcom and VMware story).

Meta rose 2.72 percent to 623.22 dollars. Today's Meta reporting is its five tranche senior notes offering, filed Tuesday with amounts and coupons still blank pending pricing, part of the wider hyperscaler financing wave for the AI buildout (see today's Meta notes offering story and today's hyperscalers financing story). Neither story explains the share rise itself.

Copper fell 2.28 percent to 6.39 dollars a pound. This move is explained in today's reporting: copper and tin fell as the AI selloff weakened the demand outlook for data centre and circuit board metals, while the physical market stayed tight, with Chinese import premiums near record levels (see today's copper and tin story).

Apple fell 2.10 percent to 331.23 dollars. Today's Apple reporting centred on its balance sheet rather than demand: inventory rose 154.3 percent from a year earlier to 14.54 billion dollars as the company stockpiled memory against rising prices and guided margins down (see today's Apple inventory story).

BHP fell 2.00 percent to 78.47 dollars, in line with the wider mining tape. Today's mining reporting put the sector's losses at 712 billion dollars since the February peak, with fund de-risking through September and October as higher United States rate expectations and the technology rout spread (see today's mining top 50 story).

The cross-market read is split rather than risk-on. Chips rebounded, but miners, base metals and freight kept falling, and yields rose into the afternoon events: the 10 year Treasury yield rose 6.4 basis points to 4.96 percent and the 5 year rose 6.0 basis points to 4.75 percent in the midday tables, with bond investors already paused ahead of the midterms, Wednesday's refunding announcement and the November 10 inflation report (see today's bond investors story). The midterm results tonight will test both halves of that split.
