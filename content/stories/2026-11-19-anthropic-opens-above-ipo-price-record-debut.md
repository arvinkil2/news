**Latest.** Anthropic's underwriters exercised their overallotment option in full on Friday, buying the additional 147 million shares and lifting the amount raised in the Class A offering to 93.5 billion dollars, from 81.3 billion dollars on Wednesday, the company said in a release reported by CNBC. In total Anthropic sold 1,127,000,000 Class A shares at 83 dollars, and with the concurrent 26 billion dollar sale of Class N shares to a group of technology companies including AMD, Google, Microsoft and Nvidia, aggregate gross proceeds from the IPO reached 119.5 billion dollars before expenses. The completion of the raise came as the stock itself fell 12 percent in pre-market trading on Friday during a broad tech sell-off, after Thursday's 17 percent debut close.

Anthropic shares closed at 97.17 dollars on Thursday, 17 percent above the IPO price, Reuters reported, leaving the company valued at about 1.77 trillion dollars on a fully diluted basis after the opening print had briefly put it at about 2 trillion dollars. The close came on a weak day for the wider market: stocks fell for a sixth session, with the Nasdaq down 2.3 percent and the S&P 500 down 2 percent after both had opened higher, and AMD fell 8 percent after the short seller report covered on this site earlier in the day.

Anthropic shares opened at 111.50 dollars on the Nasdaq on Thursday, 34 percent above the 83 dollar price set in the company's initial public offering, according to Deutsche Welle reporting with AP, AFP and Reuters. The opening price values the San Francisco company, maker of the Claude chatbot, at about 2 trillion dollars. NOS, crediting AFP, reported an opening price of about 111 dollars, well over a third above the introduction price. The shares trade under the ticker ANT.

The offering itself was the largest on record. Anthropic sold 980 million shares at 83 dollars, raising 81 billion dollars and valuing the company at 1.5 trillion dollars at the offer price, above market estimates of about 1.3 trillion dollars. Existing investors including Nvidia, Google and Microsoft are putting in a further 26 billion dollars in a related placement, NOS reported, taking the total raised past the previous record set by SpaceX. Demand forced the price up before trading began: the range had been estimated at 70 to 78 dollars, the price was raised to 83 dollars on Wednesday, and Bloomberg reported a week ago that the offering was already six times oversubscribed. Grey market indications on Thursday morning pointed to an opening gain of more than 20 percent; the first price beat that.

The listing fixes the ownership map drawn in the company's filing. As this site reported this morning from Amendment No. 3 to the Form S-1, Amazon will hold 14.7 percent of Anthropic after the offering and Google 13.7 percent, 28.4 percent between them, all in non-voting shares, and a group of technology and chip companies agreed to buy 26 billion dollars of non-voting stock in the placement, with Google buying 10 billion dollars of it.

Chief executive Dario Amodei used the opening bell ceremony to repeat the safety case the company has made since September. Reading from a printed text, he said Anthropic would pace the training and deployment of its systems, taking as long as is necessary to have confidence that they are safe and will remain under human control, and that the stronger financial position after the IPO strengthened the company's resolve rather than weakening it. He described the approach as a race to the top, in which the aim is to make the whole industry safer. Co-founder Daniela Amodei told CNBC the risks were real but the promise of AI was also, in her word, incredible, and that the company was founded to manage those risks.

The balance sheet behind the price is not a profitable one. DW noted that Anthropic has taken on 6.2 billion dollars in debt, is not yet making a profit, and faces very large bills for computing power and data centres. Those figures are why the debut is being read as a test of the AI trade itself: Asian stocks fell on Thursday, with Taiwan down 4 percent, as doubts about AI valuations persisted despite the IPO, and Nvidia closed 2.9 percent lower in New York on Wednesday after its post-earnings rally faded. Anthropic is the first large AI laboratory to list; OpenAI, maker of ChatGPT, has reportedly pushed its own offering to 2027.
