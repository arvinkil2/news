Student led protests in Tirana escalated for a second day on Wednesday, as demonstrators demanding better university conditions, financial support and the resignation of Prime Minister Edi Rama clashed with riot police around the education ministry. Fires broke out at the ministry and at two banks, protesters threw stones and improvised explosives, and police responded with water cannon and tear gas.

The casualty picture is contested but bounded. Police said 23 people were detained on Tuesday and that six officers and a journalist were injured; the health ministry said 13 people sought treatment at the main university hospital that day. Local media put the two day totals at about 29 injuries and 35 arrests. No deaths have been reported by police, hospitals or any outlet checked for this update.

Deputy prime minister Albana Kociu condemned the violence while defending the right to protest, opposition leader Sali Berisha praised the students, and President Bajram Begaj urged dialogue and an investigation into reports of excessive force against demonstrators and journalists. The US embassy advised its citizens to avoid protest areas and the EU delegation called for de escalation.

The movement, part of a broader wave of anti government demonstrations running since May over corruption and a contested luxury tourism project, now presents Rama with his most sustained street challenge since winning a fourth term last year.
