The US Defense Logistics Agency has committed $2 billion to acquire strategic and critical materials for the National Defense Stockpile throughout the fiscal year, Mining.com reported, citing a DLA news release. The stockpile holds materials needed for military, industrial and essential civilian requirements in a national emergency that are not available commercially in sufficient quantities, among them rare earths, tungsten, titanium and antimony, and the materials bought can range from high-purity aluminum to vanadium pentoxide.

Most of the critical mineral contracts awarded through the DLA's Contracting Services Office were targeted demand signals intended to enable domestic production, DLA Strategic Materials director Eric Mata said in the release. Domestic sourcing matters most, he said, when a material is essential to national defense, sits in concentrated or vulnerable supply chains, is difficult to replace, and is not readily available from reliable sources in the quantities or forms required.
