**Latest, October 27 (reactions):** A third exit poll, from the public broadcaster Kan, matched Channel 13: the opposition bloc at 53 seats, Netanyahu's bloc at 50 and the Arab led parties at 17. On those numbers Netanyahu's bloc has lost 14 of the 64 seats it won in 2022, and Likud itself is down about a quarter of its 2022 seat total even while remaining the largest single party. Eisenkot posted that the exit polls show a complete lack of confidence in Netanyahu and the October 7 government, and that he would act immediately to replace the government. Yair Lapid called the result a crushing defeat for the Netanyahu bloc; Yair Golan of the Democrats said the public had dismissed the government. Foreign minister Gideon Saar, of the Netanyahu camp, called the celebrations on the left premature and pointed to Likud finishing as the largest party. The Central Elections Committee rejected the request by Netanyahu and several other parties to keep polling stations open beyond the 22:00 close, and rejected the suggestion that the election was being stolen, saying it had seen no evidence of systematic tampering. First official results are expected after about 1 a.m. local time on Wednesday, with the final tally expected by October 30.

**Latest, October 27 (after polls closed):** Polls closed at 10 p.m. and the first television exit polls point to deadlock with an opposition edge. Channel 12 projected Likud as the largest party with 24 seats against 22 for Eisenkot's Yashar, and put the parties opposed to Netanyahu at 52 seats, his supporting bloc at 50 and the Arab led parties at a record 18. Channel 13 projected Likud at 25 seats and Yashar at 23, the opposition bloc at 53, Netanyahu's bloc at 50 and the Arab led parties at 17. Turnout reached 74.3 percent by 8 p.m., 5,449,277 voters, against 66.3 percent at the same hour in the 2022 election. Both exit polls put the Reservists and the People of Israel below the 3.25 percent threshold. Exit polls in Israel are often revised during the night, about 600,000 absentee ballots, most of them from soldiers, are not due to be fully counted until Thursday, and certified results are expected on November 4.

**Latest, October 27 (evening):** Turnout reached 55.7 percent by 4 p.m., the Central Elections Committee reported via ANSA, the highest figure at that stage since the committee's records began in 1973 and up from 47.5 percent at the same hour in the previous election. The pace has risen through the day, from 16.68 percent at 10 a.m. to 31.5 percent at noon, 45.9 percent at 3 p.m. and now 55.7 percent at 4 p.m. Netanyahu's Likud presented a request to extend voting past the scheduled 10 p.m. close. Polls are otherwise due to close at 10 p.m., with exit polls expected immediately after, and no result had been published when this update was written.

**Latest, October 27 (afternoon):** Turnout reached 45.9 percent by 3 p.m., 3,366,242 ballots cast, the highest figure at that stage of election day since 1973, the Central Elections Committee reported via The Times of Israel's liveblog. The pace has quickened through the day, from 16.68 percent at 10 a.m. to 31.5 percent at noon, and now stands above the same point in every election for more than five decades. Polls close at 10 p.m., with exit polls expected immediately after, and no result had been published when this update was written.

**Latest, October 27 (afternoon):** After voting in Jerusalem, Netanyahu said "they are trying to steal our elections," pointing to the arrest of officials from an Arab party in the south of the country in possession of envelopes of money, ANSA reported. Dean Livne, acting director general of the Central Elections Committee, said police had arrested a group from an Arab party near Rahat before polling stations opened, carrying envelopes of money apparently intended to buy votes, calling it an extremely serious incident but an isolated case, and said no similar episodes had been reported elsewhere. Eisenkot's Yashar party complained that its ballot papers were disappearing at dozens of polling stations, in one city at 43 of them, and called it a systematic attempt to sabotage the vote; Livne described the irregularities reported across the country as within the normal range. He also said polling stations would remain open past the 22:00 close for voters still in line. Shin Bet chief David Zini warned that foreign interference attempts were expected during the day, while saying there was no evidence of any yet.

**Latest, October 27 (midday):** Turnout reached 31.5 percent by noon, more than 2.3 million ballots cast in the first five hours of voting, the fastest pace at that stage since the 1977 election, the Central Elections Committee reported. Long lines formed outside polling stations. Netanyahu, who voted in Jerusalem just after midday, sent supporters a message saying turnout was high among his opponents on the left and among Israelis of Arab heritage, and urging his own voters to stream to the polls, calling it a fateful moment, Reuters reported. Arab candidate Ahmed Tibi said voters from Israel's Arab minority were voting in unprecedented numbers. Eisenkot visited kibbutz Nir Oz, one of the communities attacked on October 7, and said he was convinced the day would bring a change and a new spirit. No result had been published when this update was written; polls close at 10 p.m. and exit polls are expected after voting closes.

**Latest, October 27:** Turnout reached 16.68 percent by 10 a.m., more than 1.2 million ballots cast, up from 15.9 percent at the same point in the 2022 election and the highest early participation rate recorded at that stage of election day since 1981, according to the Central Elections Committee. Polls opened at 7 a.m. across Israel and voting runs until 10 p.m., with 7,331,981 people eligible to vote. Gadi Eisenkot opened the day by invoking the hour of the October 7 attack, posting at 06:29 that voters should remember that hour when they cast their ballots and presenting the vote as a chance to replace what he called the government of October 7. In Kiryat Shmona, a Netanyahu stronghold near the Lebanon border, a man kicked and tore down an Eisenkot poster as polls opened, the Associated Press reported. No result had been published when this update was written; exit polls are expected after voting closes.

Israelis vote on Tuesday in the country's first general election since the Hamas led attack of October 7, 2023, and the wars in Gaza, Lebanon and Iran that followed. About 7.3 million people are eligible. Polling stations open at 7 a.m. and close at 10 p.m., voters choose among 37 registered party lists, and only lists clearing the 3.25 percent threshold win seats in the 120 seat Knesset. A government needs 61 seats, a total no single party has ever reached alone.

The final polls, published on Friday before the blackout, disagreed on the leader but agreed on the deadlock. A Channel 12 survey put former chief of staff Gadi Eisenkot's new centrist party Yashar first with 23 seats against 21 for Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud, and Channel 13 put Yashar at 22 and Likud at 19. The public broadcaster KAN projected 52 seats for Eisenkot's bloc and 50 for Netanyahu's. Polling for i24NEWS gave the Netanyahu camp 59 seats, still two short, and the pro government Channel 14 projected Likud well ahead as a single party. Both the KAN and i24NEWS surveys gave the Arab lists a combined 14 seats, which could make them the balance of power, although most Jewish opposition parties have so far ruled out governing with them.

The campaign closed on the two questions that have run through it. The opposition's central demand is a state commission of inquiry into October 7, which Netanyahu has refused in favour of a political commission, and thousands marched in Tel Aviv on Saturday behind the victims' families making that demand, as covered on this site yesterday. Netanyahu's closing argument is security: he has cast himself as the leader who confronted Iran and its proxies, pressed President Donald Trump for more visible support in the final days, Axios reported, and received a Trump endorsement urging voters to back him to finish the job. Eisenkot's closing line was that Netanyahu wants a tie and Israel needs a decisive result, because the leader of the largest party usually receives the first mandate to form a government.

The vote is being held inside a security operation. The military has closed all land crossings with the West Bank on election day and the Hebron operation continues, with about 2,000 residents displaced and no return date, as the updated West Bank story on this site records. Whatever the count on Tuesday night, the likely next phase is bargaining: if neither bloc reaches 61, Israel faces weeks of coalition talks, a possible unity or minority arrangement, or the prospect of another election.
