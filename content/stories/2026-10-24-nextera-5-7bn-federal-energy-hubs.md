NextEra Energy has secured definitive agreements to build three federally backed energy hubs and has received 5.7 billion dollars in funding for them so far this year, chairman and chief executive John Ketchum told investors on the company's third quarter earnings call on Wednesday. NextEra will develop, build and operate the hubs for fees and will not need to invest its own capital in them.

Two hubs, in Texas and Pennsylvania, are backed by the Government of Japan and the US Department of Commerce. An initial 3.3 billion dollars, released in August, is funding procurement of turbines and other long lead equipment, and NextEra expects to build up to 10 gigawatts of gas fired generation across the two sites, with completion slated for 2032. A third hub, backed by the Republic of Korea and the Department of Commerce, will feature 6.5 gigawatts of natural gas generation serving a 5 gigawatt data centre campus in Encinal, Texas, with a first phase that could come online as early as 2029.

Ketchum said the hubs would generate at least half the earnings NextEra would expect if it owned and operated them, and that co located battery storage could raise that return. The company put its wider pipeline of potential hub projects at 30 to 40, and separately said it has joined a coalition building a privately owned hub in western Kentucky with 2 gigawatts of gas generation and 2.6 gigawatts of battery storage. Its total development backlog stands at 36.7 gigawatts.

The structure is the story. The capital is coming from two allied governments and the US Commerce Department, tied to trade deal investment commitments, while the developer takes fees and no balance sheet risk. What the reporting does not yet show is a signed power buyer: the hubs are being funded and equipped ahead of announced data centre offtake, so the demand side of the trade remains the open question.
