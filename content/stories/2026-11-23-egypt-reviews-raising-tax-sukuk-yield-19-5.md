Egypt's finance minister has ordered a review of the pricing on the country's first tax sukuk before the instrument launches. Rasha Abdel Aal, head of the Egyptian Tax Authority, said Finance Minister Ahmed Kouchouk had directed officials to study increasing the yield on the upcoming issuance by 0.5 percentage points to 19.5 percent, as an additional incentive for taxpayers.

The sukuk is a new funding channel built on a provision of the Income Tax Law that has sat unused since 2005. Taxpayers subscribe through bank branches for between 20 and 25 percent of the tax declared in last year's return, the Finance Ministry issues against those orders, and the value plus its return later settles the taxes due. The return is tax free and floating, set at the central bank's average deposit rate over the holding period minus one percentage point, which worked out at about 19 percent against October's 20 percent deposit rate.

The pilot is deliberately small and short. Maturities run from November 2026 to April 2027 for companies and December 2026 to March 2027 for individuals, keeping both the maturity and the return inside the same fiscal year, and the certificates will not trade on the exchange until the second half of 2027 while the transfer system is tested.

A half point sweetener at the design stage is a demand signal. The treasury is asking households and companies to prepay their taxes in exchange for a deposit rate linked return, and it is pricing that ask against a 20 percent central bank deposit rate. Whether 19.5 percent clears depends on how taxpayers value the tax free status against simply holding bank deposits.
