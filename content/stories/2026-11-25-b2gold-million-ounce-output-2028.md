B2Gold (TSX: BTO) expects to restore annual gold production to more than 1 million ounces from 2028 as it expands its flagship Fekola operation in Mali and ramps up its new Goose mine in Canada, president and CEO Michael Cinnamond told Mining.com. The company has cleared permitting hurdles at its Fekola Regional project, paving the way to truck ore from satellite deposits to the main processing plant starting next year, while Goose is targeting annualized production of 300,000 ounces in 2027.

B2Gold produced more than 1 million ounces annually from 2020 to 2023 before output slipped to 804,778 ounces in 2024 on lower ore grades at Fekola. The company cut this year's production guidance to 820,000 to 920,000 ounces from an earlier 820,000 to 970,000 ounces, following permitting delays in Mali and operational setbacks at Goose, where a crusher fire disrupted operations. Malian authorities approved permits for Fekola Regional in August, after several delays as Mali overhauled its mining laws. The satellite deposits are expected to contribute 150,000 ounces annually from next year, and Fekola is forecast to produce 390,000 to 420,000 ounces this year. Goose is expected to produce about 200,000 ounces this year.

Sustaining group production beyond 2028 will also depend on extending output at the Otjikoto mine in Namibia and the Masbate operation in the Philippines. B2Gold plans to decide next year whether to develop its Gramalote gold deposit in Colombia, where a smaller operation could produce about 225,000 ounces annually during its first five years; the project's estimated $800 million development cost needs updating, Cinnamond said.

Cinnamond, who took over as president and CEO in June, said acquisitions are not the priority: the company first has to execute its existing catalysts to unwind the discount at which its shares trade to peers.
