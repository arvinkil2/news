Uruguay's government on Wednesday announced a data center technology park to be built by Spain's Solaria, an investment of about 5.7 billion dollars that President Yamandu Orsi described as one of the most important in the country's recent history. About 5.5 billion dollars is for the data centers themselves, to be built in Canelones, bordering Montevideo, and in San Jose to the west, with a first phase of 200 megawatts, and a further 200 million dollars for a solar plant in Florida department, in the center south, to supply the park.

The project is expected to create more than 3,000 construction jobs and will operate under Uruguay's free zone regime, which carries tax benefits. Solaria already generates solar power in Uruguay through two photovoltaic plants selling to UTE, the state electricity distributor, and the country runs an electricity matrix that is 98 percent renewable, the draw for pairing new data center load with dedicated solar rather than grid purchases alone.

Update, October 16: Datacenter Dynamics reported the signed deal totals 500MW of data centre capacity. The first phase comprises a 200MW data centre and a 350MW solar project, scheduled for completion in 2029, with a second phase adding a further 300MW of data centre capacity and an option to expand the solar project. The first phase is expected to create 3,100 construction jobs and 1,500 permanent jobs on completion.
