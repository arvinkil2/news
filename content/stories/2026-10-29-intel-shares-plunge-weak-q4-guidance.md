**Latest, October 31:** Friday's regular session turned the after hours fall into a historic one. Intel closed on October 30 down 28.58 percent at 65.76 dollars, from a prior close of 92.07 dollars, its worst single day since 1974 on the reporting of TechCrunch, JKN and Seoul Economic Daily. The rout cut about 12 billion dollars from the value of the United States government's roughly 10 percent stake in the company, and about 6 billion dollars from Nvidia's holding, though the government stake remains worth roughly three times what it cost in 2025. The guidance and Fab 28 detail below is unchanged; Friday added the market's full verdict, not new company information.

**Latest, October 30:** The fab incident the original reporting left open is now quantified, by Intel itself. On the earnings call, finance chief David Zinsner said the company's Israel plant, Fab 28 in Kiryat Gat, has been shut down for about two weeks since the mid-October explosion that killed two workers, and that Intel expects to restart production by the end of this week, without specifying when the plant returns to full capacity. Zinsner put the revenue cost at about 300 million dollars in the fourth quarter, almost all of it in 10nm server products, with a similar impact expected in the first quarter of 2027, and said the outage reduces fourth quarter non-GAAP gross margin by about 2 percentage points, with most wafer write-offs falling in the fourth quarter as Intel assesses which work-in-progress wafers can be recovered. The affected output is the Intel 7 process family behind Xeon server processors, and Intel's other 10nm-capable fabs cannot fully offset the loss while server demand is strong. Tom's Hardware reported the call's fab remarks on October 30; the explosion itself was covered here on October 15.

Intel reported third quarter revenue of 16.51 billion dollars, up 21 percent from 13.7 billion a year earlier and in line with the 16.5 billion analysts expected, with adjusted earnings of 52 cents a share against expectations of 40 cents, CNBC reported. The quarter split along the industry's fault line. Data centre and AI revenue rose 78 percent to 7.3 billion dollars, while client computing and physical AI revenue fell 5 percent to 8.1 billion and missed estimates. Investing.com put Intel Foundry revenue up 58 percent at 6.7 billion dollars and adjusted gross margin at 44.1 percent, up from 40 percent a year earlier.

The fourth quarter outlook is what the market traded on. Intel guided revenue to between 15 billion and 16 billion dollars and adjusted earnings to 25 cents a share, against analyst expectations of 17.1 billion dollars and 43 cents, and guided gross margin down to about 38 percent. The midpoint of the revenue range is about 9 percent below consensus. Shares fell about 20 percent in extended trading, after a run that had left the stock up about 150 percent this year despite a roughly 23 percent fall in October.

Chief executive Lip-Bu Tan said demand for Intel's CPUs is still strong despite near term PC headwinds. Finance chief David Zinsner, who signed the filing, said the company sees the PC slowdown continuing at least through the first half of next year, with global PC shipments having fallen 4 percent in the second quarter as memory and component costs rose, according to Counterpoint Research figures cited by CNBC.
