UK long-term net migration stood at 77,000 in the year ending June 2026, the Office for National Statistics said on Thursday, down from an updated estimate of 204,000 in the year ending June 2025. Excluding the coronavirus pandemic, when international travel restrictions were in place, it is the lowest recorded net migration estimate since 1997, when migration was measured with surveys rather than administrative data. The peak was 944,000 in the year ending March 2023.

The fall was driven by non-EU+ nationals. Their net migration fell to 258,000 from 379,000 a year earlier, because immigration by that group dropped by more than 100,000 while emigration stayed broadly stable. Against the year ending June 2025, non-EU+ arrivals for work-related reasons fell by 45,000, study-related arrivals by 33,000 and asylum-related arrivals by 19,000. Of the non-EU+ nationals who arrived in the latest year, 47 percent came for study, 25 percent for work and 13 percent for asylum-related reasons.

Total long-term immigration was 738,000, down 17 percent from 893,000 a year earlier. Non-EU+ nationals accounted for 550,000 of the arrivals, EU+ nationals for 79,000 and British nationals for 109,000. Total emigration was 661,000, broadly stable against an updated 689,000 a year earlier.

More British and EU+ nationals left than arrived. British net migration was negative 137,000, broadly stable on a year earlier, and EU+ net migration was negative 45,000, negative for the fourth consecutive year on this measure. The ONS cautioned that the British figures rest on an extended 15-month projection because the latest source data were unavailable, and are subject to greater uncertainty and revision than usual. The release is provisional, and the bureau has moved to revision-based intervals for the non-EU+, EU+ and total estimates.
