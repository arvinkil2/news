Novartis has received nine reports of tumors in patients previously treated with Zolgensma, its one time gene therapy for spinal muscular atrophy, since the treatment was first approved in 2019. In two of the cases, the company says, tumors showed evidence of vector DNA integration into the patient's DNA, a risk already listed in the product's prescribing information, and both patients were treated and remain under monitoring.

STAT reported that seven of the instances were previously unknown and that Novartis disclosed the information to neurologists earlier this week, before an advocacy group publicised the cases on Thursday. SMA Europe said the relevant regulatory authorities are actively assessing the evidence, and Novartis said it notified health authorities in each case and will work with them on next steps. More than 6,000 patients have been treated with Zolgensma across trials, managed access programmes and commercial use.
