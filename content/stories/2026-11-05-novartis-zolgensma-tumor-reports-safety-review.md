Latest: The US Food and Drug Administration said on November 6 that it is investigating a serious risk of malignancy linked to Zolgensma after receiving postmarketing reports of malignancies in treated patients, including confirmed reports of product related insertional oncogenesis involving the GLI1 oncogene. Three cases involved tumors associated with GLI1, and in two of them molecular analysis of tumor tissue demonstrated Zolgensma vector DNA integration into the host genome, with the promoter region of the construct inserted upstream of or within the GLI1 gene; how GLI1 was switched on in the third case remains under investigation. The reported tumors include cancers of the central and peripheral nervous systems, tumors in the spinal and paraspinal regions, and a kidney tumor.

The FDA determined that the risk of neoplasia, including malignancy, applies to both of Novartis's onasemnogene abeparvovec products, Zolgensma and Itvisma, because they contain the same vector and construct, and said no cases of neoplasia have been reported after Itvisma to date. The agency said the overall benefits of both products continue to outweigh their potential risks for their approved uses, that it is evaluating whether regulatory action is appropriate, and that it is expeditiously evaluating the adequacy of the current prescribing information, which describes the tumor risk as theoretical, and of the long term follow up studies. Patients receiving either product should be monitored for tumor formation, the FDA said.

Novartis has received nine reports of tumors in patients previously treated with Zolgensma, its one time gene therapy for spinal muscular atrophy, since the treatment was first approved in 2019. In two of the cases, the company says, tumors showed evidence of vector DNA integration into the patient's DNA, a risk already listed in the product's prescribing information, and both patients were treated and remain under monitoring.

Reporting on November 6 put the nine cases in sequence. Two cases had previously emerged in published case reports in 2023 and 2025, in neither of which the treatment was definitively linked to the cancer, and the new disclosure confirms that seven more cases have occurred since then, two of them involving DNA integration. Novartis said it notified health authorities after each case and will continue to work with them on next steps. More than 6,000 patients have been treated with Zolgensma across trials, managed access programmes and commercial use, and the therapy recorded more than 1 billion dollars in sales last year and 1.1 billion dollars through the first nine months of 2026.

Patient groups are pressing for more detail. SMA Europe called the circulating reports deeply concerning for families and urged the company to provide clear information on what is known, what remains uncertain and whether any additional monitoring is recommended. In the United States, Cure SMA said three of the nine patients have had their tumors tested and that one of those cases appears unrelated to Zolgensma. Cure SMA said it has reached out to Novartis and is reviewing the safety information with clinical and scientific experts while remaining in contact with regulators.

The cases land on a long standing scientific concern. The adeno-associated virus used to deliver Zolgensma has been known for decades to integrate into the genomes of certain animals, and regulators have called for greater monitoring of gene therapy recipients since a liver cancer case in a UniQure trial participant in 2021, a case in which that treatment was later exonerated. No regulator has announced a label change or restriction for Zolgensma in response to this disclosure; the FDA's November 6 communication, carried in the Latest paragraphs above, has since confirmed product related insertional oncogenesis in the two GLI1 integration cases while leaving causation open for the nine cases as a group.
