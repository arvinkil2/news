Shareholders sued Alibaba on Thursday in Manhattan federal court, in a proposed class action accusing the Chinese e-commerce company of defrauding them by concealing its ties to Alice Huang, the Megaspeed executive suspected of helping Beijing gain access to advanced Nvidia AI chips, whose relationship with former Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang contributed to his resignation last week. The plaintiffs say Alibaba knowingly understated, in its 2024, 2025 and 2026 annual reports, the risks that actual or suspected export control violations could harm its business, and that its shares fell 14 percent over four days last week as its ties to Huang and Megaspeed were reported, including that it had become Megaspeed's dominant customer. The suit, brought by shareholder Withida Bihan, seeks unspecified damages for holders of Alibaba's American depositary shares between May 23, 2024 and November 11, 2026, and also names chief executive Eddie Wu as a defendant; Alibaba did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
