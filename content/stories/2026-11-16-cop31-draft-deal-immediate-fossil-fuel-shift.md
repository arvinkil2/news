Latest: the revised draft cover decision published on Thursday afternoon in Antalya no longer contains explicit references to moving away from fossil fuels, UN News reported. The text instead emphasises clean energy and electrification, including Turkey's proposed target of raising electricity's share of global final energy consumption to 35 percent by 2035, and on adaptation finance it reaffirms the goal of tripling funding by 2035 while leaving how that increase is calculated for further discussion. Australia's Chris Bowen, who is leading the negotiations, said COP31 would be the first climate summit to acknowledge that the world will fail to hold warming to the 1.5 degree Celsius Paris limit, and pledged to keep consultations going through the night ahead of Friday's deadline, while civil society groups said the negotiations were still struggling and that developing countries were being asked to do more without adequate financial support.

Co-host Australia opened the second week of the COP31 climate talks in Antalya with a draft political deal calling for an immediate transition away from fossil fuels and a tripling of finance for climate adaptation in developing countries to at least 120 billion dollars. The 18-page draft, with 144 items, also carries co-host Turkey's goal of raising electrification of the world economy to 35 percent by 2035, and says the 1.5 degree Celsius limit agreed in Paris is now out of reach, urging faster action to limit the overshoot and return warming below it as soon as possible.

Negotiators now have until Friday to agree a final text, and the fight will be over whether the core language survives. E3G analyst Cosima Cassel said the test is whether the final decision keeps the ambition on overshoot, the clean electrification language and the adaptation finance progress, while Greenpeace Germany head Martin Kaiser criticised the draft for lacking binding commitments. Australia's climate minister Chris Bowen, who chairs the negotiations, said the general tone of the talks had been constructive.
