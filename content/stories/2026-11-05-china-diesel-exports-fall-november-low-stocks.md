Latest: Energy Aspects has cut its forecast for China's November diesel exports by 400,000 tons to about 580,000 tons, below the roughly 800,000 ton trader estimate reported here on November 5, citing reduced domestic inventories, OilPrice reported on November 6. The consultancy said the forecast could be revised up if refiners lift shipments as domestic demand eases in mid November, and GL Consulting said total refined product exports could still rise from October even if some diesel cargoes are cancelled or rescheduled.

Chinese refiners have sold about 500,000 metric tons of diesel for November loading so far, and early estimates put the month's total at about 800,000 tons, down from 1.33 million tons in August and 1.8 million tons in September, traders and executives with knowledge of the export plans told Reuters. Shipments surged in August and September after Beijing lifted the export restrictions it had imposed from April to July to protect domestic supply during the Strait of Hormuz disruption, and that surge pulled domestic inventories down.

The cut comes even after China raised its refined product export quota for the fourth quarter to 20.44 million tons, up 44 percent from a year earlier, according to GL Consulting. Its analysts said quota headroom does not guarantee physical exports, and that an upward revision to the November plan is possible while the monthly schedule remains under adjustment.
