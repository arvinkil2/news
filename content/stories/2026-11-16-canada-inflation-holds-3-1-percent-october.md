Canada's Consumer Price Index rose 3.1 percent year over year in October, matching its 3.1 percent increase in September, Statistics Canada said on Monday, against the 3.2 percent rise analysts polled by Reuters had expected. Prices rose 0.2 percent month over month, also below the 0.3 percent expected.

Upward pressure was broad based, stemming from higher prices for natural gas, gasoline, groceries and clothing, with gasoline prices up 30.9 percent year over year, while a decline in cell phone plan prices and slower rent growth pushed the other way. Excluding energy, the index rose 2.0 percent in October after increasing 2.1 percent in September.
