The European Commission has approved a Belgian state aid scheme of up to 2.04 billion euros over 20 years to support the construction and operation of the first offshore wind farm in the Princess Elisabeth zone in the North Sea. The farm is expected to have a capacity of up to 735 megawatts and to generate up to 2.6 terawatt hours of renewable electricity a year.

The aid will be awarded through a competitive bidding process, with the result expected in the fourth quarter of 2027, and paid as a monthly variable premium under a two way contract for difference, calculated against the farm's monthly capability rather than its actual output. The scheme replaces a 2024 measure whose aid was never granted because the legal deadline of December 31, 2025 passed, and tender prequalification will require bidders to diversify their turbine and component supply chains away from dependence on imports from outside Europe.
