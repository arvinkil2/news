Bank of Japan Governor Kazuo Ueda, presenting the bank's semiannual report to the Senate financial affairs committee, said the bank will continue to raise the policy interest rate and adjust the degree of monetary accommodation, after holding the overnight call rate at around 1.25 percent at last month's meeting. He said consumer price inflation excluding fresh food has been running at around 2 percent and is likely to accelerate to a level clearly above 2 percent, as wage increases pass into selling prices and higher crude oil, semiconductor and import prices feed through, while underlying inflation sits at a level generally consistent with the 2 percent target. Ueda flagged an upward risk to that picture, warning that medium to long term inflation expectations could rise and push underlying inflation above target, and said the bank will weigh the timing and pace of further hikes against the Middle East situation, global AI related demand and the yen.
