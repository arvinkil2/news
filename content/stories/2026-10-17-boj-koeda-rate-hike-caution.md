Bank of Japan policy board member Koeda Junko told local leaders in Kumamoto that the bank should adjust its policy rate in a timely manner and should not become overly cautious about raising it, pointing to considerable upside risks to prices alongside still accommodative financial conditions. She put the nominal neutral rate in a range of about 1.1 to 2.5 percent, against a policy rate of around 1.25 percent since the September hike, and said underlying inflation appears to be anchoring at about 2 percent.

Koeda placed greater weight than before on upside price risks from geopolitical shocks and climate change, noting that yen weakness during this year's Middle East escalation kept import prices rising instead of offsetting them. She also endorsed the bank's balance sheet path, with monthly JGB purchases cut by about 200 billion yen a quarter until March 2027 and set near 2 trillion yen a month from April 2027.
