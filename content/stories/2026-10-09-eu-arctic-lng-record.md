EU ports received 167 cargoes from the Yamal LNG plant between January and September 2026, a record 12.18 million metric tonnes, up 9.5 percent from the same period last year, according to analysis by German environmental group Urgewald of Kpler shipping data, reported by gCaptain October 9. Europe accounted for 85 percent of Yamal's recorded deliveries by volume even as the project's worldwide deliveries fell 1.3 percent. The estimated bill reached 7.88 billion euros: 2.88 billion in the first quarter, 3.08 billion in the second, 1.92 billion in the third.

Third-quarter deliveries fell 13.6 percent year over year to 2.21 million tonnes, but September shipments of 792,440 tonnes came in slightly above the same month last year, worth an estimated 818 million euros. September showed no noticeable impact from the EU's April 25 restrictions on Russian LNG under eligible existing short-term contracts, per Urgewald. The broader ban covering eligible existing long-term contracts takes effect January 1, 2027, which is driving the front-loading: buyers are maximizing imports before the cutoff, mostly under long-term contracts that the short-term restrictions do not touch.

The logistics bind is as tight as the commercial one. Yamal depends on a small fleet of specialized Arc7 ice-class LNG carriers built for Arctic winter conditions; European terminals and shipyards offer the nearby destination that lets those vessels discharge, take maintenance and return quickly. Winter makes eastbound voyages along the Northern Sea Route toward Asia far harder, so the trade's dependence on Europe grows exactly as the ban approaches. The October 18 deadline for the Trump administration to implement provisions of the Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act, signed September 18, adds uncertainty: the law authorizes sanctions against vessels moving Russian energy to evade sanctions and against people connected to Russian energy projects including Yamal LNG, though how broadly Washington applies those powers is unknown.
