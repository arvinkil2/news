Latest: Anthropic is pressing ahead, with the roadshow expected to begin next week and a Nasdaq listing under the ticker ANT in a matter of weeks, but the price investors will underwrite has fallen again, CNBC reported on Friday. The company had been looking toward a $2 trillion valuation until recently, the target when it filed confidentially with the SEC, and stakeholders now call even $1.5 trillion a stretch, with some expecting a listing below that level and one investor putting $1 trillion to $1.25 trillion as the more likely range, against a private valuation of $965 billion in May. Gil Luria, an analyst at D.A. Davidson, said investors will expect the offering to be priced at least at the low end of previous expectations, and that the market needs this listing to go well. Anthropic declined to comment. The shift follows the Nasdaq's fall of more than 5 percent on Thursday, its steepest drop in 19 months, on fears that the midterm results will slow the data centre buildout.

Earlier: Potential investors in Anthropic's planned initial public offering are raising doubts about whether the company can proceed with a roadshow next week in time for a November listing, Reuters reported, citing people familiar with investor discussions. Investors are pushing for a more attractive valuation to commit, with some viewing the recently discussed $1.5 trillion valuation as being at the high end of expectations, and the company may need to offer a bigger discount given its record of quarterly losses and its large commitments to partners in the AI buildout.

The Wall Street Journal, as reported by Investing.com, put the pressure in sharper terms, with one investor suggesting pricing may fall closer to $1 trillion and backers debating whether the company should adjust its pricing range or delay the timeline altogether. The same reporting put Anthropic's cash reserve at $146.6 billion as of September 30, giving it room to curb its capital target or postpone the debut if it chooses.
