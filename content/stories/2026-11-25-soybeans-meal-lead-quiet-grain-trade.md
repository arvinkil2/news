Soybeans did the work in Chicago on Tuesday. January soybeans rose 0.4 percent to 12.34 dollars a bushel, a third straight higher close to monthly highs, on strong soybean meal demand and technical buying, with December meal futures jumping to 351.40 dollars a short ton after reaching a high of 352.90 dollars. March corn edged up 0.1 percent to 4.50 and a quarter dollars, little changed in a narrow range, and March wheat fell 1 percent to 5.89 and a quarter dollars, with pre-first notice day selling and a stronger dollar cited by Dow Jones.

The supply side explains the split. USDA's Crop Progress report of November 23 put the 18-state corn harvest at 94 percent complete as of November 22, advancing 6 percentage points on the week but still 2 points behind the 2021-2025 average, with Illinois at 98 percent and Indiana and Minnesota at 97 percent. The winter wheat crop was 95 percent planted, with 54 percent rated good or excellent, up from 53 percent the previous week, the third consecutive weekly improvement, which weighed on wheat prices.

World markets told a tighter story than Chicago. Paris milling wheat fell on Tuesday after three straight higher sessions, but the premium for European futures over Chicago expanded to 1.15 dollars a bushel, the widest in fifteen months, a spread consistent with the Black Sea disruption the World Food Programme described to the UN Security Council on Monday. European corn futures have fallen in only two of the past thirteen sessions and trade almost 3 dollars a bushel above Chicago.

Trading thins from here. The USDA and the CBOT are closed on Thursday for Thanksgiving, and the CBOT holds a half session on Friday, closing at 1:05 p.m. Eastern. With harvest nearly complete, attention shifts to South American weather and to whether the export pace can work through the short fund position still reported in corn and wheat.
