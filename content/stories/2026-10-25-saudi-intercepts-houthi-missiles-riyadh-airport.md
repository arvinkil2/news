**Latest, October 27:** The Saudi-led coalition said its forces intercepted three ballistic missiles launched toward the Eastern Province and four drones headed for the kingdom's southern regions on Tuesday, and that the missile interceptions caused no casualties, coalition spokesman Major-General Turki al-Maliki said. Saudi Civil Defence issued emergency alerts in the Eastern Province and the Al Kharj governorate before the interceptions and lifted them afterwards. The coalition also said it had intercepted three further drones across the Riyadh and southern regions on Monday evening. The Houthis had not commented on the Tuesday claims when Al Jazeera reported them. The claims are the coalition's own; the coalition is a party to the conflict and they are not independently corroborated here, so this update is carried at grade C. The interceptions came as senior officials from Saudi Arabia, Turkiye and Pakistan met Iranian counterparts in Tehran in talks aimed at de-escalating the regional conflict, according to the same report.

**Latest, October 26:** The Houthis said on Monday that they had attacked pumping stations along Saudi Arabia's East-West oil pipeline, the 740-mile Petroline that carries crude to the Red Sea port of Yanbu and bypasses the Strait of Hormuz, and that they had also attacked Riyadh airport. Saudi Arabia had not confirmed any attack on the pipeline as of early Monday. The pipeline was shut for two weeks in September after drone attacks damaged pumping stations and halted loadings at Yanbu, and had since raised flows to 5.8 million barrels per day. The claim is the Houthis' own and is not independently corroborated here.

**Earlier, October 26:** The Saudi-led coalition conducted airstrikes on Sanaa early on Monday and said it had destroyed two Houthi ballistic missile launchers, including one it said had been used to fire a missile toward Riyadh that Saudi air defences intercepted overnight. Coalition spokesman Turki Al-Maliki, announcing the strikes in posts on X, said one launcher had been used in a recent Houthi missile attack targeting Riyadh and that operations continue to dismantle the Houthis' ballistic missile network. Arab News reported that Riyadh residents were woken by a Civil Defense alert on their phones at 3:42 a.m. local time warning of a potential aerial threat, followed minutes later by an alert saying the threat had passed, and gave no casualty figure for the overnight interception or the Sanaa strikes. Anadolu reported that the Houthis had not immediately responded to the coalition's claims. The claims are the coalition's own and are not independently corroborated here.

**Latest, October 25:** Debris from two intercepted ballistic missiles injured two people, a Saudi citizen and an Asian expatriate resident, and damaged three residential buildings and seven vehicles in Khamis Mushait and Ahad Rufaydah in the south, a Saudi civil defence spokesperson said, in an Arab News report of the same Sunday barrage whose interceptions are carried below.

The Saudi-led coalition said on Sunday that its forces intercepted and destroyed a ballistic missile launched toward Riyadh that morning, a drone in the Riyadh region and a cruise missile aimed at the southwestern city of Khamis Mushait, and later reported the interception of 10 ballistic missiles fired toward southern Saudi Arabia. Saudi civil defence said it had also responded to falling debris in the Mecca and Medina regions on Saturday after intercepting two missiles, a claim the Houthis denied, saying they had not targeted the two cities. The attacks came as King Khalid International Airport resumed operations, with international flights returning to Terminal 2 on Sunday two weeks after a Houthi attack halted operations there, and Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree warned airlines and travellers that the airport remained unsafe and a military target. Inside Yemen, the Houthis advanced on Jabal Sabr, the mountain above Taiz, where the United Nations says 800,000 people are trapped.
