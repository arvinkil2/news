The US Department of Transportation said it has obligated 550 million dollars for North Carolina's S-Line passenger rail project, under a project specific agreement between the Federal Railroad Administration and the state transportation department. The S-Line will connect Richmond, Virginia, to Raleigh, North Carolina, with portions cleared for speeds of up to 110 miles per hour and journey time savings of up to 90 minutes on the corridor, according to the state.

The money is a stage, not a new award. North Carolina received a grant of nearly 1.1 billion dollars for the project under the previous administration, and Amtrak and the state contribute a further 20 percent in matching funds. The S-Line uses a CSX owned freight corridor whose right of way the two states partly purchased between Petersburg and Raleigh, and ground was broken in Raleigh in 2024. In April the department separately awarded nearly 300 million dollars for the Piedmont Corridor between Raleigh and Charlotte.

The project is designed to relieve freight corridor congestion while letting both states run more frequent passenger services, and the state is leading transit oriented development planning along a 95 mile section through Apex, Franklinton, Henderson, Norlina, Raleigh, Sanford, Wake Forest and Youngsville, including community mobility hubs.

Federal Railroad Administrator David Fink called the obligation a milestone in the partnership between the agency, Amtrak and the state. For the market, the signal is continuity: a large inherited intercity rail commitment has passed from award into obligation, the contractual stage at which cancellation becomes materially harder, at a moment when the administration's treatment of predecessor transport programmes has been an open question for state sponsors and contractors alike.
