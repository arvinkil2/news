US natural gas rose 15.68 percent to 3.05 dollars per million British thermal units in the midday tables, extending the morning jump and again the largest move in the tables by a wide margin. No story in today’s edition explains a US move of this size. The edition’s gas reporting is about Europe, where the Dutch benchmark made a four year high this morning on storage at 74.62 percent (see today’s European gas story), and about EQT’s all-stock acquisition of Range Resources (see today’s EQT story), and neither of those is a US price story.

Tesla rose 6.09 percent to 394.89 dollars, taking its weekly gain to 7.29 percent. Today’s big tech earnings story records that Tesla reported on October 21, in the same week as Meta, but no Tesla specific driver for today’s rise appears in today’s reporting.

Tanker freight rose 5.15 percent to 942.77 dollars, up 8.20 percent on the week. No clear driver for the rise appears in today’s reporting. This site’s week ahead preview names product tanker demand as exposed to Friday’s expiry of Russia’s diesel export ban and to the OPEC+ meeting on December quotas, which is context for the rate rather than a cause of today’s move.

Brent crude fell 4.07 percent to 111.97 dollars a barrel in the site tables, while WTI crude fell 0.40 percent to 102.86 dollars. The edition’s new diplomatic development is today’s Mecca Pact story, which reports senior Saudi, Turkish and Pakistani officials meeting Iran’s leaders in Tehran in a de-escalation effort, and reports no outcome from the talks as of Monday morning. Dry bulk freight fell 1.86 percent to 12.38 dollars, moving in the opposite direction to tanker rates, and no driver for the fall appears in today’s reporting.

Semiconductors rose 2.94 percent to 492.27 dollars, Broadcom rose 2.47 percent to 329.62 dollars and Nvidia rose 2.12 percent to 215.43 dollars. The bid lands at the start of the week that today’s big tech earnings story frames as the test of whether AI capital spending pays, with Microsoft and Alphabet reporting on Wednesday after the Federal Reserve decision and Amazon on Thursday (see today’s big tech earnings story). The Nasdaq 100 rose 1.20 percent and the S&P 500 rose 0.72 percent.

In rates, the 10 year Treasury yield eased 1.4 basis points to 4.97 percent and the 30 year eased 2.2 basis points to 5.29 percent, holding the long end below the levels that dominated earlier in October (see today’s Treasury yields story). Gold rose 0.66 percent to 4,034.40 dollars an ounce and silver rose 0.89 percent to 54.19 dollars, both holding above the lines this morning’s gold story set out (see today’s gold story).

The midday read is selective risk-on, not a broad relief rally. Tech was bid ahead of earnings, crude was offered from six month highs and duration rallied a little, a combination that fits positioning into Wednesday’s Federal Reserve decision rather than a verdict on it. The two energy benchmarks moving in opposite directions, gas up hard and crude down, say the day’s largest moves remain supply and positioning stories, not macro ones.
