The European Commission on Wednesday adopted a set of legislative proposals to provide up to 45 billion euros in additional financial support to Ukraine in 2027, structured as a loan raised through joint EU debt under a new coordinated support instrument for Ukraine and covering about two-thirds of the country's estimated 70 billion euro financing gap for next year. Under the plan Politico reported from the Commission document, repayment would come from future Russian war reparations or, if Moscow does not pay, from frozen Russian assets in the EU, disbursements would be conditional on reforms Ukraine must implement and could stretch into late 2028, and EU leaders will discuss the proposal in Brussels at the end of next week. The instrument is separate from the 45 billion euro 2027 tranche of the existing Ukraine Support Loan that the Commission proposed approving on October 30, and the Commission wants all 27 member states to take part, though the Czech Republic, Hungary and Slovakia have already said they do not wish to.
