Amazon has signed an agreement to purchase the proprietary technology behind the Decart Optimization Stack (DOS) and to bring Decart's co-founders, Dean Leitersdorf, Moshe Shalev and Orian Leitersdorf, along with their team of AI researchers and engineers, to Amazon, the company announced on November 23. Amazon said Decart's team has worked closely with it since 2025 to deliver performance results on Trainium, its custom AI chip, using a full-stack approach across software and hardware, and that the technology will help it optimize inference and custom silicon performance so customers can train and run models faster and at lower cost. Completion is subject to customary closing conditions, including regulatory clearance, and Amazon disclosed no price.

TechCrunch reported on November 24 that the deal is valued at a reported $7.5 billion, citing Bloomberg, and that Amazon is not taking Decart's world models work, only the optimization technology and the team behind it. The same report said Anthropic had tried to acquire the startup in the same price range, and that Nvidia, which invested in Decart's $300 million funding round at a $4 billion valuation, was also rumoured as a prospective buyer. Amazon's interest is its Trainium programme: Anthropic already runs workloads on Trainium based data centres, and better optimization software is what would let Amazon offer those facilities to more customers as an alternative to Nvidia hardware.

The agreement converts the talks this site reported on October 11, when the Wall Street Journal put the negotiating price at around $7 billion, into a signed transaction. What Amazon is buying is not the company as a standalone business but the optimization stack and the people who built it, a structure closer to an acqui-hire of a research team plus its core technology than to a conventional takeover.
