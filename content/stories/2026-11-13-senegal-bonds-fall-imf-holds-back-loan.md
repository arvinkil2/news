The International Monetary Fund will not take Senegal's proposed 2.2 billion dollar lending programme to its executive board until the authorities complete an agreed budget framework, take corrective measures over the misreporting of previously undisclosed public debt and secure financing assurances from partners, spokesperson Julie Kozack said at the Fund's regular press briefing on Thursday. The conditions sit under the staff-level agreement reached in September for a 36-month Extended Credit Facility. Bloomberg reported Senegalese bonds fell on Friday as the dispute bit.

The obstacle is domestic. Senegal's finance ministry said last week that a parliamentary committee had asked it to withdraw the 2027 budget bill, and the National Assembly, where former prime minister Ousmane Sonko's PASTEF party holds the majority, had already rejected an amended 2026 budget, leaving the fiscal framework the programme depends on unpassed. Kozack said the Fund is not a party to the discussions between the government and the Assembly, and that what matters is that the authorities can implement the agreed policies through Senegal's own legal and institutional framework.
