Novaya Gazeta closed its Moscow newsroom on Monday, holding its last editorial meeting a month after announcing it was being forced to shut down. The paper, one of the few outlets inside Russia still criticising the war on Ukraine, was designated a foreign agent in September, was ordered to vacate its premises, and said banks had since stopped processing the reader donations it depended on after moving wholly online; its editor-in-chief Sergei Sokolov had his home searched in October, and another editor was arrested in April.

Founding editor Dmitry Muratov, who shared the 2021 Nobel Peace Prize, told journalists gathered for the closure that Russians who advocate for peace had been denied the right to read what they choose, while the Kremlin played the closure down as part of a normal cycle of change in a competitive media landscape. Founded in 1993 with money from Gorbachev's Nobel Peace Prize, the paper lost six journalists to murder over its life, among them Anna Politkovskaya in 2006.
