**Tanker freight (+5.45% to 938.5)** was the day's biggest mover, and the cause is the least ambiguous: the Hormuz casualty event on the tanker On Peace and Monday's fatal sinking of the Royad Mammadov in Romania's Black Sea waters have repriced what it costs to move energy through two of the world's most exposed chokepoints. See today's Hormuz tanker campaign story and the Black Sea ship story for the incident detail.

**Uranium miners (+5.44% to 42.38)** were the second-biggest mover, but no clear single driver surfaced in this morning's reporting. Nuclear buildout sentiment is the obvious backdrop, with Google's Constellation nuclear power deal and the DOE's Vistra nuclear loan in today's infrastructure reporting, but neither ties directly to today's miner move.

**Broadcom (+4.55% to $379.00)** extended its rally with no fresh driver in today's reporting. The gain follows the company's $42 billion Anthropic financing arrangement covered in the October 5 Broadcom story, and sits inside a broader chip bid, AMD hit a new 52-week high and Marvell rose more than 7 percent in today's technology reporting.

**Corn (+1.91% to 506.8), soybeans (+1.50% to 1,300.0), wheat (+1.48% to 702.5)** all rose together on Black Sea risk. The sinking of the Royad Mammadov, which was carrying corn from Izmail to Ravenna, plus two more vessel incidents in three days, is raising the war-risk premium on grain loadings from the region. The move also lands ahead of Friday's WASDE report, with harvest running at a normal pace (corn 23 percent, soybeans 25 percent harvested). See today's Black Sea story.

**Lithium and battery equities (+1.81% to 71.4)** rose even as lithium carbonate prices fell 22.5 percent in September on a Chinese pricing-agency methodology change, per today's mining reporting. No clear single driver for today's equity move appears in the morning's reporting.

**Amazon (+1.58% to $255.38)** rose within a generally firm megacap tape. The company's $1 billion pledge to data-center host towns, disclosed amid local opposition to AI infrastructure projects, is the freshest Amazon news in today's reporting (see the infra-deals roundup); whether it drove the move is not established.

**Rates** were quieter: the US 10-year fell 3.8bp to 5.27 percent, cooling from fresh 24-year highs as traders cut October Fed-hike odds, per today's rates reporting. It does not crack the top movers list.

The cross-market read is risk-on with a geopolitical surcharge. Equities tilted toward growth and infrastructure names, chips, nuclear-linked miners, Amazon, while the surest repricing was in the real world: tanker freight paying for Hormuz and the Black Sea. Bonds barely moved, which says the market is pricing transport disruption, not a demand shock.
