Iraq has rejected an assessment commissioned by OPEC+ that puts its maximum oil production capacity below 5 million barrels a day, according to people familiar with the matter, setting up a possible clash at the group's meeting on production targets this weekend. The study, carried out by external consultants, is meant to set the capacity figures that underpin members' 2027 baselines, and Baghdad has asked that its future targets rest on a capacity of about 6 million barrels a day, against a current production requirement of 4.431 million barrels a day that the group left unchanged for December.

The gap is wide: the International Energy Agency puts Iraq's sustainable capacity at 4.87 million barrels a day, close to the consultants' ceiling and well short of the figure Iraq is seeking. Iraq reported production of 4.399 million barrels a day to OPEC for September, while the IEA estimated September output at 4.10 million, and Oil Minister Basim Mohammed Khudair said earlier this month that production exceeds 5 million barrels a day.
