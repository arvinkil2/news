Thousands of people marched in cities across France on Saturday, the second consecutive Saturday of demonstrations, a week after the October 17 protests ended in rioting and looting. Interior minister Laurent Nunez had banned the Paris demonstration, and its route was moved at the last minute from the Place de la Bastille to the Porte d'Italie; gatherings were also banned in several other cities, among them Lyon's centre and Nice. More than 70,000 police were deployed across the country.

Arrests reached 166 by late afternoon, 85 of them in Paris, 11 in Marseille and 70 elsewhere, after more than 8,000 checks on people and vehicles in which police in Paris seized more than 90 dangerous objects. In Marseille, youths threw bottles at the Vieux-Port and police used tear gas, and police also used tear gas in Lyon, Lille, Toulouse and Nantes, with clashes reported in Toulouse. Fuel prices are the immediate grievance, and the marches also carry the wider cost of living, public services and school conditions, the complaints that began with student protests in September. Unions and the Yellow Vests are among the movements on the street, the Yellow Vests have called a further protest day for Saturday, October 31, and the government published the decree for its strategic diesel stock sale the same day, with the price benefit due at the pump from November 1.
