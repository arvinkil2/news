**Latest:** The interior ministry said on Sunday that police made 1,314 arrests during Saturday's protests, with 53 officers hurt in the clashes, 75 fires attended and 24 shops looted. The ministry said investigations had been opened and that the rest of Saturday night, after the clashes died down, was calm. Interior Minister Laurent Nunez had said late on Saturday that two protesters were seriously hurt: one in Toulouse, where the prefecture said a 20-year-old man suffered a severe hand wound, and one in Clermont-Ferrand, where the prefecture said a man's hand was hurt without the loss of fingers. The regional daily La Nouvelle Republique reported a third serious injury in Poitiers, where a 19-year-old was hurt in his left eye during a riot police charge.

Interior Minister Laurent Nunez said nearly 1,000 people were arrested across France on Saturday and that the number was expected to rise. The marches passed off well in most cities, he said, but police later faced what he called an extremely elevated level of violence from ultra-left groups. In Paris, the prosecutor's office said 64 people, including six minors, were arrested and 40 were placed in custody, most over suspected weapons offenses or participation in a group preparing violence, after officers stopped and checked more than 3,000 people. In Orleans, thousands marched against a congress of Marine Le Pen's National Rally, with police putting the crowd at 11,000 and organizers at 26,000, and in Nantes thousands of protesters broke through police lines to enter the city centre, where officers used tear gas and a water cannon.

The marches were largely peaceful during the day but gave way at nightfall to clashes in Paris and Marseille, Reuters reported. In Paris, where about 110,000 people marched in the southeast of the capital, riot police fired tear gas and used water cannon against a group of about 500 hooded young people throwing stones after the march ended, a security official said. In Marseille, young people blocked a street with metal sheets, set trash bins on fire and threw stones and bottles at police, clashes continued after sunset, and police said two jewelry stores were looted. There were also clashes in Lyon, Bordeaux and Grenoble, and tear gas charges in Clermont-Ferrand, Lille, Nantes and Nice. Central Paris spent the day in a precautionary lockdown, with shopkeepers boarding storefronts, more than 20 metro stations shut, and the Louvre, the Musee d'Orsay and the Paris Opera closed. The unrest also spread beyond France on Saturday, with tear gas and pepper spray in clashes in Brussels and protests in several Italian cities for a second consecutive weekend. Interior minister Laurent Nunez accused far left activists and criminal groups of trying to stir up violence, while hard left leader Jean-Luc Melenchon, who joined the Paris march, predicted the protests would happen again every week until President Emmanuel Macron addresses the discontent.

Thousands of people marched in cities across France on Saturday, the second consecutive Saturday of demonstrations, a week after the October 17 protests ended in rioting and looting. The interior ministry counted close to 410,000 people mobilised across the country, while the CGT union put the figure at more than 800,000. Interior minister Laurent Nunez had banned the Paris demonstration, and its route was moved at the last minute from the Place de la Bastille to the Porte d'Italie; gatherings were also banned in several other cities, among them Lyon's centre and Nice. More than 70,000 police were deployed across the country.

The interior minister put arrests at 135 by early afternoon, most of them preventive, after extensive checks in which Paris police seized dangerous objects including metal balls, slingshots and an airsoft weapon. In Marseille, youths threw bottles and police used tear gas, and police also used tear gas in Lyon, Lille, Toulouse and Nantes, with clashes reported in Toulouse. Fuel prices are the immediate grievance, and the marches also carry the wider cost of living, public services and school conditions, the complaints that began with student protests in September. Unions and the Yellow Vests are among the movements on the street, the Yellow Vests have called a further protest day for Saturday, October 31, and the government published the decree for its strategic diesel stock sale the same day, with the price benefit due at the pump from November 1.
