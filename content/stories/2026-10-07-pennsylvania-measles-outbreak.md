## Latest (Saturday, October 17)

The CDC announced on Thursday that it will send a scientist from its Epidemic Intelligence Service to Pennsylvania to help tackle the outbreak, and that it is releasing nearly 4 million dollars to aid the state's measles response, The Guardian reported. A CDC spokesperson told NBC News the agency would keep working closely with Pennsylvania health officials, and said director Erica Schwartz recommends the MMR vaccine as the most effective way to prevent measles. Pennsylvania had confirmed 1,222 measles cases as of Wednesday, and the state has now confirmed six measles-associated deaths. The national tally the CDC reported on Friday was 4,410 cases, up 330 from its update the previous week.

The outbreak remains centred on Lancaster County, which had more than 415 confirmed cases as of October 9 and is the largest county in Pennsylvania without its own health department. County commissioner Alice Yoder said officials are very concerned, that lacking a local health department is a clear disadvantage, and that county officials currently learn of cases and deaths only from media announcements; she is advocating for the county to create its own health department. Of Pennsylvania's 67 counties, 60 sit under the centralized state system rather than their own health departments. Reuters has reported that the CDC has shifted to using National Center for Health Statistics mortality data for measles-related deaths rather than relying on state health departments, a change that has drawn scrutiny and that sits behind the death-count gap recorded in earlier updates to this story.

## Latest (Saturday, October 10)

CDC data released Friday pushed the 2026 national measles tally to 4,080, the most cases in the United States since 1991 and on track to double 2025's full-year total of 2,289, per CIDRAP's reading of the CDC update. Pennsylvania's outbreak now stands at 1,136 confirmed cases, up 157 in a week, with Lancaster County (415) and Mifflin County (167) hardest hit and 99 percent of the state's cases in unvaccinated people. The CDC has quietly sent $100 million to states for outbreak response, the New York Times reported, while new CDC director Erica Schwartz told a Wall Street Journal event that the MMR vaccine is "absolutely safe, absolutely effective." The unresolved gap remains the death toll: Pennsylvania reports five measles-associated deaths, but the CDC recognizes only two.

## Latest (Wednesday, October 7)

State health officials reported Wednesday that Pennsylvania's 2026 measles tally has reached 1,078, with 1,066 of those linked to the outbreak that began in April. Hospitalizations rose to 207 and the five deaths are now detailed: a newborn, a 6-week-old infant, an 18-year-old, a 20-year-old and a 40-year-old, all unvaccinated, with the two infants too young to have received the vaccine. The state logged 145 cases in the last seven days, and officials say the outbreak does not appear close to ending.

## Earlier

Pennsylvania's measles outbreak has passed 1,000 confirmed cases, with the state health department's dashboard recording 1,004 cases, 198 hospitalizations and five deaths as of Monday. About a third of cases have occurred in people under 18, and the state's kindergarten MMR coverage sits at 92.7 percent, below the 95 percent threshold for herd immunity.

The national picture is worse than it has been in decades: the CDC counted nearly 4,000 measles cases across the country as of October 1, the highest total since the early 1990s, with Pennsylvania running hundreds of cases ahead of South Carolina, the second-hardest-hit state.
