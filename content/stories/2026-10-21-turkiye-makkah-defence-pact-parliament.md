Turkiye has sent the Makkah Joint Defense Agreement to its parliament for ratification, three parliamentary sources said on Tuesday. The agreement is a defence pact between Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Turkiye.

Parliament Speaker Numan Kurtulmus, who is currently in Kyrgyzstan, will submit the agreement to parliament's foreign affairs commission after returning to Turkiye, two of the sources said. That referral is the first formal step in the Turkish ratification process. No vote has taken place, and this story does not report the pact's operative clauses, because the sources described the referral, not the text.

The referral is reported, not officially announced, and it is graded accordingly. What can be said on the record is the direction of travel. Saudi Arabia's defence relationships are being formalised on several tracks at once: Britain confirmed this week that RAF Typhoons will fly defensive missions over Saudi Arabia, a story this site published on October 20, and Egypt's parliament has a Saudi defence authorisation debate adjourned to October 26. A ratified Turkiye, Saudi Arabia and Pakistan instrument would add a treaty layer to that pattern, linking a NATO member's defence industry and armed forces to the Gulf's largest military spender and to Pakistan.

The next verifiable step is documentary: publication of the agreement in the parliament's commission agenda, followed by a commission report and a plenary vote. Until the text is published, claims about mutual defence obligations under the Makkah agreement should be treated as unconfirmed.
