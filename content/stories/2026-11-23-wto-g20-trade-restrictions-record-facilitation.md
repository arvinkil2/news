The number of tariff increases and other non-facilitating trade measures introduced by G20 economies rose sharply in the first half of 2026 and the stockpile of such measures reached a record level, according to the WTO's latest report on G20 trade measures, published on Monday. G20 economies account for around 80 to 85 percent of global GDP and 75 percent of international trade.

G20 economies introduced 206 new restrictive and other non-facilitating measures in the first half of 2026, compared with 194 in all of 2025. Import related measures amounted to 2.0 trillion dollars, 12.9 percent of G20 imports, in the first half of 2026, against 2.8 trillion dollars and 19.1 percent in all of 2025, the two highest levels on record and around ten times higher than those recorded over the past 15 years. Export related measures, including tariff changes and export controls, soared to 711 billion dollars, 4.8 percent of G20 exports, from 126 billion dollars and 0.9 percent in 2025, also the highest on record.

At the same time, facilitating measures accelerated. G20 economies introduced 282 new trade facilitating measures in the first half of 2026, exceeding the 225 recorded in all of 2025. Import facilitating measures covered an estimated 977 billion dollars, 6.4 percent of G20 imports, and export facilitating measures reached 560 billion dollars, 3.8 percent of G20 exports, more than five times the 2025 level. Much of the export facilitation increase reflected temporary responses to trade disruptions associated with the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, including tariff reductions and removals, the WTO said.

As of June 30, 2026, the stockpile of import related restrictive measures introduced since 2009 and still in force reached 4.5 trillion dollars, affecting 29.6 percent of G20 imports, up from 27.9 percent at the end of 2025. The export stockpile reached 1.3 trillion dollars, covering 9.1 percent of G20 exports, up from 5.4 percent. Director-General Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala said the stockpile is at its highest level since WTO trade monitoring began in 2009, welcomed the facilitation effort, and urged members to exercise restraint in introducing new restrictions.

Despite the restrictions, world merchandise trade volume growth in 2026 was revised upward on October 8 to 3.9 percent, more than double the March baseline of 1.9 percent, driven largely by surging demand for AI enabling goods, while commercial services trade growth is now forecast at 3.3 percent, down from 4.8 percent in March, largely because of the Middle East conflict's impact on transport and travel services. Trade remedy actions remained widely used: 209 new actions in the first half of 2026, with the monthly average of initiations rising to 31.3 from 28.8 in 2025, and anti-dumping the most used instrument.
