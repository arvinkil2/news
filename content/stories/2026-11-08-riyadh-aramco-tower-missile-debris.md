Latest: Explosions were heard in Riyadh early on Tuesday local time, Agence France-Presse reported, and residents received a text-message alert telling them to seek shelter because of an air threat before the kingdom's Civil Defense said the danger had passed. AFP, whose journalists in the city heard the explosions, stated no cause and reported no damage or casualties. TASS reported that coalition spokesman Turki al-Maliki posted on X that Saudi-led air defences had intercepted and destroyed a ballistic missile and a cruise missile launched by the Houthis toward Riyadh, and that a ballistic missile had earlier been intercepted over the south of the kingdom; that account is the coalition's own and is carried as such.

Latest: Saudi civil defence authorities raised a fresh alert in Riyadh on Monday and said minutes later that the danger had passed, Reuters reported, a day after the Houthi attack that damaged the Aramco building. No new strike, damage or casualties were reported in connection with the Monday alert.

Latest: Sunday was also the first day back in classrooms for Riyadh's students, who had been learning online since October 11 as a precaution after Houthi attacks on the capital, and Arab News reported that the morning's attacks came at around 8 a.m., as students were arriving and in class. Teachers told the paper that pupils stayed calm, moved away from windows and followed evacuation plans for each floor, and the report gives no casualty or damage detail for the morning attacks beyond the ministry's account below.

Saudi Arabia's Ministry of Energy said on Sunday that an administrative building belonging to Saudi Aramco in Riyadh was subjected to an attempted attack, and that falling debris caused minor damage to the building, with no injuries or deaths reported. The Saudi-led coalition said it had intercepted a missile fired by Yemen's Houthi rebels targeting Riyadh.

Yonhap, reporting Reuters and AFP, said witnesses and sources reported a high-rise in the King Abdullah Financial District was targeted and the Aramco Tower in the district was damaged, and one witness told AFP it was certain the tower had been targeted. The reporting does not establish whether the building was hit directly or damaged by debris from the intercepted missile, and Saudi authorities gave no figure for the scale of the damage.

The attack came hours after Saudi Arabia's Education Ministry, late on Saturday, announced the resumption of in-person classes in Riyadh after weeks of remote schooling imposed because of earlier strikes.
