US District Judge Loren AliKhan on Wednesday declined to issue a preliminary injunction against taxpayer funded government advertisements that promoted partisan messages and President Donald Trump ahead of the midterm elections. The decision lets an earlier temporary restraining order, which had blocked the administration from developing, airing or distributing some of the challenged ads through November 25, lapse without a longer ban replacing it.

The Democratic National Committee had argued that the television spots were illegal government sponsored propaganda intended to promote the president's campaign message weeks before the November elections. AliKhan wrote that the temporary order had been granted when the election was less than a week away. "But the November 2026 midterm elections have come and gone," she wrote, adding that the party was now claiming harm from December congressional runoff elections or from the start of the 2028 presidential cycle. "At this juncture and on this record, the court is unpersuaded," she wrote.

Democrats sued the administration in October after it began airing ads paid for at least in part by a 20 million dollar Department of Homeland Security contract. In court papers the committee challenged at least 11 government created ads that aired on television or appeared on administration social media feeds. Some glorified the president with highlight reels of speeches, promoted his tax cuts and emphasised support for law enforcement.

The White House has defended the ads as public service announcements of the kind past administrations have made to promote policies, and the Justice Department argued there was no threat of harm because the government had already stopped using public funds to air the ads. A similar case in New York also ended with a judge declining a long term ban last week. The DNC said it was disappointed and would fight on; neither court has ruled on whether the ads broke the law.
