Latest: China's Ministry of Commerce and Ministry of Science and Technology published Announcement No. 48 of 2026 on Friday, proposing to add 40 technologies to the national catalogue of technologies prohibited or restricted from export, and opening public consultation from October 16 to November 15. The list covers 23 artificial intelligence technologies, including large models, agents, compute clusters and information processing, with technology already in the public domain through open source release or published papers excluded; five biopharma technologies, including cell and gene therapy, small nucleic acid drugs and pathogen related platforms, with licensing deals that do not transfer ownership given facilitation such as automatic licensing; and 12 solar technologies covering polysilicon, wafer and cell manufacturing, including the key equipment that carries them.

The registration duty starts now, not when the catalogue is finalised. From the date of the announcement until the revised catalogue takes effect, exporters must register on the commerce ministry system before exporting any listed technology, covering transfers of ownership, licensing for use overseas, and changes of owner or controller to a foreign party, including exports already under way. That turns a consultation document into an operating control on China-West technology dealmaking in three sectors at once.

Earlier: China's Ministry of Commerce is moving to restrict exports of select biopharma technology by domestic companies, according to Endpoints News, with the proposed limits covering cell and gene therapy, siRNA and pathogen-related technology. Ordinary drug licensing deals would be preserved under the approach described, leaving the main channel for China-West life sciences deals open.

Shares in WuXi AppTec fell 2.19 percent on the report. The measure was described as a proposal being pursued, not an enacted ban, and no timeline was reported.
