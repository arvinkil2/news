The UK marketing authorisation for voxelotor (Oxbryta) has been withdrawn, the MHRA said in its November safety roundup. The sickle cell medicine was withdrawn from the UK and worldwide market in September 2024 while its benefit and risk balance was reviewed, after data suggested an unfavourable imbalance in vaso-occlusive crises and fatal events. Following the review, the agency concluded the benefits no longer outweigh the risks in the indicated population. The drug has not been available in the UK since 2024, so no patient action follows.

The same roundup records that a new GLP-1 receptor agonist, orforglipron (Foundayo), has been licensed in the UK. Unlike other GLP-1 medicines, orforglipron is not protein-based and can interact with other medicines. The agency also updated the product information for tirzepatide (Mounjaro) after a lactation study showed the drug passes into breast milk in very low amounts and is expected to be broken down rather than absorbed by a breastfed child.

The MHRA roundup also carried interim supply notices, including interim United States stock of ChloraPrep antiseptic solution to mitigate a UK supply disruption, and a Class 2 recall of one batch of Brintellix 5 mg tablets mislabelled as 10 mg, issued November 24.

In the European Union, the EMA's Pharmacovigilance Risk Assessment Committee held its monthly meeting from November 23 to 26 and neither started nor concluded any referral procedures. The committee's workload for the period covered 11 safety signals, 71 periodic safety update report assessments and 40 risk management plans.
