The Competition and Markets Authority has fined Firmenich, Givaudan and International Flavors and Fragrances a combined 85 million pounds for breaking competition law, with Givaudan fined 39,960,000 pounds, International Flavors and Fragrances 24,570,000 pounds and Firmenich 20,640,000 pounds. The CMA found the companies shared sensitive information through WhatsApp messages, calls and in-person meetings to coordinate pricing and responses to commercial requests from Unilever, including price decreases, and agreed not to hire each other's staff working on the Unilever account, while Givaudan and International Flavors and Fragrances also coordinated the level and timing of general price increases.

All three companies admitted breaking the law, approached the CMA for leniency and settled, so they cannot appeal the decision, the CMA said.

In its parallel decision, the European Commission fined the same three companies a combined 471.9 million euros for the cartel across the European Economic Area from March 2019 to March 2023, with Givaudan fined 252,363,000 euros, Firmenich 137,689,000 euros and International Flavors and Fragrances 81,915,000 euros after leniency and settlement reductions. The Commission said all three exchanged commercially sensitive information, including pricing, about their dealings with Unilever, and that Givaudan and International Flavors and Fragrances also shared information about their wider consumer and fine fragrance businesses. The Commission said its investigation was coordinated with the CMA, the Swiss Competition Commission and the US Department of Justice.
