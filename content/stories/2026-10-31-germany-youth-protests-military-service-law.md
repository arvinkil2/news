Young people demonstrated in more than 30 German cities on Saturday against the new military service law, with tens of thousands gathered at Berlin's Alexanderplatz by early afternoon and rallies also reported in Munich, Hamburg, Cologne, Hanover, Halle and Erfurt. The Bundestag passed the law on Thursday: military service is to be compulsory if not enough volunteers enlist to meet the Bundeswehr's needs, and most of the protesters were school students taking part in what organisers called an education strike, demanding that service stay voluntary alongside calls for a voting age of 16, free public transport and a rent freeze. Police described the demonstrations as largely peaceful, with isolated incidents including bottles thrown at journalists in Berlin, a burnt out car in Erfurt whose connection to the protests was unresolved, and road blockades in Hanover and Munich; in Halle, police kept the march apart from a counter demonstration by the AfD youth organisation. The turnout figures of 50,000 for Berlin and up to 80,000 nationwide are the organisers' expectations, not official counts.
