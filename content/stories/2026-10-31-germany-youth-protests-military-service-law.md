Latest: the day ended in scattered clashes after the marches. In Frankfurt, where police counted about 8,000 demonstrators, officers were injured by thrown firecrackers, Hesse interior minister Roman Poseck said via dpa, police used water cannon and broke up the gathering, and AFP reported via France 24 that a weapons shop was stormed and looted and dozens of people were detained. In Hamburg, people in black set construction barriers on fire near the main railway station as evening fell and police answered with water cannon and pepper spray, dpa reported. Bottles and fireworks were thrown during a separate evening march by left groups in Berlin, officers were punched and kicked in Bremen, and marches in Cologne and Stuttgart were stopped or dissolved over masking and pyrotechnics, while Munich stayed largely calm. The police union said the main demonstrations had been largely peaceful and blamed the later incidents on masked radical left activists. Organisers, who had called for peaceful protests, claimed more than 100,000 participants nationwide, a figure no police total has confirmed, and said they would join Europe wide protests on November 20.

Young people demonstrated in more than 30 German cities on Saturday in protests called by the youth initiative GenZ regelt, demanding more money for schools, addiction help and psychotherapy, action on rising living costs, social justice and stronger climate protection, and rejecting both compulsory military service and an expansion of state surveillance. Police put the Berlin gathering at the Brandenburg Gate at about 5,000 people, with about 2,500 in Munich and in Freiburg, more than 1,500 in Frankfurt, about 1,200 in Mainz and several thousand in Hamburg and Cologne, where turnout stayed below the 15,000 registered in advance; in total the crowds were far smaller than the 182,100 people who had said online that they wanted to take part. Police described the day as largely peaceful with no major incidents and no arrests reported: in Cologne the planned march was called off after some participants covered their faces and used pyrotechnics, and continued as a stationary rally, and in Stuttgart police halted the march several times over masking and smoke devices. Retailers in several city centres had boarded up shop windows beforehand, fearing a repeat of the violence seen in the French student protests on October 17, which the German organisers cited as their model.
