Costa Rica denied entry to Francesca Albanese, the United Nations special rapporteur on the occupied Palestinian territory, when she arrived at Juan Santamaria International Airport on Thursday to receive an honorary doctorate from the University of Costa Rica. The director general of migration, Omer Badilla, said in a video statement that Albanese was the subject of an international alert over links to terrorist acts, and that the decision was consistent with national security measures.

Albanese, an Italian national and an independent expert appointed by the UN Human Rights Council, rejected the terrorism link and called the refusal a disgrace. She joined the honorary doctorate ceremony by video call from the airport, telling an interviewer that authorities had taken her passport. UN human rights chief Volker Turk said he was deeply concerned and urged Costa Rica to reconsider, calling the decision difficult to reconcile with the country's longstanding commitment to human rights. Italy's foreign ministry said she remained in the airport's international area and had not formally entered Costa Rican territory.

Costa Rica's Constitutional Chamber granted a habeas corpus petition and ordered that Albanese not be expelled while it rules, and on Friday evening authorities moved her to a hotel near the airport before travel to Mexico on Saturday. In the habeas proceedings, the only basis authorities gave was her placement on the US Treasury's OFAC sanctions list in July 2025, according to the national human rights ombudsman.

The episode lands on a government that has deepened security cooperation with the United States and moved closer to Israel under President Laura Fernandez, a notable shift for a country long associated with multilateral institutions.
