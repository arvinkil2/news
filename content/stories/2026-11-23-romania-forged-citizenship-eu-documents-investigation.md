Forged citizenship certificates were used to obtain genuine Romanian identity documents for people including a sanctioned former Gazprom executive, the former head of a Russian state explosives manufacturer and a member of a family at the core of Ramzan Kadyrov's rule in Chechnya, according to a cross-border investigation published by Le Monde on Monday and supported by Journalismfund Europe.

Romania restores citizenship to descendants of people born on territory it held before 1940, a legal route used by hundreds of thousands of Moldovans and Ukrainians. The investigation found two fraud channels on top of it: fabricated ancestry claims supported by documents from Moldova and Ukraine, and, after Romania tightened requirements in 2024, directly counterfeited citizenship certificates that bypassed the standard process. Prosecutors' files cited by the team say 89 forged certificates were used to obtain 91 genuine Romanian ID cards between 2022 and 2024, and Romanian courts are hearing hundreds of cases to annul documents obtained this way; the team identified almost 800 annulment cases in total.

The reporting also found large scale fictitious residence registration: Romanian authorities estimate more than 18,700 citizens from former Soviet republics registered fictitious residences, and investigators found about 10,000 people from Moldova, Ukraine and Russia had obtained Romanian documents through addresses in one northern commune area, in some cases without property owners' knowledge. Prosecutors have identified a group of seven Ukrainian nationals alleged to have submitted more than 900 fraudulent naturalisation applications in 2025 alone, mainly for Russian citizens, working with local notaries, lawyers and translators. Prices cited in prosecution material reached tens of thousands of euros per client, while social media advertisements aimed at Russian speakers offered Romanian passports for roughly 4,000 to 7,000 euros.

Romania was warned about forged documents and intermediary influence in its citizenship process as early as 2018, the investigation found. Some alleged beneficiaries have travelled in the Schengen area or hold companies in Slovakia, Spain, Portugal, Latvia and the United Kingdom, which turns a Romanian administrative fraud into an EU wide screening question: how many of the documents remain valid, and how quickly can annulment cases work through the courts. Status: reported (investigation based on court and prosecution records); annulment and criminal cases ongoing.
