**Latest, October 30:** The Saudi-led coalition said its air defenses intercepted and destroyed a drone launched by Yemen's Houthis in the Mecca region on Friday morning, and that a Houthi cruise missile targeting western Saudi Arabia was also intercepted and destroyed, Al Jazeera reported, citing coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki al-Malki. The coalition also said a ballistic missile fired toward the kingdom's eastern region was destroyed on Thursday, in addition to the two intercepted over Riyadh. The Houthis denied the Mecca claim: spokesman Yahya Saree said the group did not target the holy city or its region, that its operations target Saudi oil facilities and military bases, and he dismissed the coalition's account as fabricated. Neither Al Jazeera nor the Associated Press, which carried the same coalition statement and the same denial, reported damage or casualties from the Friday interceptions, and neither interception claim has independent verification.

**Earlier, October 29:** Yemen's Houthis said on Thursday evening that they had carried out air strikes on oil facilities in Saudi Arabia, naming the refinery at Ras Tanura, a refinery at Rabigh and an oil facility at Abqaiq, and said they had again struck King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh and King Fahd Airport in Dammam, Interfax reported. Interfax named no weapon, gave no time for the alleged strikes and attributed the report to Middle Eastern media and the Houthis' own statement. It said no comment from the Saudi side had been received, and no damage, casualties or interceptions were reported. The claim is uncorroborated and is carried here as the Houthis' account.

Saudi air defenses shot down two ballistic missiles fired by the Houthi militia in Yemen toward Riyadh on Thursday, and the Coalition to Restore Legitimacy in Yemen said the missiles were "intercepted and destroyed," Arab News reported. Civil defense had issued danger warnings for Riyadh, Najran and Al-Kharj before announcing the danger had passed about 20 minutes later, and the first report carried no casualties or damage from Thursday's missiles. Saudi Civil Defence subsequently reported that shrapnel from the interceptions wounded one citizen in Riyadh and damaged a house and a vehicle, Arab News reported on October 30, carrying the Civil Defence account via the Saudi Press Agency. Two days earlier, debris from two intercepted missiles had damaged a building, a construction project and a vehicle in Riyadh. Separately, a Yemeni government military spokesman said government forces had launched 982 strikes against Houthi targets in 24 hours across Taiz, Al-Jawf, Marib and Al-Bayda, a figure no independent source has corroborated.
