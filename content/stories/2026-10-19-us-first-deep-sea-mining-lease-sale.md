The Marine Minerals Administration on Monday issued its Final Leasing Notice for sale PACM-1, the first offshore mineral lease sale in US history, offering two lease areas totalling about 31.5 million acres of seabed near American Samoa at an auction on November 19. Each area carries a minimum bid of 3 million dollars and a 20 year primary term, with production royalties of 2 percent for the first five years of commercial mining rising to 5 percent thereafter, and the areas hold potential deposits of polymetallic nodules and ferromanganese crusts containing nickel, cobalt, copper and manganese. Winning a lease would allow only preliminary work such as mapping and environmental surveys; exploration, testing and commercial extraction would need separate federal approvals, and environmental groups and American Samoa's governor have pressed for stronger safeguards and monitoring.

Update, October 20: the final notice has now been published in the Federal Register as document 2026-21462, American Samoa Outer Continental Shelf Pacific Mineral Lease Sale 1, confirming the November 19 auction as a matter of formal record. No terms in the October 19 report changed on publication.
