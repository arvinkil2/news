China's foreign ministry announced sanctions on Monday against Paraguay's communications minister Gustavo Villate and Belize's foreign minister Francis Fonseca over their countries' ties with Taiwan, per Reuters reporting from Beijing. Foreign ministry spokesperson Mao Ning called the two politicians "willing to serve as pawns" for Taiwan, saying they had "seriously interfered in China's internal affairs, spread false information about Chinese cyberattacks and attacked and smeared China." The measures, effective the same day, bar the ministers' entry visas to Hong Kong and Macau and prohibit Chinese organisations and individuals from transactions, cooperation or other activities with them, the ministry said in two notices. Paraguay and Belize are two of just 12 countries that maintain formal diplomatic relations with Taiwan, and Reuters notes the sanctions are largely symbolic, since ministers from Taiwan's diplomatic allies generally do not travel to China; such sanctions have previously been applied mainly to Western politicians for their support of democratically governed Taiwan.

The immediate triggers were the ministers' recent contacts with Taipei. Belize's Fonseca met Taiwan foreign minister Lin Chia-lung in Belize last month, and Paraguay's Villate met Taiwan deputy foreign minister Chen Ming-chi in Paraguay in June, per Reuters. The sanctions came two days after Taiwan's National Day, which Villate marked with a social media post reaffirming Paraguay's friendship with Taiwan, and shortly before the Belize embassy in Taiwan posted a message celebrating the 37th anniversary of the two countries' ties. Mao warned the remaining countries with ties to Taiwan that they should not be "repeatedly manipulated by Taiwan independence separatist forces," adding that doing so "has no future" and "will only cause them to miss development opportunities."

Beijing has been stepping up pressure on Taiwan's remaining diplomatic allies to sever ties. The last country to switch was the Pacific nation of Nauru in 2024, after Honduras ended its decades-long ties with Taipei the previous year. Neither Paraguay's Ministry of Information and Communication Technologies nor Belize's foreign ministry immediately responded to Reuters' requests for comment outside regular office hours, and Taiwan's foreign ministry had no immediate comment.
