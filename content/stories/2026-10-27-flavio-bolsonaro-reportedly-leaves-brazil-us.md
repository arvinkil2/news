**Latest, October 28:** From Miami, Bolsonaro has claimed the presidency. In an open letter shared with journalists by his allies and addressed to his 60 million voters and the Brazilian people, he declared: "I am the elected president." He claimed his victory was denied by an electoral coup carried out by President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva and the Supreme Court, and that the election was stolen by a criminal organisation that has established a tyranny. Deutsche Welle, reporting the letter with AFP, AP and Reuters, notes that he provided no evidence of electoral fraud and that international observers found Sunday's voting was fair. He said he had left the country so that he could be the leader his people deserve, and that he would lead the opposition from the United States. That is a reversal of his position on Monday, when he said on Instagram that he had no intention of leaving the country, would hand over his passports and would defend himself against what he called a travesty. As a sitting senator he has immunity from prosecution, DW reported, but he loses it in February when his Senate term ends. DW said the departure and Florida arrival, following the path his father Jair Bolsonaro took after the previous election, could deepen the rift between Brazil and the United States.

**Latest, October 28:** Argentina has confirmed the crossing on the record. Argentine immigration authorities told AFP that Bolsonaro was still in Brazil until he crossed into Argentina at 01:50 GMT on Wednesday over the Tancredo Neves International Bridge between Foz do Iguaçu and Puerto Iguazú, entering “with valid travel documents and no legal impediment”; he also holds an Italian passport, and how he travelled the more than 1,500 kilometres from Brasília to the border remains unknown. A private jet then took him and his family to Guayaquil for a brief refuelling stop and on to Florida, where, according to Department of Homeland Security border records reported by AFP, he entered the United States on his cancelled diplomatic passport. Brazilian police said there is no official record of his departure from Brazil, that they are investigating his failure to hand over his passports, and that this does not classify him as a fugitive. He has refused to recognise Sunday’s result and has promised to seek an annulment of the election, AFP reported.

**Latest, October 28:** He is in Miami. A United States official told the New York Times that Flavio Bolsonaro landed in Miami on Wednesday, and he was seen just after 9:30 a.m. leaving a plane at Opa-locka airport, a private airport near the city, in a Reuters photograph. The Times reports he crossed from Brazil into Argentina by land on Tuesday night with his wife and two daughters, then flew privately to Guayaquil, Ecuador, and took another flight from there. Brazilian authorities had cancelled his passports earlier on Tuesday in a money laundering investigation, the paper reports, and he is separately subject to a police inquiry over millions raised from a disgraced banker for a biopic of his father.

Update, Wednesday: the United States has said it will admit him. "The State Department will issue visas to Flavio Bolsonaro and his family and allow them entry into the United States," a senior State Department official said on Tuesday. Reuters reported, citing three sources, that Bolsonaro left Brazil for the United States after refusing to comply with the Supreme Court order, and that one source close to the family said he had landed in Florida, without saying where. He has yet to concede to Lula, who took 51.8 percent of the runoff vote, and President Trump said on Monday that the Brazilian vote "looked like another rigged election," without mentioning the departure. Lula has accused Washington of colluding with the Bolsonaros to restore them to power. His exact whereabouts remain unconfirmed.

Senator Flavio Bolsonaro has reportedly left Brazil for the United States after losing Sunday’s presidential runoff to President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva, according to reports by Folha de S.Paulo and Reuters citing people close to the senator, with Folha reporting that his flight was due to land in the United States within hours. The reports follow a Supreme Court order, which Bolsonaro said in a social media video on Monday had been served on him outside his father’s home, requiring him to surrender his passports; AFP cited a police official saying the passports were cancelled earlier on Tuesday and that there was no official record of a departure.

Bolsonaro is the subject of at least two federal investigations, one of them into corruption, money laundering and tax evasion allegations linked to money received from a banker to finance “Dark Horse,” a biopic about his father, former president Jair Bolsonaro. His Liberal Party acknowledged the runoff result on Tuesday.
