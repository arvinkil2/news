New limits and approval requirements for the voluntary suspension of mining operations have been introduced in New South Wales under the Mining Amendment (Mine Rehabilitation) Regulation 2026. The framework requires mining leaseholders to meet specific conditions before suspending operations, adds notification and consent requirements, and sets a maximum cumulative suspension period to prevent mines remaining in suspension indefinitely.

The state government says the amendments respond to recommendations from the NSW Audit Office's review of mine rehabilitation regulation. They also amend standard mining lease conditions to improve clarity and strengthen the enforceability of rehabilitation obligations.

Under the revised conditions, large mines can continue to be regulated to large-mine standards after an environmental protection licence is relinquished or cancelled. Leaseholders must also prepare updated rehabilitation outcome documents when a development consent is modified in a way that could affect rehabilitation obligations, and the rules clarify forward programs, rehabilitation reports and completion statements.

The regulation also creates a pathway to amend prospecting applications before a decision, introduces application fees for Group 12 minerals covering hydrogen and non-metals, and streamlines group security deposit determinations. Public consultation on the draft took place in May 2026.
