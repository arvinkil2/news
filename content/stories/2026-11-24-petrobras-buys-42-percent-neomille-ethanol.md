Petrobras said on November 23 it will acquire a 42 percent stake in Neomille S.A., a corn ethanol producer in the CerradinhoBio Group, for R$2.8 billion, about $472 million. CerradinhoBio will retain 58 percent and remain the controlling shareholder. The transaction is subject to contractual conditions including approval by CADE, Brazil's competition authority.

Of the total, R$1.8 billion is a primary capital contribution paid into Neomille through the subscription of newly issued shares. The remainder buys shares from existing shareholders and includes a contingent earn-out of up to R$397 million, and the price is subject to adjustment. Neomille operates corn ethanol plants in Goiás and Mato Grosso do Sul with roughly 800 million litres of annual capacity, expected to reach about 1.5 billion litres within two years.

The move returns Petrobras to ethanol production, Brazil's largest renewable fuel market, which it had exited earlier in the decade, and the companies point to opportunities in sustainable aviation fuel projects built on the ethanol platform. For Petrobras the structure is notable: state capital taking a large minority position alongside a private controlling shareholder, rather than the full operating control the company historically preferred in biofuels.
