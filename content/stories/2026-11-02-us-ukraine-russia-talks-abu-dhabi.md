**Latest, November 10:** The talks began in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday, the first US mediated meeting between Ukraine and Russia since February, and are scheduled to run for two days, RIA Novosti reported, with the opening set for 11:50 a.m. local time. Ukraine's delegation is headed by Rustem Umerov and includes Kyrylo Budanov, Sergiy Kyslytsya and Davyd Arakhamia; Russia's is led by Vladimir Medinsky, with deputy foreign ministers Sergei Ryabkov and Mikhail Galuzin and military intelligence chief Igor Kostyukov; Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff mediate for the United States. The European role is disputed in public. French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot told France Info that Britain, Germany and France "will indeed be in the meeting room", while Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov insisted the talks will still be held in the trilateral Ukraine, Russia and US format, with Europeans reached through separate channels. Deutsche Welle reported that EU representatives are expected on the sidelines rather than in the main trilateral sessions, and that the first meeting pairs Ukrainian negotiators with the US envoys and representatives of the United Kingdom, France, Germany, Italy, NATO and the European Commission to align positions ahead of the Russia sessions.

The European Commission's own account of that alignment meeting, in a statement dated November 9, is narrower than Barrot's. The Commission said representatives of the European partners, Ukraine and the United States met ahead of the trilateral discussions with Russia to coordinate on ending the war, that any future arrangements must respect sovereignty, territorial integrity and independent decision-making and provide credible security guarantees for Ukraine, and that the parties will keep working in partnership through the negotiating process with the objective of moving toward broader multilateral discussions involving European partners. The statement does not use the word ceasefire.

**Latest, November 3:** Russia confirmed on Tuesday that the Abu Dhabi talks will go ahead on November 10 and 11, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov told reporters, with presidential aide Vladimir Medinsky as Moscow's chief negotiator. Peskov also said the Kremlin hopes to finalise plans in the coming days for a trilateral meeting between Presidents Vladimir Putin, Donald Trump and Xi Jinping; President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has insisted Kyiv must be at the table for any settlement talks. Separately, First Lady Melania Trump will bring Russian and Ukrainian representatives to the White House on Thursday to discuss a local ceasefire in Kherson, and will deliver remarks on the Oleshky ceasefire at 11 a.m. Eastern. The six day Oleshky ceasefire she brokered ended on Monday, and Ukraine has accused Russia of using the pause to reinforce its positions, a claim reported as Kyiv's and not independently verified.

**Earlier, November 3:** Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy arrived in Berlin on Tuesday for talks with German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, seeking more air defence missiles to intercept Russian ballistic strikes and support for Ukraine's energy sector ahead of winter, Deutsche Welle reported. Zelenskyy said the agenda covered diplomatic coordination ahead of the coming meetings, air defence, energy resilience and continued pressure on Russia through sanctions and the use of frozen Russian assets. Germany has already supplied several Patriot systems, and Zelenskyy told EU leaders in October that Ukraine would need an additional 70 billion euros (78 billion dollars) to get through 2027. The visit coincides with the opening of the two day Berlin Security Conference.

US envoy Steve Witkoff said on Monday that the United States will bring together delegations from Ukraine and Russia in Abu Dhabi on November 10 and 11, and a senior US official told Reuters that Witkoff and Jared Kushner, the lead US negotiators, are expected to represent the United States. Witkoff said the discussions will cover post war security agreements, de escalation, humanitarian measures and ending the war, and that President Vladimir Putin agreed to the new round after meeting him in St. Petersburg two weeks ago.

The official said US negotiators will also meet at working level this week in Brussels with representatives from France, Germany, Britain, the European Union and NATO to discuss Ukraine's security guarantees, Europe's future security framework and economic support for Ukraine's reconstruction. The last US mediated peace talks took place in February, and Ukraine and Russia remain far apart on terms.
