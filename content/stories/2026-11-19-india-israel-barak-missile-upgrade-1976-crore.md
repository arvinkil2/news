India's Ministry of Defence signed a contract worth 1,976.40 crore rupees, about $211 million, with Israel Aerospace Industries (IAI) on November 18 in New Delhi for the upgradation and obsolescence management of the Barak point defence missile system fitted on Indian Navy warships, Marine Insight reported, citing the ministry's Press Information Bureau release. Defence Secretary Rajesh Kumar Singh was present at the signing, and the contract was concluded under India's Other Capital Procurement Procedure.

The work covers a comprehensive overhaul of the system's hardware and software, intended to add enhanced features and extend the range of the ships' anti-air defence. The Barak-1 point defence system, developed by IAI with Rafael Advanced Defence Systems, intercepts aircraft, drones and anti-ship missiles, and has been fitted since 2000 on frontline warships including the carrier INS Vikramaditya and Delhi-class destroyers. The upgrade was cleared by India's Defence Acquisition Council in August 2025 as part of proposals worth around 67,000 crore rupees.
