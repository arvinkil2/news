Senator Chris Coons plans to make Ukraine's access to SpaceX's Starshield a focus of Senate oversight, saying a hearing should determine why service was disrupted and what the changes mean for US support to Kyiv, Breaking Defense reported from Halifax on Friday. Coons, a senior Democrat on the Senate Appropriations defence subcommittee, is a leading contender to chair the Foreign Relations Committee when Democrats take control of the Senate.

"Look at Starshield being turned on and off. It is killing civilians. It is undermining the world's confidence in the United States as a supplier of key security equipment," Coons said. "Those are services paid for by American taxpayers under contract. This is not Elon Musk's personal toy."

Ukraine's charge d'affaires Denys Sienik said on November 9 that speed limitations were in place on Ukrainian occupied territory and across the contact line, which he described as the first line of defence and the first point for intercepting Russian drones, and that Kyiv still had no clarity from the Pentagon or SpaceX over the cause or who ordered it.

The oversight push is already formal. On November 13, Senators Jeanne Shaheen and Coons wrote to SpaceX chief executive Elon Musk demanding the company preserve documents and communications relating to the disruption of Ukraine's access to Starlink and Starshield, with written confirmation due by November 30. Their letter said restrictions between October 13 and 24 disrupted services used by Ukrainian forces, including interceptor drones defending civilian population centres, and that renewed Starshield disruptions were reported on November 12. It also recorded conflicting accounts of who authorised the limits, with Musk attributing them to US military decisions, Secretary Hegseth suggesting SpaceX could impose them under its terms of service, and Secretary Rubio stating that Starlink decisions rested with SpaceX.
