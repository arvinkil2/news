Duke Energy has secured regulatory approval for a new rate class for large load data centers in North Carolina, with the new rates taking effect on January 1. The North Carolina Utilities Commission approved a rate schedule for operators above 50 megawatts, a mandatory 15-year minimum contract for data centers above 100 megawatts, and minimum billing at 75 percent of contracted demand whether or not the power is used.

Operators must also make upfront payments covering direct grid interconnections, new transmission lines and upstream generation built for their load, and the rules add a stranded generation charge if a customer exits early, plus interim curtailment provisions during capacity shortages. Annual costs are expected to rise by an average of about 3.4 percent over two years for Duke Energy Progress customers and 3.7 percent for Duke Energy Carolinas customers, with residential bills up 4.7 and 4.8 percent respectively.
