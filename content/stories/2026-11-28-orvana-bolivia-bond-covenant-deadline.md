Orvana Minerals faces a bondholders' meeting on Monday after its Bolivian unit breached financial conditions on about $25 million (C$35.5 million) in bonds issued to expand its Don Mario gold and copper plant. Empresa Minera Paititi has until December 8 to restore compliance unless bondholders extend the deadline, waive the requirements or amend them. The remaining bonds mature on February 19, according to Orvana's annual results released after markets closed on Wednesday. Orvana shares dropped 3 percent to C$1.29 on Thursday morning in Toronto, valuing the company at about C$176 million.

Don Mario, about 380 kilometres east of Santa Cruz de la Sierra, is processing 1,300 tonnes of stockpiled oxide ore a day. Orvana aims to reach its design capacity of 1,700 tonnes a day by the end of December, weeks before the bonds mature. The company expects the plant to produce 22,000 to 26,000 ounces of gold and 14 million to 16 million pounds of copper in fiscal 2027, after producing 1,597 ounces of gold and 411,274 pounds of copper in the year ended September 30, including commissioning output.

The Bolivian unit breached financial covenants as of both June 30 and September 30. Orvana cited the accounting impact of Bolivia's new official exchange rate regime and road blockades in May and June that delayed work at Don Mario. The subsidiary fully repaid its first bond program on November 7. Orvana's liquidity forecasts assume lenders will not demand early repayment because of the breaches, and the company is supporting the subsidiary through intercompany funding while Don Mario ramps up; a separate $25 million prepayment facility falls due in three instalments between April and June 2027.

The bond talks follow a stronger financial year. Orvana swung to fiscal 2026 net income of about $36 million, from a year earlier loss of $12.3 million, as revenue rose 84 percent to $182 million and operating cash flow more than tripled to $57.1 million. The group ended September with $42 million in cash against $120.3 million in current liabilities, and expects to cover $111.7 million in commitments due within a year through cash, forecast operating cash flow, renewed revolving credit lines and VAT refunds. At Orovalle in northern Spain, gold production rose 29 percent to 37,775 ounces in fiscal 2026, above guidance.
