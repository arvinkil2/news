**Latest, November 3:** The reopening has entered its second phase, and the money has started coming back. Binance said withdrawals for additional assets would resume progressively from 04:00 UTC on Tuesday under its Security Incident Recovery Update No. 6, rolling out asset by asset as security and readiness checks complete, prioritised by user demand. The phase covers nine networks, including BNB Smart Chain, Ethereum, Solana, XRP Ledger, Sui, Arbitrum, Optimism, Tron and Base, and restored 51 further tokens, 31 on Ethereum and 20 on Solana, after five stablecoins earlier brought the Ethereum total restored on Tuesday to 36. On Monday the exchange also reopened withdrawals for several fiat currencies, including the US dollar, the UAE dirham, the Vietnamese dong, the Philippine peso and the Indonesian rupiah, adding to 23 currencies already available. DefiLlama data cited by Cointelegraph put net outflows on Monday at 2.57 billion dollars, down from 6.1 billion dollars on Sunday, and by Tuesday Binance led the exchanges DefiLlama tracks with about 2.3 billion dollars of net inflows over 24 hours, saying that by 03:00 UTC hourly deposits exceeded withdrawals. Binance repeated that it has replaced approximately 95 percent of the affected assets, with the remaining 5 percent consisting of less liquid tokens being acquired through market and over the counter purchases, and that losses will be covered from company resources without passing them to customers. In a November 2 security advisory the exchange warned that scammers are impersonating its representatives and offering accelerated withdrawals, priority processing, compensation help or instructions to move assets into a purported Binance vault for a fee. Separately, North Korea denied responsibility for the attack on Tuesday, with Kim Yo Jong, the sister of leader Kim Jong Un, dismissing the FBI's attribution as absurd rhetoric. Binance has not announced a fixed completion time for the remaining assets.

**Latest, November 2:** The withdrawal total has kept climbing since the reopening. By Monday morning, clients had withdrawn about 4.9 billion dollars from Binance, the Financial Times reported, citing DeFiLlama data, a sum equal to about 3 percent of the total value of assets held on the exchange the day before the cyberattack. The figure is a cumulative gross total since withdrawals resumed on Sunday, and it sits above the 4.649 billion dollars in withdrawals Binance itself counted in Sunday's first nine and a half hours. Chief executive Richard Teng said the exchange will reimburse clients for all losses arising from the hack from its own funds.

**Latest, November 1:** The reopening has its first numbers, and they show a large but orderly exit. Binance said it processed 4.649 billion dollars in withdrawals against 1.01 billion dollars in deposits between 06:00 and 15:30 UTC on Sunday, leaving net outflows of about 3.64 billion dollars, 2.1 percent of the 171 billion dollars in assets under management in its October 28 proof of reserves. Withdrawal volumes spiked at the restart, then declined and are "stabilising as expected," the exchange said, while deposit volumes "have remained substantial"; all 15 networks and assets in the first phase had reopened by 12:36 UTC, and withdrawals are now processed from newly generated wallets added to its proof of reserves list. The restoration is still partial: Binance is still testing the remaining networks and assets, including Cardano, Chainlink, Hyperliquid, Litecoin and NEAR, and its status page at 11:22 a.m. ET showed withdrawals suspended for 531 of the 552 coins listed, with 14 others, including bitcoin and ether, partly restored. DefiLlama, which tracks 131.1 billion dollars across the exchange's wallets, put Binance's 24-hour net outflows higher, at 4.05 billion dollars as of 11:37 a.m. ET, while the next ten largest exchanges it tracks took in about 368 million dollars combined. An updated reserves report based on an October 31 snapshot, computed with zero-knowledge proofs, is projected for release on Monday.

**Latest, November 1:** Binance has published its updated Proof of Reserves and says the affected withdrawals are not yet back. The snapshot, dated November 1, puts the exchange's bitcoin holdings at 593,356, about 102 percent of customer balances, with ether at about 101 percent, USDT at about 106 percent, BNB at about 111 percent, Solana at about 101 percent and XRP at about 102 percent; the page shows assets against customer balances and does not show liabilities. Separately, Sunday's reporting put the replenishment at two different levels: Le Monde, with AFP, reported Binance saying close to 90 percent of affected balances had been replenished, while Al Jazeera reported Binance saying it had replaced approximately 95 percent of the affected assets; Binance has not published a single reconciled figure. Le Monde reported withdrawals for the affected assets remain temporarily suspended while the replenishment is under final verification, and Al Jazeera reported withdrawals being restored in stages, with bitcoin, ether and some stablecoin services reopened and further services to follow as security checks are completed. Changpeng Zhao posted on Sunday that affected assets are being replenished and that withdrawals for them will reopen once the process is complete, with timing dependent on the final checks. No restart time has been given. The phased resumption reported earlier today covers networks and assets outside the affected set; for the tokens actually taken, day three of the suspension is not the end of it.

**Latest, November 1:** Binance has begun resuming withdrawals. The exchange said on Sunday that withdrawals had resumed in phases after the suspension that followed the attack, and that the first batch network opened in phase one was the bitcoin network, AFP reported. Binance had earlier said it was preparing to resume the first phase between 0600 and 0900 GMT on Sunday. The resumption is phased rather than a full restoration at once: Binance said it is prioritising tokens based on the past week's user demand data, so that each network and asset pair can be restored with stability and security checks completed before access reopens. "User funds remain safe. Binance will absorb the financial impact of the stolen funds, not our users," the exchange said on X. AFP gives no new coverage percentage; the funding behind the reopening remains the more than 6.34 billion dollars Binance said on Saturday it had set aside from company funds to cover losses from a theft it estimates affected about 6.3 billion dollars of assets. The reopening lands on the schedule Binance set on Friday, a phased restoration within about 34 hours landing on Sunday, and it is the first operational test of whether outflows stay orderly.

**Latest, October 31:** President Donald Trump has declined to endorse the FBI's attribution. Answering journalists at Mar-a-Lago on Saturday evening, hours after the bureau's alert, Trump said the United States is now figuring out what happened and does not know whether the theft is connected with North Korea, and that if it is, Washington will discuss the matter with North Korea. The remarks, reported by TASS, stop short of the bureau's finding that Pyongyang was responsible, and they are the first suggestion that the theft could be raised with North Korea directly rather than handled only as a law enforcement matter.

**Latest, October 31:** The FBI has formally attributed the Binance theft to North Korea. In an alert dated October 31, the bureau said the Democratic People's Republic of Korea was responsible for the theft of more than 6 billion dollars in virtual assets from Binance on or about October 29, and that it refers to this North Korean malicious cyber activity as TraderTraitor. The FBI, together with the US Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation, said it has observed the actors converting the stolen assets primarily into Ethereum, and expects them to convert them further using DeFi and instant swap services as they prepare later laundering stages. The bureau urged THORNode operators, RPC node operators, instant exchanges, DeFi services, blockchain analytics firms and other virtual asset service providers to block transactions with or derived from the addresses the actors are using, and published a long list of Ethereum addresses it says are holding or have held assets from the theft and are operated by or closely connected to TraderTraitor actors. The attribution confirms, as a government finding, the assessment TRM Labs and Chainalysis gave on October 30; Binance's own figures for the incident remain an estimated total impact of about 6.3 billion dollars, above the bureau's more than 6 billion dollar theft figure.

**Latest, October 31 (corrected):** Binance says it remains on track to reopen withdrawals on Sunday, subject to security checks and final confirmation, CNBC reported on Saturday. The exchange's own second security incident update, issued on Friday evening, set the target as a phased restoration of withdrawals within about 34 hours, which lands on Sunday, November 1, with the first proof of reserves snapshot taken on Saturday and its results due by Monday, November 2. An earlier version of this update, published at 13:09 Toronto time, carried a more detailed schedule, with account band batch times on Sunday and a full reopening at 1:00 p.m. Eastern on Monday, and a specific account of the breach route involving a phony Ledger Live app. Those details do not appear in Binance's statement or in CNBC's article and are withdrawn. On the route, CNBC reported that how the attack took place is still unclear, and that co-chief executive Richard Teng has said it involved a more complex process rather than misuse of a single engineer's permissions.

**Latest, October 31:** Binance says its coverage of the hack losses has reached 90 percent. In a statement reported by TASS and RIA Novosti, the exchange said assets in BTC, ETH, USDT, BNB, SOL, USDC, XRP, USD1, RLUSD and ZEC are secured in full, and that it will continue purchasing tokens until coverage reaches 100 percent for each of them. The statement gives no dollar amount for the share covered or for the tenth still outstanding, does not name the tokens making up the remainder, and sets no date for completion; the allocation behind the coverage remains the more than 6.34 billion dollars from its own funds and the SAFU reserve that Binance announced on October 30, against its preliminary estimate of about 6.3 billion dollars of affected assets.

**Latest, October 30:** Binance has moved from pledging to cover the losses to paying them. The exchange said it has begun recovering affected assets for users and has reserved more than 6.34 billion dollars from its own funds to settle the incident, of which 5 billion dollars is already held in the same tokens that were affected. The reserve does not include the roughly 3 billion dollars of frozen or protected assets, which Binance said are still being recovered and verified. The remaining shortfall is being bought through market and over-the-counter channels so that the assets returned match the tokens taken, and Binance warned that this may take some time because some of the tokens have low liquidity and over-the-counter settlements are not instant. The exchange said it would keep using company resources to cover confirmed shortfalls and that all user funds will ultimately be verified publicly through its Proof of Reserves. Separately, Reuters reported that Binance is working with law enforcement around the world, including the FBI and the US Secret Service, citing a person familiar with the situation; the Secret Service did not respond to Reuters and no agency has confirmed its role on the record.

**Latest, October 30:** The attribution has landed, and it points at North Korea. TRM Labs said it attributes the theft with high confidence to TraderTraitor, a North Korean state-sponsored hacking unit, based on on-chain and off-chain intelligence, and estimated that about 6.36 billion dollars in digital assets moved out of Binance wallets across 10 blockchains, most of it within about 20 minutes. TRM said preparations began at least 36 hours before the theft, with the attackers using the Tornado Cash mixing service to fund their wallets in advance and obtaining transaction signatures ahead of time, features it said match earlier North Korea linked heists. Chainalysis co-founder Jonathan Levin said his firm also assessed that the hack was likely carried out by TraderTraitor, citing links to previous attacks. The FBI has previously identified the same unit as responsible for the 1.5 billion dollar Bybit hack in February 2025. Binance has not confirmed the attribution, and it remains an investigator assessment rather than an established finding. On recovery, TRM Labs policy head Ari Redbord, a former federal prosecutor, told CNBC that a freeze stops the money moving and recovery then follows through legal process, with stablecoins the most recoverable asset class because Tether and Circle can freeze, burn and reissue; Chainalysis research head Eric Jardine said returning frozen assets can take months or years and has no standard timeline. Fundstrat's Sean Farrell estimated about 3.3 billion dollars remained unfrozen and that Binance's SAFU emergency fund, worth about 1.2 billion dollars, could cover roughly a third of the remaining losses with the balance from the exchange's own funds, and said the breach does not currently appear to create meaningful solvency risk for Binance, with the key tell being how quickly withdrawals resume and whether outflows stay orderly.

**Latest, October 30 (reopening):** Binance now has a reopening plan. In a service update at 04:36 UTC, co-founder and co-chief executive Yi He said deposits remain unaffected and operate as normal, that the team is rebuilding the withdrawal service and its related address controls, and that withdrawals are expected to resume within 48 hours. Later on Friday the exchange said its investigation had found additional points of breach beyond the compromised laptop it first identified, while remaining on track to resume withdrawals in about 45 hours. On the size of the loss, the estimates should be read as a range, not a settled figure: Binance puts total impact at about 6.3 billion dollars with about 3 billion dollars frozen on chain, TRM Labs puts the outflow at about 6.36 billion dollars and Chainalysis at about 6.4 billion dollars, while the earliest on-chain outflow estimates on Thursday ran from about 2.5 billion to 3.3 billion dollars. Binance has not confirmed a final stolen total, and how much of the estimated impact is ultimately lost depends on the frozen assets.

**Earlier, October 30:** Binance put a number on the breach and a route for it. “The estimated total impact sits at around $6.3B,” the exchange said in a statement on October 30. “We have worked with blockchain partners to freeze affected assets, with ~$3 billion frozen on-chain so far. Binance will cover all losses from the SAFU fund and the company’s funds. User funds are safe.” Its initial investigation, the statement said, suggests the incident “may involve a compromised DevOps engineer’s laptop, which allowed an attacker to gain access to a service container and submit unauthorized withdrawal transactions,” and it has “found no evidence of any wallet private key leak.” The exchange promised a detailed technical post-mortem once the investigation is complete and an updated Proof of Reserves once remediation is done. The 6.3 billion dollars is Binance’s estimate of total impact; it is not a confirmed theft total, and it sits above the on-chain outflow estimates of 2.5 billion to 3.3 billion dollars reported on Thursday and above Arkham’s roughly 5.84 billion dollar figure for the holdings of wallets it labels as the attacker’s. Cointelegraph reported that Blockaid, the security firm that first sized the outflows, assessed the operation as a compromise of signing infrastructure without a key extraction, and described it as very similar to the February 2025 Bybit breach, in which attackers compromised a developer’s credentials at the wallet provider.

**Earlier, October 29:** Binance suspended all deposits and withdrawals as a precaution while it investigated, and said at least 3 billion dollars in funds had been frozen following what it called a potential security issue, Al Jazeera reported. The exchange said it would cover all losses, that user funds are safe, that its security teams are working to trace and freeze further assets, and that it will publish a full post-mortem when its investigation is complete; it also said it has engaged law enforcement. Bitcoin fell about 5 percent after the incident, dropping below 77,000 dollars. The frozen total is Binance's figure for assets frozen, not a confirmed amount stolen, and the exchange has still not confirmed a total taken.

Earlier, Binance had limited withdrawals as its investigation continued, Axios reported on Thursday evening. Analysts and traders who follow the crypto markets estimate that between 1.7 billion and 5.7 billion dollars was taken in the suspected attack, a range that brackets the on-chain outflow estimates below and is not a Binance figure: the exchange has not confirmed the total taken or who was behind the attack, and Zhao and a Binance spokesperson both said user funds are safe, Axios reported.

Binance is investigating a suspected hack after billions of dollars left its hot wallets in about 40 minutes on Thursday afternoon. Blockchain security firm Blockaid put the suspicious transfers at roughly 3.3 billion dollars across Ethereum, Arbitrum and Avalanche, including about 2.5 billion dollars from the wallets known as Binance 14 and Binance 16 and about 780 million dollars of USDC from a Binance smart account, Cointelegraph reported. The outflows ran from 20:24 to 21:04 UTC, the receiving addresses were newly created, and funds were pushed onward within minutes, according to Crypto Briefing. Blockchain researcher Spreek first flagged the activity, reporting that assets from a Binance-linked Ethereum wallet had moved to a new address through a contract call funded via Tornado Cash, and that wallets connected to the transfers had begun selling USDT on decentralized exchanges.

Changpeng Zhao, the exchange's founder and former chief executive, addressed the outflows on X at about 21:15 UTC. "User funds are safe! It does look like a hack on Binance," he wrote, crediting Tether and Circle with quick freezes and adding that he no longer manages Binance but, as a shareholder, expects all user funds to be fully covered. Binance itself has confirmed less. "Our teams are aware of reports of a potential security issue and are actively investigating," the company said, warning users to distrust unofficial links and messages. A spokesperson told Cointelegraph the exchange had not confirmed any impact on user funds.

The stablecoin issuers moved against the receiving wallets. Tether chief executive Paolo Ardoino said the company had frozen more than 1.567 billion dollars of USDT linked to the suspected exploit, and Circle chief executive Jeremy Allaire said Circle had frozen more than 800 million dollars of USDC in coordination with law enforcement. Together the freezes exceed 2.3 billion dollars. Ether and other tokens have no equivalent freeze mechanism, which is why the estimates of what left the exchange, from about 2.5 billion to 3.3 billion dollars, are not a measure of the final loss. Arkham Intelligence labeled a set of wallets "binance-hacker-1029" and put their holdings at about 5.84 billion dollars; Cointelegraph notes that figure is the wallets' reported holdings, not a confirmed estimate of stolen funds.

Crypto prices fell as the reports spread. Bitcoin dropped below 80,000 dollars and ether below 2,300 dollars, BNB fell by as much as 6 percent, and roughly 600 million dollars of leveraged positions were liquidated within an hour, according to Coinglass data reported by FXStreet and Crypto Briefing, which put liquidations at 627.7 million dollars, mostly long positions.

What is not yet known is whether Binance's preliminary account of the route, a compromised DevOps laptop into a service container, is the whole of it, who was behind the attack, and how much of the 6.3 billion dollar estimated impact will ultimately count as lost after the frozen assets are dealt with. The exchange's promised post-mortem, and whether the frozen stablecoins are returned, will settle those questions.
