**Latest, October 30:** The attribution has landed, and it points at North Korea. TRM Labs said it attributes the theft with high confidence to TraderTraitor, a North Korean state-sponsored hacking unit, based on on-chain and off-chain intelligence, and estimated that about 6.36 billion dollars in digital assets moved out of Binance wallets across 10 blockchains, most of it within about 20 minutes. TRM said preparations began at least 36 hours before the theft, with the attackers using the Tornado Cash mixing service to fund their wallets in advance and obtaining transaction signatures ahead of time, features it said match earlier North Korea linked heists. Chainalysis co-founder Jonathan Levin said his firm also assessed that the hack was likely carried out by TraderTraitor, citing links to previous attacks. The FBI has previously identified the same unit as responsible for the 1.5 billion dollar Bybit hack in February 2025. Binance has not confirmed the attribution, and it remains an investigator assessment rather than an established finding. On recovery, TRM Labs policy head Ari Redbord, a former federal prosecutor, told CNBC that a freeze stops the money moving and recovery then follows through legal process, with stablecoins the most recoverable asset class because Tether and Circle can freeze, burn and reissue; Chainalysis research head Eric Jardine said returning frozen assets can take months or years and has no standard timeline. Fundstrat's Sean Farrell estimated about 3.3 billion dollars remained unfrozen and that Binance's SAFU emergency fund, worth about 1.2 billion dollars, could cover roughly a third of the remaining losses with the balance from the exchange's own funds, and said the breach does not currently appear to create meaningful solvency risk for Binance, with the key tell being how quickly withdrawals resume and whether outflows stay orderly.

**Latest, October 30 (reopening):** Binance now has a reopening plan. In a service update at 04:36 UTC, co-founder and co-chief executive Yi He said deposits remain unaffected and operate as normal, that the team is rebuilding the withdrawal service and its related address controls, and that withdrawals are expected to resume within 48 hours. Later on Friday the exchange said its investigation had found additional points of breach beyond the compromised laptop it first identified, while remaining on track to resume withdrawals in about 45 hours. On the size of the loss, the estimates should be read as a range, not a settled figure: Binance puts total impact at about 6.3 billion dollars with about 3 billion dollars frozen on chain, TRM Labs puts the outflow at about 6.36 billion dollars and Chainalysis at about 6.4 billion dollars, while the earliest on-chain outflow estimates on Thursday ran from about 2.5 billion to 3.3 billion dollars. Binance has not confirmed a final stolen total, and how much of the estimated impact is ultimately lost depends on the frozen assets.

**Earlier, October 30:** Binance put a number on the breach and a route for it. “The estimated total impact sits at around $6.3B,” the exchange said in a statement on October 30. “We have worked with blockchain partners to freeze affected assets, with ~$3 billion frozen on-chain so far. Binance will cover all losses from the SAFU fund and the company’s funds. User funds are safe.” Its initial investigation, the statement said, suggests the incident “may involve a compromised DevOps engineer’s laptop, which allowed an attacker to gain access to a service container and submit unauthorized withdrawal transactions,” and it has “found no evidence of any wallet private key leak.” The exchange promised a detailed technical post-mortem once the investigation is complete and an updated Proof of Reserves once remediation is done. The 6.3 billion dollars is Binance’s estimate of total impact; it is not a confirmed theft total, and it sits above the on-chain outflow estimates of 2.5 billion to 3.3 billion dollars reported on Thursday and above Arkham’s roughly 5.84 billion dollar figure for the holdings of wallets it labels as the attacker’s. Cointelegraph reported that Blockaid, the security firm that first sized the outflows, assessed the operation as a compromise of signing infrastructure without a key extraction, and described it as very similar to the February 2025 Bybit breach, in which attackers compromised a developer’s credentials at the wallet provider.

**Earlier, October 29:** Binance suspended all deposits and withdrawals as a precaution while it investigated, and said at least 3 billion dollars in funds had been frozen following what it called a potential security issue, Al Jazeera reported. The exchange said it would cover all losses, that user funds are safe, that its security teams are working to trace and freeze further assets, and that it will publish a full post-mortem when its investigation is complete; it also said it has engaged law enforcement. Bitcoin fell about 5 percent after the incident, dropping below 77,000 dollars. The frozen total is Binance's figure for assets frozen, not a confirmed amount stolen, and the exchange has still not confirmed a total taken.

Earlier, Binance had limited withdrawals as its investigation continued, Axios reported on Thursday evening. Analysts and traders who follow the crypto markets estimate that between 1.7 billion and 5.7 billion dollars was taken in the suspected attack, a range that brackets the on-chain outflow estimates below and is not a Binance figure: the exchange has not confirmed the total taken or who was behind the attack, and Zhao and a Binance spokesperson both said user funds are safe, Axios reported.

Binance is investigating a suspected hack after billions of dollars left its hot wallets in about 40 minutes on Thursday afternoon. Blockchain security firm Blockaid put the suspicious transfers at roughly 3.3 billion dollars across Ethereum, Arbitrum and Avalanche, including about 2.5 billion dollars from the wallets known as Binance 14 and Binance 16 and about 780 million dollars of USDC from a Binance smart account, Cointelegraph reported. The outflows ran from 20:24 to 21:04 UTC, the receiving addresses were newly created, and funds were pushed onward within minutes, according to Crypto Briefing. Blockchain researcher Spreek first flagged the activity, reporting that assets from a Binance-linked Ethereum wallet had moved to a new address through a contract call funded via Tornado Cash, and that wallets connected to the transfers had begun selling USDT on decentralized exchanges.

Changpeng Zhao, the exchange's founder and former chief executive, addressed the outflows on X at about 21:15 UTC. "User funds are safe! It does look like a hack on Binance," he wrote, crediting Tether and Circle with quick freezes and adding that he no longer manages Binance but, as a shareholder, expects all user funds to be fully covered. Binance itself has confirmed less. "Our teams are aware of reports of a potential security issue and are actively investigating," the company said, warning users to distrust unofficial links and messages. A spokesperson told Cointelegraph the exchange had not confirmed any impact on user funds.

The stablecoin issuers moved against the receiving wallets. Tether chief executive Paolo Ardoino said the company had frozen more than 1.567 billion dollars of USDT linked to the suspected exploit, and Circle chief executive Jeremy Allaire said Circle had frozen more than 800 million dollars of USDC in coordination with law enforcement. Together the freezes exceed 2.3 billion dollars. Ether and other tokens have no equivalent freeze mechanism, which is why the estimates of what left the exchange, from about 2.5 billion to 3.3 billion dollars, are not a measure of the final loss. Arkham Intelligence labeled a set of wallets "binance-hacker-1029" and put their holdings at about 5.84 billion dollars; Cointelegraph notes that figure is the wallets' reported holdings, not a confirmed estimate of stolen funds.

Crypto prices fell as the reports spread. Bitcoin dropped below 80,000 dollars and ether below 2,300 dollars, BNB fell by as much as 6 percent, and roughly 600 million dollars of leveraged positions were liquidated within an hour, according to Coinglass data reported by FXStreet and Crypto Briefing, which put liquidations at 627.7 million dollars, mostly long positions.

What is not yet known is whether Binance's preliminary account of the route, a compromised DevOps laptop into a service container, is the whole of it, who was behind the attack, and how much of the 6.3 billion dollar estimated impact will ultimately count as lost after the frozen assets are dealt with. The exchange's promised post-mortem, and whether the frozen stablecoins are returned, will settle those questions.
