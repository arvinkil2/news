**Latest (Monday, October 12):** the Gulf of Mexico is coming back online. Energy majors Shell, Chevron and BP were seeking to redeploy personnel and restore production at their platforms in the Gulf of Mexico after Hurricane Isaias made landfall early Saturday, per a Reuters-relayed report read in full. The restoration effort follows the Energy Department's emergency release of 4 million barrels from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve over the weekend.

The death toll from Hurricane Isaias rose to four on Saturday, according to the BBC and CNN, and the U.S. Energy Department approved an emergency transfer of 4 million barrels of oil from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve in response to the storm, per RIA Novosti and Kommersant, which named ExxonMobil and BP America as recipients. The storm struck near Destin, Florida late Friday as a Category 2 hurricane with 105 mph winds before weakening to a post-tropical cyclone.

Hurricane Isaias struck near Destin, Florida late Friday as a Category 2 hurricane with 105 mph winds, leaving at least two people dead after trees fell on homes in Escambia County, Florida, and Birmingham, Alabama. More than 868,000 customers were without power across Florida, Alabama and Georgia on Saturday morning, per PowerOutage.us, before the storm weakened to a post-tropical cyclone. The National Hurricane Center warns of flash, urban and river flooding, with 4 to 8 inches of rain expected across southern Alabama, Florida's panhandle and Big Bend, southwest Georgia, and up to 15 inches in localized areas. Alabama's governor warned of possible spinoff tornadoes; Florida's governor said power crews were assessing damage.

Isaias made landfall the same week Hurricane Simon strengthened to Category 4 off Mexico's Pacific coast, keeping both U.S. coasts under storm watch and regional power and fuel logistics under pressure. It is one of the latest-forming Atlantic hurricanes on record, tying the mark of October 8, 1905; all eight earlier 2026 named storms stayed at tropical-storm strength or below.
