Nigeria's federal government is set to inaugurate Project WHEAT-NESS, a programme to strengthen the country's wheat-based consumer foods industry and improve its competitiveness, the Federal Ministry of Industry, Trade and Investment said in a statement issued in Abuja on Tuesday. The launch and stakeholder mobilisation will be held at the Transcorp Hilton in Abuja, bringing together government officials, private operators, financial institutions, researchers and development partners.

The scale of the problem is the ministry's starting point. Nigeria consumes and imports an estimated six to seven million metric tonnes of wheat annually, leaving millers, bakeries and food manufacturers exposed to international price moves, supply chain bottlenecks and naira volatility. Wheat feeds directly into bread, noodles and other flour-based staples, making the import bill a standing transmission channel into consumer food prices.

The programme is organised around four intervention areas: value chain development covering farm inputs, aggregation, milling, processing, logistics and retail; enterprise development for micro, small and medium sized businesses in the sector; a Made-in-Nigeria marketing campaign to build consumer confidence in local foods; and a proposed Wheat-Based Consumer Foods Value Chain Development Fund to mobilise financing for the industry. Minister of State for Industry John Enoh said the initiative would strengthen local production, processing, enterprise productivity and market access.

The honest limitation is measurement. The government has yet to set measurable targets for domestic output, import substitution or cost reduction, and recent official claims put domestic wheat production at about 420,000 tonnes a year, a small fraction of consumption. WHEAT-NESS is therefore a value chain and financing framework first; whether it moves the import share will depend on targets and funding that have not yet been announced. Wheat in the site market tables fell 0.78 percent on the day, a reminder that the global price side of Nigeria's exposure moves independently of Abuja's programme calendar.
