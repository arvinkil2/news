Japan's core consumer price index, which excludes fresh food but includes fuel, rose 2.1 percent in September from a year earlier, Statistics Bureau data showed on Friday, up from 1.7 percent in August, matching the median market forecast and exceeding the Bank of Japan's 2 percent target for the first time in nine months. Headline inflation was also 2.1 percent, up from 1.9 percent, and the index excluding both fresh food and fuel, the measure the Bank of Japan watches as a gauge of demand-driven pressure, rose 2.4 percent, up from 1.9 percent and above the 2.3 percent expected.

Reuters reported that higher fuel costs from the prolonged Middle East conflict, elevated import prices driven by the weak yen and price pressures from robust AI demand are blunting the effect of government fuel subsidies. The Bank of Japan, which raised its key rate to a 31-year high of 1.25 percent last month, is expected to stand pat at next week's meeting but may revise up its inflation forecast and signal readiness to keep raising borrowing costs, sources told Reuters.
