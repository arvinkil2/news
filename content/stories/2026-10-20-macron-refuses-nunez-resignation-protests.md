**Latest:** President Emmanuel Macron said on Thursday that France will ban what he called illegitimate protests in the coming days, while demonstrations organised by groups such as trade unions may go ahead on alternative routes outside city centres to protect businesses, Al Jazeera and Reuters reported. Speaking after a meeting with intelligence chiefs and ministers, Macron called last Saturday's riots political and seditious acts by ultra-left groups whose aim, he said, is to bring down the Republic, an attribution the hard left has challenged since the prime minister first made it on Tuesday. Thursday's protests were banned in parts of Paris until 8:00 pm, including around the Elysee, parliament and the main stations, and in Lyon, Lille and Grenoble, and local authorities have banned all demonstrations planned for Saturday in Lyon. Macron said he will chair a defence council on Friday and announce new decisions afterwards. Interior Minister Laurent Nunez said elite tactical police units and a military unit normally used in counterterrorism operations on French soil will also be mobilised this weekend. The student union UNEF has called rallies for Saturday and the yellow vest movement has filed plans for a march through central Paris, while Paris businesses have begun boarding up shop windows and Hermes said it had stepped up security at its French stores.

**Latest:** French police said protests will be banned in parts of Paris on Thursday from 8:00 am to 8:00 pm, covering the Elysee presidential palace, both houses of parliament, the Louvre and the Champs-Elysees, and also Bastille and Republique squares and the Chatelet-Les Halles shopping area, Arab News reported, citing AFP. Speaking in Doha, Macron said leaders in campaign mode, on the other side of the Atlantic and in the region, had made what he called stupid comments on the unrest, without naming President Donald Trump or Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, both of whom had commented on it on Tuesday. Justice Minister Gerald Darmanin said 2,500 people had been taken into custody during and after the protests, of whom 174 had been jailed and 917 formally prosecuted, and an Ifop poll carried in the same report put Marine Le Pen ahead in the first round of the presidential election, with Jean-Luc Melenchon her most likely runoff opponent.

**Latest:** Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu blamed the weekend's looting on an ultra-left movement that, he said, seeks to create profound disorder in the country all the way through to the presidential election, and promised a massive security deployment ahead of further protests called for Saturday, France24 reported. Interior Minister Laurent Nunez said 60,000 police and security personnel had been deployed last Saturday and that he expected protests on a similar scale this Saturday. The interior ministry put Saturday's turnout at 550,000 against organisers' 1.1 million, nearly 3,000 shops were affected by looting, and Lecornu said furlough support for affected businesses would start on Tuesday, with interest-free loans available from Friday and demonstrations banned in Marseille on Monday. Nearly 150 people had been jailed and more than 800 brought before the courts, the AFP report said. Hard-left leader Jean-Luc Melenchon challenged Lecornu to produce evidence for the ultra-left claim or apologise.

**Latest:** Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu said on Tuesday, in a post on X, that new curfews will be imposed where necessary, with local authorities coordinating with mayors on whether to impose them in their prefectures, Al Jazeera reported. Monday night passed without major incidents, police confirmed, after Saturday's demonstrations drew more than half a million people and ended in looting and arson in numerous locations, with more than 2,500 people arrested. Digital Minister Anne Le Henanff is due to meet social media bosses on Tuesday over looted goods being sold online, Lecornu meets union officials on Wednesday and political parties on Thursday, and demonstrators have called further protests for this Saturday, which the main trade unions have not backed.

President Emmanuel Macron refused the resignation of Interior Minister Laurent Nunez on Monday, after a weekend of violent protests over rising living costs and student grievances in cities including Lyon. The Elysee said Nunez submitted his resignation on Sunday, and that the president refused it given the necessity for continued leadership of law enforcement at this time.

Macron coupled the refusal with an admission. He spoke of failures that led to the violence and asked Nunez to take all necessary steps to ensure transparency over the failures in planning and intelligence that preceded it, and to put in place new operational methods for security forces. Protesters in Lyon burned a tourism office and carried placards depicting the president with a guillotine, according to the agency photograph captioning the report.

The decision keeps the interior minister in place while making the presidency the owner of the inquiry into what went wrong. It also lands in the same week as the National Assembly's budget votes, covered separately in today's brief, where the government's spending plans are one of the grievances driving the street movement.
