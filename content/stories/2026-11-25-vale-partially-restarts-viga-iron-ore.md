Vale (NYSE: VALE) is partially resuming operations at its Viga iron ore mine in southeastern Brazil after receiving approvals from local and federal governments for the mine, which halted operations in January 2026 after water overflows. The company said it had received the required approvals from the state of Minas Gerais, with backing from the Federal Prosecution Office, and from Brazil's National Mining Agency and the state's environment and sustainable development department.

The restart leaves Vale's 2026 iron ore production guidance unchanged at between 335 million and 345 million tonnes. Shares in Vale were down 4.2 percent to $9.38 by midday Tuesday in New York, valuing the company at $39.9 billion, as iron ore miners fell with the broader metals complex.

The water overflows affected operations at the Viga and Fabrica mines in central Minas Gerais. The local government fined Vale 1.7 million reais (about $287,979) for environmental damage, CNN Brasil reported. Vale has since committed to cleaning waterways and monitoring water quality in surrounding regions. The Viga clearance follows Vale's September announcement that the Fabrica mine would reopen, marking another step toward restoring the affected operations in the region.
