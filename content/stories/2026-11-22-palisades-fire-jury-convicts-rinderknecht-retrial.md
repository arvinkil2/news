The man accused of sparking last year's deadly Palisades Fire was found guilty of arson charges on Friday, after his first federal trial earlier this year ended in a mistrial, the Associated Press reported. Jurors convicted Jonathan Rinderknecht, 30, of lighting a fire on January 1, 2025, that burned undetected deep in root systems before flaring back up on January 7.

The Palisades Fire killed 12 people as it incinerated entire neighbourhoods in Los Angeles and Malibu, becoming one of the most destructive wildfires in California history. Law Commentary reported the fire destroyed more than 6,800 structures, and that Rinderknecht was convicted of two federal counts, destruction of property by means of fire and arson affecting property used in interstate commerce.

Prosecutors said Rinderknecht, a former Uber driver, deliberately ignited the small brush fire known as the Lachman Fire shortly after midnight on January 1, 2025, near a hiking trail overlooking Pacific Palisades. His first trial ended with jurors deadlocked, reported as 10 to 2 in favour of acquittal.

Rinderknecht faces a minimum of 5 years and a maximum of 40 years in federal prison. Sentencing is scheduled for March 17, 2027, before US District Judge Anne Hwang.
