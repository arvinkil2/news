Metals rebounded at Wednesday's close after the liquidation that dominated the previous two weeks. Gold closed at 3,589.44 dollars a troy ounce, up 0.93 percent from Tuesday's 3,556.37 dollar close. Silver rose 1.16 percent to 41.40 dollars, platinum rose 1.97 percent to 1,301.20 dollars and palladium rose 1.00 percent to 863.80 dollars. The gold to silver ratio closed at 86.7.

The estate market tables, on their front series, show the same direction with larger moves: gold up 1.43 percent to 3,586.20 dollars, silver up 3.95 percent to 41.63 dollars and copper up 2.18 percent to 5.45 dollars a pound. DTN's outside markets summary, on December futures, had copper up 1.85 cents at 5.3875 dollars, with December gold down 1.20 dollars at 3,560.70 dollars and December silver down 8 cents at 40.70 dollars at the grain close, before the later spot rebound.

Context matters more than the day's gains. Gold is down 3.6 percent over the past week and 17.8 percent so far in 2026, and Wednesday's close sits 33.6 percent below the all-time closing high of 5,405 dollars set on January 29, 2026, within a 52-week range of 3,538.19 to 5,405 dollars. Silver is down 8.12 percent on the week and 42.49 percent this year. Wednesday's session therefore reads as a pause and partial short covering in a correction, not a reversal, consistent with Tuesday's steadier trade.

Thursday brings no US settlement to test the move, with markets closed for Thanksgiving, so the next full read on whether the rebound holds comes with Friday's shortened session and the December Federal Reserve decision now dominating the rates backdrop for precious metals.
