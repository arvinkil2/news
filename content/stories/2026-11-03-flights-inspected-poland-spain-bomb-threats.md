The count widened sharply on Tuesday. Al Jazeera, carrying Reuters, reported at least seven flights targeted across Europe in one day, plus a bomb threat against the Crown Iris, a cruise ship sailing from Cyprus to Israel, and no explosive device found on any aircraft or on the ship. Ryanair took four of the threats: a Dublin to Rome flight was escorted by two Italian fighter jets into Rome Fiumicino, a Lisbon to Rome flight was checked and cleared, the Manchester to Murcia flight activated the airport emergency protocol on landing, and a Dublin to Sofia flight was mentioned among the Bulgarian cases alongside a Wizz Air flight from Bergamo. In Britain, the Metropolitan Police said it was aware of threats against four aircraft due to depart from or arrive at Heathrow and a further warning at Manchester, with no security issues identified. Osprey Flight Solutions described seven confirmed bomb threats in a single day as extraordinary; no official link between the cases has been established, and several countries are investigating whether they were coordinated.

Three passenger flights were inspected after bomb threats in Poland and Spain, two after landing in Warsaw and one at Murcia in southern Spain, the BBC reported on Tuesday. A Boeing 737 Max from London Heathrow made an unscheduled landing at Warsaw Modlin, which later said air operations had resumed, a Ryanair flight from Manchester with 121 people on board including crew was inspected at Murcia, and a flight from Copenhagen to Warsaw Chopin had landed safely on Sunday after a similar threat. The cases follow a Wizz Air flight from London Luton that made an emergency landing in Budapest a day earlier, escorted by a Hungarian fighter jet, where security checks found nothing.
