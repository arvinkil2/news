DoorDash has put the next layer of financing in place for its acquisition of Maplebear Inc., which does business as Instacart. In a Form 8-K filed on November 23, the company said it has entered a senior unsecured delayed draw term loan facility of $1.792 billion under a Term Loan Credit and Guaranty Agreement with JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. as administrative agent. DoorDash may increase commitments before funding to a maximum of $2.0 billion.

The term loan is available in a single draw around the closing of the acquisition and will fund the purchase, the refinancing of certain Instacart debt and related fees. It has no required amortization, matures on the third anniversary of funding, may be prepaid without penalty, and carries an initial margin of 1.00 percent over Term SOFR, stepping down to 0.875 percent or 0.750 percent at higher credit ratings. A ticking fee of 0.08 to 0.10 percent on unfunded commitments starts on February 25, 2027.

At the same time, DoorDash upsized its revolving credit facility by $1.5 billion, from $2.0 billion to $3.5 billion, maturing August 5, 2031, usable for working capital, general corporate purposes and certain permitted buybacks and acquisitions. The effectiveness of the term loan permanently reduced the $13.0 billion 364 day bridge facility committed at signing on October 28 to $11.208 billion.

The merger itself, under an Agreement and Plan of Merger dated October 28, 2026, will merge a DoorDash subsidiary into Instacart, leaving Instacart as a DoorDash subsidiary. Each permanent financing layer that replaces bridge capacity lowers the refinancing risk attached to the largest acquisition in the food delivery sector.
