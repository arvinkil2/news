The European Commission proposed on Wednesday a one year delay to the EU's methane emissions rules for imported oil and gas, which were due to take effect on January 1, 2027 and would have required foreign suppliers to monitor and report methane emissions. Under the proposed changes, importers would have until May 31, 2029 to prove that their oil, gas and coal imports during 2028 complied with methane monitoring and reporting rules equivalent to those of the EU, and the Commission said the postponement of the import related provisions is designed to avoid additional pressures on the security of supply of imported fossil fuels in the current geopolitical context. The move followed pressure from France, whose president wrote to the Commission president last month requesting a delay and warning of legal risks for importers, and from the United States, Europe's biggest liquefied natural gas supplier, which warned in June that the law could disrupt fuel deliveries; EU countries and the European Parliament must still negotiate and approve the delay, and Czechia and Italy are among those that have demanded a longer, three year postponement.
