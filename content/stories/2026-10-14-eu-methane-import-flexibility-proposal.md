The European Commission proposed an amendment to the EU Methane Regulation, adopted in 2024 as the first EU wide law to cut methane emissions in the energy sector, that would move importers to one harmonised compliance date of January 1, 2031 for the requirements on exporters' measurement, reporting and verification equivalence, instead of phased deadlines running from 2027 to 2030. Penalties for importers would in principle apply only from 2032 and only where an importer cannot show it took all necessary measures, and the Commission said it would keep pushing for wider recognition of exporting countries' own regimes as equivalent. Brussels put the savings at close to 1 billion euros to 2031, including about 320 million euros a year in administrative savings between 2028 and 2031 across roughly 130 importers of crude oil, natural gas and coal, and the proposal now goes to the European Parliament and the Council for adoption.
