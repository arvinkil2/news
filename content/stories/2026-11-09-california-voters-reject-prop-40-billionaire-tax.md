California voters rejected Proposition 40, a ballot measure for a one-time 5 percent tax on the total wealth, including shares, of residents worth more than 1.1 billion dollars, with the outcome projected on Monday after nearly a week of counting since the midterm election. The tax, backed by healthcare union leader Dave Regan of SEIU-United Healthcare Workers West, was aimed at about 200 billionaires in the state and was estimated to raise about 100 billion dollars to backfill federal healthcare funding cuts and state budget gaps. It failed amid divisions among Democrats and labour unions: Governor Gavin Newsom opposed it over concerns the wealthy would leave the state, Google co-founder Sergey Brin gave more than 100 million dollars to a political organisation funding measures that could block it, according to the Los Angeles Times as cited by the BBC, and Regan pledged to seek a billionaire tax again in 2028.
