TechCrunch asked what Nvidia's security team knew, and when, before Jensen Huang's resignation. The company's longtime chief security officer and head of product security, David Reber, stepped down in August, citing health reasons after a diagnosis of young onset Parkinson's disease more than two years earlier, and was replaced by Sasa Zdjelar, who previously spent 17 years at ExxonMobil. TechCrunch reported that nothing public connects either change to the Megaspeed matter, that neither man responded to its questions, and that Nvidia has not answered them either. The piece is analysis rather than new disclosure, and it is carried here as a single-sourced item: the open question it raises, whether concerns about Huang's relationships or the federal scrutiny of Megaspeed ever reached the security function, now sits with the board's independent investigation.

NTT Docomo Business will pilot using waste heat from a data center in or near Tokyo to warm the rearing water of a land based Pacific white shrimp farm, in a joint project with IMT Engineering, Hibiya General Equipment and NTT Green and Food announced this week and set to run until March 2028. The project, part of a Tokyo Metropolitan Government initiative on unused data center heat, aims to verify the cost reduction and business viability of heat reuse aquaculture and to establish a model that can be deployed at other sites. Low temperature data center heat has few established uses, which is the constraint the pilot is designed to test.

Data center cooling firm Airsys has led a pre-seed round in ThermAI, a UK startup whose six GPU nodes heat water for households while selling the compute remotely through its FlightDeck platform. Terms were not disclosed. The system pairs Nvidia RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition GPUs built by 2CRSi with Airsys spray cooling that recovers outlet water at up to 60 degrees Celsius, stored in Sunamp heat batteries. ThermAI is targeting deployment in 100 social housing homes in 2027 after initial pilots, and the University of Nottingham's Professor Jonathan Hirst has signed a letter of intent to take part as a compute customer for drug discovery and molecular dynamics research.
