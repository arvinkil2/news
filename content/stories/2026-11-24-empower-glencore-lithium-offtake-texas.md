Empower EIT announced on Monday a binding offtake deal with Glencore for 100,000 tonnes of lithium over 10 years, alongside a strategic equity partnership with Maverick Power chief executive Tom Currier, who joins its board of directors. The Dallas based lithium developer is preparing to advance its flagship Texas project toward commercial production, betting on oil and gas expertise, proprietary direct lithium extraction technology and a modular development strategy.

The company says its Project Summit in the Texas Panhandle will be the first commercial deployment of a model that integrates resource ownership, drilling operations, lithium extraction and battery grade lithium carbonate production under one roof, through its EmTECH Powered by LiMAX platform. Chief executive Patrick Dowling said that when a global powerhouse like Glencore commits, the market has spoken.

Unlike developers pursuing large, capital intensive facilities, Empower plans to start with smaller units that can be expanded as operations and market conditions warrant. It plans to drill its first two wells at Project Summit and develop an initial 250 tonne per year lithium production unit, with commissioning and first commercial sales targeted for the fourth quarter, and to expand through 1,250 tonne trains to an ultimate 5,000 tonne per year operation. The company is targeting commercial production in 2027.

The company has not yet disclosed detailed resource estimates or project economics for Summit. Management says internal modelling indicates a 5,000 tonne per year facility could operate at breakeven even under the depressed lithium prices recorded in June 2025, and Empower also plans to license its extraction and conversion technology. It opened a Washington office in October and has been engaging with federal agencies and financing institutions about support for US lithium production, without disclosing an award.
