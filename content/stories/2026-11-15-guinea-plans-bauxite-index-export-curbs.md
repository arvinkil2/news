Guinea plans to establish a national pricing benchmark for bauxite, to be known as the Guinea Bauxite Index, as part of a wider effort to support prices for its top export. Mining and Geology Minister Bouna Sylla told reporters in Conakry late on Friday, November 13, that the index could be established as early as next year and would be modelled on the London Metal Exchange, with the aim of providing greater transparency and curbing price volatility.

The index is only one half of the plan. Sylla said the government is also looking at limiting exports of the ore, after a rapid expansion of shipments drove global prices down. Guinea exported 165 million tonnes of bauxite in the first nine months of this year, putting it on track to exceed the 183 million tonnes shipped in all of 2025. The minister said regulation of bauxite production could be published before the end of this month, because, in his words, the oversupply is not good for the market, for producers, or for the country in terms of tax revenue.

Bauxite is the raw material for alumina, which is smelted into aluminium. Unlike copper or aluminium itself, it has never had a widely accepted exchange or index price, and cargoes trade on negotiated terms that vary with alumina content, reactive silica and freight. Most Guinean ore goes to China, where it is refined into alumina for the country's smelters, so any Guinean benchmark or export limit lands directly on Chinese refining costs.

The proposal is not new. Sylla first announced the index in July 2025, and analysts at CRU noted at the time that a workable benchmark would need published adjustments for alumina content, silica levels and logistics costs, on a weekly or monthly basis. What is new this week is the explicit link to supply discipline: an index paired with production regulation and possible export limits is a pricing strategy, not just a transparency measure.

No index methodology, launch date beyond next year, or export limit level has been published. The test for the plan is whether Chinese buyers and other importers accept a Guinean benchmark in contract negotiations, and whether Conakry follows through with the production regulation the minister promised before the end of November.
