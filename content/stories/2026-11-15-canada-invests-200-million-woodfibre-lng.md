Canada will invest C$200 million, about $140 million, in the Woodfibre LNG export project under construction near Squamish, British Columbia, north of Vancouver. Reuters reported the commitment on November 13, citing a person familiar with the matter, and Bloomberg reported that Energy Minister Tim Hodgson announced the funding in Vancouver the same day, describing it as a conditional grant paid if the company hits benchmarks for investing in Canada.

The money is for capacity, not completion. It will fund cooling system upgrades and the commissioning and mooring of floating LNG storage, lifting the plant's planned output by up to 24 percent, from 2.1 million tonnes a year to between 2.5 million and 2.6 million tonnes. The owners are also exploring a second phase for the project, according to the Reuters report.

Woodfibre is 70 percent owned by Pacific Energy Corporation, a unit of the Singapore-headquartered RGE group founded by Indonesian businessman Sukanto Tanoto, and 30 percent by Calgary-based Enbridge. All of the plant's initial output is contracted to BP under long-term agreements. Construction began in 2023 on the west side of Howe Sound, and the project presents itself as the world's first net zero LNG export facility.

The two reports differ on timing, and the difference matters for when the added capacity could ship. Reuters reporting put the owners' operations target at late 2027, while Bloomberg described the facility as about 75 percent complete and scheduled to start operations next year. Neither report reconciled the two, so the start date should be treated as unsettled until the project company confirms it.

Politically, the investment is export diversification in cash form. The federal government is supporting an expansion of west coast liquefaction aimed at overseas, principally Asian, buyers, following the September approval of a major expansion at the Shell-led LNG Canada project in Kitimat.
