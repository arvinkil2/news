PG&E has filed a 10-year Electrical Undergrounding Plan with the California Office of Energy Infrastructure Safety, proposing to move about 5,000 miles of electricity distribution lines underground between 2028 and 2037. The work would span more than 30 counties, concentrated in the high-wildfire-risk areas of northern and central California where undergrounding most reduces ignition risk.

The utility claims $117 billion in long-term benefits through wildfire prevention and avoided outages, plus roughly $6 billion in avoided vegetation-management, operations and maintenance costs. The filing lands in a complicated capital context: in early September PG&E said it would defer about $2 billion of investments next year as part of a strategic review of its businesses, after concluding that a proposed revamp of California's wildfire response did not do enough to protect the company and its peers from disaster costs. An amended state Senate bill has raised fresh concerns around wildfire-liability exposure.

PG&E, the parent of Pacific Gas and Electric Co., emerged from bankruptcy in 2020 after its equipment was linked to several deadly fires; California's utilities have faced growing wildfire-related liabilities ever since. Whether regulators bless a 5,000-mile undergrounding program while the company is simultaneously pulling back $2 billion of near-term capex is the question the filing now forces.
