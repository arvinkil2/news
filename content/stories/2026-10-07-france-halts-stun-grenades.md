## Latest: fourth protest round holds despite concessions

The fourth nationwide round went ahead Thursday, and it looked like the first three. High school pupils across France launched a fresh day of protests, blocking entrances to hundreds of schools for more than a week running, with squads of riot police deployed and occasional violent clashes. The Associated Press reported students barricaded the Helene Boucher high school in northeastern Paris with garbage bins before police moved in with tear gas. At least a dozen demonstrations were planned across France later in the day, including a rally at the Place de la Bastille in Paris, where tear gas and a water cannon were fired at protesters.

The scale keeps climbing. Police have made more than 6,600 arrests since late September, when students, most of them under 18, began protesting to demand more education funding, according to the Interior Ministry. The Education Ministry said 375 schools were fully or partially closed Thursday.

Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu addressed the nation Wednesday evening, promising a plan to respond to the crisis by the end of October and denying that security forces had been given "orders to confront" the young people. The students were unconvinced. "We don't want words, we want action," one 17-year-old told AFP from a march in Nantes, and another outside a Rennes high school said she was "disappointed and unconvinced" by the remarks.

France24 reported Wednesday that more than 6,100 people have been arrested since the school demonstrations began in mid-September, a cumulative tally that frames the scale of the unrest. The article itself was unreachable from this desk, so the figure stands on France24's reporting alone pending corroboration.

Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez ordered the pause "until the facts have been clarified," the BBC reported Wednesday, after conflicting accounts of the Lens incident: authorities say the boy picked up the grenade, while students allege he was hit directly by an officer's shot. The police watchdog IGPN has opened 18 investigations into whether officers used proportionate force during the protests.

The move follows weeks of escalating school demonstrations that began in mid-September, with hundreds of thousands marching Tuesday, 488 arrested, and more than 500 schools fully or partially closed Wednesday. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu blamed the radical-left LFI party for sowing chaos ahead of April presidential elections; a fourth protest round is planned for Thursday.
