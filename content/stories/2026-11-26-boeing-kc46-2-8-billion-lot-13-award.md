The US Air Force has awarded Boeing 2.8 billion dollars to build a new batch of KC-46 Pegasus air refuelling tankers, the Pentagon announced on November 24. The award, for the programme's 13th production lot, covers 15 aircraft, with work expected to wrap up by the end of June 2030. The aircraft are manufactured at Boeing's facilities in Seattle.

The award brings the total number of refuellers under contract to 203, including foreign sales, according to Boeing. The company has delivered 116 Pegasus aircraft to the Air Force, six to Japan and two to Israel, and the US cleared a further sale to Qatar in August.

The KC-46, based on the 767 commercial jet, is being bought to recapitalise the ageing KC-135 fleet. Lot 13 is the final lot under the programme's original fixed-price contract from 2011, under which development and production problems drove billions of dollars in losses for Boeing. The Air Force intends to continue production under a new extension programme ordering up to 75 additional aircraft from Lot 14, but officials have said future aircraft will come with price increases.

The service has also pledged not to move forward with the extension unless the jet's lingering category 1 deficiencies are resolved. The key planned upgrade is the RVS 2.0 remote vision system, which operators use to guide the refuelling boom, expected to be fielded from early 2028. The Air Force is meanwhile launching a new analysis of its longer-term refuelling plans, with an emphasis on enhancing existing aircraft.
