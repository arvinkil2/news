Guinea is seeking backing from US, Chinese and multilateral financiers for a planned $1.9 billion deep-water port, Mines Minister Bouna Sylla told Reuters on November 16. Sylla said feasibility studies are complete and the government is engaging potential financiers including the US International Development Finance Corporation, Chinese investors, the World Bank, the African Development Bank, the Africa Finance Corporation and the International Finance Corporation, though he gave no timeline for selecting investors or starting construction.

The port is planned with a 20-metre draft able to handle 200,000-tonne Capesize vessels. It would be Guinea's biggest infrastructure project since Simandou, the roughly $20 billion iron ore development developed by Rio Tinto, China's Baowu Steel and other partners, which shipped its first output last year.
