The Food and Drug Administration on Monday approved Bayer's drug to reduce the recurrence of stroke, making available the first in a novel class of medications that hold the promise of preventing blood clots more safely, STAT reported. The drug, asundexian, will be sold under the brand name Sunharbo and inhibits the clotting protein factor XIa, a mechanism thought to stop the formation of harmful clots without increasing the risk of bleeding that limits the use of existing blood thinners in this setting.

In the Phase 3 OCEANIC-STROKE trial, which enrolled people who had already had a stroke and were taking common stroke treatments, 6.2 percent of patients who took Sunharbo experienced a secondary stroke, against 8.4 percent on placebo, a 26 percent risk reduction; STAT calculated that 53 patients need to be treated for one year for one recurrent stroke to be prevented. China approved asundexian under the same Sunharbo brand name on October 26, the first approval of a factor XIa inhibitor anywhere in the world.
