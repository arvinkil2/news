A US court of appeals on Tuesday affirmed the validity of the defaulted bonds issued by Venezuela's state oil company PDVSA and maturing in 2020, saying pre-approval from the country's National Assembly was not required, Reuters reported. The Second Circuit decision upholds the district court judgment that the issuance was valid, against arguments that Venezuela's constitution made legislative approval a condition of the debt.

The ruling matters beyond the bondholders because the 2020 bonds are secured by a majority stake in the parent of Citgo Petroleum, the Houston based refiner that is Venezuela's crown jewel overseas asset. With the validity challenge removed at the appeals level, the holders' collateral claim stands while a separate court supervised process over Citgo's parent and other Venezuelan creditors continues.
