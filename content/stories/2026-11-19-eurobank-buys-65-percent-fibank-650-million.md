Eurobank has agreed to acquire a 65 percent stake in Bulgaria's First Investment Bank, or Fibank, from its founders Ivailo Mutafchiev and Tzeko Minev for 650 million euros, the lenders said on Wednesday, in a deal that values the whole bank at about 1 billion euros and would make Eurobank the country's leading lender with a market share of around one fifth.

The price of 6.73 euros per share will be paid entirely in cash. Immediately after the 65 percent purchase completes, Eurobank will make a mandatory tender offer for the remaining shares: the state-owned Bulgarian Development Bank, which holds about 18 percent, has committed to tender its stake, and the remaining 17 percent is free float. Eurobank then plans to merge Fibank with its Bulgarian subsidiary Postbank in the fourth quarter of 2027, creating Bulgaria's largest bank by assets and deposits.

Fibank is Bulgaria's fifth-largest bank, with consolidated total assets of 10.6 billion euros, gross loans of 5.6 billion euros, customer deposits of 8.2 billion euros and tangible book value of 1.05 billion euros at September 2026. Eurobank will fund the purchase from excess capital and a planned 750 million euro share capital increase through an accelerated bookbuild for qualified investors, subject to an extraordinary general meeting on December 9, 2026; its anchor shareholder Fairfax Financial, with about 32.9 percent of the voting rights, supports the deal. Eurobank put its pro-forma CET1 ratio at close to 14 percent at closing, and targets more than 70 million euros a year of after-tax cost savings once the merger is fully synergised, which it does not expect until 2029. Completion of the stake purchase is expected in the first half of 2027, subject to regulatory approvals.
