**Latest.** Jensen Huang has said he would be honored to return as Nvidia's chief executive if the board decides his return is in the best interest of shareholders, employees, the company and America, in a statement posted on X, his first public statement since his resignation. Huang wrote that he "did not live up to the standards expected of everyone at NVIDIA" and had made "serious mistakes in my personal life", that he owns his mistakes completely, and that he is committed to cooperating with the board's independent investigation. An open letter circulated earlier this week calling for his return attracted more than 10,000 signatures from current and former Nvidia employees, as well as Elon Musk, Amjad Masad and Travis Kalanick, and argued Huang was removed without meaningful due process. An unnamed source on Nvidia's board told The Washington Post on Monday there were no plans to reinstate him, and a senator reached by Politico called reinstatement "one of the worst ideas I've heard today".

Former Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang has agreed to testify before the Senate Banking Committee, a spokesperson for Chair Tim Scott confirmed on Tuesday. Scott had formally requested appearances by Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick at separate hearings and had warned Huang could be subpoenaed if he refused to appear voluntarily; the date Scott sought was December 3, and no hearing date has been confirmed.

The panel's scrutiny centres on whether China gained access to advanced US AI chips and on Huang's relationship with Alice Huang, the Megaspeed executive US authorities suspect of funnelling chips to Chinese companies. Huang has also agreed to testify at a closed-door hearing before three House committees running their own investigation, and the Senate appearance would be his first public testimony before Congress.
