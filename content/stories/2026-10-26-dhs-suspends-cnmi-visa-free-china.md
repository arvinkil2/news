The Secretary of Homeland Security has suspended the CNMI Economic Vitality & Security Travel Authorization Program, which since February 2025 has allowed prescreened nationals of China to enter the Northern Mariana Islands without a visa for up to 14 days. The suspension takes effect at 2 p.m. EDT on October 30, covers new applicants and travelers already approved, and will stay in place while DHS evaluates the program with the State and Interior departments. Travelers already in the islands may finish their authorized stays, but future visits will require a consular visa.

DHS said more than 3,000 Chinese nationals used the program in fiscal 2025, vetted by Customs and Border Protection rather than a consular officer, and judged that the volume creates unacceptable security and law enforcement risks. The notice points to the risk of intelligence collection against the Tinian air base now being restored and Guam's installations, to earlier cases of Chinese nationals smuggled by boat from the CNMI to Guam and later found near military sites, and to birth tourism straining the islands' medical system. It acknowledges the cost to CNMI businesses built on Chinese tourism and says the risks outweigh it.
