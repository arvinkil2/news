The European Commission said Hungary satisfactorily fulfilled 126 of the 127 milestones and targets behind its first and final payment request for 10 billion euros under the Recovery and Resilience Facility, the centrepiece of NextGenerationEU. The one item not fulfilled in full is an interim evaluation report on the effectiveness of tax rules adopted to reduce tax avoidance; if the Commission confirms its assessment after Hungary's observations, the amount corresponding to that milestone will be proportionally reduced from the 10 billion euros. The request's flagship measures include joining the European Public Prosecutor's Office, establishing the Integrity Authority and steps on judicial independence and public procurement transparency.

Poland completed 76 of the 77 milestones and targets behind its sixth and final request for 12.7 billion euros, covering clean energy, business and innovation, digitalisation, healthcare and food security. The one target not fully met concerns upgrading and extending ports that service offshore wind farms, and it faces the same proportional reduction mechanism. For both countries, the Commission has sent its preliminary assessment to the Economic and Financial Committee, which has four weeks to give its opinion, and each government has two months to present observations before a final assessment; payment for the greenlit milestones can follow a positive committee opinion and a Commission payment decision. Both final requests were submitted on September 30, 2026, ahead of the facility's closure at the end of 2026.
