Nigeria Mining Week, running October 12 to 14 in Abuja, heard investors and stakeholders press for clearer rules, better coordination and stronger security as preconditions for putting more capital into Nigerian mining, Vanguard and the News Agency of Nigeria reported. A separate strand of the programme targeted support for artisanal miners, who account for much of the country's actual production in gold and other minerals but operate largely outside formal channels.

A mining company voice at the event also called for a comprehensive approach to tackling illegal mining, a recurring theme in Nigerian sector debates alongside site invasions and kidnapping risk in parts of the north and middle belt. No new fiscal terms, licence rounds or security deployments were announced in the reporting.

Nigeria has promoted solid minerals for years as a diversification away from oil, with lithium, gold and other deposits drawing exploration interest, but formal investment has lagged the geology. The week's consistent message from the buy side was procedural rather than fiscal: predictable rules and physical security first.

The event closes today. Any follow through would show up in licence processing, formalisation schemes for artisanal miners and state level agreements, none of which were detailed this week.
