Sebastiaan Bock, the newly appointed chief executive of Barrick International, met Mali's prime minister Abdoulaye Maiga in Bamako on Wednesday, the first visit by a foreign executive of the gold miner since Barrick and the Malian state settled their long running dispute a year ago. Bock arrived in the capital on Tuesday leading a company delegation, and Maiga congratulated him on his appointment as the two sides discussed the future of the partnership, according to a government statement.

Bock told the prime minister that Barrick would comply fully with Mali's new mining code, the law at the centre of the dispute. The code, adopted in 2023, raised taxes on miners and increased the state's share of projects, and Barrick resisted its application to Loulo Gounkoto, its flagship complex in western Mali and one of the largest gold mines in Africa.

The standoff escalated through 2024 and 2025: four Malian Barrick executives were detained in November 2024 and held until the settlement, an arrest warrant was issued for then group chief executive Mark Bristow, and officials seized gold stocks held at the mine. The dispute ended in November 2025 with an agreement that restored control of the complex to Barrick and settled the competing claims.

Bock, previously Barrick's chief operating officer for Africa and the Middle East, handled the Mali file through its final phase. His programme continues with a visit to the Loulo Gounkoto complex on Thursday and Friday, where he is expected to meet managers and workers and inspect operations. Barrick kept the mine running throughout the dispute, and the Bamako visit is the public test of whether the settlement holds in day to day operations under the new code.
