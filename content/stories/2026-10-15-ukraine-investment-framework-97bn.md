The Ukraine Investment Framework has unlocked 9.7 billion euros of investment in Ukraine through EU guarantees and blended finance, the European Commission said, including up to 650 million euros directed at private sector projects, as the bloc shifts part of its support from grants to instruments that pull in other people's money.

Guarantee structures are the Commission's answer to arithmetic: the EU budget cannot fund Ukrainian reconstruction at the scale the damage assessments describe, but guarantees can multiply a euro of budget into several euros of lending by the EIB, the EBRD and national development banks. The framework's private sector window is the test of whether any of that capital reaches companies rather than only sovereign and municipal borrowers.

The announcement sits alongside this week's 2.86 billion euro military disbursement in the politics edition; together they describe the two halves of the EU's Ukraine position, weapons now and leveraged reconstruction finance next, with the frozen assets question still unresolved above both.
