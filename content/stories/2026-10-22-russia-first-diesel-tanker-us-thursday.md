**Latest, October 23:** A second tanker carrying Russian diesel is expected in Norfolk, Virginia, on November 2, five days ahead of the New York-bound Agioi Fanendes, gCaptain reported. The Vietnamese-flagged PVT Azura, reportedly carrying 109,000 barrels of Russian diesel, left St. Petersburg on October 15 and is signaling Norfolk as its destination, according to VesselFinder data cited by gCaptain. The tanker is reportedly not headed to the Port of Virginia's commercial terminals but to a facility associated with US military operations; gCaptain said the precise discharge location and the cargo's intended use have not been independently confirmed, and the US Coast Guard did not respond to its request for comment.

**Latest, October 23:** A Malta-flagged tanker carrying 40,000 metric tons of Russian diesel is heading towards New York following the agreement with US President Donald Trump, LSEG data showed on Friday, Reuters reported. The medium-sized tanker Agioi Fanendes was loaded with 40,000 metric tons of ultra-low sulphur diesel in the Russian Baltic port of Primorsk on October 22, according to shipping data, and is expected to arrive in the United States on November 7. Bloomberg ship-tracking put the cargo at almost 43,000 metric tons, just under 320,000 barrels, and said the vessel intermittently signalled New York after leaving Primorsk on Thursday. Last week, other export diesel cargoes were loaded at Primorsk but their final destinations were not clear, with most vessels signalling Gibraltar, the Atlantic Coast or Port Said, according to LSEG data cited by Reuters.

**Latest:** Czech President Petr Pavel said a ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine was "quite close" until President Trump lifted sanctions on Russian diesel, in an interview conducted on Tuesday with the Axel Springer Global Reporters Network, which includes Politico. Pavel said coordinated United States and European sanctions on Russian oil, oligarchs and other industries were putting Russia "under strain," and that the diesel deal gave Russia "time to breathe" and a moral boost. "It will be much harder now," he said, adding that the deal gave Russia new hope it could disregard Ukraine and Europe and deal directly with the United States, potentially with China: "I don't know yet if we can call it betrayal, but I would call it a mistake." The White House said on Wednesday that Trump "successfully negotiated" the deal to supply the global market with "massive amounts of diesel and bring prices down."

Russia will send its first diesel tanker to the United States on Thursday, Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak said on Wednesday, with the vessel carrying 40,000 tonnes of diesel and the paperwork nearly complete, and he did not name the port of departure. Novak said more shipments would follow and that Russia would supply as much as it could to meet US demand, under the agreement in which President Trump said Russia would export 300,000 tonnes initially, 500,000 tonnes in November and 1 million tonnes after that. Russia lifted its diesel export ban on October 9 after Ukrainian drone attacks on its refineries had caused domestic shortages, and analysts cited by Reuters expect the planned deliveries to have little effect on global diesel prices.
