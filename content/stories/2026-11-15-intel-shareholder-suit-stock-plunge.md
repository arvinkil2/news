Shareholders sued Intel on Friday in a proposed class action in federal court in San Jose, California, accusing the chipmaker of defrauding them by concealing how a prolonged slowdown in personal computer sales and an inventory glut would weigh on revenue. The suit, led by the City of Southfield Fire and Police Retirement System, a Michigan pension fund, seeks unspecified damages for shareholders between July 23 and October 29, 2026, and also names Chief Executive Lip-Bu Tan and Chief Financial Officer David Zinsner as defendants.

Intel shares fell 28.6 percent on October 30, wiping out about $140 billion of market value, one day after the company projected lower than expected fourth-quarter revenue and profit and forecast a prolonged slowdown in PC sales. The plaintiffs say Intel had the motive and opportunity to defraud because it raised capital in mid-August, halfway through the quarter, selling $23 billion of stock at $95 a share to fund the buildout of its contract manufacturing business. Intel did not immediately respond to a request for comment on Sunday.
