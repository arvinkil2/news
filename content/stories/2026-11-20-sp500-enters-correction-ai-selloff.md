**Latest, Sunday evening.** US stock futures jumped on Sunday evening after the bruising week that pushed all three major indexes into correction territory. At 20:10 ET, S&P 500 futures were up 2.2 percent to 7,090.0 points, Nasdaq 100 futures were up 2.9 percent to 25,845.75 points and Dow Jones futures were up 1.6 percent to 48,964.0 points, Investing.com reported. The Cboe Volatility Index had closed on Friday at 35.89, its highest level since the tariff turmoil of April 2025, and investors now look to Wednesday’s October PCE price index, the Federal Reserve’s preferred inflation measure, in a holiday-shortened week with US markets closed on Thursday for Thanksgiving.

US stocks closed sharply lower on Friday, with the S&P 500 falling 3.7 percent to 6,910.39 and entering a correction, defined as a fall of more than 10 percent from a recent record. The benchmark has now fallen for seven consecutive sessions, its longest losing streak since February 2020, and sits more than 10 percent below the all-time high it set on October 6.

The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.3 percent to 22,617.73, wiping out its gains for 2026 and leaving it down 2.7 percent for the year. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 1.9 percent, or 949.73 points, to 48,147.33, and also closed more than 10 percent below its recent record. For the week, the S&P 500 lost 8.6 percent, the Dow 8 percent and the Nasdaq 10 percent, the worst weekly performance for US stocks since April 2025, when the announcement of reciprocal tariffs set off a global selloff. The Nasdaq's weekly fall was close to its worst since March 2020.

Chip stocks led the decline. Advanced Micro Devices fell 12.6 percent and Broadcom 12.1 percent, and the PHLX Semiconductor Index sank 9.2 percent. Nvidia fell for the ninth time in ten sessions, on a day when this site reported that SEC enforcement staff are examining potential accounting issues at the company. Anthropic, whose record initial public offering was meant to steady sentiment toward AI names, fell about 9 percent in its second session of trading.

The selling caps a week in which investors have reassessed the AI buildout on several fronts at once: the scale of data center spending and the borrowing behind it, tighter export restrictions on advanced chips, and expectations of tighter Federal Reserve policy. The rout began in Asia, where the Nikkei, Kospi and Taiex all fell more than 5 percent on Friday, and spread through miners and copper before the US open. Whether the fall stays an AI valuation reset or widens into credit now depends on the coming week's data and on whether buyers return at correction levels.
