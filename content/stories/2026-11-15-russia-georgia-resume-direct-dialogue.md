Russia and Georgia are resuming direct dialogue at the level of special representatives, Interfax reported, citing Izvestia and the Russian Foreign Ministry. Moscow said it has already established contact with Giorgi Zurabashvili, the prime minister's special representative for relations with Russia, who was appointed by Prime Minister Irakli Kobakhidze on October 21 and also serves as first deputy foreign minister; Russia's counterpart is Deputy Foreign Minister Mikhail Galuzin.

The ministry said Russia "invariably advocates building pragmatic and mutually beneficial relations with Georgia" and supports Tbilisi's decision to resume the dialogue. The talks are to focus on practical tasks, expanding trade, tourism, transport links and cultural exchanges, with the two countries' agricultural agencies already working to simplify mutual supplies of farm products. No date for a first meeting was given. Georgia and Russia have had no diplomatic relations since the 2008 war, and Kobakhidze said in October that the appointment did not signal a new stage in relations.
