The Central Bank of Ireland says risks stemming from developments in the rest of the world have increased and remain high, pointing to AI-driven debt financing, rising sovereign risk, ongoing geopolitical tensions and elevated cyber security risks, and warning that the potential for one shock to trigger and amplify other risks at the same time also remains high. The bank says global growth is increasingly dependent on AI-related investment that is increasingly funded by debt, which, combined with high valuations and complex and circular financing structures, has increased risks to the global financial system, and that sovereign bond yields have risen across the curve, limiting fiscal space to respond to future shocks.

Governor Gabriel Makhlouf said Ireland's financial system has proved resilient, with households and businesses holding healthy balance sheets and loan performance at historically healthy levels for most borrowers, but that rising energy and debt costs and any disruptive labour market effects from AI could challenge that resilience. The bank is maintaining its existing macroprudential policy stance for its capital buffers, mortgage measures and policies for non-banks.
