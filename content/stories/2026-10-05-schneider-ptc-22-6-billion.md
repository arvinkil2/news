France's Schneider Electric said Monday it had agreed to buy PTC in an all-cash deal valuing the US software company's equity at around $22.6 billion, confirming reports from Sunday. The $205-per-share offer implies an enterprise value of $23.7 billion for Boston-based PTC and represents a 42.3 percent premium to its last closing price — and a 46.1 percent premium to the 30-day volume-weighted average.

The financing structure, disclosed in deal coverage, calls for roughly $5-6 billion of new equity alongside $16-17 billion of new debt, per secondary reporting. Schneider estimates €250 million of cost synergies by year three and approximately €800 million of revenue synergies from cross-selling across complementary customer footprints. The transaction is expected to be immediately low-single-digit accretive to adjusted earnings per share in the first full year. Watch the equity-raise pricing given the share-price fall.

Investors hated the price, at least initially. Schneider's shares fell nearly 10 percent in early Paris trading as investors weighed the size of the acquisition and the premium, wiping close to €15 billion ($17 billion) off its market capitalisation. PTC shares jumped 34.4 percent in US premarket trading. Jefferies noted that AI disruption fears are weighing on software valuations — which allows acquiring PTC at a decade-low valuation but could still weigh on Schneider after the deal. Rival Autodesk rose about 3.8 percent premarket on read-through expectations for industrial-software valuations. The FT's Oct 4 reporting that Wall Street IPO demand is cooling is the capital-market backdrop to the penalty: public-market exit and equity-funding windows are narrowing, relevant to Schneider's decision to fund PTC partly with new equity into a falling share price.

The strategic logic is the data-center buildout. Schneider, once known for fuses and circuit breakers, now supplies cooling units, server racks and power distribution for data centers, and the PTC acquisition adds product-design and lifecycle-management software to its industrial software and AI business. CEO Olivier Blum told investors that PTC's engineering and design data would strengthen Schneider's ability to deploy AI across customers' industrial operations: "Data is becoming a very critical layer" for extracting value from AI.

Closing is expected in the third quarter of 2027, subject to PTC shareholder approval and regulatory clearances. Key items to watch are the pricing and timing of the new equity raise, antitrust review in the US and EU, and whether Schneider can defend the strategic premium while its own shares trade lower.
