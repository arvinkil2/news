Schneider Electric said Monday it had agreed to buy PTC, the Boston-based industrial software company, in an all-cash deal valuing PTC's equity at around $22.6 billion — $205 per share, a 42.3 percent premium to its last close, implying a $23.7 billion enterprise value. It is the largest acquisition in the history of Schneider, France's third-most-valuable listed company.

The deal will be financed through a combination of equity and new debt and is expected to close in the third quarter of 2027. Schneider said the combination would create a scaled industrial software and AI business built around open, interoperable systems, targeting €250 million in annual run-rate cost savings by the third year after closing alongside about €800 million in expected revenue synergies. It follows Schneider's June agreement to buy Cognite Holding, a privately held AI software and industrial-data provider.

The market's reception was brutal on the buyer. Schneider's shares fell nearly 10 percent in early Paris trading — wiping close to €15 billion off its market capitalization — as investors weighed the size, the premium, and software valuations under AI disruption. "AI disruption fears are still weighing on software valuations, which allows acquiring PTC at a decade low valuation but could still weigh on Schneider post deal," Jefferies said. PTC shares jumped 34.4 percent in U.S. premarket trading.

The strategic logic is the data-center backbone moving up the stack. Schneider, once known for fuses and circuit breakers, now supplies cooling units, server racks and power-distribution equipment for data centers — and booming U.S. data-center demand is driving its earnings. PTC's product-design and lifecycle-management software, plus its AI-powered tools, give Schneider the engineering-data layer CEO Olivier Blum calls "very critical" for deploying AI across industrial customers. In July, Schneider lifted its annual revenue forecast after record first-half results on exactly that demand.
