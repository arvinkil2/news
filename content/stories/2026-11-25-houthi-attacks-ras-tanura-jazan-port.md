Saudi air defences destroyed four drones over Ras Tanura on Wednesday morning, coalition spokesman Turki Al-Maliki said, with fragments from the interceptions falling and no injuries in an initial assessment. The coalition did not say the refinery had been hit. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said the strike was accurate and direct and had caused fires at the site. NASA satellite imagery reviewed by Reuters showed thick black smoke around the refinery. Saudi Aramco and the Saudi government did not respond to Reuters requests for comment.

Separately, a projectile fired by the Houthis struck Jazan port in southwestern Saudi Arabia on Wednesday, causing minor damage, the coalition said. No civilian casualties were reported, and authorities were working to identify the type of projectile.

Oil prices climbed on the reports. Brent crude traded at $93.60 a barrel at 10:16 a.m. ET, up $2.20 or 2.41 percent, and West Texas Intermediate traded at $83.51, up $1.46 or 1.78 percent. PVM analyst Tamas Varga told Reuters the Houthi statement set off the rally by raising the geopolitical risk premium, with short covering before the US Thanksgiving holiday a possible amplifier, and LSEG's Zhenqiang Seto cited uncertainty over OPEC+ policy for 2027 before Sunday's meeting.

The Ras Tanura refinery processes 550,000 barrels per day, about 16 percent of Saudi refining capacity, and its adjacent terminal can load about 6.5 million barrels per day of crude. Loadings resumed in late June after a halt of about four months that followed a March 2 drone attack.

Wednesday's attacks are the third day of escalation. On Tuesday a strike on the Riyadh tower that houses the Public Investment Fund injured 11 people, an attack the coalition blamed on the Houthis, and Saree warned that Jeddah port and all Saudi ports on the Red Sea coast would be targets if the coalition bombed Hodeidah. The coalition then struck Hodeidah port and targets in Saada and Amran.
