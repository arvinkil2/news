Latest: A hostile aerial projectile struck the Jazan refinery in southwestern Saudi Arabia on Thursday, killing a Nepalese worker and injuring several other workers of different nationalities, an official source at the Saudi Energy Ministry said in a statement carried by the Saudi Press Agency. The projectile started a fire that was later brought under control, and some operations at the refinery were temporarily halted while the damage was assessed. The ministry did not identify the attacker, did not quantify lost production and gave no restart time. The Saudi-led coalition said earlier on Thursday that the Houthis had attacked the refinery. The Jazan refinery can process about 400,000 barrels per day.

Latest: Saudi Arabia said early on Thursday that its air defences intercepted and destroyed two ballistic missiles launched toward Riyadh, with an explosion heard over the capital around midnight local time after authorities issued and then lifted an alert for a hostile air threat, DW reported. The Saudi military also said it destroyed a Houthi cruise missile launched toward the west of the country and a drone near the port of Jeddah. Residents in northern and eastern Riyadh reported hearing the blast. No casualties were reported from the Thursday interceptions.

Earlier latest: coalition spokesman Turki al-Maliki said coalition forces intercepted a drone targeting the port of Jeddah on Wednesday, with no civilian injuries from falling debris, and that Saudi air defences intercepted and destroyed ballistic missiles launched by the Houthis toward Riyadh, Al Jazeera reported. An explosion was heard in Sanaa after the coalition statement. Houthi-affiliated Al Masirah TV reported new Saudi strikes on Hodeidah port, and Houthi official Mohammed Ali al-Houthi said some of the recent attacks there had targeted storage tanks and cranes. Al Jazeera also reported government forces battling the Houthis on several fronts around Taiz with coalition air support.

Saudi air defences destroyed four drones over Ras Tanura on Wednesday morning, coalition spokesman Turki Al-Maliki said, with fragments from the interceptions falling and no injuries in an initial assessment. The coalition did not say the refinery had been hit. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said the strike was accurate and direct and had caused fires at the site. NASA satellite imagery reviewed by Reuters showed thick black smoke around the refinery. Saudi Aramco and the Saudi government did not respond to Reuters requests for comment.

Separately, a projectile fired by the Houthis struck Jazan port in southwestern Saudi Arabia on Wednesday, causing minor damage, the coalition said. No civilian casualties were reported, and authorities were working to identify the type of projectile.

Oil prices climbed on the reports. Brent crude traded at $93.60 a barrel at 10:16 a.m. ET, up $2.20 or 2.41 percent, and West Texas Intermediate traded at $83.51, up $1.46 or 1.78 percent. PVM analyst Tamas Varga told Reuters the Houthi statement set off the rally by raising the geopolitical risk premium, with short covering before the US Thanksgiving holiday a possible amplifier, and LSEG's Zhenqiang Seto cited uncertainty over OPEC+ policy for 2027 before Sunday's meeting.

The Ras Tanura refinery processes 550,000 barrels per day, about 16 percent of Saudi refining capacity, and its adjacent terminal can load about 6.5 million barrels per day of crude. Loadings resumed in late June after a halt of about four months that followed a March 2 drone attack.

Wednesday's attacks are the third day of escalation. On Tuesday a strike on the Riyadh tower that houses the Public Investment Fund injured 11 people, an attack the coalition blamed on the Houthis, and Saree warned that Jeddah port and all Saudi ports on the Red Sea coast would be targets if the coalition bombed Hodeidah. The coalition then struck Hodeidah port and targets in Saada and Amran.
