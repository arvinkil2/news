Latest: President Donald Trump, speaking to reporters in Dubuque, Iowa on Sunday, said he had spoken to Carney again and that the two sides have the concept of a deal that would be great for American farmers and for the United States. Trump said the scope of an agreement would include the Keystone XL pipeline project, which he supports, adding that Canada would build it and pay for it and the United States would give the route. It was not immediately clear how the project, whose key permit former President Joe Biden revoked in 2021, would be revived. A spokesperson for Dominic LeBlanc, Canada's minister responsible for trade with the United States, had no further comment beyond Carney's remarks, Canadian Energy Minister Tim Hodgson's office declined comment, and South Bow, the company spun out of TC Energy's oil pipelines business, was not immediately available for comment.

Prime Minister Mark Carney said on Sunday that he had a "good conversation" with President Donald Trump on the future of Canada-U.S. trade. In a post on X, Carney said the talk built on recent constructive discussions on the outlines of a mutually beneficial trade deal that would create jobs, boost affordability for families, and fully respect Canada's languages, culture and sovereignty, and that teams from both countries "will now be working in the coming weeks to deliver that outcome." The conversation follows Canada's walkout from formal talks in August over what Carney called unreasonable last-minute U.S. demands, and a week in which Trump lifted some 50 percent tariffs on Canadian goods while adding others.
