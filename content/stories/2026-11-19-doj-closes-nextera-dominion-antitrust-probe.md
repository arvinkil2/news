The US Department of Justice said on Thursday that its Antitrust Division has closed its investigation into NextEra Energy's acquisition of Dominion Energy, a $66.8 billion combination of two electricity generation companies that compete in wholesale power and capacity auctions in PJM Interconnection and ISO New England. The division said it remained concerned, after reviewing documents, interviewing industry participants and running economic modelling, that the merged firm could have the incentive to deactivate a still viable New England plant to lift capacity payments across its combined portfolio.

The investigation closed after NextEra and Dominion told the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission in a docket filing that they will commit, without qualification, to the ISO New England internal market monitor's proposal whether or not FERC imposes it as a condition of approval. Under that proposal the market monitor may investigate any plant the merged firm proposes to deactivate over a five year period starting with the May 2028 capacity auction, and NextEra would be barred from deactivating a plant the monitor finds still has economic value. The department said the commitment protects New England consumers from potentially hundreds of millions of dollars in higher electricity costs, and the transaction still requires FERC approval.
