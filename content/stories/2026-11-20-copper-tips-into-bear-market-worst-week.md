Copper tipped into bear market territory on Friday. Comex copper for December delivery, the most active contract in New York, dropped as much as 3.4 percent to $5.4405 a pound ($11,994 a tonne), its lowest since the end of March, before recovering to $5.4995 around midday, down 2.4 percent on the day. That level stood 20.2 percent below the record settlement of $6.8885 reached on September 9, and a close under $5.51 would confirm a bear market on the usual definition of a 20 percent fall from the peak. The contract has fallen for seven sessions in a row and was down more than 9 percent since the previous Friday, its steepest weekly loss of 2026. A year-to-date gain that reached 22 percent in September has turned into a loss of 2 percent, and the metal was on course to end November about 15 percent lower.

London and Shanghai fell with New York. Three-month copper on the London Metal Exchange fell 2.3 percent to $12,235 a tonne in official trading after touching $12,020, the lowest since March 24, and was down about 9 percent on the week, its biggest weekly fall since early April 2025. London prices stood 18 percent below their September 10 peak of $14,875. In Shanghai, futures had their worst week in 15 years, according to Bloomberg.

The fall is being driven by the unwinding of bets on the artificial intelligence boom, a strong dollar and fading prospects of US tariffs on the metal, a combination that has overwhelmed evidence of acute shortage in China. Kyle Rodda, senior financial market analyst at Capital.com, told Reuters that evidence is mounting that the AI investment boom is waning and that the Federal Reserve will have to get more aggressive to control inflation, with a strengthening dollar also weighing on the metal. Albert Mackenzie, copper analyst at Benchmark Mineral Intelligence, told Bloomberg the AI-driven part of the price may have been overdone. AI shares continued to fall on Friday, with Anthropic trading about 9 percent below Thursday's close after its market debut. This site reported earlier on Friday that traders have booked an estimated 30,000 to 100,000 tonnes of US-held refined copper to ship to China as the tariff bet unwinds.

The physical market in China tells the opposite story to the futures market. The Yangshan premium paid to import copper slipped to $495 a tonne on Friday from Thursday's record $500, having been $360 on Wednesday and less than $100 in September. Shanghai Futures Exchange stocks rose by 3,204 tonnes this week to 30,846 tonnes, still less than a day's worth of Chinese consumption, and premiums at these levels have made it profitable to move metal out of the United States, where stockpiles built up over the past year on tariff bets, with tens of thousands of tonnes due to arrive in China in January. Codelco was offering Chinese buyers 2027 cathode at $250 to $280 a tonne, less than it charged this year.

Mining shares have taken the larger loss. Eleven of the largest copper-exposed miners had lost a combined $127 billion, or 15 percent of their market value, since the previous Friday, and $208 billion, or 23 percent, over the past month. Freeport-McMoRan fell another 5.4 percent on Friday, taking its loss for the week to 22 percent and cutting its market value to $67 billion, from close to $100 billion a month ago. Southern Copper gave up 4.3 percent to $116 billion and has shed 32 percent in a month. Only BHP, Glencore, Teck Resources and Anglo American were still showing gains for the year among the group.
