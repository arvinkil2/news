The Global X Uranium ETF, ticker URA, is back at the centre of the nuclear fuel trade as US policy shifts reset expectations for enrichment, conversion and domestic uranium supply, the Motley Fool reported, in a week when uranium miners in the site tables fell 4.92 percent, the sector's second worst group.

The policy story runs ahead of the physical one. Washington's push to rebuild a domestic fuel chain, from HALEU availability to strategic stockpile purchases, has pulled capital into a small set of listed names faster than mines and mills can respond, which is why the ETF and its miners now trade as a policy instrument as much as a commodity one.

That cuts both ways, as this week's session showed: when risk budgets shrink, the same concentration that powered the rally makes uranium miners the first position trimmed, regardless of the uranium price itself.
