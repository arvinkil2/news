Corn dropped 5 percent to 475.2, the sharpest move in the market tables, after USDA's WASDE report reset grain price expectations (see today's record farm safety net story, which notes today's WASDE repricing and crop revenues falling below support levels). Wheat fell 2.5 percent to 666.2 on the same reset.

Copper miners jumped 4.1 percent, now leading the metal after lagging it Thursday, as copper added another 2.7 percent to $6.70 a pound on day two of the Centinela strike, with BMO warning the production impact could show from November (see yesterday's Centinela strike story).

Dry bulk freight fell 3.7 percent to 13.94. No story today directly explains the day's move; the closest context is iron ore's 18-month low on weak steel mill margins, which points to soft bulk demand (see today's iron ore story).

Silver rose 3.5 percent to $61.15 an ounce and silver miners gained 2.7 percent, extending the precious-metals bid even as the day's read turned more risk-on. No single story today explains the silver move; gold added 1.4 percent to $4,216.10 and gold miners rose 2.8 percent on the same bid, with the mining roundup noting gold project advances in Quebec and Ontario (see today's mining also-notable story).

Tanker freight gave back 3 percent to $982.50, trimming yesterday's war-risk run after President Trump said the US will not attack Iran before the November 3 midterms, easing immediate disruption fears (see today's Trump-no-Iran-strike story). Brent was essentially flat at $104.21.

Tech rebounded from Thursday's OpenAI-revenue-miss selloff: Amazon rose 2.8 percent, aided by the news it built its 1,000th Kuiper satellite and is weeks from a service launch, Microsoft added 2.3 percent and Tesla 2.1 percent, with the Nasdaq 100 up 0.4 percent (see today's technology also-notable story).

The cross-market read is rotation with a soft risk-on tilt. Money came out of grains on the WASDE fundamentals reset and out of tanker freight on the de-escalation window, while it flowed into copper miners on strike-driven supply fear, into precious metals and miners on the continuing safety bid, and back into beaten-down megacap tech. Brent's near-flat $104.21 against falling freight suggests crude is pricing the status quo while shipping rates price the reprieve.
