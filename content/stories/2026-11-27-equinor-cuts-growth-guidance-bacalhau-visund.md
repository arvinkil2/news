Equinor has lowered its 2026 production growth guidance back to about 3 percent, reversing a recent raise to about 4 percent, after temporary shut-ins at two fields. Partner Galp confirmed in its own announcement that production at the Bacalhau floating production vessel offshore Brazil has been suspended for an estimated five to eight weeks, with no injuries reported.

The Bacalhau outage followed a dropped object incident on November 18 that damaged equipment on the vessel. Equinor has informed Brazil's regulator, the ANP, that production is shut in, and is investigating with its contractors. Bacalhau, which started production in October 2025, is Equinor's largest international field, with capacity reported at 220,000 barrels a day. Galp said it is maintaining its own 2026 guidance, with average production still expected at about 130,000 barrels of oil equivalent a day.

The second outage is on the Norwegian continental shelf. The Visund field will be shut down for around two months following a fire in the emergency power switchboard room on November 4. Together the shut-ins are expected to cut Equinor's average full year output by 15,000 to 20,000 barrels of oil equivalent a day, according to reporting of the company's update.

The timing adds to a week of supply losses elsewhere, including the Jazan refinery halt in Saudi Arabia and continued Ukrainian strikes on Russian refining, and it lands two days before OPEC+ meets to set output policy for 2027.
