The 10 defendants, former employees and trading partners of three Taiwan distributors
for Texas Instruments and Analog Devices, allegedly ordered military-grade analog and
radio-frequency chips using the name of the National Chung-Shan Institute of Science
and Technology, with false written promises of no export to US-sanctioned parties, then
shipped the parts through Hong Kong and mainland-controlled buyers after relabelling
them "Made in Taiwan" despite manufacture in the US, Thailand, the Philippines and
South Korea.

Prosecutors are seeking 2 to 10 years for each defendant and confiscation of alleged
gains, which totalled up to NT$135.83 million for the largest of three groups, with the
trial to be held at the New Taipei District Court; charges were brought under fraud,
forgery and money-laundering law because breaking US export controls is not itself a
crime in Taiwan.
