The UK government has announced a new publicly owned grid company — Great British Grid — that will compete against private-sector energy network operators, in the biggest structural intervention in Britain's transmission market in a generation. Startup costs will be covered by the state-owned clean-power investment vehicle Great British Energy, with a long-term budget to be set in future spending reviews.

The move targets a market the government describes as lacking competition: Great Britain's transmission network operates as a heavily regulated private regional monopoly, and solar and storage developers applying for transmission-level connections have no choice of connection partner. Alongside the new company, the government plans self-build reforms letting developers construct their own connections in some cases, and earlier competitive tendering for transmission projects.

The numbers explain the intervention. Energy regulator Ofgem estimates transmission owners will need to spend up to around 70 billion pounds — about $93 billion — over 2025 to 2031 on grid upgrades, and connection queues have ballooned even after reforms removed more than 300 gigawatts of zombie capacity. Whether a state-owned competitor can actually accelerate connections — or simply adds a new layer to an already complex market — is the open question.
