Novartis announced on November 23 that Leqvio (inclisiran), its twice yearly small interfering RNA therapy for lowering LDL cholesterol, met the primary endpoint of ORION-4, a Phase 3 cardiovascular outcomes trial in people with established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. Adding Leqvio to standard of care lipid lowering therapy produced what the company called a statistically significant and clinically meaningful reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events.

ORION-4 randomized more than 16,000 participants, 16,124 according to trial reporting, in the United States and the United Kingdom. The primary composite covered coronary heart disease death, myocardial infarction, fatal or non fatal ischemic stroke, and urgent coronary revascularization. The trial was led by the University of Oxford with the TIMI Study Group and co sponsored by Oxford and Novartis, and dosing was on day 1, at three months, and then every six months.

The limit of this readout is what is missing. Novartis did not disclose the hazard ratio, confidence interval, absolute risk reduction, event counts, or results for the secondary endpoints, which include cardiovascular death and the composite of coronary heart disease death or myocardial infarction. The safety case in the release rests on the lead investigator describing inclisiran as well tolerated. Full results are planned for publication in the first half of 2027.

Together with the ongoing VICTORION-2 PREVENT trial, expected in 2027, the outcomes program covers more than 33,000 participants. Leqvio is already approved in more than 100 countries to lower LDL cholesterol. Whether an outcomes win of unknown size expands its use against cheaper oral and injectable rivals depends entirely on the numbers the company has not yet shown.
