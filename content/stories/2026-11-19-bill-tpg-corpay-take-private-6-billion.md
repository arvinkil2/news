BILL, the finance platform used by nearly 500,000 businesses, has entered into a definitive agreement to be acquired by TPG in partnership with Corpay for $63.25 per share in an all-cash transaction that values the company at about $6.0 billion on a fully diluted basis. TPG will take a majority interest through TPG Capital, its US and European private equity platform, and Corpay will acquire a minority interest. The price is a 31 percent premium to BILL's 60-day volume-weighted average price through November 16, the last full trading day before media reports that the company was in acquisition discussions.

Corpay, the S&P 500 spend management company, said in its own release that it will invest $1.1 billion for an approximately one-third equity stake in BILL, with an option to acquire 100 percent of the company in 2030, and that it expects the investment to be accretive to its cash earnings per share in 2027.

The transaction was approved unanimously by BILL's board and carries no financing conditions. It is expected to close in the first half of 2027, subject to approval by BILL stockholders and required regulatory approvals. Starboard Value, BILL's largest stockholder, and chief executive and founder René Lacerte have agreed to vote their shares in favour of the agreement. Lacerte will continue to lead the company as chief executive after the close, and BILL will become privately held, with its shares delisted from the New York Stock Exchange.

BILL, headquartered in San Jose, runs an integrated platform covering accounts payable, accounts receivable, expenses, forecasting and procurement for small and midsize businesses, and says its network of more than 9 million members processes over 1 percent of US GDP annually. Goldman Sachs and Qatalyst Partners advised BILL on the sale.
