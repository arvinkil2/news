The Bureau of Land Management is considering 35 data center related applications for development on public land in the western United States, according to a tracker published by the Center for Western Priorities and built from the bureau's land records. Of the 35, the tracker classifies 17 as applications to build data center campuses and 13 as supporting infrastructure for AI data centers, with five it could not classify from the information available.

One cited project in eastern Utah would cover more than 9,000 acres and be powered by small modular nuclear reactors. The Center for Western Priorities, a conservation group, argues the administration should have to seek public feedback before data centers go on public land, with site specific environmental review for each proposal.
