The US Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control sanctioned more than two dozen Iranian exchange houses, front companies and key personnel under Operation Economic Outcast, targeting the shadow banking system overseen by the Central Bank of Iran. The designations cover the Iran-based rahbar company Rahbar Bazargani Nesim Baharan Qeshm Company and three of its officials, the exchange houses Mina Fashki and Partners Exchange Company and Mellat Exchange Company, front companies in Dubai, Hong Kong and Türkiye, and the Iran Center for Exchange of Currency and Gold, which Treasury called an essential intermediary matching exporters to importers for tens of billions of dollars of trade a year. Secretary of the Treasury Scott Bessent said Iran's shadow banking system is "the last financial lifeline of a bankrupt regime." The action was taken under Executive Orders 13902, 13224 and 13871, and all property of the designated persons in the United States or in the control of US persons is blocked.

In a companion release, Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network said a Financial Trend Analysis had identified approximately $14.7 billion of financial activity from January 2025 to August 2026 potentially linked to Iranian shadow banking, based on suspicious activity flagged by US financial institutions. The largest routes ran from mainland China-based banks to UAE-based banks at about $2.6 billion, from Singapore-based banks to UAE-based banks at about $981 million, and from UK-based banks to UAE-based banks at about $922 million. FinCEN also flagged the use of companies registered in the Marshall Islands and the British Virgin Islands, usually at addresses linked to corporate registries.
