The Food and Drug Administration has issued a complete response letter for ivonescimab, Summit Therapeutics said on Sunday, meaning the biologics licence application cannot be approved in its present form. The application covered ivonescimab, a PD-1 x VEGF bispecific antibody developed with China's Akeso, combined with platinum chemotherapy for patients with locally advanced or metastatic non-small cell lung cancer who had already received an EGFR targeted therapy.

The FDA determined that substantial evidence of effectiveness is not established without a statistically significant overall survival improvement, and that the progression free survival benefit seen in the Phase III HARMONi trial does not on its own support a positive benefit risk profile, according to Summit. HARMONi met its progression endpoint, and Summit said a longer follow-up analysis showed a survival hazard ratio of 0.76 across the full population, but the primary survival analysis did not reach statistical significance. Summit said it will continue ivonescimab's wider development programme, including first line lung cancer trials, and noted that no regimen has shown a statistically significant survival benefit in this treatment setting.
