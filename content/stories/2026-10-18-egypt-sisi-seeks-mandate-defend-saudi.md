**Latest (Monday 19:59 ET):** Egypt's House of Representatives adjourned the vote on authorising the armed forces to support Saudi Arabia to October 26, Al Jazeera reported on Monday, after a second session on the request ended without a decision. The opposition Egyptian Social Democratic Party, which holds nearly a dozen seats, told its lawmakers to abstain, saying parliament had not been given enough information about the mission's objectives, duration, cost or the risks to Egyptian troops, although two members of parliament told AFP that most of the party's lawmakers support the measure. The authorisation would let President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi approve any Saudi request for defence assistance, and sending troops on a combat mission abroad requires a two-thirds parliamentary majority under the constitution. Al Jazeera reported that the National Defence Council, chaired by Sisi, gave its approval on Sunday, which firms up the account carried below from a single lawmaker's post. The Grand Imam of al-Azhar urged Arab and Muslim leaders on Sunday to mediate the decision and avert further bloodshed. Saudi Arabia is due to deposit $5.3 billion into Egypt's central bank this month, and the EGX30 index fell early on Monday amid concern about possible military involvement before recovering to close 0.4 percent higher.

Egyptian President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi said on Saturday that Egypt would take any measures necessary to defend Saudi Arabia if asked, amid escalating Houthi attacks on the kingdom, and asked parliament to authorise that action in advance. Speaking at a graduation ceremony at the Egyptian Military Academy, Sisi said it was inconceivable that Egypt would allow Saudi Arabia, its security or its stability to be harmed, and stressed that Egypt was not seeking to attack anyone and would act only in defence.

The request has a timetable. Sisi said he knew the step required the approval of the Egyptian parliament and asked that an emergency session be held so it could give the authorisation if Egypt is asked. Parliament announced a plenary session at 1 p.m. on Sunday in response, without disclosing the agenda. Sisi said Egypt would act in accordance with guidance from Saudi King Salman and Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, who visited Cairo on September 15 after fighting in Yemen reignited, to discuss security and Red Sea navigation among other issues.

One step beyond the speech was reported on Sunday morning but rests on a single source: Mostafa Bakry, a member of the House of Representatives, posted that the National Defence Council, meeting under Sisi, had approved sending troops to Saudi Arabia on a defensive mission. The presidency's own account of that meeting pledged solidarity and all necessary assistance to defend the kingdom but did not explicitly announce a deployment. That claim is reported here as one lawmaker's account, not as a confirmed decision.

The context is the renewed Houthi campaign against Saudi Arabia, which in recent days has hit Riyadh's international airport and Saudi Aramco facilities, and the collapse of the Yemen truce in July. Egypt joined the Saudi led coalition that intervened in Yemen in 2015, and Saudi Arabia and other Gulf states have provided Egypt with billions of dollars in aid, central bank deposits and investment since 2013. Sisi also used the speech to warn against threats to Red Sea shipping, the channel where Houthi attacks already cost Egypt Suez Canal revenue, and to confirm he will leave office in 2030 under the amended constitution.
