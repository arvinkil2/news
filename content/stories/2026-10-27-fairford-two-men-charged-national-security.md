Two men from Croydon in south London, Omid Bakhshandeh, 41, and Mirwais Sherzad, 47, were charged on Tuesday with three offences each: engaging in conduct in preparation for an act of terrorism, attempting to collect and to record or photograph information likely to be useful to a person preparing an act of terrorism, and assisting a foreign intelligence service. Prosecutors allege the conduct took place between February and September 2026 at RAF Fairford in Gloucestershire and RAF Brize Norton in Oxfordshire, and Bethan David, head of the Crown Prosecution Service's Counter Terrorism Division, said the CPS will allege the pair were collecting information intended to be useful to the Iranian intelligence service. The Fairford charges relate to September 20, when, prosecutors say, a blue gas canister was thrown from a Toyota car near the base, the men tried to enter the base and were arrested nearby.

The men appeared at Westminster Magistrates' Court by videolink from police custody, spoke only to confirm their names and dates of birth, entered no pleas, and were remanded in custody to appear at the Old Bailey on November 14. Britain has publicly attributed the September incident near RAF Fairford, a base used by the United States, to Iran, which has denied any involvement; the charges are allegations that have not been tested in court.
