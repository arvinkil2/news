British prosecutors charged two 24-year-old men from the borough of Westminster in London on Tuesday following the counter-terrorism investigation into last month's incident at RAF Fairford. Mohmed Abdulkarim was charged with one offence of assisting a foreign intelligence service, contrary to section 3(2) of the National Security Act 2023, over conduct between August 31 and September 28 that prosecutors say was likely to materially assist a foreign intelligence service in carrying out UK-related activities. Nouman Essghir was charged with one offence of entering a prohibited place for a purpose prejudicial to the UK, contrary to section 4(1) of the same act, over approaching or being in the vicinity of a prohibited place on August 22. Frank Ferguson, chief crown prosecutor for the CPS Special Crime and Counter Terrorism Division, said the CPS had decided to prosecute the two following the Counter Terrorism Policing investigation into what he called a high-profile incident involving RAF Fairford.

Assistant Commissioner Laurence Taylor, head of national counter-terrorism policing, said police believed the foreign intelligence service Abdulkarim is accused of assisting was Iran's. The first hearing was due at Westminster Magistrates' Court on Tuesday, and five other men arrested during the investigation remain on police bail. The investigation began on September 27, when three suspicious vehicles were seen near the Gloucestershire base, which is used by the United States, a major incident was declared and nearby residents were evacuated. Iran has denied any involvement in the incident; the charges are allegations that have not been tested in court.
