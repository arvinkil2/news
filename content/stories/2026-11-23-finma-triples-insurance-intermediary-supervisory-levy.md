FINMA is more than tripling the cost of supervising Switzerland's insurance intermediaries and passing the bill to the industry. The annual supervisory levy for registered untied intermediaries rises from 475 francs to 1,575 francs from the start of 2027, invoiced for the first time in September 2027, with part of the increase covering a shortfall accumulated since supervision began in 2024 over an expected period of around ten years. Registration fees also rise, to 675 francs for individuals and 1,075 francs for legal entities.

The reason is what supervision found. Since the Insurance Supervision Act expanded FINMA's remit at the start of 2024, the authority has uncovered more than 1,000 market participants operating without authorisation, removed around 3,200 entries from the intermediary register, and ordered follow up consultations with clients in several thousand cases in 2026. Violations ranged from missing qualifications and cold calling to incorrect advice and fraud.

FINMA supervises more than 12,000 intermediaries by triage rather than individually, working through roughly 1,500 reports and complaints a year and concentrating on systematic, persistent violations. It says the problems proved considerably more serious than expected despite information and prevention campaigns.

For financial conditions the story is small but telling: conduct supervision is getting more expensive across European finance, and regulators are funding it by levy rather than absorbing it. Intermediaries who entered the register on December 31 of the previous year are liable for the following year's levy.
