**Latest.** The Senate passed the bill on Thursday night in a late sitting, with the opposition voting with the government, The Guardian reported. The lower house is set to sit for a short period on Friday morning, a day later than expected, to pass the legislation as amended in the Senate. The Coalition secured a statutory review of the reservation policy's impact on investment after five years, and Western Australia's carve-out is permanent. Resources Minister Madeleine King said the scheme would mean cheaper gas but would not name a figure.

Australia's Senate is set to pass a law on Thursday requiring producers of liquefied natural gas to reserve up to a fifth of their output for domestic use, in an effort to cut prices and cover supplies forecast to fall short. The scheme takes effect from January 2028, with the energy regulator setting the exact reserved amount so that domestic supply reaches 110 percent of forecast demand, and the centre-left Labor government secured the conservative opposition's backing after agreeing to exempt Western Australia, the biggest LNG exporting state.

Gas producers have opposed the policy, warning that its must-sell provisions would drive prices down too far and deter investment in new supply, with Santos' Gladstone LNG project thought to be the hardest hit because it lacks spare gas beyond contracted volumes. The Senate on Thursday rejected a Greens-backed amendment for a 25 percent tax on gas exports, and the governments of China and Japan, the two largest buyers of Australian gas, have also expressed concerns over the scheme.
