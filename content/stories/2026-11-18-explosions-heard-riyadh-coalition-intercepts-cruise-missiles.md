**Latest.** Reuters reporting carried by Fox News identified the tower struck on Thursday as the Public Investment Fund Tower, the 80-storey headquarters of Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund and the tallest building in Riyadh, and said the attack destroyed windows across up to eight floors and bent portions of its metal facade. Entrances leading into the King Abdullah Financial District were closed and people were being evacuated from the area. Coalition spokesman Turki Al-Malki said Saudi air defences intercepted and destroyed four ballistic missiles launched by the Houthis toward the southern region on Thursday, and that the coalition destroyed a ballistic missile launch platform in Dhamar, Yemen, that it said was used in an attempt to attack Riyadh the same day.

Saudi Civil Defense said on Thursday that one of the towers in Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District was targeted in a Houthi attack, and that Civil Defense teams responded at the site. The attack caused material damage to several floors of the tower and to neighbouring buildings, and no injuries were reported. Civil Defense called the targeting of civilian objects a flagrant violation of international humanitarian law. Saudi Arabia's Foreign Ministry condemned continued Houthi attacks on civilian and economic facilities in Riyadh, said the kingdom asserts its full right to take all necessary measures to defend its sovereignty and protect its citizens and residents, and called on the UN Security Council's permanent members to implement the relevant resolutions. Arab News reported no Houthi claim of responsibility for the Thursday attack.

A powerful explosion rattled homes in Riyadh on Wednesday, AFP journalists and residents said, with residents reporting that it came without the mobile phone alert usually issued during aerial threats and that smoke was seen near the airport in the northeast of the city. The Saudi-led coalition said its air defences intercepted and destroyed a cruise missile launched by Yemen's Houthis towards the Riyadh region within minutes, that it had earlier intercepted another cruise missile heading for the same region, and that later on Wednesday it intercepted two Houthi drones in the Riyadh and southern regions, while Saudi civil defence activated early warning systems in the southern regions of Jazan, Najran and Khamis Mushait.

Reuters separately reported a loud explosion in Riyadh on Wednesday night, citing an eyewitness, and said Saudi Arabia's civil defence directorate had activated the national early warning platform to notify Riyadh residents of a possible threat before later announcing on X that the alert was cancelled. Neither report stated casualties or damage.
