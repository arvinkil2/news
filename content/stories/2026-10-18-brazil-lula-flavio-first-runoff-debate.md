**Latest (Monday 05:44 ET):** A BTG/Nexus poll released on Monday put Flávio Bolsonaro at 50.3 percent of valid votes and Lula at 49.7 percent, a gap inside the poll's two point margin of error. In the stimulated scenario Flávio had 46.5 percent to Lula's 45.9 percent, with 4.7 percent blank or null and 2.9 percent undecided, and in the spontaneous scenario the two were tied at 44.5 percent each. The poll interviewed 2,000 voters on October 16 to 18.

Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro met on Sunday night in the first debate of Brazil's presidential runoff, a Band broadcast that UOL characterised as a series of attacks and provocations with few proposals. Lula pressed Flávio over the R$134 million he sought from ex-banker Daniel Vorcaro for a film about Jair Bolsonaro, over his past legal work and militia links, and over his late reversal on ending the 6x1 work schedule, and challenged both men to open their families' bank and telephone secrecy before the vote; Flávio countered with the lobbyist Roberta Luchsinger's claims about Lula's son and refused to answer the Vorcaro question directly. On the economy, Flávio promised rapidly falling interest rates and attacked food prices, while Lula defended growth above 3 percent, cited 716,000 Covid deaths under the previous government against Flávio's health plans, and said the cost of living was higher under Jair Bolsonaro.
