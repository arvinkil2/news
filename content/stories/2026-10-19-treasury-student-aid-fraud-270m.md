The US Treasury said on Monday that its Financial Crimes Enforcement Network has identified approximately 270 million dollars in suspicious financial activity potentially linked to fraud against federal student aid programs, and has referred the findings to federal investigators. Financial institutions have filed nearly 100 suspicious activity reports since FinCEN's July 2026 alert on student aid fraud, and FinCEN's review of those reports identified more than 20,000 potentially suspicious transactions since February 2026.

FinCEN described two main patterns: ghost student schemes, in which criminals use stolen personal information to enrol fake students and collect aid in a victim's name, and straw student schemes, in which fraud rings pay real people for their information, enrol them, and keep the aid refunds. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the administration would work with financial institutions and law enforcement to recover stolen funds, and Education Secretary Linda McMahon said a new FAFSA fraud detection tool introduced this year stops fraud at the start of the application process.
