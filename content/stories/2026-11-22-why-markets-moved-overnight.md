Natural gas was the day's biggest mover, up 14.69 percent to $3.75 per mmBtu, as cold forecasts and short covering squeezed the market higher (see today's natural gas story). It rose against the tape: almost everything else fell.

Nvidia fell 14.01 percent to $125.53, the largest single-stock drop in the tables, extending the AI-led selloff that put the S&P 500 into correction this week. This morning's reporting ties the sector's slide to that correction and to the accounting focus of the SEC probe into Nvidia covered earlier this week. Broadcom fell 12.19 percent to $304.41 and the semiconductor index fell 10.55 percent, with no clear single driver in this morning's reporting beyond the broad chip selloff. The tech sector index fell 7.27 percent and Tesla fell 7.64 percent to $345.82, again with no single company catalyst in this morning's reporting.

Precious metals sold off with risk assets rather than sheltering from them. Silver futures fell 7.33 percent to $41.07 an ounce and gold futures fell 4.62 percent toward $3,500 an ounce, squeezing miners whose costs have risen into the rally (see today's gold story). Uranium miners fell 6.56 percent, silver miners 5.59 percent and gold miners 5.00 percent.

The cross-market read is risk-off with one weather exception. Equities, chips and miners fell together, the US 5-year yield dropped 16.5 basis points as bonds were bid, and the only strong gains came from natural gas, where a physical forecast, not sentiment, did the work.
