Australia's benchmark bond yield fell below its US counterpart for the first time in more than a year, Bloomberg reported on Friday, a crossover that captures the week's central divergence: the United States repricing towards more tightening while Australia is priced as finished.

The Reserve Bank of Australia lifted its cash rate by 25 basis points to 4.85 percent on November 3, but the 6 to 3 vote and the removal of its tightening bias left markets treating the cycle as complete. Trading Economics put the Australian 10-year yield at 5.07 percent on November 12, little changed on the session, after a month in which it had fallen about 24 basis points even as Treasury yields climbed. Market pricing implies a high probability that Australian rates have peaked, with a quarter-point cut fully priced by the end of 2027.

The US side of the spread did the moving. Hot October consumer and producer price data, and hawkish remarks from Federal Reserve officials, pushed the US 10-year to 5.15 percent by Friday's close, up about 30 basis points on the week, with a December Fed increase priced as certain. Analysts tracking the crossover note that the Australia to US differentials at two, five and ten years have narrowed by roughly 99, 111 and 89 basis points respectively from their 2026 highs.

Domestic data supported the Australian side of the trade. Consumer sentiment fell in November, with mortgage holder sentiment at a record low, and business conditions were at their weakest in years, reinforcing the view that the RBA has no case to follow the Fed higher.

For the Australian dollar, the crossover matters mechanically. A benchmark yield that no longer pays more than Treasuries removes one of the currency's structural supports at the same moment US yields are rising for policy reasons. Whether the spread stays negative depends on which central bank blinks first: Australian data soft enough to pull the RBA towards cuts, or US inflation cool enough to stop the Fed's repricing. This week, only the first of those was in evidence.
