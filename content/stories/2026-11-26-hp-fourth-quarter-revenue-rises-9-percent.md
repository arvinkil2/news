HP reported fiscal fourth-quarter results on November 24 for the quarter ended October 31, 2026. Net revenue was 16.0 billion dollars, up 9.3 percent from 14.6 billion a year earlier. GAAP diluted earnings per share were 0.84 dollars, flat year over year and at the top of the company's 0.74 to 0.84 dollar outlook range, while non-GAAP diluted earnings per share fell 10.8 percent to 0.83 dollars. Both per-share figures include tariff refunds. Net earnings were 769 million dollars, against 795 million a year earlier.

Personal Systems drove the quarter, with net revenue of 11.8 billion dollars, up 14 percent year over year, at a 3.0 percent operating margin. Commercial sales rose 23 percent on premium category demand. Printing net revenue was 4.3 billion dollars, down 1 percent, at a 19.7 percent operating margin, as lower supplies volume offset gains in industrial graphics and subscription services. Interim chief executive Bruce Broussard said AI PCs reached nearly 60 percent of shipments in the quarter, and that the company took steps to address memory and storage cost pressures.

For the full fiscal year, net revenue was 60.5 billion dollars, up 9.5 percent, operating cash flow was 4.0 billion dollars and free cash flow 3.3 billion, and HP returned 2.3 billion dollars to shareholders. The company raised its quarterly dividend to 0.31 dollars per share. Fourth-quarter returns to shareholders were 0.8 billion dollars.

The print separates demand from profitability in the PC market. Unit demand, led by commercial refresh and AI PCs, is strong enough to grow the top line at 9 percent, but component inflation is taking the profit out of that growth, which is the same memory cost dynamic behind the AI server price increases covered on this site this week. HP's chief executive transition, with Emma Walmsley taking over on January 4, 2027, was announced earlier this month and was not new in this release.
