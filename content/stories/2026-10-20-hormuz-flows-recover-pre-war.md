Kpler estimated that about 40 percent of Gulf crude exports excluding Iran bypassed the strait last month, as Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates moved more crude through pipelines that avoid Hormuz. A shuttle trade has also spread, in which tankers load inside the Gulf, cross the strait with their tracking signals off, and transfer the crude to long-haul ships off Oman, with the United States providing air cover along the route off the Omani coast, Yonhap reported. Iraqi crude can be resold at 20 dollars a barrel above its pre-war price, worth about 40 million dollars extra per very large crude carrier, and Hamad Hussein, chief commodities economist at Capital Economics, said the system for moving crude through the strait has become established and exporters are more willing to accept the risk.
