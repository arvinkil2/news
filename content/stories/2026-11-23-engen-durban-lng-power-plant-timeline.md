Engen Petroleum, the South African fuel group owned by Vitol, plans to start building a R40 billion (about $2.3 billion) liquefied natural gas power plant in Durban in about two years. Engen, with consortium partners VTTI and ACWA Power, expects to break ground on the proposed 1.9 gigawatt plant in late 2028, pending a final investment decision, and targets first production in the second half of 2031.

The project includes an LNG import terminal with peak regasification capacity of about 5 billion cubic metres a year, together with pipeline infrastructure. It received Strategic Integrated Project status from the South African government in September 2025.

Engen said development remains subject to successful participation in South Africa's second gas to power procurement round, receipt of all regulatory and environmental approvals, and other project milestones. Vitol is separately building a $130 million oil products storage hub at a former Durban refinery site, expected to complete next year.

South Africa, which generates most of its electricity from coal, is targeting large volumes of new gas fired capacity over the coming decade and a half; a privately developed LNG import terminal at Durban would give the country a second gas entry point on its east coast and an anchor customer for imported cargoes.
