A federal judge has refused to dismiss the NAACP's Clean Air Act lawsuit against xAI, clearing the case over the gas turbines powering the company's Colossus data centers near Memphis to proceed to the merits. In an opinion released on Friday, October 30, US District Judge Debra M. Brown held that the plaintiffs have statutory standing under the Clean Air Act, that the combustion turbines are not mobile sources under Mississippi's State Implementation Plan, and that the Act's citizen suit provision does not violate Article II of the Constitution or the private nondelegation doctrine.

The suit, filed on April 14, 2026 by the NAACP and its Mississippi State Conference and argued by the Southern Environmental Law Center, alleges that X.AI Corp. and MZX Tech LLC installed and operated gas turbines at the Colossus site in Southaven, Mississippi, without the preconstruction and operating air permits the Clean Air Act requires. The complaint as reported by TechCrunch cites at least 35 combustion turbines; coverage of the opinion puts the Southaven count at 27 turbines run from August to December 2025, supplying power to data centers across the state line in the Memphis area. The turbines were treated by Mississippi regulators as temporary or mobile units, the classification the court has now rejected as a basis for exemption.

xAI had argued both that the turbines were exempt as mobile sources and that citizen suits of this kind are unconstitutional because they exercise executive power. Judge Brown rejected the second argument directly: because citizen suit plaintiffs sue on their own behalf, in their own name, and seek penalties that redress harm to themselves, they are not exercising the federal government's executive power. The ruling follows an October order in the same case that allowed the federal government to join while denying a Justice Department request to end the suit, the second time this month the court has refused to shut the case down.

The NAACP's director of environmental and climate justice, Abre' Connor, called the decision another important victory in the effort to hold xAI accountable, saying the group's position has been clear that xAI cannot operate gas turbines without the required permits. The plaintiffs seek injunctive relief and civil penalties that reporting on the complaint puts at up to 124,426 dollars per day for each violation. A ruling on a motion to dismiss is not a finding that the law was broken; xAI must now defend the turbine operations on the merits.

The case has become the leading legal test of the build first, permit later approach that let Colossus come online at unusual speed. A permitted permanent power facility is reported to be in the works, but the lawsuit targets how the turbines have run in the meantime, and an injunction would bear directly on the power supply of a data center that depends on the plant. It lands as political resistance to data centers hardens elsewhere, with New York recently passing a moratorium on new data centers and other states weighing guardrails.
