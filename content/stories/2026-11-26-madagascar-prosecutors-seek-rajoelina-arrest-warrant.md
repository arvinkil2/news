Madagascar's anti-corruption pole, the PAC, has requested arrest warrants against former president Andry Rajoelina and his former prime minister Christian Ntsay in the affair of the five Boeing 777 aircraft that were provisionally registered in Madagascar and ended up in Iran. The first deputy prosecutor at the PAC, Jessé Ramorasata, announced the request in a video released by the prosecutor's office, describing the allegations as complicity in financing terrorism and financing the acquisition of heavy weapons. The warrants have not been issued: a judge must still decide.

The case turns on the destination of the aircraft. Five Boeing 777s, provisionally registered in Madagascar, were found in Iran in July 2025 with a private Iranian company that is under European Union and United States sanctions and that the prosecution says belongs to a terrorist network. When the affair broke in July 2025, Ntsay, then head of government, said that neither he nor the president had been informed of the operation. Neither man had responded publicly to the warrant request as of Monday evening, and both remain presumed innocent.

The proceedings now run in two parts. The first concerns the registration and transfer of the aircraft and involves 32 defendants, Malagasy, Iranian and Indian; it was tried in early November and returns to the court of appeal on November 27 after a defence appeal, and the reclassification as terrorism financing extends to those 32 defendants. The second part opened after a fresh complaint by Madagascar's civil aviation authority in October: 13 further people have been charged, 11 of them placed in provisional detention, and it is in this second case that the prosecutor wants the former executive pair brought before the court. In total 45 people are implicated across the two procedures.

Both men left Madagascar in October 2025 when the regime fell. Rajoelina was stripped of his Malagasy nationality in October 2025, and Colonel Michael Randrianirina, who took power, was sworn in on October 17, 2025. In late October 2026 the Constitutional High Court cleared the way for proceedings against Rajoelina before the PAC. If a judge grants the request, Antananarivo can ask Interpol for a red notice, a route Malagasy justice has already taken against Mamy Ravatomanga, a businessman close to the former regime. Status: warrants requested November 23, 2026; judicial decision pending; appeal hearing in the first case set for November 27, 2026.
