Compass Datacenters has postponed a planned $380 million sale of asset-backed securities after an operational failure at one of its data centers prompted investors to withdraw from the offering, Bloomberg reported on November 25, citing people familiar with the matter. The company no longer plans to complete the deal this year and expects to return to the ABS market in 2027.

The incident occurred on November 17, when a cooling system failure caused the facility's temperature to exceed its contractual limit for 148 minutes, breaching the site's environmental service level agreement. According to the reporting, technicians from a partner company were performing maintenance on one cooling unit while the number of other units offline had already reached the allowable limit, and Compass attributed the incident to human error and inadequate supervision of third-party personnel rather than to equipment or design defects.

The transaction is backed by six data centers with a combined capacity of 216 megawatts. Pricing guidance on one $85 million security had widened from about 1.95 percentage points over the benchmark to as high as 2.1 percentage points before the deal was pulled, and the withdrawal of a major investor caused the offering to miss its initial pricing timeline last week.

Compass is jointly owned by Brookfield Infrastructure Partners and the Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan. The postponement contrasts with the same week's $4.5 billion GPU-backed loan being prepared for CoreWeave, and underlines that in data center securitization the collateral is not only the building and the tenant contract but the operating record of the facility itself.
