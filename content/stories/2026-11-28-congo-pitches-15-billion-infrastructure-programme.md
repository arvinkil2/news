The Democratic Republic of Congo is seeking backers for about $15 billion of infrastructure projects, using a copper boom to sell a programme of railways, ports, bridges and fibre networks to international investors. Transport Minister Jean-Pierre Bemba set out the list at the Makutano Mining Forum in Kinshasa this week.

Named items include a $250 million, 45-hectare dry port and logistics terminal at Kwala, a January tender for a road-rail bridge across the Congo River between Kinshasa and Brazzaville, rehabilitation of the line toward Angola's Lobito port, and bidding in early 2027 for a Banana to Kinshasa railway serving the new Atlantic deep-water port. A 30-year concession for the Dilolo to Sakania railway was signed with Mota-Engil Africa in August, with the government estimating investment at about $1.26 billion and the concessionaire indicating up to $1.8 billion over the term.

The pitch leans on real volumes. Congolese copper exports passed 1 million tonnes in the three months through September, the first quarter above that mark, even as prices have fallen about 15 percent this month. The same forum announced $965 million of mining-sector investment, $700 million from financial institutions and $265 million from the International Finance Corporation, though only a small share was tied to named projects.

The gap between the two numbers is the story. Fifteen billion dollars is about 60 percent of Congo's proposed 2027 budget of roughly $24.8 billion, so none of it is state-funded: every project needs a concessionaire, a lender or a development financier willing to take Congolese execution risk. The tenders in January and early 2027 are the first test of whether the pitch converts.
