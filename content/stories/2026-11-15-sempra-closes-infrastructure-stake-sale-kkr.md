Sempra announced on November 12 that it had completed the sale of a 45 percent equity interest in Sempra Infrastructure Partners to affiliates of KKR, with the Canada Pension Plan Investment Board as an indirect co-investor. The KKR-led consortium now holds 65 percent of the partnership. Sempra retains 25 percent, and an affiliate of the Abu Dhabi Investment Authority keeps its existing 10 percent stake.

The consideration is mostly deferred. Sempra received $4.56 billion in cash at closing, subject to post-closing adjustments, with the remainder of the price payable through deferred instruments and promissory notes running, on the reported schedule, as far as 2034. The September 2025 purchase agreement set an aggregate base purchase price of $9.99 billion for the 45 percent interest. Sempra expects a preliminary gain of about $2.80 billion to $3.30 billion before tax, or $2.00 billion to $2.40 billion after tax, alongside roughly $450 million to $550 million of transaction costs, and it stresses that both figures remain preliminary.

The closing ends Sempra's controlling financial interest. The company deconsolidated Sempra Infrastructure Partners effective November 12 and will account for its retained stake under the equity method within its Sempra Infrastructure segment. The partnership holds a portfolio of LNG export and related infrastructure assets, including interests associated with Cameron LNG in Louisiana, and the sale follows the August disposal of Ecogas Mexico. A finance leadership change took effect the next day, with Justin C. Bird becoming chief financial officer and Karen L. Sedgwick moving to lead Southern California Gas Company.

The strategic read is a clean split. Sempra is recycling capital out of merchant-exposed export infrastructure and into its regulated utilities in Texas and California, where returns are set by regulators rather than by LNG spreads. KKR and its partners, by contrast, are paying close to $10 billion for control of contracted export capacity at a moment when global buyers are signing long-dated supply deals.

Approvals were cleared in advance: the parties had previously reported Mexican antitrust and Federal Energy Regulatory Commission approvals, with the Hart-Scott-Rodino waiting period expired, and they had extended their outside termination date to November 27, 2026 before closing inside it.
