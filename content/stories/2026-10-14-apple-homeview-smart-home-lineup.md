Apple made its largest smart home push in years on October 13. The centerpiece is HomeView, a $299 hub with a seven-inch display, a FaceTime camera, and microphones and speakers in its base, sold in countertop and wall-mounted versions. It runs Apple's updated Siri AI, ships with Calendar, Messages, Photos, FaceTime, Notes, Reminders, Music and Podcasts built in, and acts as the dashboard for lights, locks, thermostats and camera feeds. Preorders are open and the device goes on sale November 13 in four colors.

The differentiator Apple is selling is household awareness. HomeView recognizes up to six people and personalizes what is on screen, messages, contacts, calendar and apps, to whoever is standing in front of it. Around the hub, Apple rounded out the line: a refreshed HomePod mini with a new chip at $149, a new Apple TV 4K built around Siri AI with remote-free voice control, and first-party accessories including a smart deadbolt lock, security cameras and video doorbells.

The competitive frame is direct. Amazon's Echo Show and Google's Nest Hub have owned the smart display category while Apple's home efforts, the HomePod line in particular, stayed niche, and reporting has long tied delays in this lineup to Siri AI's readiness. Apple is now betting that an assistant that knows who is speaking can reopen the category. The revenue at stake is not the $299 hub itself; it is which company's identity, assistant and services layer sits in the kitchen when the rest of the home is automated.
