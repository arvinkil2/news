**Latest, October 27:** The Department of Energy's loans office, now called the Office of Energy Dominance Financing, closed the $5.1 billion package for more than 2.6 gigawatts of new or upgraded power generation, enough for nearly 1.8 million homes, supporting hundreds of construction related jobs, Reuters reported. Gregory Beard, the loan office director, said in a release that bringing new, dependable power online is strengthening energy security and lowering electricity costs for families and businesses across Iowa and Wisconsin, although Reuters noted any lowering of costs could take many years because the loans run over decades. The package began as a conditional loan of $3 billion proposed in the last days of the previous administration in January 2025.

The US Department of Energy has closed a $5.1 billion loan package to Alliant Energy to expand power generation and bolster the grid at plants in Iowa and Wisconsin, Reuters reported. The investments, from the department's loans office, will support new natural gas fired generation, energy storage and upgrades to existing generation facilities.

The closing lands as Alliant's Iowa capacity plans face local resistance: Linn County supervisors voted this week to deny rezoning for the company's proposed 720 megawatt Morgan Valley gas plant near Cedar Rapids, a project Alliant ties to data centre driven load growth, and the Iowa Utilities Commission could still approve that plant over the county's objections.
