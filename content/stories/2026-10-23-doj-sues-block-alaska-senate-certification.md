The suit, filed in US District Court in Alaska, asks a judge to declare that certifying a result affected by voter confusion would breach the state's duties under the National Voter Registration Act, CNBC reported. It quotes Alaska Elections Director Carol Beecher's finding that Daniel J. Sullivan's candidacy, which took 2.5 percent in the August 28 open primary, was for the purpose of confusing or misleading voters, and argues that his votes would transfer to Peltola under ranked-choice voting after his elimination. Peltola leads Sullivan by a narrow two points in the FiftyPlusOne polling average cited by CNBC, and no Alaska official or campaign had responded in the account as published.
