Saturn Metals has pushed its planned investment decision for the Apollo Hill gold project into early 2028 after an additional environmental review requirement emerged. The Western Australian Environmental Protection Authority has advised that Apollo Hill's approval application must undergo a four week public review period.

Saturn expects the additional public consultation and subsequent assessment to add approximately three months to the project's development timetable, shifting its planned final investment decision from late 2027 to early 2028. The company said it had already commissioned or completed work addressing possible supplementary information requests, although further requirements could emerge during the assessment.

Saturn has also postponed release of Apollo Hill's definitive feasibility study from December 2026 to early 2027, following financial, engineering and technical reviews that identified opportunities to further optimise the proposed development. Managing director Ian Bamborough said the additional time could be used to strengthen the project's technical foundations and better integrate the study into Saturn's emerging project execution plan.

Under the updated indicative schedule, construction and commissioning would follow regulatory approvals, financing and the final investment decision. The company is targeting first gold in early 2029, subject to the remaining development requirements. Apollo Hill is the counterpoint to Springpole's approval in Ontario on the same morning: in both jurisdictions the timetable, not the geology, is setting project value.
