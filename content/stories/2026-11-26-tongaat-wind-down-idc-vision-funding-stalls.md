Tongaat Hulett has commenced the wind-down of its South African sugar operations and told growers to stop delivering cane, after a 350 million rand bridging facility from the Industrial Development Corporation failed to materialise. The business rescue practitioners said the funding uncertainty left the company unable to sustain normal operations without taking on obligations it could not be certain of meeting, and the milling season will close early.

The IDC had approved the 350 million rand bridging loan on condition it received confirmation of how it would be repaid. The plan was for repayment from funding Standard Bank would provide to Vision Group, which has agreed to buy Tongaat's South African sugar business; an alternative, under which Vision would buy Tongaat's sugar stocks and Tongaat would repay the IDC from the proceeds, was still under discussion when chief executive Gavin Dalgleish wrote to growers on November 24.

Behind the bridge sits the larger state commitment. Bloomberg reported, as carried by Moneyweb, that the IDC plans to invest 4.5 billion rand, about 257 million dollars, for a 27.78 percent stake in an acquisition vehicle owned by Vision Group. The IDC approved that investment in August, but it remains subject to conditions Vision has yet to meet. The practitioners said the subscription and shareholders' agreements for the Vision acquisition have been signed and the remaining steps are resolving outstanding matters with the SA Sugar Association and securing ministerial approval under the Public Finance Management Act.

SA Canegrowers warned the wind-down leaves about 18,000 growers with nowhere to deliver and called on the IDC, Vision, Standard Bank, the practitioners and the trade department to resolve the funding mechanism urgently. Tongaat operates three sugar mills and is the country's biggest local refiner of white sugar. The group was removed from the Johannesburg Stock Exchange on November 3.
