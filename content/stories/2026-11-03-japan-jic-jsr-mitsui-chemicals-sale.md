Latest: Mitsui Chemicals said on Monday it has entered a deal to acquire JSR from JIC Capital for 1.8 trillion yen, about $11.6 billion, to expand its chipmaking materials offerings. The cash payment is 1.84 trillion yen, funded by 1.64 trillion yen in bridge loans from Mitsui's main banks and a 200 billion yen equity investment from the Development Bank of Japan's venture capital arm. Mitsui said the combined company stands to become Japan's largest semiconductor materials maker by sales, and it is targeting about 40 billion yen in annual incremental profit by 2035. The company warned its debt to core earnings ratio will temporarily exceed 4.0 after closing and said it plans to refinance the bridge loan within a year of closing to protect its credit rating. Mitsui shares rose 1.3 percent on Monday, after slumping nearly 12 percent on Friday when Reuters first reported the deal.

Japan Investment Corp, the state backed fund, plans to sell JSR to Mitsui Chemicals, Reuters reported on November 3, citing two people with knowledge of the matter. The transaction could exceed 1.3 trillion yen, or $8.2 billion, and a final decision may be reached within the month.

JIC bought the semiconductor materials maker for about 900 billion yen roughly two years ago, saying at the time that JSR would lead consolidation in the sector. The reported sale would instead hand JSR to a domestic chemical company at a higher valuation, as artificial intelligence investment lifts prices for chip related businesses.
