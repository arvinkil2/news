Latest: Mitsui Chemicals' board approved the acquisition on November 9 and the company signed a share purchase agreement with funds managed by JIC Capital, its disclosure shows. The 1.84 trillion yen price, about 11.61 billion dollars, splits into 1,392.1 billion yen of equity value, including the Development Bank of Japan's 200 billion yen investment, and 447.9 billion yen of interest-bearing debt and other items, with the final consideration subject to closing adjustments. Mitsui will hold all voting rights after completion, and DBJ's stake will be converted into non-voting preferred shares. Funding is a 1.64 trillion yen bridge loan from Mitsui's principal banks, to be refinanced through bank loans, bonds, mezzanine financing and a share sale of up to 150 billion yen. JSR, the world's second-ranked maker of ArF and EUV photoresists on Mitsui's own presentation and owner of US metal-oxide resist developer Inpria, reported revenue of 440.72 billion yen and profit attributable to owners of 59.33 billion yen for the year ended March 2026. Mitsui plans to sell JSR's life sciences unit and other non-core businesses, targets completion in the second half of the fiscal year ending March 2028 subject to competition and foreign-investment approvals, and still aims for about 40 billion yen of additional annual profit by fiscal 2035. JIC Capital chief executive Shogo Ikeuchi said seven or eight potential buyers were considered in talks held since spring. Mitsui shares slumped 8.5 percent on Monday, Reuters reported; the company's market capitalisation stood at 664.6 billion yen at Friday's close on LSEG data, well below the price it is paying.

Earlier: Mitsui Chemicals said on Monday it has entered a deal to acquire JSR from JIC Capital for 1.8 trillion yen, about $11.6 billion, to expand its chipmaking materials offerings. The cash payment is 1.84 trillion yen, funded by 1.64 trillion yen in bridge loans from Mitsui's main banks and a 200 billion yen equity investment from the Development Bank of Japan's venture capital arm. Mitsui said the combined company stands to become Japan's largest semiconductor materials maker by sales, and it is targeting about 40 billion yen in annual incremental profit by 2035. The company warned its debt to core earnings ratio will temporarily exceed 4.0 after closing and said it plans to refinance the bridge loan within a year of closing to protect its credit rating. Mitsui shares rose 1.3 percent on Monday, after slumping nearly 12 percent on Friday when Reuters first reported the deal.

Japan Investment Corp, the state backed fund, plans to sell JSR to Mitsui Chemicals, Reuters reported on November 3, citing two people with knowledge of the matter. The transaction could exceed 1.3 trillion yen, or $8.2 billion, and a final decision may be reached within the month.

JIC bought the semiconductor materials maker for about 900 billion yen roughly two years ago, saying at the time that JSR would lead consolidation in the sector. The reported sale would instead hand JSR to a domestic chemical company at a higher valuation, as artificial intelligence investment lifts prices for chip related businesses.
