Latest: President Donald Trump, answering journalists at Mar-a-Lago on Saturday evening, called a New York Times question about whether he planned to resume the war with Iran next week a stupid question and would not say either way, TASS reported. He said the United States had destroyed almost all of Iran's weaponry and many of its production facilities, that one or two facilities remained to be dealt with, and that dealing with them can be done in 10 minutes. One of them, he said, could be the presumed nuclear facility under the Kolang-Gaz-La massif, and he said attention should probably be paid to that mountain, which TASS said is called Mount Kirka in the United States. Iran has previously said Trump's statements about a presumed new Iranian nuclear facility are groundless, TASS noted.

Latest: Secretary of State Marco Rubio said in Atlanta on Saturday that President Donald Trump has not made a decision about fresh strikes on Iran and that he had no announcement to make, AFP reported. Rubio, who is hosting the G20 foreign ministers for a two day meeting, said the United States can achieve its military objectives if it seeks to, criticised Tehran for targeting commercial vessels, and said Trump prefers a diplomatic solution to end the war. He would not characterise the progress of talks or any timeframe for them.

Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei told the top commanders of the armed forces that a renewed American or Israeli attack must be met with an immediate and effective response, without geographical limitations, to achieve maximum deterrence. The statement, from his office, said he had ordered the military target bank updated so that American and Israeli assets are within reach, and that Iran would determine the timing and conditions for ending any renewed attack. Axios reported the meeting was the first publicly disclosed session between Khamenei and all the top commanders since the war began; he has not been seen or heard in public since he was wounded in a strike on the war's first day.

The statement is a rhetorical escalation, not an operational announcement, and no new attack or Iranian action was reported alongside it. It lands while Brent crude approaches 118 dollars on risk around the Strait of Hormuz, where a tanker was hit by limpet mines on Thursday, and while Qatari officials are in Tehran seeking to revive a ceasefire track with Washington.
