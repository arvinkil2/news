The European Commission and High Representative Kaja Kallas adopted a new strategy for the Arctic on Tuesday, replacing the 2021 policy and framing the region as Europe's Northern Neighbourhood, a framework for structured cooperation with Arctic and like-minded partners that complements the Arctic Council and works in complementarity with NATO. "The Arctic is Europe's Northern Neighbourhood, close, connected and critical to our future," Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said, with more than half a million EU citizens living in the region across Denmark, Finland and Sweden. The flagship projects are security and connectivity led: a Maritime Security Hub with an Arctic Cable Hub to monitor and repair North Atlantic and Arctic submarine cables, joint procurement and resource sharing to expand European icebreaker capacity, a surveillance pilot in 2027, a Pan-Arctic Cable linking Greenland directly to Europe, and the OceanEye ocean monitoring initiative with partners including Canada and Norway, backed by 1 billion euros of cohesion funding for 2021 to 2027 and 100 million euros a year for polar research. On hydrocarbons, the strategy remains committed to ensuring that oil, coal and gas stay in the ground, including in Arctic regions, Reuters reported, but it no longer repeats the 2021 pledge to work towards a multilateral legal obligation barring further Arctic hydrocarbon development.
