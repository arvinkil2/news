The go-ahead will be announced in Aberdeen on Wednesday morning by Fahnbulleh and the North Sea Transition Authority's chief executive Stuart Payne, and the government will argue that granting environmental approval does not break Labour's manifesto commitment because Jackdaw is not a new licence. The field was first approved in 2022, but a judge ruled last year that its environmental approval had been incorrectly granted because regulators had not counted the emissions from burning the gas it will produce, and Shell and Equinor resubmitted their application last September. No decision has been taken on the larger Rosebank field, which is expected later this year and would produce about seven times Jackdaw's carbon dioxide emissions, while the campaign group Uplift said Jackdaw has been oversold and would meet only about 2 percent of UK gas demand for roughly a decade.
