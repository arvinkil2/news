**Latest:** The Energy Secretary has agreed that the regulator can grant consent for gas to be produced at the licensed Jackdaw field, and the North Sea Transition Authority has granted that consent, the government said on Wednesday. Jackdaw will start to supply gas to British homes and businesses from as early as the end of this month, is expected to increase domestic gas production by around 6 percent at its peak over a lifespan of up to 11 years, and the developer estimates it will add an estimated 2.9 billion pounds to the UK economy, create 27 new direct jobs and safeguard 273 existing jobs. The Energy Secretary assessed that the project will not prevent the UK from meeting its legally binding limits on carbon emissions and achieving net zero by 2050, and the government said the decision is consistent with its commitment not to issue new licences, because Jackdaw is already licensed. Alongside the decision, the Chancellor announced a new Energy Wealth Fund to reinvest oil and gas revenues into renewable and low carbon energy, starting with an initial 2 billion pounds, with future North Sea revenues flowing into the fund over time.

The go-ahead will be announced in Aberdeen on Wednesday morning by Fahnbulleh and the North Sea Transition Authority's chief executive Stuart Payne, and the government will argue that granting environmental approval does not break Labour's manifesto commitment because Jackdaw is not a new licence. The field was first approved in 2022, but a judge ruled last year that its environmental approval had been incorrectly granted because regulators had not counted the emissions from burning the gas it will produce, and Shell and Equinor resubmitted their application last September. No decision has been taken on the larger Rosebank field, which is expected later this year and would produce about seven times Jackdaw's carbon dioxide emissions, while the campaign group Uplift said Jackdaw has been oversold and would meet only about 2 percent of UK gas demand for roughly a decade.
