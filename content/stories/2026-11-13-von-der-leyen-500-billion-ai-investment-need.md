European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said artificial intelligence companies in the European Union would require investments worth 500 billion euros, with the funding most needed to develop highly advanced AI models and in chips and other hardware. She spoke on Friday after the discussion with EU leaders in Dublin, where artificial intelligence was the meeting's main topic and leaders weighed how Europe keeps pace with the United States and China.

Von der Leyen said Europe is creating more tech startups than the US but that many fail to receive the support they need to scale, and that the EU must attract private investment, reform its investment rules and agree innovation funds, with money also to come from the new long-term EU budget. She said she will present an ambitious proposal on more funding for technological sovereignty, a push meant to reduce Europe's dependence on US and Chinese technology.
