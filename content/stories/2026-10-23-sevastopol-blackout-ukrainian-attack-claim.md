Sevastopol was temporarily left without electricity on Friday as a result of a Ukrainian attack on energy infrastructure outside the city, its Russian-installed governor, Mikhail Razvozhaev, said in a statement carried by TASS, RIA Novosti and Lenta.ru. A special regime was introduced at facilities in the city, social facilities switched to reserve power-supply schemes, and specialists were working to restore electricity to homes, he said, advising residents to save phone charge, use gadgets only for emergency communication and turn screen brightness to the minimum.

None of the reports gave a restoration timetable, and no individual facilities were named. Ukraine had not confirmed any attack on Sevastopol's power system, and the governor's account had not been independently corroborated.
