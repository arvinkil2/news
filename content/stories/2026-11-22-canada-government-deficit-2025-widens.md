The consolidated Canadian general government, which combines federal, provincial, territorial and local governments, recorded a deficit of 63.1 billion dollars in 2025, higher than the 52.4 billion dollar deficit reported in 2024, Statistics Canada reported. As a percentage of nominal gross domestic product, the deficit increased from 1.7 percent in 2024 to 1.9 percent in 2025.

The federal government's deficit increased by 7.4 billion dollars to 36.7 billion dollars, as growth in expenses of 11.2 billion dollars, up 2.1 percent, outpaced growth in revenues of 3.8 billion dollars, up 0.7 percent. The consolidated provincial, territorial and local governments' deficit increased by 3.4 billion dollars to 26.4 billion dollars.

The Canadian general government's gross debt, total liabilities at market value, increased by 131.9 billion dollars, up 3.9 percent, and stood at 3,524.4 billion dollars in 2025. The release includes revisions to the 2023 and 2024 reference periods alongside the new 2025 figures, and the annual data correspond to the end of the fiscal year closest to December 31.

Statistics Canada's consolidated measure is not the deficit Ottawa announces in the Public Accounts, which covers the federal fiscal year to March 31 on a different accounting basis. The two series answer different questions, and the consolidated one is the right gauge of the total borrowing requirement Canadian governments together place on bond markets.
