Semiconductors rose 6.01 percent, the largest move in the tables, with Broadcom up 3.99 percent and the wider tech sector up 3.23 percent. This morning's technology reporting gives no single product or earnings driver for the basket move; it reads as a rebound session for the chip complex after a week of heavy losses, with Nvidia still down 13.19 percent on the week despite the day's gain.

Nvidia itself rose 2.87 percent to 173.73 dollars in the regular session, before the board story moved the stock the other way after hours. The board met late Wednesday on Jensen Huang's future and had announced no decision by Thursday morning, with the shares down about a further 6 percent in early premarket trading (see the updated Nvidia board story). The close and the overnight tape are telling two different stories about the same stock.

Tesla rose 2.91 percent and the Nasdaq 100 rose 2.28 percent, extending the technology-led tone of the close. No story in this morning's reporting explains the Tesla move, and none is claimed here.

Microsoft fell 2.26 percent while most of the complex rose. This morning's technology synthesis treats the Microsoft, Meta and Amazon declines as name-specific moves on their own accounts rather than a sector signal, and no single reported driver for Microsoft appears in today's beats.

Rare earth miners rose 2.49 percent, with lithium and battery miners up 1.82 percent and uranium miners up 1.48 percent, while gold miners fell 1.48 percent and silver miners fell 1.52 percent. Today's mining story on the split notes the critical-mineral gains came without a matching commodity move, on a session dominated by project news in those metals, including PMET's larger Quebec lithium study and Uzbekistan's plan to double uranium output by 2030 (see today's mining stories). The precious miners tracked their metals lower, with gold futures down 1.05 percent.

Natural gas rose 1.99 percent to 3.28 dollars per million BTU and is up 11.27 percent on the week, the strongest weekly move in the commodity tables. No clear single driver for the gas move appears in this morning's reporting.

The cross-market read is a split tape rather than a single risk impulse. At the close, investors bought the chip complex and critical-mineral miners while selling precious metals and the largest software names, and US yields barely moved, with the 10-year up 1.5 basis points to 5.00 percent. Overnight, the story rotated from prices to governance: the market's biggest chip name went into Thursday with a board decision pending and nothing said, which is now the session's main overhang.
