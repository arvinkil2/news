The European Union has rescheduled its summit with the Gulf Cooperation Council, the Commission announced, without fixing a new date in its statement. The EU GCC summit is a young institution, created to give structure to a relationship that runs on energy, capital and security, and its postponement lands awkwardly.

The agenda that will now wait is the substance of the bloc's Gulf policy: long term gas and hydrogen supply as Europe reorders its energy map around the Hormuz closure, Gulf sovereign investment into European industry, and the migration and security files where Brussels needs Gulf capitals as partners. Gulf states, for their part, have been diversifying their diplomatic calendars eastward, and an EU delay is noticed in Riyadh and Abu Dhabi.

Scheduling statements rarely announce their own significance. But in the same week that European leaders spent two days in Brussels on China and Ukraine, the Gulf file slipping is a fair marker of where the bloc's diplomatic bandwidth actually is.
