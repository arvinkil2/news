Character.AI, the consumer chatbot platform founded in 2021 by former Google engineers Noam Shazeer and Daniel De Freitas, filed for Chapter 11 protection in Delaware on October 12. The filing lists estimated assets and liabilities each in the $50 million to $100 million range. The company said it will keep the platform running during a court-supervised sale and expects the process to finish in late November or early December; if no buyer will keep it operating, the service will be wound down.

Disney Worldwide Services is the stalking-horse bidder. Its $20 million offer buys a non-exclusive, perpetual license to certain Character.AI intellectual property and certain company claims, plus transition services from former Character.AI employees Disney hired last month, including former chief executive Karandeep Anand, who joined Disney as chief technology officer. The bid sets the floor for the auction rather than a final price, and the deal needs court approval. The turn is striking: Disney previously sent Character.AI a cease-and-desist over chatbots modeled on its characters.

The collapse has several causes running together: heavy compute costs, leadership departures after Google's 2024 deal, which paid about $2.7 billion to license the technology and rehire its founders, and a stack of lawsuits and regulatory claims over chatbot safety, including cases brought by families of teenagers. The company settled many suits and barred under-18 users from open-ended chats. For the wider sector, Character.AI is the first large consumer AI name to fail with safety litigation as a stated headwind, which reprices the legal risk attached to companion chatbots generally.
