Deep Yellow has withdrawn its target of making a final investment decision on the Tumas uranium project in Namibia during the fourth quarter of 2026, blaming continued weak equity market conditions for uranium developers. The company set no replacement date, leaving the timing of full scale construction dependent on financing progress and an improved funding environment.

The deferral is not a construction halt. Civil and concrete works at the processing plant site continue, fully funded from existing cash reserves, with about 170 tonnes of reinforcing steel installed across the counter current decantation, clarification, leaching and beneficiation areas, four local contracts worth 3.7 million Australian dollars awarded, environmental clearance certificates renewed to 2029, and 440 safety inductions completed. Deep Yellow says the spending is deliberate de risking ahead of the investment decision, and that an optimisation study and updated value case are nearing completion and will be published as a comprehensive project update.

"Despite the compelling global uranium market fundamentals, challenging equity market conditions continue to constrain funding options for uranium developers," managing director and chief executive Greg Field said. "We believe it is prudent to defer FID beyond Q4 2026 while maintaining construction momentum and progressing our funding strategy to ensure Tumas is well positioned to advance when market conditions improve."

The context is this month's equity tape. Uranium miners fell 8.92 percent in the week to October 17 in this site's market tables, part of a broad washout across growth metal equities even as the underlying metals held most of their ground. Tumas, a fully permitted greenfield project in the Erongo region, is exactly the kind of long life, low cost supply the uranium market says it needs next decade; its financing timetable is now hostage to the share prices of the developer sector rather than to the uranium price itself.

What to watch is the promised project update. Its revised economics, and any shift toward debt, streaming or strategic partner funding and away from a straight equity raise, will show how a tier one uranium developer prices its way through a closed equity window.
