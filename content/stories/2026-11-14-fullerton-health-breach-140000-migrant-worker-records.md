Fullerton Health Group said unauthorised access to a standalone server supporting its Migrant Worker Primary Care Plan programme compromised about 140,000 work pass holder records, about 43,000 of them belonging to people whose passes are no longer valid, potentially exposing full names, foreign identification numbers, dates of birth, mobile numbers, email addresses and residential addresses, but no health, medical or financial information. The group, one of four anchor operators appointed by the Ministry of Manpower to run the plan, said it became aware of the incident on October 31, notified the ministry on November 2 and alerted the public on November 9; it isolated the server and is building an entirely new server to host a new enrolment portal, and its security provider's preliminary investigation found no abnormalities across its other monitored systems. The ministry said it notified affected workers directly in their native languages through its FWMOMCare app and that access to care under the plan has not been disrupted, while the Cyber Security Agency said at least 12 cyber incidents involving Singapore organisations were reported between October 24 and November 13, six of them in healthcare and community care.
