Latest: King Sultan Ibrahim said in a palace statement on Saturday that his conditional pardon order for Najib Razak was clear, explicit and without any ambiguity, that there should be no confusion over its interpretation, and that there should be no prolonged uncertainty over implementing it, Reuters reported. The statement came a day after the Attorney-General's Chambers suspended Najib's transfer to house arrest, saying questions had arisen over the legal interpretation of the pardon and of a court order issued on Friday allowing him to leave jail, and that the issue should go to a court for determination. The chambers said it had never disputed or intended to suspend the pardon order itself. Al Jazeera reported that Najib's lawyers said there was no basis to keep him in prison, and that the prime minister's office later said the attorney general was seeking an audience with the king to express an intent to comply, while insisting legal questions remained for a court. The transfer had not taken place when these reports were published.

Earlier: UMNO, the party Najib Razak once led, said it had completed all payment processes to the Kuala Lumpur High Court on Friday morning for the 50 million ringgit fine, about $12.32 million, raising the money through a fundraising campaign. The payment was a condition of the pardon Malaysia's king granted in September, the second royal pardon in Najib's case, allowing him to serve the remainder of his sentence under house arrest.

Najib, prime minister from 2009 to 2018, was found guilty of corruption linked to 1Malaysia Development Berhad, the state fund he co-founded, and has been in prison since 2022; he denies wrongdoing. Reuters said it was not immediately clear when the transfer to his home would take place, and an aide did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
