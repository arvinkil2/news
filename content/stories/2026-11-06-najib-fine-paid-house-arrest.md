**Latest.** Najib Razak reached his Kuala Lumpur home on Tuesday night in a vehicle escorted by police, a Reuters journalist reported, beginning the house arrest the High Court had cleared earlier that day and ending four years in prison. Home Minister Saifuddin Nasution Ismail said the court set aside last week's order and replaced it with a new one allowing the house arrest to start immediately. His lawyer, Muhammad Shafee Abdullah, said Najib would first return to Kajang Prison to complete formalities and be briefed on the conditions, and that he will serve the remainder of his term at his home in Langgak Duta, an affluent enclave in Kuala Lumpur, through August 2028.

The transfer cost the government a minister the same day. Transport Minister Anthony Loke said on Tuesday he was resigning with immediate effect, keeping a pledge he made in September when the conditional pardon was granted, Bloomberg reported. Loke also leads the Democratic Action Party, which holds the largest number of seats in parliament among the parties in government, and the resignation adds to the strain on Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim's coalition after a run of state election defeats, with the Malacca state polls due on Saturday.

Earlier, Tuesday: Malaysia's High Court has cleared the way for Najib Razak to start his house arrest, state news agency Bernama reported on Tuesday, in a Reuters item carried by Arab News. Najib had been expected to be transferred from jail last week, but the process was stopped after an intervention by the attorney general, whose chambers had said the legal questions over the pardon should go to a court for determination. The report gives no date for the transfer and no conditions attached to it.

Latest, Sunday update: Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim said he has instructed Attorney General Mohd Dusuki Mokhtar to seek an audience with the king immediately, to explain respectfully why the pardon decision was accepted in the SRC case while complications arise from Najib's second case, the 1MDB case, Bernama reported, in an account carried by Scoop on November 7 and read in full in this run. Anwar said the authority and decision of the king had never been questioned. The audience request is the government's stated next step; Najib's transfer had still not taken place in the reporting read.

Earlier, Saturday: King Sultan Ibrahim said in a palace statement on Saturday that his conditional pardon order for Najib Razak was clear, explicit and without any ambiguity, that there should be no confusion over its interpretation, and that there should be no prolonged uncertainty over implementing it, Reuters reported. The statement came a day after the Attorney-General's Chambers suspended Najib's transfer to house arrest, saying questions had arisen over the legal interpretation of the pardon and of a court order issued on Friday allowing him to leave jail, and that the issue should go to a court for determination. The chambers said it had never disputed or intended to suspend the pardon order itself. Al Jazeera reported that Najib's lawyers said there was no basis to keep him in prison, and that the prime minister's office later said the attorney general was seeking an audience with the king to express an intent to comply, while insisting legal questions remained for a court. The transfer had not taken place when these reports were published.

Earlier: UMNO, the party Najib Razak once led, said it had completed all payment processes to the Kuala Lumpur High Court on Friday morning for the 50 million ringgit fine, about $12.32 million, raising the money through a fundraising campaign. The payment was a condition of the pardon Malaysia's king granted in September, the second royal pardon in Najib's case, allowing him to serve the remainder of his sentence under house arrest.

Najib, prime minister from 2009 to 2018, was found guilty of corruption linked to 1Malaysia Development Berhad, the state fund he co-founded, and has been in prison since 2022; he denies wrongdoing. Reuters said it was not immediately clear when the transfer to his home would take place, and an aide did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
