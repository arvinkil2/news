The Bank of Korea's Monetary Policy Board raised the base rate by 25 basis points, from 3.00 percent to 3.25 percent, at its final meeting of the year on Thursday. Five members supported the increase, while Hwang Kunil and Lee Soohyung voted to hold at 3.00 percent. The decision was forecast by 32 of the 34 analysts in a Reuters poll, and it takes the base rate to its highest level since November 2024, after increases in July and August and a pause in October.

The board said domestic economic growth has further strengthened, inflation is projected to remain above the target level for a considerable time, and financial stability risks persist. Growth is now forecast at 3.9 percent for this year and 4.1 percent for next year, both significantly higher than the August forecasts of 3.3 percent and 2.9 percent, with 3.0 percent projected for 2028. The bank credited continued strong exports and investment, driven by the semiconductor sector, and a gradually accelerating recovery in consumption.

Consumer price inflation is projected at 2.7 percent for this year, unchanged from August, and 2.4 percent for next year, raised from 2.3 percent, while core inflation is projected at 2.6 percent in both years, up from 2.5 percent. Both measures stood at 2.8 percent in October. The board said it will decide the timing of further increases while assessing inflation, growth and financial stability, and Governor Shin Hyun-song said the policy response continues until there is confidence that inflation will converge stably to the target. The board's conditional rate projections for six months ahead now reach an upper end of 3.75 percent, up from 3.50 percent, the Seoul Economic Daily reported, with the 21 projections spread across 3.25, 3.50 and 3.75 percent.

On financial stability, the bank noted that household loan growth has accelerated across housing-related and other loans, and that expectations of rising housing prices in Seoul and its surrounding areas remain high even as the pace of increases there has slowed somewhat. The won has stayed broadly stable against the US dollar despite foreign outflows from Korean stocks, supported by a large current account surplus. The board also raised the rate on the Bank Intermediated Lending Support Facility by 25 basis points, to 1.50 percent.
