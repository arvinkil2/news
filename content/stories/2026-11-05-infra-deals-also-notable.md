IFC Africa strategy. Managing director Makhtar Diop told the Africa Financial Summit in Luanda that the International Finance Corporation's annual commitments have exceeded 100 billion dollars, with a capital mobilisation ratio of 3.4 times across all regions and Africa accounting for about 25 percent of commitments. On the continent the mobilisation ratio has risen from about 0.60 to about 1.50 dollars of other investors' money per IFC dollar, and Diop said the target is five times within a few years, with equity financing, local currency lending and guarantees taking a larger share. IFC engagement in Angola has risen from 150 million dollars to more than 1 billion dollars in two years, he said.

Nigeria MSME loan. The Bank of Industry and the IFC have signed a 50 million dollar loan to expand finance for Nigerian micro, small and medium enterprises, Premium Times reported on November 4. It is a small facility against the strategy above, and an example of the wholesale route the IFC is using to reach borrowers its own balance sheet cannot.

Torm tanker joint venture. Danish product tanker owner Torm has struck a deal for eight newbuild tankers through a joint venture structure, Splash247 reported on November 4. Fleet renewal through joint ventures keeps part of the capital commitment off the owner's own balance sheet; no price was carried in the report read in this run, so no figure is claimed.

European energy prices. Eurostat reported on November 4 that non household gas prices in the European Union fell 6.7 percent year on year and that non household electricity prices fell further in the first half of 2026. The prints matter for the energy cost assumptions under European industrial deals, including the chip and ingredients transactions in today's edition.
