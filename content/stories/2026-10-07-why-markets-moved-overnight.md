Natural gas added 3.7 percent to $3.23 by midday, extending the energy war-risk bid into a second day. Today's commodities reporting keeps the Gulf escalation front and center: Qatar is moving empty LNG ships into the Gulf in a move that could boost exports, and gCaptain reporting names a South Korean operator emerging as a new force in Arctic LNG shipping, see today's commodities roundup. Crude, by contrast, gave back the morning's escalation gains: Brent slipped 0.3 percent to $100.87 and WTI fell 0.6 percent to $88.93. The morning's tanker-freight spike cooled too, the freight proxy down 1.1 percent on the day after opening up 6.7 percent, though it remains up about 17 percent on the week.

Uranium miners fell 4.5 percent to 39.9, the sharpest move on the board, reversing the morning's 4 percent jump. Today's mining roundup offers no sector catalyst for either leg of that swing: its uranium items are DevEx's Nabarlek drill results and project news, not a sector event, see today's mining roundup. The pullback was part of a broad miner selloff: rare earth miners dropped 3.0 percent, copper miners 2.8 percent, silver miners 2.8 percent, and gold miners 2.5 percent, with Rio Tinto down 2.9 percent and BHP down 2.0 percent. No single driver for the miner weakness appears in today's reporting beyond the continued softness in bullion.

Silver fell 1.5 percent to $60.26 and gold slipped 1.2 percent to $4,138.10, continuing the rotation out of metals into energy that today's commodities roundup flagged this morning. Copper was the exception among industrial metals, up 1.1 percent to $6.67.

Wheat dropped 2.5 percent to $686.80 with no driver named in today's reporting; Friday's USDA WASDE report is the next scheduled catalyst on the grains calendar. Dry bulk freight rose 3.6 percent to 14.6, also with no single driver named in today's stories.

The cross-market read at midday is a war-risk bid in energy against a rates market bracing for the afternoon. The US 10-year yield rose 4.7 basis points to 5.32 percent, fully reversing the morning's 4.2bp decline after the weak 3-year auction, as traders positioned for the 2pm ET release of the September FOMC minutes and today's $39 billion 10-year auction, see today's Treasury auction story. The dollar firmed 0.4 percent to 102.26 on the same pre-minutes caution. Megacaps were mixed: Amazon added 1.0 percent while Meta fell 1.9 percent, with no fresh named catalysts in today's tech reporting. The morning's shipping-and-crude repricing has cooled intraday, but gas and the freight market's week-on-week surge show the market still pricing a longer Gulf disruption; what happens next belongs to the Fed minutes and the afternoon auction.
