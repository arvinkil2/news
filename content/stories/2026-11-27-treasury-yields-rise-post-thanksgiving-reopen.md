Treasury yields rose across the curve on Friday as the cash market reopened for a shortened session after the Thanksgiving holiday. The two-year yield rose 4.6 basis points to 4.680 percent and the 10-year yield rose 6.6 basis points to 4.913 percent in Asian trading, according to Tradeweb figures reported by Dow Jones. Reuters, reporting later in the session, put the two-year at 4.6891 percent, up 5 basis points, and the benchmark 10-year at 4.9156 percent, up 7 basis points.

The move reverses the direction of the previous eight sessions, in which the 10-year yield fell steadily to 4.85 percent at Wednesday's close despite strong growth and inflation data. Strategists at SEB said the rally since mid-November had been fuelled by the equity selloff, perceived risks of overtightening and lower inflation expectations, and that stabilising risk appetite could push yields back up. Friday's equity rebound did exactly that, reducing demand for haven assets.

The repricing leaves the two-week decline in the 10-year yield at roughly 25 basis points, so Friday's rise is a partial unwind rather than a change of trend. The dollar was on course for a third straight monthly gain, up 1.7 percent against major peers in November to about 103.8, supported by expectations that the Federal Reserve is almost certain to lift interest rates in December and by haven flows from the month's equity losses, Reuters reported.

Attention now turns to the Federal Open Market Committee meeting on December 8 and 9. A 25 basis point increase is fully priced, according to LSEG data cited by Dow Jones, and markets anticipate about 71 basis points of increases by mid-2027. Jefferies economist Mohit Kumar noted that the Fed's pre-meeting blackout period starts on Saturday, so little new guidance will arrive before the decision, and said Jefferies doubts the Fed will match current market pricing. Societe Generale strategists said a restrictive Fed path is largely priced in, capping upside for five-year yields unless policymakers turn more hawkish.
