Polling for Spain's November 29 snap election is consolidating around a right-bloc majority. The 40dB survey published Oct 6 has the PP at 31.6 percent, with the combined PP-Vox vote share at 50 percent. Separate seat projections put the pair at roughly 205 seats in the 350-seat Congress, 29 above the 176-seat absolute majority. A separate survey reported the PP leading the PSOE by 7.6 points.

The campaign backdrop is the congressional rejection of Sanchez's housing decrees that triggered the snap call. The Sumar "Broad Front" rebrand scheduled October 17 will test whether the fragmented left can coordinate. No major candidate announcement or coalition pact was reported overnight.

A PP-Vox government would end the EU's last major Socialist administration, with direct implications for EU fiscal coordination. Spanish assets are already pricing the turnover.

Chatham House's October 5 analysis frames the campaign as a European event, not just a Spanish one. It argues Sanchez felt he had little choice, no full budget since the fragile coalition formed in July 2023, a January decree regularizing 500,000 undocumented migrants, July's Ceuta migrant surge, and Congress's rejection of the emergency housing legislation, and that his La Moncloa speech deliberately pitched the election against Trump, Milei and Flavio Bolsonaro as bogeymen to mobilize the left. On the right, the PP's "Ming Vase strategy" of avoiding mishaps over the 50-day campaign faces the unresolved question of what a Vox coalition would look like, which Chatham House notes likely cost Feijoo victory in 2023. The analysis closes with the continental stakes: Spain's vote is the first in a series, France (spring 2027), Italy, Poland, Finland, Greece, and disruptive elections make it harder to build a coherent joint European approach to security, a problem Washington is watching (status: analytical).
