**Latest.** US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer on Wednesday determined under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 that the acts, policies and practices of 14 economies relating to structural excess capacity and production in manufacturing are unreasonable and burden or restrict US commerce. The 14 economies are China, the European Union, Singapore, Switzerland, Indonesia, Malaysia, Cambodia, Thailand, Korea, Vietnam, Taiwan, Bangladesh, Japan and India. USTR published economy-specific reports supporting each determination, and said no determination has been made on Mexico or Norway at this time. The investigations were opened on March 11, 2026 and covered 16 economies.

USTR proposed responsive tariffs for public comment in each case, in most cases net of existing Section 301 duties and with exemptions for certain goods. The headline proposals are up to 20 percent on goods of China not listed in the notice's annexes, and up to 10 percent on the listed Annex A goods; 15 percent on all goods of the European Union, Japan, Korea, Taiwan and Switzerland; up to 18 percent on Bangladesh, Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia and Thailand; up to 20 percent on Singapore, with a proposed adjustment to 10 percent if Singapore reaches a reciprocal trade agreement; up to 25 percent on India, with 17 percent if India reaches such an agreement; and up to 35 percent on Vietnam, with 20 percent under a reciprocal agreement. For China, USTR said the proposal accounts for the commitments and the 20 percent rate in the Kuala Lumpur Joint Arrangement reached by President Trump and President Xi on October 30, 2025. Written comments and hearing requests are due by January 5, 2027, and USTR will hold hearings on the proposed actions on January 25, 2027.

In a separate action the same day, Greer invited public comment on modifying the existing Section 301 action against Brazil. USTR proposed raising duties on out-of-quota raw and refined sugar imports on a nondiscriminatory basis, and ending the current exemption for finished aircraft from Brazil by imposing an additional 10 percent tariff, saying Brazil has made no meaningful progress after more than a year of talks and continues to export significant amounts of out-of-quota sugar and finished aircraft, currently exempt, to the United States. Hearing requests are due by December 7, 2026, written comments by December 30, 2026, and a hearing is set for January 14, 2027. Brazil already faces a 25 percent tariff on certain goods under the final action taken on July 20, 2026.

Earlier on Wednesday, before the determinations were published, China called the prospect of new US tariffs "political maneuvering" and said the two countries should work together to maintain their trade truce, Bloomberg Law reported. "So-called overcapacity is a false proposition," Foreign Ministry spokesman Guo Jiakun said at a regular press briefing in Beijing. Guo said both sides should work together to implement the consensus reached by their heads of state and safeguard the stable development of China-US economic and trade relations.

The Trump administration had been preparing to raise tariffs on goods from China, the European Union and 14 other economies, the Wall Street Journal and Bloomberg reported on November 24, citing people familiar with the plans. The new duties were expected to cover most products from the targeted economies, with steel, aluminium and automobiles likely excluded, and to sit on top of the separate 10 to 12.5 percent duties announced in July against 60 economies.
