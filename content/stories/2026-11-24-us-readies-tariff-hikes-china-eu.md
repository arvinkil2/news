**Latest.** China called the prospect of new US tariffs on its exports "political maneuvering" on Wednesday and said the two countries should work together to maintain their trade truce, Bloomberg Law reported. "So-called overcapacity is a false proposition," Foreign Ministry spokesman Guo Jiakun said at a regular press briefing in Beijing. Guo said both sides should work together to implement the consensus reached by their heads of state and safeguard the stable development of China-US economic and trade relations. The comments were a response to reports that the Trump administration is preparing to announce the findings of its overcapacity investigation.

The Trump administration is preparing to raise tariffs on goods from China, the European Union and 14 other economies, and US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer could announce the findings of the Section 301 investigation into industrial overcapacity, opened on March 11, as soon as this week, the Wall Street Journal and Bloomberg reported, citing people familiar with the plans. The new duties are expected to cover most products from the targeted economies, with steel, aluminium and automobiles likely excluded, and would sit on top of the separate 10 to 12.5 percent duties announced in July against 60 economies.

Rates are not final. Economies that reached trade agreements with Washington, including the EU, Japan and South Korea, are expected to face 15 percent or less, in line with those pacts, while China is likely to receive a higher rate than most, and Vietnam and India could also face higher rates. S&P 500 and Nasdaq futures extended their early losses after the reports, around 8:00 a.m. ET.
