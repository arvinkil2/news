Amgen announced on November 24 that it has settled the United States patent litigation over ABP 206, its investigational biosimilar to Opdivo (nivolumab), with Bristol Myers Squibb, E. R. Squibb and Sons, and Ono Pharmaceutical. Under the settlement, Amgen will launch ABP 206 in the United States on October 1, 2028, subject to FDA approval, before the expiry of patents that could otherwise have been asserted against it. The settlement terms are confidential.

Amgen's biologics licence application for ABP 206 has been submitted to and accepted for review by the FDA, and the company expects the agency to act on it by the end of 2026. ABP 206 is a monoclonal antibody binding the PD-1 receptor, the same target as Opdivo, which is used across multiple cancer indications. Bristol Myers and Ono had sued Amgen in September 2026 under the Biologics Price Competition and Innovation Act, alleging infringement of seven patents, including one claiming nivolumab's amino acid sequence.

The fixed date has a second use. Opdivo earned 5.9 billion dollars in United States sales in 2025 and is expected to feature in Medicare's 2029 price negotiation round, for which the drug list is due by February 1, 2027. A November 5 CMS rule lets a biosimilar maker cite a publicly announced, precise launch date as evidence its product will reach the market when asking Medicare to delay negotiation of the brand drug. Amgen's release and filing do not say whether it will make that request, and the December 10 deadline for delay requests falls next month.
