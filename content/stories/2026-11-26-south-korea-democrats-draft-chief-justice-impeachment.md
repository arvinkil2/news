South Korea's ruling Democratic Party is drafting an impeachment motion against Supreme Court Chief Justice Jo Hee-de, a week after lawmakers adopted impeachment as party policy following the court's decision to overturn part of the ruling against former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo in the martial law case. The party's task force says the grounds are procedural, not the ruling itself: it accuses Jo of infringing the president's appointment power, refusing to testify at a parliamentary audit, and breaching the special counsel law's deadline for ruling in Han's case, and it is also examining allegations that the court prepared to cooperate with the December 2024 martial law declaration and misspent court funds.

The party holds enough Assembly seats to pass the motion on its own, after which the Constitutional Court would decide whether to remove Jo, but its leaders are weighing the timing against the December 2 constitutional deadline for next year's budget and the risk of a public backlash. The task force aims to finish drafting within one to two weeks and to hold the Assembly vote before the end of the year.
