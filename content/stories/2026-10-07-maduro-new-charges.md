**Latest (Thursday filing):** US prosecutors unsealed a superseding indictment charging Maduro, 63, and Flores, 69, with conspiracy to commit torture, accusing the couple of presiding over a system of repression that used violence against political opposition, arbitrary detention and the torture of detainees, including US citizens, to entrench themselves in power. The indictment lists electric shock, asphyxiation, rape, forced nudity, prolonged isolation, starvation, punishment cells and psychological torture, including threats against victims' families. It accuses Flores of playing a key role, ordering torture and receiving updates on specific prisoners. Both remain in a Brooklyn detention center, where a judge denied Flores's request for house arrest Thursday, and the trial is set for June 2027.

Former Venezuelan president Nicolas Maduro and his wife Cilia Flores were expected to face new US charges over alleged torture of Americans who were in Venezuelan custody in recent years, CNN reported Wednesday, with the announcement possibly coming as early as Thursday. Maduro and Flores were captured from their Caracas home by US special forces on January 3 and are jailed in Brooklyn pending trial on narcoterrorism conspiracy charges, to which they have pleaded not guilty.

The charges, now filed, add a human-rights dimension to a prosecution that has so far centered on drug trafficking, and they land while Washington negotiates the terms of Venezuela's oil reopening with acting president Delcy Rodriguez.
