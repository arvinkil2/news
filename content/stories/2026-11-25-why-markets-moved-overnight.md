Natural gas was the largest mover, up 9.66 percent to 3.64 dollars per million Btu, a fourth consecutive rally session as cold forecasts met European storage at 68.9 percent full against a five-year average near 89 percent (see today's gas rally story). The December contract expires today, adding positioning pressure to the weather bid.

Semiconductors fell 8.27 percent and Nvidia fell 5.86 percent to 155.38 dollars, with Broadcom down 3.98 percent, as the chip complex led a wider technology selloff that began in Asia on tariff fears (see today's Asia stocks story). The reversal erased Monday's rebound across the AI trade.

Tanker freight rose 7.68 percent, extending a weekly gain of more than 26 percent, after Iran's Revolutionary Guard said two tankers had hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz (see today's Hormuz story, updated this morning). Freight is pricing the disruption risk that crude itself is not.

Brent crude fell 3.89 percent to 87.84 dollars a barrel. An expected fourth-quarter surplus and recovering supply outweighed the Hormuz headlines five days before OPEC+ meets on 2027 policy (see today's Brent story).

Copper miners fell 4.27 percent, tracking the technology reversal as the AI-linked bid that lifted miners on Monday unwound (see today's copper miners story).

The cross-market read is a split tape rather than a single risk move: winter energy and freight are bid on physical tightness and disruption, oil is offered on forward surplus, and the growth complex is de-rating together, chips first and miners behind them. Yields eased slightly into today's US data cluster, with the GDP second estimate due at 08:30.
