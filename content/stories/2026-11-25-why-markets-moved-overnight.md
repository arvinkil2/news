Natural gas was the largest mover, up 9.99 percent to 3.65 dollars per million Btu. Today’s gas rally story links the move to cold forecasts, EU storage at 68.9 percent full and the December contract expiry today.

Nvidia fell 7.85 percent to 143.19 dollars. Today’s Nasdaq story reports AI stocks selling off for a second straight day, with Nvidia down as much as 9.15 percent intraday after giving back much of the rally that followed Jensen Huang’s return.

Semiconductors fell 6.18 percent to 120.67 dollars. The Nasdaq story and today’s Asia stocks story both tie the chip selling to AI investment concern and to tariff fears ahead of the US findings on excess industrial capacity.

Broadcom fell 5.33 percent to 285.04 dollars, part of the same chip selloff covered in the Nasdaq and Asia stocks stories. No story in today’s files gives a Broadcom-specific cause beyond that wider selling.

Tesla fell 4.71 percent to 366.58 dollars. No story in today’s files explains this move.

Silver futures fell 4.64 percent to 38.87 dollars an ounce, extending a weekly fall of 13.62 percent. No story in today’s files explains today’s fall; today’s commodities roundup had silver steady on Tuesday after two weeks of liquidation.

Lithium and battery shares fell 4.35 percent to 51.36 dollars. No story in today’s files explains this move; today’s Core Lithium story reports a first concentrate shipment since the Finniss restart, but it does not present that shipment as the cause.

Corn rose 4.26 percent to 452.5 cents a bushel. No story in today’s files explains this move; today’s grain story described corn as quiet on Tuesday, up 0.1 percent, with the US harvest 94 percent complete.

The cross-market read is risk-off in growth and metals, with a separate physical bid in gas. The S&P 500 fell 2.05 percent and the Dow Jones fell 0.87 percent, Brent crude fell 1.77 percent to 89.78 dollars despite the Hormuz headlines, and Treasury yields fell, with the 10-year down 9.5 basis points to 4.81 percent and the 5-year down 10.3 basis points to 4.61 percent.
