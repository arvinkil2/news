EnEarth Holdings has signed a deal to buy Storegga's 30 percent stake in Scotland's Acorn carbon capture and storage project, becoming its lead developer. Storegga put the stake up for sale last December after a strategic review concluded the project was entering a more capital intensive phase, a move that had raised questions over Acorn's viability. Storegga personnel who developed the project will transfer to EnEarth as part of the deal.

Acorn, based at St Fergus in Aberdeenshire, aims to capture and store between 5 million and 10 million tonnes a year of carbon dioxide from industrial, power and hydrogen customers in Scotland, storing it in a depleted North Sea gas field more than 100 kilometres north east of Aberdeen and reusing existing pipeline infrastructure. The remaining shareholders are Shell, Harbour Energy and North Sea Midstream Partners.

The purchase price was not disclosed; reporting put it in the tens of millions of pounds. EnEarth's managing director Nikolas Rigas said the company aims to take a final investment decision by about 2029, which could allow carbon storage to begin in the early 2030s, and that development is expected to cost more than £1 billion. The project still needs substantial further UK government support beyond the £200 million committed in June 2025, whose terms the government has since changed from a grant toward loan and equity investment.
