Mexico's merchandise trade balance swung back into surplus in October, INEGI reported on November 27. The surplus was $298 million, against a deficit of $606 million in September, as exports rose 30.2 percent year on year to $86,009 million and imports rose 30.8 percent to $85,711 million.

The improvement came from the non-oil balance, whose surplus widened from $2,748 million in September to $4,591 million in October, while the oil deficit deepened from $3,354 million to $4,293 million. Non-oil exports rose 31.2 percent on the year, with sales to the United States up 36.8 percent; manufactured exports rose 31.8 percent to $81,243 million, led by electrical and electronic equipment, up 73.6 percent, while automotive exports rose a more modest 5.6 percent. Oil exports fell 3.4 percent to $1,738 million, with crude volumes of 408,000 barrels a day, down from 717,000 a day a year earlier, even though the Mexican export mix averaged $97.45 a barrel.

On a seasonally adjusted basis the surplus was larger, at $2,068 million against $823 million in September, with exports up 5.64 percent on the month. For the first ten months of 2026 the cumulative balance is a surplus of $9,552 million, against a deficit of $2,872 million in the same period of 2025.

The external position matters for the rates debate in Mexico City. Banco de México cut its policy rate by 25 basis points to 6.25 percent on November 5, in a 3 to 2 vote, and its minutes, published November 19, said the majority intends to hold that level for a prolonged period even as the Federal Reserve is priced to raise US rates in December. A trade account in surplus, with export growth concentrated in US-bound manufactures, is one of the supports for holding that line without further peso weakness.
