Latest: Typhoon Champi had injured 527 people in Taiwan, Taiwan's Fire Department said, up from 350 reported earlier in the evening, and one man was killed after being swept by waves from the island of Liuqiu. More than 167,000 homes lost electricity at one point, though most had been reconnected, Taipower said, and the transport ministry counted 242 flights cancelled on Saturday, down from almost 1,000 on Friday. The Central Weather Administration put the weakening storm about 150 kilometres east of Taiwan after it glanced off the northeast coast in the early hours of Saturday, with gusts of about 126 kilometres per hour near its centre, on a forecast track turning north towards the Chinese coast and then into southern Japan. Large parts of northern and central Taiwan, including Taipei, declared a second day of office and school closures on Saturday.

Earlier: Typhoon Champi had killed one person and injured 350 in Taiwan as of 7:30 p.m. on Friday, Taipei time, with no one listed as missing, Taiwan's Central Emergency Operation Center reported, up from 163 injured six hours earlier and 94 in the morning. More than 4,300 people had been evacuated from their homes, and power was cut to thousands of households in Taiwan and to about 8,400 households in Japan's Okinawa islands. The Central Weather Administration put the storm's centre about 12 miles east-northeast of Yilan at midnight on Saturday, moving north and then north-northeast at about 11 miles per hour with maximum sustained winds of about 96 miles per hour, and warned of torrential rain in Yilan's mountains overnight, with more than 400 millimetres already fallen there on Friday and up to 900 millimetres possible between Thursday and Saturday.

Earlier: Typhoon Champi killed one person and injured 94 in Taiwan by late Friday morning, the disaster response centre said. The man who died was swept into the sea off a beach in Liuqiu Township, Pingtung County, and road accidents accounted for the largest share of the injuries. The centre said 1,286 people had been moved to safety. Work and classes were suspended across 21 counties and cities, leaving only Kinmen operating as usual, and the Central Weather Administration maintained sea and land typhoon warnings, with the storm's centre about 330 kilometres east of Eluanbi on Friday morning and its core forecast to be closest between Friday night and Saturday morning. The Civil Aeronautics Administration reported 262 domestic flights and 715 international and cross-strait services cancelled, and the Maritime and Port Bureau counted 10 ferry routes and 96 sailings suspended. President Lai Ching-te urged residents to heed official updates, avoid coastlines and mountain areas, and comply with evacuation measures.

Taiwan will suspend trading on its stock exchange on Friday as Typhoon Champi approaches, the Taiwan Stock Exchange said, with settlements scheduled for Friday postponed to the next business day and offices and schools shut in Taipei, Hsinchu, Taichung, Tainan and Kaohsiung. As of 6 p.m. local time on Thursday, 126 domestic flights and 40 international and cross-strait flights had been cancelled, according to Taiwan's Civil Aviation Administration. Champi was 713 kilometres southwest of Okinawa with top sustained winds of 213 kilometres per hour, equivalent to a Category 4 hurricane, and is forecast to pass closest to northern Taiwan late Friday into Saturday, with extreme rain over greater Taipei that could bring flash floods and landslides.
