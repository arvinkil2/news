Latest: Typhoon Champi killed one person and injured 94 in Taiwan by late Friday morning, the disaster response centre said. The man who died was swept into the sea off a beach in Liuqiu Township, Pingtung County, and road accidents accounted for the largest share of the injuries. The centre said 1,286 people had been moved to safety. Work and classes were suspended across 21 counties and cities, leaving only Kinmen operating as usual, and the Central Weather Administration maintained sea and land typhoon warnings, with the storm's centre about 330 kilometres east of Eluanbi on Friday morning and its core forecast to be closest between Friday night and Saturday morning. The Civil Aeronautics Administration reported 262 domestic flights and 715 international and cross-strait services cancelled, and the Maritime and Port Bureau counted 10 ferry routes and 96 sailings suspended. President Lai Ching-te urged residents to heed official updates, avoid coastlines and mountain areas, and comply with evacuation measures.

Taiwan will suspend trading on its stock exchange on Friday as Typhoon Champi approaches, the Taiwan Stock Exchange said, with settlements scheduled for Friday postponed to the next business day and offices and schools shut in Taipei, Hsinchu, Taichung, Tainan and Kaohsiung. As of 6 p.m. local time on Thursday, 126 domestic flights and 40 international and cross-strait flights had been cancelled, according to Taiwan's Civil Aviation Administration. Champi was 713 kilometres southwest of Okinawa with top sustained winds of 213 kilometres per hour, equivalent to a Category 4 hurricane, and is forecast to pass closest to northern Taiwan late Friday into Saturday, with extreme rain over greater Taipei that could bring flash floods and landslides.
