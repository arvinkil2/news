The US government awarded its first commercial deep-sea mining leases on November 19, selling two areas of seabed off American Samoa to Wetstone US Ventures, a New York based startup. Wetstone was the only company to submit bids and paid the minimum bid of $3 million for each area, $6 million in total, for 20-year leases covering about 31.5 million acres northeast of the Manua Islands. A second registered participant, Norway's Adepth Minerals, attended but did not bid.

The leases authorise surveys, mapping and other exploratory work, not commercial extraction, which would need further approvals and environmental review. Lease terms set royalties at 2 percent of production value during the first five years and 5 percent thereafter. The sale, run by the Interior Department's Marine Minerals Administration at a venue in Thousand Oaks, California, drew several dozen protesters and advances the administration's April 2025 directive to accelerate access to ocean floor critical minerals, including nickel, cobalt, copper and manganese, as Washington seeks to counter China's dominance of mineral supply chains.

The auction went ahead three days after US District Judge Jill Otake in Hawaii ruled that the National Marine Fisheries Service had violated the Endangered Species Act by assessing only the lease sale itself and not the exploration, development and mining that could follow, and voided the agency's letter of concurrence. The Marine Minerals Administration said it had responded by starting a fresh consultation with the fisheries service, but it declined to postpone the sale. Earthjustice, which represents Samoan plaintiffs, said the ruling made the auction unlawful and that it would push for the leases to be voided.

Three lawsuits now challenge the programme: the Matai council of Samoan chiefs case on federal authority, filed October 19; the case behind Judge Otake's ruling; and a suit filed November 18 by Finafinau and the Natural Resources Defense Council over the adequacy of the environmental review. The administration is considering further seabed auctions near the Northern Mariana Islands, Virginia and Alaska, so the legal outcome here will set the template for the rest of the programme.
