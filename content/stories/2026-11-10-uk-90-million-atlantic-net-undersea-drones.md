The United Kingdom will invest 90 million pounds in new drones, sensors and artificial intelligence systems for the Royal Navy under a framework agreement with Ocean Infinity Solutions, a Hampshire based company. The Ministry of Defence announced the framework on November 10 at the International Sea Power Conference in Oslo, and said it was due to be signed the same day.

The system, called Atlantic Net, is the first phase of the Royal Navy's Atlantic Bastion programme, which aims to build a hybrid force of crewed and uncrewed vessels, a key recommendation of the Strategic Defence Review. Atlantic Net will field a network of autonomous systems across the North Atlantic: advanced sensors to detect submarines and hostile activity, and uncrewed systems to patrol at depth. The network is designed to give submarine hunting warships and Astute class submarines earlier warning and a clearer picture of activity beneath the surface.

The stated purpose is the protection of critical undersea infrastructure, including communications cables and energy networks, alongside protection of shipping routes and the nuclear deterrent. The programme works alongside NATO and Joint Expeditionary Force allies.

The framework will support almost 200 skilled jobs at Ocean Infinity and in its immediate supply chain, according to the ministry. Minister for Defence Readiness and Industry Luke Pollard said the investment gives the Royal Navy new technology to find threats earlier and protect critical infrastructure while backing British business.

For this beat, the item matters as procurement structure as much as strategy: the state is buying persistent undersea surveillance as a framework service from a commercial robotics operator, integrating maritime systems, underwater sensing, communications, remote operations and data services into a single capability directed by the Royal Navy.
