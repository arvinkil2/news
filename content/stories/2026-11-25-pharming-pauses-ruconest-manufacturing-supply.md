Pharming has temporarily paused manufacturing of Ruconest, its recombinant C1 esterase inhibitor for acute hereditary angioedema attacks, after a routine simulation of the aseptic manufacturing process, a media fill, failed at the company's third-party fill-finish manufacturer. Pharming said the pause is likely to lead to supply constraints in the United States and that it has notified the FDA.

Product already at specialty pharmacies or in patients' hands is not affected and can be used as directed, the company said. Current supply is available through mid-December. Beyond that point, availability depends on the investigation, the release of finished inventory and the resumption of production, none of which has a confirmed date. Pharming said production will not restart until the root cause has been identified and resolved and the manufacturing process has been successfully requalified, and that it will update the market once the investigation concludes.

The company said it is working with patients, healthcare providers, the Hereditary Angioedema Association and its specialty pharmacy partners to maintain access to treatment options, and encouraged patients to review their hereditary angioedema action plans with their physicians. The manufacturing matter does not affect any other Pharming medicine.

The pause lands while Pharming is also searching for a new chief executive and, per trade reporting yesterday, awaiting a leniolisib readout and weighing deals. A supply interruption in its established US product is the more immediate problem: hereditary angioedema attacks are treated on demand, so a gap in availability is felt by patients directly rather than through a dosing schedule that can be shifted.
