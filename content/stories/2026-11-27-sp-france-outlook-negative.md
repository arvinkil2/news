S&P Global Ratings on Friday revised France's sovereign outlook to "negative" from "stable" and affirmed the country's rating at "A+/A-1", citing growing budget pressures as weak economic growth and higher interest costs make it harder to reduce the deficit. The move, made in a scheduled review, leaves all three major agencies with a negative outlook on French debt: Fitch holds France at A+ with a negative outlook since October 30, and Moody's holds it at Aa3, also negative.

S&P has projected a 2027 deficit of 5.6 percent of GDP, against the 5 percent target of Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu's government and the European Commission's estimate of 5.4 percent, and the 2027 budget remains unresolved, with the government lacking a majority in the National Assembly. Economy Minister Roland Lescure said the government took note of the decision and called it a demonstration of the "absolute necessity" of a budget that repairs public finances, with a deficit of no more than 5 percent of GDP.
