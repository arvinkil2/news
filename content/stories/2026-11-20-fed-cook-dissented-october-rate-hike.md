Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook said she dissented at the Federal Open Market Committee's October meeting, where the committee held the policy rate steady, because she preferred to raise the target range by another quarter percentage point. Cook, who supported September's increase to 3.75 to 4 percent, said she is skeptical that a single quarter-point increase will be enough to change the path of inflation and that financial conditions are too accommodative while inflation remains stubbornly above target.

Cook put the 12-month change in the PCE price index at 3.8 percent in October and core PCE inflation at 3.2 percent, noting that inflation has exceeded the Fed's 2 percent target for 67 months. Waiting longer to act risks falling behind the curve and requiring larger increases later, she said, adding that holding the rate steady is itself a policy action that will widen the output gap and add to inflationary pressure.
