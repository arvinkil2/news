**Latest.** The Securities and Exchange Commission announced its own parallel charges on Friday, saying it had obtained a temporary restraining order, an asset freeze and expedited discovery against Backward Hat Capital, its co-founders Phillip Williams and Devin Johnson, Johnson's company Traders Point Capital, and Joseph Anderson. The SEC complaint alleges the defendants fraudulently raised approximately 560 million dollars from more than 2,000 investors between about July 2022 and the present, made more than 250 million dollars in Ponzi-like payments to earlier investors, and transferred more than 220 million dollars to themselves. Anderson is charged with creating fake audit and quality of earnings reports for investors; the SEC charges violations of the Securities Act of 1933 and the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, and, for Williams and Traders Point Capital, the Investment Advisers Act of 1940. The U.S. Attorney's Office for the Southern District of New York has also filed charges against Williams and Johnson, the SEC said.

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission filed a complaint in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Indiana against Phillip Williams and Devin Johnson, both Indiana residents and executive officers, and 11 companies that collectively operated as the Backward Hat Common Enterprise, alleging they ran a Ponzi scheme that received more than 550 million dollars from at least 2,100 participants. The defendants claimed participant funds would trade leveraged retail foreign currency contracts and touted historical returns exceeding 6 percent monthly without a single losing month; the complaint alleges they conducted little to no forex trading and instead misappropriated participant funds for personal use or paid fake trading profits to other participants, with solicitations continuing at least through October 2026.

The district court entered a restraining order on Wednesday freezing the assets of the defendants and four relief defendants and allowing the CFTC to inspect their records, and on Friday appointed Kevin B. Duff as temporary receiver to take control of the company defendants, certain relief defendants and the individual defendants' assets. The CFTC seeks restitution, disgorgement, civil monetary penalties, trading and registration bans and a permanent injunction. The Securities and Exchange Commission filed a parallel civil action seeking an emergency restraining order, and the FBI arrested Williams and Johnson on Wednesday.
