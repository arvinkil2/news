Japan ran a trade deficit of 476.5 billion yen in October, 57.8 percent wider than the 301.9 billion yen deficit of October 2025, the Ministry of Finance said in its provisional trade statistics. Exports rose 20.5 percent from a year earlier to 11,768.8 billion yen, led by semiconductors and related goods, up 49.7 percent, and motor vehicles, up 19.9 percent, while imports rose 21.6 percent to 12,245.3 billion yen, led by mineral fuels, up 36.5 percent, with the petroleum import bill up 60.5 percent in value.

The full month deficit is smaller than the 810.4 billion yen shortfall recorded in the month's first 20 days, pointing to a late month swing toward surplus. Exports to the United States rose 23.9 percent to 2,173.9 billion yen against imports from the United States up 19.7 percent, and exports to Taiwan rose 42.7 percent and to South Korea 31.4 percent, reflecting the semiconductor cycle running through East Asian supply chains.
