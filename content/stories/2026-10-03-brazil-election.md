Brazilians vote Sunday in the first round of the presidential election, and the polls agree on one thing: it is effectively a tie. Datafolha's final poll has Lula at 42 and Flávio Bolsonaro at 40; AtlasIntel/Bloomberg's late-September survey put it at 45.3 to 42.2, with the simulated runoff at 47.6–47.7 — a dead heat. No candidate is near the 50% of valid votes needed to win outright, so a runoff on October 25 is the base case.

The Bolsonaro clan is contesting without its patriarch: Jair Bolsonaro is serving a 27-year sentence for the 2022 coup attempt, with his son Flávio carrying the movement's banner. The result will decide fiscal policy, the investment posture toward commodities and foreign capital, and Brazil's alignment in a region being pulled between Washington and Beijing.

Watch the Superior Electoral Court count from Sunday evening, with Minas Gerais, São Paulo, and the Northeast as the telling margins — and watch for institutional stress in any contested runoff, given the former president's imprisonment.
