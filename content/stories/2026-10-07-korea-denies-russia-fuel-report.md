South Korea's presidential office on Wednesday rejected a claim by Ukraine's top sanctions official, Vladyslav Vlasiuk, that fuel loaded at South Korean ports onto seven tankers, including vessels under British and EU sanctions, had eased a Russian fuel crisis caused by Ukrainian drone strikes on refineries. Seoul said it had "consistently enforced sanctions and export controls against Russia" since the 2022 invasion, and that it was examining the facts and would act if export control problems were confirmed. The accusation deepens a diplomatic rift that began last month over the transfer of two North Korean prisoners of war, which Seoul had meant to keep secret; Kyiv says closed diplomatic channels are working to resolve it.
