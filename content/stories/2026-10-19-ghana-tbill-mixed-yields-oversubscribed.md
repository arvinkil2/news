Ghana's latest treasury bill auction was marginally oversubscribed on bids and heavily rationed on acceptance. Investors tendered 5.173 billion cedis against a target of 5.019 billion, and the treasury accepted 3.481 billion, MyJoyOnline reported from the Bank of Ghana's auction results.

Demand concentrated at the front. The 91-day bill drew 2.944 billion cedis of bids, 70.5 percent of the total, of which 2.202 billion was accepted. The 182-day drew 1.109 billion and accepted 692.30 million, and the 364-day drew 1.117 billion and accepted 587.61 million.

Yields moved in opposite directions along the short curve. The 91-day yield rose 2 basis points to 4.62 percent and the 182-day rose to 6.31 percent from 6.27 percent the previous week, while the 364-day yield fell 4 basis points to 9.71 percent. A government that can decline a third of the bids on offer while its one-year bill yield falls is funding itself on its own terms, but the tenor mix leaves the rollover calendar dominated by 91-day paper.
