India has relaxed rules allowing vessels more than 20 years old to register under its flag, according to a government order, potentially expanding its domestic shipping fleet and reducing reliance on foreign carriers.

The order, No. 31 of 2026 from the Directorate General of Maritime Administration, sets conditions for 15 to 20 year old oil tankers to join the registry, including specified Condition Assessment Programme ratings. For vessels above 20 years, including oil tankers, bulk carriers, general cargo vessels and offshore ships, entry requires case-by-case approval by a five-member DGMA committee, which examines technical condition, classification records and statutory certification. The order also removes exit age limits for Indian-flag ships, which may now keep operating at any age subject to safety and technical compliance. Previously, vessels older than 20 years were not permitted to register.

India, a major consumer of oil, gas and other commodities, relies heavily on foreign carriers for its imports. Indian-flagged vessels get priority in securing certain government and state-run company cargo contracts before foreign-flagged ships are considered, and a larger flagged fleet improves India's access to ships in times of crisis. The relaxation reverses age restrictions introduced in January this year.
