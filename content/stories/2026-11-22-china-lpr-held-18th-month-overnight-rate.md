China's one year loan prime rate came in at 3.00 percent on Friday, unchanged from the previous month, and the over five year LPR, on which many lenders base their mortgage rates, was held at 3.50 percent, according to the National Interbank Funding Center. Both rates have now been unchanged for 18 straight months, since the last cut in May 2025, and the November fixing matched the expectations of most participants in a Reuters survey of 28 market watchers.

Reuters reported that narrowing net interest margins deterred lenders from trimming borrowing costs despite weak credit demand. A minority of survey respondents, five of the 28, had expected a cut, which keeps attention on easing steps outside the LPR, such as reserve requirement cuts or targeted lending.

The LPR's signalling role has in any case shrunk. Since November 2, overnight reverse repo operations at 1.25 percent have been the People's Bank of China's main daily operation, replacing the seven day repo as the framework's anchor and effectively lowering its floor by 15 basis points from the previous 1.40 percent. The central bank plans to let strong companies price loans off the overnight rate, further reducing the benchmark's role.

The next LPR announcement falls on December 21, moved from Sunday December 20. For now, Chinese monetary easing is arriving through the operating framework and bank funding costs, not through the rates printed on household mortgages.
