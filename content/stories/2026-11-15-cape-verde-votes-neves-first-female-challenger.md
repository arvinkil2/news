Voting ran from 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. on Sunday across Cape Verde, the Atlantic archipelago of about 500,000 people off the coast of Senegal. More than 300,000 people were eligible to vote at home, and nearly 77,000 diaspora Cape Verdeans were registered to vote at embassies abroad, in a country where more citizens live overseas, mainly in Portugal and the United States, than on the islands.

President Jose Maria Neves, 66, is seeking a second term. A member of the African Party for the Independence of Cape Verde, the country's oldest party, he served 15 years as prime minister before winning the presidency in 2021, and his party took 37 of 72 seats in legislative elections in May, which made Francisco Carvalho prime minister. Analysts tipped Neves to perform best among the six candidates.

His strongest challenger is Joana Rosa of the Movement for Democracy, a former justice minister and the first woman to run for president since independence from Portugal in 1975. Her party governed for 10 years before the May legislative vote. Under Cape Verde's hybrid parliamentary-presidential system the president holds veto authority and a mediating role, so the contest shapes how the new parliamentary majority will be checked.

Emigration dominated the campaign. The tourism-dependent archipelago has lost many of its young people to jobs abroad, and both leading candidates made it their central theme: Neves promised a youth summit in a second term, while Rosa said government should make it possible for young people to build their futures at home and stop the continued exodus.

A candidate needs more than 50 percent of the vote to win outright. If none clears that bar, the top two go to a runoff on November 29. Official provisional results are expected within two to three days.
