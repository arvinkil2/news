TAL Daiichi Life Australia Pty, a unit of Japan's Daiichi Life that already holds 20.36 percent of Challenger, has submitted an indicative proposal to buy the more than 79 percent of the Australian investment manager it does not own, Challenger said on Monday. The offer of 11.80 Australian dollars a share is a 19.8 percent premium to Challenger's last close on Friday and values the company at 7.94 billion Australian dollars, about 5.53 billion US dollars. The proposal is subject to conditions including satisfactory due diligence, and Challenger's board said it is considering the indicative offer.

The market moved first. Challenger shares jumped more than 15 percent on Monday to 11.33 Australian dollars, their highest level since October 2018, and were up 16.6 percent at 11.49 dollars later in the session. In a separate statement, Daiichi Life confirmed that its unit had submitted the non-binding proposal and said no decision had been made on proceeding with a binding transaction. Daiichi first entered the register in April 2025, acquiring a 15.1 percent stake from MS&AD Insurance Group Holdings, and has since built its holding to 20.36 percent.

The two headline values in circulation reconcile cleanly. The 7.94 billion Australian dollars is the value of all of Challenger at the offer price; the roughly 4.4 billion US dollars reported by Bloomberg is the cost of acquiring only the shares Daiichi does not already hold. Challenger also reported on Monday that assets under management rose 5 percent in the first quarter of its 2027 financial year, on strong annuity sales at home and offshore. The story stays developing: there is no binding offer, no board recommendation, and no timetable yet.
