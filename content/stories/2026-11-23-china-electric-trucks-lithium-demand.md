Surging electric truck sales in China, driven by higher oil prices, are driving demand for lithium, Reuters reported on November 23, calling it an unexpected bright spot for a battery metal weighed down by oversupply worries. Electric truck sales in China hit 600,000 in the first ten months of the year, up 45 percent and accounting for 30 percent of sales across truck sizes, according to BofA Global Research cited by Reuters.

Growth is strongest in heavy trucks, which carry the largest batteries and therefore the most lithium, with sales up 68 percent to 262,000 units. Chinese heavy electric truck exports more than doubled to 16,823 units in the four months after the strikes on Iran began on February 28, data from China's General Administration of Customs showed.

Electric trucks globally will use nearly 7 percent of lithium supply this year and 12 percent by 2030, equivalent to demand from up to 11.5 million battery electric cars, according to YJ Lee of the Arcane Green Metal Fund, speaking to Reuters. Consultancy SFA Oxford has raised its 2026 forecast for lithium demand from heavy-duty electric vehicles by 95 percent in the last year.

CATL, the world's largest battery maker, told Reuters that commercial vehicles are the next major growth area after passenger vehicles and energy storage. The demand shift lands while lithium and battery equities are still falling, with the mining table's lithium and battery basket down 4.33 percent on Friday and 11.54 percent on the week, so the truck data is a demand signal the market has not yet priced.
