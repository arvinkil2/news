Shi Bingzhe, 34, was handed 41 charges in a Singapore court on November 13, all for acquiring the benefits of criminal conduct, and is the fifth person charged in the Nvidia chips case, after Aaron Woon Guo Jie, Alan Wei Zhaolun, Chinese national Li Ming and Jenny Lim were charged earlier with fraud, money laundering and fraudulent trading. Four firms said to be linked to exporting computer servers that might contain Nvidia chips to Malaysia, Aperia International, A-Speed Infotech, Aperia Cloud Services (II) and Luxuriate Your Life, were charged in July with false representation, and police have placed a prohibition of disposal order on a bungalow valued at about $55 million and seized about $1 million from bank accounts linked to the probe.

The case began with a United States investigation into whether DeepSeek, the Chinese AI start-up, got around American restrictions on advanced Nvidia chips by buying them from third parties in countries including Singapore.
