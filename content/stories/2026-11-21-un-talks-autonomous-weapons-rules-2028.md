The 128 countries meeting in Geneva under the UN Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons agreed on Saturday to elaborate a possible accord regulating lethal autonomous weapons over the next two years, with the work to determine which systems could be banned under international humanitarian law and which could be permitted but regulated. Nearly 100 nations had pushed for swift adoption of legally binding rules, but major arms producers including the United States, Russia and Israel opposed that course, and decisions under the convention require consensus.

The compromise replaced the word negotiate with elaborate in the agreed text, a downgrade the conference chair, Swiss diplomat Julien Thoeni, acknowledged while calling the outcome a significant step for humanitarian disarmament. The Stop Killer Robots coalition called the mandate watered down and said delaying a binding instrument puts civilians at risk, and Human Rights Watch called the result disappointing, saying the technology is racing ahead of the rules.
