Australia will overhaul the annual performance test for superannuation funds to make room for emerging assets, Treasurer Jim Chalmers announced in Melbourne on Wednesday. Treasury will introduce a new voluntary benchmark for investments in asset classes the existing indices represent poorly, such as venture capital, agriculture, startups, housing and energy, capped at up to 5 percent of a fund's portfolio and benchmarked against inflation rather than a financial index, and Chalmers estimated the loosening could unlock up to $90 billion of investment.

The package has two further parts. The test will expand to cover externally directed platform products, bringing in 1.4 million more members, phased in with results reported in 2027-28 before full application in 2028-29, and Treasury will specify the indices used in APRA determinations, with a regular review of the test every three to five years. Chalmers said the 5 percent cap is a starting point subject to future reviews and insisted the changes strengthen rather than water down a test whose failure rate has fallen from 16 percent in its first year to 2 percent.
