**Latest (Tuesday 07:27 ET):** President Trump said he is open to a trilateral summit with Putin and Xi, but would focus it on nuclear arms rather than Ukraine. Trump said on Thursday that Washington was holding talks with Moscow and Beijing aimed at denuclearization, and told Newsmax the United States was discussing the issue with Russia and China, Arab News reported, carrying the Journal's account. The Journal report did not say whether Putin's proposed summit was linked to the denuclearization discussions. Kyiv responded that a Russia, China and US summit would prolong the war, France24 reported. The proposal itself remains a reported proposal: it rests on the Journal's sourcing, and no meeting, agenda or date has been agreed by the three capitals.

President Vladimir Putin proposed a three way meeting with President Donald Trump and President Xi Jinping in November to discuss ending the war in Ukraine, raising the idea in an October 9 telephone call with Trump, the Wall Street Journal reported, via Kommersant. The Journal, citing a former Russian intelligence officer, said the same call also yielded a deal for the US to buy Russian diesel and facilitate its sale on the global market, and that Putin's suggested settlement would favor Moscow. Russia has passed the proposal to Beijing through diplomatic channels, and the Chinese side has signalled it would not object if Trump agrees to take part. The Kremlin has proposed holding the meeting at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen on November 18 and 19, and spokesman Dmitry Peskov told the Journal there is no clear understanding yet on the meeting itself or its potential agenda, although all three sides acknowledge the hypothetical possibility of holding one. Russia and the United States hold the world's two largest nuclear arsenals, while China has rapidly expanded its own, according to the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute, which is the arithmetic behind Trump's denuclearization framing.
