Tencent president Martin Lau, speaking on the company's third quarter earnings call, said the new US regulation barring Chinese companies from remotely accessing advanced GPUs will have an unfortunate impact on Tencent's business. "It does set everyone back. It sets everyone back in China," he said, adding that everyone has less advanced compute to work with regardless of when they started leasing or building capacity outside China, and that the successor to Tencent's Hy4 model will probably take longer in the short run.

Lau said the ban would not hamper Tencent's inference workloads, which made-in-China chips should be able to cover, and that over time the domestic supply chain would come on board, with the latest restriction likely to speed that up as development effort concentrates on it. He said Tencent can no longer offer high-end GPU cloud services, while its non-GPU cloud business, where revenue rose 35 percent year on year against 8 percent growth overall, can still build capacity outside China itself.
