US imports of crude oil from Venezuela averaged 701,000 barrels a day in July 2026, the most since May 2017, before easing to 655,000 barrels a day in August, the Energy Information Administration said on November 6. Imports started the year at 200,000 barrels a day and rose in most months, supported by high refinery demand on strong global margins, the easing of US restrictions on purchases from Venezuela, and a Gulf Coast refining fleet configured for heavy sour grades of the kind Venezuela produces.

The destination of Venezuelan exports has flipped in the process. The EIA, citing Vortexa data, said most Venezuelan crude exports went to China in 2025, while in 2026 Venezuela has exported no crude to China and the United States has instead been the largest single destination by volume.
