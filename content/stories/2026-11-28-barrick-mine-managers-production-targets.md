Barrick Mining has given mine managers more responsibility in setting and meeting production targets after years of missed forecasts eroded investor confidence in the world's third largest gold miner by 2025 output, Barrick International chief executive Sebastiaan Bock said. Barrick had made a habit of issuing three year production forecasts and resetting them a year later, hurting credibility, group chief executive Mark Hill told Mining Forum Americas in Colorado Springs in September.

"A year ago, we worked with mine leaders to rebuild our guidance from the ground up, and we gave them more responsibility and accountability for meeting their new targets," Bock said in emailed replies this month. The international operations he oversees have collectively met or exceeded annual production guidance for seven straight years. "In 2027, investors should judge us by whether we continue to improve safety, meet our guidance, control costs, and deliver our growth projects on time and on budget," he said.

The comments come as Barrick prepares to float its North American gold business as American Barrick Gold, with an initial public offering expected by early next year that will give investors direct exposure to its interests in Nevada Gold Mines, the Fourmile project in Nevada and Pueblo Viejo in the Dominican Republic. The Toronto based parent intends to retain majority ownership while developing its international gold and copper assets, including the Lumwana expansion in Zambia and the delayed Reko Diq project in Pakistan. Barrick shares gained 3.7 percent to $51.70 in Toronto by mid Friday, valuing the company at about $84 billion, though the stock is down 11 percent from a year ago.

On costs, Bock said third quarter copper all in sustaining costs of $4.52 a pound were a quarterly figure, with the year to date at $4.05, and that full year copper costs are expected at or very slightly above the top of the $3.45 to $3.75 guidance range. Drivers included higher diesel prices, local currency appreciation, higher royalties linked to stronger copper prices and additional waste stripping. Full year copper production guidance of 190,000 to 220,000 tonnes is unchanged.

The Lumwana expansion remains on budget and on schedule for first copper at the end of the first quarter of 2028, with all major long lead equipment procured, and is expected to double the mine's copper production. On Reko Diq, where development was slowed in April amid the regional security situation, Bock said Barrick expects to provide an update on its review by mid 2027, with any acceleration depending first on security and then on a financing plan and capital estimate.
