The West Virginia Public Service Commission has approved a 2.7 billion dollar plan by Monongahela Power and Potomac Edison, FirstEnergy's West Virginia utilities, to build a 1.2 GW combined-cycle natural gas plant and 70 MW of solar, Utility Dive reported. The commission's November 17 decision approved recovery of construction and financing costs through a monthly surcharge.

The surcharge is expected to raise residential bills by 1.18 dollars a month, or 0.9 percent, initially, will be recalculated every six months on actual construction-work-in-progress balances, and will run until the assets enter base rates after the gas plant comes online, expected in late 2031. The plant, called the Maidsville Energy Center, is expected to cost 2.48 billion dollars.

The project was prompted by an unnamed data center customer requiring an initial 100 MW in 2028, rising incrementally to 1,012 MW by 2033, according to commission staff. A commission analysis found Mon Power could face a 60 MW capacity shortfall in 2028, growing to 2,194 MW in 2034 with new large loads, amid surging PJM capacity prices.

Consumer advocates and environmental groups opposed the project, arguing the data center load may not materialise and the surcharge shifts project risk to ratepayers. The Sierra Club argued the project would raise the utility's 4 billion dollar rate base by 67 percent and said large-load tariffs should ensure recovery from the customers driving the upgrades. The commission noted the surcharge mechanism prevents credit downgrading from a lack of funds from operations, and said that if construction ceases or is extraordinarily delayed it will open a proceeding on the applicable recovery treatment.
