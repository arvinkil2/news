PC Gold has delivered a prefeasibility study outlining a $1.59 billion pre-tax valuation and a maiden 951,000 ounce gold ore reserve at its Spring Hill project in the Northern Territory, about 200 km southeast of Darwin. The study establishes the potential for a standalone open pit and underground operation producing approximately 1.03 million ounces of gold over an initial 9.5 year mine life, at an average of 120,000 ounces annually during its first five full years.

The study estimates a pre-tax net present value of $1.59 billion and an internal rate of return of 66 percent, based on a gold price of $5,500 per ounce. Pre-production capital is estimated at $413 million, with life of mine all-in sustaining costs of $2,870 per ounce and a projected payback period of 1.3 years from first gold. Executive chair and chief executive Ashley Pattison said average annual free cash flow of $277 million from a 4 million tonne per annum operation demonstrated the quality of the asset.

The maiden probable ore reserve comprises 35.5 million tonnes grading 0.83 grams per tonne gold, including approximately 901,000 ounces from open pit operations and 50,000 ounces underground. The reserve was estimated at a more conservative A$4,500 per ounce and is built on a resource covering only 1.2 kilometres of strike. The broader production plan includes approximately 12 percent inferred mineral resources by contained ounces, which will require further drilling to improve confidence.

PC Gold has completed approximately 40,000 metres of drilling since the resource estimate's assay cut-off, with an updated estimate targeted for March 2027. The company is targeting completion of its definitive feasibility study and a final investment decision in the third quarter of 2027, ahead of first gold in early 2029.
