Britain has sanctioned eight construction and property development firms involved in settlement expansion in the occupied West Bank, subjecting each to an asset freeze and director disqualification. The firms named are Har Hevron Development Company, Hemdat Shomron, S.A.G. (Velvel) Construction and Development, Lev HaHar Yazamut, Harey Zahav, Ariel Residential Judea and Samaria, Ami Gvirtzman Architects 2021 and Z.F. Building Company, which the government said have helped to plan, build, supply or market housing and public infrastructure in Israeli settlements.

The same package sanctions Tzav 9, an Israeli activist movement the government said has organised and carried out blockades of crossings, ports and roads used by humanitarian aid convoys bound for Gaza, most recently in October. Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband said protecting the two-state solution is the government's guiding aim, with a direct message on the E1 project: do not bid, do not build, and do not profit from its destruction.

The designations are the first under the expanded Global Human Rights and International Humanitarian Law sanctions regime, laid on October 13, and deliver on Miliband's September 8 pledge to respond to accelerating settlement expansion. The government said more settlements have been approved under the current Israeli government in four years than in the previous twenty, and that it continues to welcome trade with green line Israel while distinguishing it from activity in settlements.
