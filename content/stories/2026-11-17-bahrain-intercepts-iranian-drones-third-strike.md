**Latest.** Kuwait and Bahrain dealt with fresh Iranian aerial attacks early on Wednesday, and the Saudi-led Coalition to Restore Legitimacy in Yemen said its precision strikes destroyed 60 Houthi military targets across Yemen the same night, Arab News reported. Kuwait's General Staff said any explosions heard were its air defences intercepting hostile attacks and urged the public to follow safety instructions. The Bahrain Defense Force said it intercepted a number of aerial attacks from Iran; Bahrain's interior ministry issued two shelter warnings overnight, each followed by an all-clear, and Civil Defense evacuated a multistory building in Souq Waqif as a precaution and put out a fire there, with the cause still under investigation and no casualties reported. Coalition spokesperson Maj. Gen. Turki Al-Maliki said the strikes hit Houthi locations in Sanaa, Amran, Saada and Hodeidah, destroying tunnels housing underground ballistic missile storage and drone assembly and storage workshops; no casualty figures were given for the Yemen strikes.

Kuwait's army said on Tuesday evening that its air defence systems were working to prevent drone attacks on the country, in a statement posted on X and reported by Anadolu Agency. The army said any explosion sounds heard would come from its air defences engaging what it called enemy attacks, gave no detail on who launched the drones or where they were aimed, and urged the public to follow safety instructions. No casualties or damage were reported. The statement came hours after the Bahrain interception and the US strike on Qeshm Island, making Kuwait the second Gulf state to engage incoming drones on Tuesday.

American warplanes hit an Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps missile launch site on Qeshm Island in the Persian Gulf on Tuesday, a US military official told Bloomberg, confirming the strike Iran's semi-official Mehr news agency had reported earlier in the day. The official, declining to be named, said the strike answered Iranian aggression targeting US personnel and assets; Mehr said the assault caused no casualties or damage to critical and public-service infrastructure. President Trump separately said the US is investigating a report about potential Iranian plans to target Washington. Brent crude rose toward $94 a barrel on the fresh flare-up, still up more than 50 percent for the year. Earlier on Tuesday, Iran's parliamentary speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf warned Tehran would retaliate against civilian infrastructure near US and Israeli bases if Washington struck the same in Iran, and Qatar, the main mediator between Washington and Tehran, was due to meet Iranian representatives on Tuesday.

Iran's semi-official Mehr news agency reported that a US attack on Iran's Qeshm Island on Tuesday caused no damage to residential and commercial areas or infrastructure, citing a Hormozgan provincial official, Reuters reported. There was no immediate US confirmation of a strike on Qeshm, which sits at the Strait of Hormuz, and the report is carried here as Iran's account of the day's exchange.

Iran fired drones toward a US military radar system in Bahrain on Tuesday, and Bahrain said its air defences intercepted them after authorities issued a security alert urging residents to seek shelter. The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said its target was the radar system at the Sheikh Isa base, home to the US Fifth Fleet, and US Central Command said the drones were intercepted without damage to US equipment or injury to American personnel; Bahrain announced an all-clear later in the morning, with no deaths or injuries reported.

The attack was the third Iranian strike in five days, following a wave Bahrain repelled on Sunday and an initial attempt on Friday, and it ends a roughly two-month lull in Gulf strikes. Qatar warned that the attack on the US base threatens peace talks, and Reuters has reported that President Trump has told advisers he may not attack Iran again if there are no further provocations from the IRGC or the Houthis.
