The Council of the European Union on Wednesday authorised the signature and provisional application of the Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement and the Investment Protection Agreement between the EU and Indonesia, the Council said in a statement. Once fully implemented, up to 98.5 per cent of tariff lines will be liberalised and 96 per cent of Indonesian tariffs will disappear within five years, saving an estimated 600 million euros a year in customs duties, with Indonesian duties of up to 50 per cent on EU cars eliminated within five years. The agreements will now be signed and sent to the European Parliament for its consent ahead of formal conclusion, and both sides aim to bring them into application quickly because Indonesia graduates from the EU's generalised scheme of preferences on January 1, 2027, when its exports lose preferential tariffs under that scheme.
