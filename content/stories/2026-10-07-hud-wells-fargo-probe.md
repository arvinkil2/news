The Department of Housing and Urban Development's Office of Special Investigations opened an inquiry into Wells Fargo's mortgage programs aimed at boosting Black homeownership, sending CEO Charlie Scharf a letter Wednesday examining whether the bank "has violated or intends to violate" the Fair Housing Act's ban on race-based discrimination in lending. HUD's assistant secretary Craig Trainor said the bank's 2017 and 2022 commitments — including a $210 million push for racial equity in homeownership and a Special Purpose Credit Program tied to refinancing — raise questions about race-based lending decisions, and directed the bank to preserve all records including personal-device messages. Housing Secretary Scott Turner called the practice "immoral, unethical and un-American"; Wells Fargo shares slid about 2 percent to $80 in afternoon trading after the Wall Street Journal first reported the probe. The bank declined to comment.
