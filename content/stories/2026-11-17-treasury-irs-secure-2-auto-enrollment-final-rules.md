The US Department of the Treasury and the Internal Revenue Service issued final regulations on Tuesday implementing the SECURE 2.0 Act provision that expands automatic enrollment in workplace retirement plans. Covered 401(k) and 403(b) plans generally must automatically enroll eligible employees at a contribution rate of 3 percent, rising by at least one percentage point a year until it reaches at least 10 percent, subject to a 15 percent maximum, and employees may opt out or choose a different rate.

The regulations clarify the statutory exceptions for qualifying small and new businesses, governmental plans, church plans and SIMPLE 401(k) plans, give employers flexibility on the timing and amount of automatic increases, simplify participant notices, and explain how the requirements apply after plan mergers. They generally apply to retirement plan years beginning after December 31, 2027. Treasury estimates the provision will increase retirement savings by an average of $4.2 billion annually over the 2023 to 2032 period.
