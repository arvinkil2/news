Banque de France governor François Villeroy de Galhau warned that France risks being "strangled by interest rates," the Financial Times reported on October 5. In a separate interview with Le Figaro reported the same week, Villeroy said that "beyond a 5% deficit, France would clearly put itself in danger," adding that the apparent calm of markets can, in his experience, turn abruptly.

France's sovereign-risk premium tightened its grip on Monday. One outlet reported the 10-year OAT-Bund spread touched 152 basis points intraday, after closing Friday at 146.9bp, up 16.6bp on the session. A French data site showed the 10-year OAT at 4.90% against a Bund at 3.43% as of Friday's close. The Friday level was already the widest since January 2012 and the OAT yield the highest since 2002. One tracker now shows France wider than Italy and Greece. The €43 billion 2027 budget has not restored confidence, and Tuesday's demonstrations follow a second weekend of student unrest, adding the street premium that has kept buyers away. The presidential election in April 2027 sits over every fiscal arithmetic.

The warning rests on the arithmetic Villeroy laid out before the National Assembly's finance committee in March 2025: France's interest payments on its public debt were already higher than the defence budget and on course to exceed the education budget, with public debt at 110% of GDP and the deficit at 5.4%, against a target of bringing it to 3% by 2029.

What to watch is the parliamentary budget process. The 10-year OAT-Bund spread sits at its widest since the 2012 eurozone debt crisis, and investors are judging how much of the government's consolidation arithmetic can survive the legislature.
