Under the framework, apps connected to a medical device, such as one paired with an asthma inhaler, generally should be described in the drug's official prescribing information, while standalone apps such as symptom trackers and dose reminders belong on the label only if the company can show the app is essential for safe and effective use or delivers a meaningful clinical benefit. In most cases, what a companion app shows its users will be treated as promotional material, the same way the agency handles other drug advertising. FDA officials said the guidance is meant to give developers a consistent, risk-proportionate pathway as companion software moves beyond simple reminders toward tools that adapt to each patient.
