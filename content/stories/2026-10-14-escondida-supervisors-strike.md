BHP and the supervisors and staff union at Escondida have reached a deal that averts the strike called for 8:00 a.m. on Wednesday, ending the stoppage threat at the world's largest copper mine within hours of its scheduled start. The breakthrough came after BHP withdrew its demand that workers share private biometric data with third parties, the clause the union had identified as the deal breaker in the failed final mediation round.

The settlement closes a dispute that had carried unusual weight for a supervisors' stoppage. The unions involved represent about a quarter of Escondida's direct employees, the mine had already absorbed a fatal accident shutdown in late September, and copper had rallied to within sight of its record near 14,900 dollars a ton partly on the risk that Escondida would stop. London copper hit 14,695 dollars a ton on Wednesday, its highest since September 23, before the resolution.

Attention now shifts to the pattern rather than the mine. Labour's hand across Chile's copper belt has been strengthened by prices, with Antofagasta's Centinela also returning to the table this week, and every settlement struck at these levels resets the cost base the industry carries into 2027.
