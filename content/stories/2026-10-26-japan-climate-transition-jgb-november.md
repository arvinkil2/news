Japan's Ministry of Finance announced on Monday that it will sell about 250 billion yen of five-year Climate Transition Japanese government bonds in November. The auction is set for November 2, 2026, with issue on November 4 and maturity on September 20, 2031. The sale will use a Dutch style yield competitive auction, with bids at intervals of 0.1 basis point, the ministry said. Dow Jones Newswires reported the same terms on Monday.

The Climate Transition Bond is Japan's labelled sovereign instrument for funding the green transformation programme, issued under the government's Climate Transition Bond Framework rather than as conventional JGB supply. The five-year tenor has been a regular part of that programme, at the same approximate size: the framework's fifth issuance paired a five-year tranche of about 250 billion yen with a ten-year tranche of the same size.

Timing gives a routine announcement a market read. The auction lands on the first business day after the Bank of Japan's October 29 to 30 meeting, at which the bank is expected to hold its policy rate at 1.25 percent and to judge underlying inflation at its 2 percent target, as covered in yesterday's edition. Five-year JGB yields have risen with the hiking cycle, and the October 14 conventional five-year auction drew a bid to cover ratio of 3.56 times, up from 3.42 times at the previous sale. Whether labelled transition paper clears at a discount to that conventional curve, and by how much, is the small, clean signal this sale will send about dedicated demand for transition assets at higher Japanese yields.
