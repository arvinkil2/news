President Trump said Friday he struck a deal with Vladimir Putin for Russia to supply more than 4.8 million tons of diesel to American and global markets, a sharp reversal of years of US pressure on Russian energy and an attempt to bring down fuel prices ahead of the November 3 midterm elections.

Trump said on social media that the phone call with Putin, in which he had been expected to discuss a suspected plague case in Russia, produced an agreement for Moscow to immediately supply over 300,000 tons of diesel, followed by 500,000 tons in November, 1 million tons immediately thereafter, and up to 3 million more tons within a short period depending on the condition of Russian refineries. Shortly after the announcement, the Treasury Department issued a temporary general license allowing Russian diesel loaded onto tankers as of Friday to move without sanctions until April 2027.

The Kremlin confirmed the arrangement, saying Putin and Trump devoted significant attention to prospects for resolving the Ukraine crisis and discussed Iran and bilateral relations, and that Russia confirmed its readiness to supply oil and oil products to American and global markets. Russian Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak said Moscow would start lifting diesel export restrictions ahead of schedule and could begin supplies to the US as early as October, with the domestic market fully supplied.

The deal is the largest easing of Russian fuel sanctions since the Ukraine war began. Russia had banned diesel exports since July as Ukrainian strikes on its refineries squeezed domestic supply, and the International Energy Agency says Russian diesel output has fallen about 30 percent. The US has not imported Russian oil or gas since the 2022 ban, and the announcement comes weeks after Trump signed a sweeping Russia sanctions law that directs tariffs of up to 100 percent on the top importers of Russian oil and gas.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy called it "a weak decision, unfortunately, a weak decision by strong partners," saying Russia would repay it with further terror and that it plays into Moscow's hands. Rep. Don Beyer called the announcement infuriating, arguing Trump was lifting sanctions on Russian fossil fuels. The White House did not say who would pay for the diesel or whether deliveries would arrive before Election Day.

Diesel futures dipped on the news Friday, but the national average diesel price was still $6.23 a gallon, according to AAA, down from a record $6.52 on September 22. One strategist told MarketWatch the move may be too little, too late to bring meaningful relief before the midterms, since Russia's own refinery damage constrains how much it can physically ship.
