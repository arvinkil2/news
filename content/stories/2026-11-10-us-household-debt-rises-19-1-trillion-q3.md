Total US household debt rose 334 billion dollars in the third quarter of 2026, a 1.8 percent increase from the second quarter, to 19.1 trillion dollars, the Federal Reserve Bank of New York reported in its Quarterly Report on Household Debt and Credit. Mortgage balances rose 257 billion dollars to 13.4 trillion dollars, credit card balances rose 22 billion dollars to 1.29 trillion dollars, and auto loan balances rose 21 billion dollars to 1.7 trillion dollars.

The troubled share edged up. Five percent of outstanding debt was in some stage of delinquency, up 0.3 percentage points from the second quarter, a level the bank said is consistent with pre-pandemic conditions, and transitions into early delinquency rose for mortgages and auto loans while transitions into serious delinquency improved for credit cards and student loans. The New York Fed's accompanying analysis traces much of the card stress to pandemic era lending, when a rising share of new cards went to borrowers in lower income neighbourhoods with lower credit scores, and expects new card delinquencies to keep improving if there is no macroeconomic shock.
