Brazil's presidential runoff is set for October 25, and Bloomberg's scene setter frames the choice voters face after a first round that also produced this week's Senate vote to end the six day workweek, a win for President Lula's camp that was covered here on October 14. Business circles have been repositioning since Flavio Bolsonaro's polling lead emerged, and the runoff now dominates Brazilian asset pricing into month end.

President Trump has rejected international election monitors for the US midterms, breaking a two decade tradition of inviting OSCE observers, The Hill reported. The decision lands weeks before the vote and has drawn criticism from Democrats who argue that the refusal itself undermines confidence in the count.

Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu said he is not seeking to attack Iran again before Israel's election, Arab News reported, a signal aimed at voters and at Washington that the current round of the war is, for now, bounded by the electoral calendar.

Panama's earthquake relief operation is scaling up rather than winding down: UN OCHA reporting put 11,460 people affected and 2,841 in emergency shelters as of October 13, with about 1,250 homes damaged and 27 people injured. Official counts report no deaths from the quake itself; the one confirmed death, from injuries sustained on October 9, was reported here on October 14.

Britain's decision to fund 17 million pounds of repairs to the Chornobyl shelter, damaged by a Russian drone strike, was published here on October 14 and is not repeated as a new story. The funding runs through an EBRD coordinated G7 programme and follows the February 2025 strike on the confinement structure.

The US Supreme Court of India docket is not the only judicial story in the region: India's Supreme Court agreed to hear an election challenge and Delhi protest cases, Bloomberg reported, while in South Africa a court judgment may force a reprint of election ballots, a logistical and legal problem weeks before voting.
