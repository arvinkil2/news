The US Department of State announced on Tuesday that it has completed the 90 day sprint to reform the International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR) launched on July 30 by Under Secretary Thomas G. DiNanno and concluded on October 28, during which the department advanced a dozen regulatory actions, with eight Federal Register notices already published. The reforms right-size the US Munitions List so that controls cover only technologies providing a critical military or intelligence advantage, and they create a new ITAR General License mechanism that will pre-approve transfers of certain defense articles to some of America's most trusted partner governments, alongside a general presumption of approval for certain applications and a target of determinations within 50 days, down from 60. The department said it adjudicated approximately 25,000 ITAR licenses valued at approximately $200 billion in fiscal year 2026, and that DiNanno will next lead work on the Foreign Military Sales process and US export policy for the Missile Technology Control Regime.
