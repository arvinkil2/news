The Justice Department's Antitrust Division has closed its investigation into NextEra Energy's acquisition of Dominion Energy, a $66.8 billion transaction the two companies announced on May 18. Both firms generate electricity and compete in the wholesale electricity and capacity auctions run by regional transmission organisations, and the deal combines overlapping generation portfolios in two of them: PJM Interconnection, covering parts of the Midwest and Mid-Atlantic, and ISO New England.

The division's concern centred on New England's capacity market. ISO New England's Internal Market Monitor told FERC, which is reviewing the transaction, that the merged firm might have the incentive and ability to deactivate a still viable plant in order to raise capacity payments across its combined portfolio, and proposed that for five years from the next capacity auction in May 2028 the monitor be able to investigate any proposed deactivation and block it if the plant retains economic value. After a detailed investigation including document review, industry interviews and economic modelling, the division shared the monitor's concern. The parties then told FERC in a filing that they will commit, without qualification, to comply with the monitor's proposal whether or not FERC imposes it as a condition.

In light of that commitment, the division closed its investigation. The department said the commitment will protect New England consumers from potentially hundreds of millions of dollars of increased electricity costs. FERC's approval process for the transaction continues.
