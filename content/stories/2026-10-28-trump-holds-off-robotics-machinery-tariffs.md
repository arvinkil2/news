President Donald Trump signed a proclamation on Wednesday under Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act of 1962 addressing imports of robotics, industrial machinery and their parts and components, the White House said, after the Commerce Department found those imports threaten to impair national security. No tariffs were imposed: the proclamation directs Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer to jointly negotiate agreements with trading partners, from any country, to address the imports, and Bloomberg reported they must report on their progress within 180 days.

The White House said the United States produces less robotics and industrial equipment than it consumes and relies on foreign imports for most types, a dependence it said threatens the industrial base, since nearly all modern defence systems and critical infrastructure sectors require such machinery. The proclamation leaves the tariff route open: the President may take further action to adjust the imports if the negotiations do not resolve the threat, and the Commerce Secretary will keep monitoring the imports and report any circumstances indicating a need for further action.
