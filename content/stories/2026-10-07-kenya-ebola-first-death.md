**Latest (Saturday, October 10):** The Africa CDC has reconstructed the patient's month-long route, and it explains how he crossed three countries undetected. The 40-year-old Kenyan businessman, who had lived in the DRC for seven years, was in Bas-Uele province (Buta, Aketi, Likati) in late August and early September. Symptoms emerged around September 15; he was admitted to Tongo Etandi Health Centre in Bondo from September 17 to 19 and misdiagnosed with a skin infection. He flew to Kisangani on September 20 and spent more than ten days seeking care there, then took a domestic flight to Beni on October 1, moved on to Kasindi in North Kivu, crossed into Uganda by road to Kampala, and flew Entebbe to Nairobi on October 3, where a relative drove him to Nairobi Hospital. He died on October 5.

No Ebola test was ever taken in the DRC despite fever and rash, and airport screenings at Entebbe and Nairobi's JKIA rely on temperature checks and self-declarations; his temperature was reportedly normal at Entebbe, possibly because of medication. Kenya has now identified 66 potential contacts under quarantine, a figure expected to rise as contact tracing continues in Uganda and the DRC. No additional cases or deaths have been reported beyond the one patient. Kenya's health ministry says it has screened more than 652,000 travellers and trained nearly 5,000 health workers; Africa CDC emergency director Dr Wessam Mankoula urged symptomatic travellers to report early, while WHO warns that Ebola stigma discourages reporting.

Kenya has recorded its first Ebola death, an imported case of the Bundibugyo strain that traveled from the Democratic Republic of the Congo, where a deadly outbreak continues spreading in the east. The victim, a man who had lived in the DRC for several years, flew to Nairobi via Kampala and Entebbe in Uganda on October 3, went straight to hospital, and died in the Kenyan capital on Monday.

Authorities have quarantined about 10 relatives, 21 health workers who treated him, and roughly 23 passengers and four crew from the Entebbe-Kampala flight, with the government working with the airline to reach them. No other infections have been found so far. WHO Representative Dr. Neema Rusibamayila Kimambo said the next few days are critical: "If we are able to ensure that we do proper contact listing, get all contacts, put them in quarantine, then that will be the key to really contain and stop any community spread."
