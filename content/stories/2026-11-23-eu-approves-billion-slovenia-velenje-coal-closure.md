The European Commission has approved, under EU state aid rules, a one billion euro Slovenian measure to cover exceptional costs arising from the closure of the Premogovnik Velenje coal mine, Slovenia's last operating coal mine. The beneficiary is the state-owned operator Premogovnik Velenje and its three subsidiaries, and the aid supports Slovenia's binding national commitment to phase out coal by 2033.

Coal extraction at Velenje will be gradually ended, completely by December 31, 2033 at the latest. Closure works in the parts of the mine where extraction has already ended run until 2033, and works for the permanent abandonment of the mining sites continue from January 2034 to June 2045. The measure covers social costs for workers who have lost or will lose their jobs because of the closure, and the costs of the safety and remediation works the closure requires, including underground safety works, repair of environmental damage from mining and re-cultivation of land.

The Commission assessed the measure under Article 4 of the Council Decision on state aid to facilitate the closure of uncompetitive coal mines and found that the aid does not exceed the exceptional costs arising from the orderly closure, and is therefore compatible with EU state aid rules. The non-confidential version of the decision will be published under case number SA.122461 in the Commission's state aid register.

The approval sits inside a larger Slovenian programme: parliament has adopted a gradual closure law envisaging about 1.13 billion euros from the state budget over 2026 to 2045, roughly 50 million euros a year, with the mine's own resources from coal sales and asset disposals covering costs first and the budget covering the shortfall. The mine feeds the Sostanj power station, and its closure programme includes rehabilitating more than 50 kilometres of mine routes and re-cultivating more than 1,100 hectares of land, with final liquidation of the operator planned for 2045.
