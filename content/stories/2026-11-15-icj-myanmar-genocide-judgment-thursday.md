The International Court of Justice will deliver its judgment at 10 a.m. on Thursday, November 19, at the Peace Palace in The Hague in the case concerning Application of the Convention on the Prevention and Punishment of the Crime of Genocide (The Gambia v. Myanmar: 11 States intervening), the court announced in a press release dated November 3, with President Judge Iwasawa Yuji to read the judgment at a public sitting.

The Gambia brought the case in 2019, accusing Myanmar of breaching the Genocide Convention in its treatment of the Rohingya after the army's 2017 clearance operations in Rakhine state drove hundreds of thousands of people across the border into Bangladesh; the Guardian reported that more than 1 million Rohingya remain in Bangladesh, in the world's largest refugee settlement, and fewer than 600,000 are estimated to remain in Myanmar. Myanmar has denied carrying out a genocide and says its actions targeted a military uprising by insurgents.

The court has never before found a state responsible for genocide, and its judgment is final with no possibility of appeal.
