UBS, Barclays, BNP Paribas and HSBC remain on the hook for €1.2 billion (about $1.3 billion) of committed acquisition financing after CVC Capital Partners moved to withdraw from its agreed acquisition of Irca, the Italian food ingredients maker, from Advent International. The banks had intended to sell the debt in the high yield bond market; the commitments are expected to stay on their books until the dispute between the two firms is resolved, since intervening to change the financing terms could expose the lenders to claims of interference with the contract.

CVC said forensic analysis during due diligence led it to conclude the near €3 billion transaction was no longer viable, and it notified Advent several weeks ago. Advent disputes CVC's right to withdraw, saying the parties signed a binding agreement after extensive due diligence. The acquisition agreement has no financing condition, which is why the bank commitments survive the buyer's attempted exit.

The episode is a reminder that hung debt is not only a rising-rate phenomenon: a diligence dispute between two of the largest private equity firms can strand underwritten financing just as completely as a market sell-off, and the banks' exit depends on litigation or a negotiated settlement between the sponsors, not on anything the lenders control.
