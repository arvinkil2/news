A proposed Canadian tax break could leave a new gold mine with about 30 percent more free cash flow in its first years of production, according to a BMO Capital Markets model published late Tuesday. Larger miners with existing Canadian operations would see a more modest gain of about 3 percent in first year operating cash flow, tapering after that, with Alamos Gold and Wesdome Gold Mines among the producers BMO expects to benefit most.

Ottawa's proposed Productivity Mega Deduction would let miners deduct the full cost of qualifying Canadian development expenses incurred on or after September 15 against taxable income in the year the expense was incurred, and equipment acquired from that date could be fully written off once available for use. Many capital deductions are currently spread over several years. The proposal makes immediate expensing permanent: it accelerates deductions rather than reimbursing spending.

BMO estimates the change would add about 7 percent to the net asset value of its model gold project, against about 2 percent for larger producers with Canadian operations, and would lift the hypothetical developer's internal rate of return to 21 percent from 20 percent. The estimates assume provinces follow Ottawa's lead. The Mining Association of Canada said on September 15 that getting deductions sooner could help marginal projects and brownfield expansions meet investment thresholds.

The bank also flagged a consolidation angle: fully taxable producers could use deductions from project spending to offset income from existing Canadian operations while adding future production, which could encourage acquisitions. Federal legislation is still pending; Ottawa reaffirmed its intention in the budget finance minister Francois-Philippe Champagne introduced on November 5.
