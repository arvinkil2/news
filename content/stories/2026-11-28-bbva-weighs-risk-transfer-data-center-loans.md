BBVA is considering a significant risk transfer transaction that would offload credit risk on part of a project finance portfolio of about 2 billion euros, according to Bloomberg reporting carried by MT Newswires on November 26. Data center loans could account for about a quarter of the portfolio, roughly 500 million euros, or $547 million.

The Spanish lender is sounding out investors on the structure, and no transaction is expected to be agreed before the start of 2027. BBVA did not immediately respond to a request for comment, and the report rests on people familiar with the matter, so the figures are reported rather than confirmed.

Significant risk transfers let a bank keep the loans and the client relationships while paying investors to absorb a first-loss or mezzanine slice of the credit risk, freeing regulatory capital. They are standard for consumer and corporate portfolios; applying one to a portfolio with a large data center component is newer.

The read is about concentration. European banks financed data centers aggressively through the AI buildout, and a risk transfer at this size suggests at least one of them is now managing the exposure as a portfolio problem, hedging the risk it originated rather than originating more of it.
