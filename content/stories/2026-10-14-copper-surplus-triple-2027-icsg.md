The International Copper Study Group, meeting in Lisbon on October 5 and 6, now expects a global copper surplus of 139,000 tonnes in 2026, rising to 514,000 tonnes in 2027 as refined production grows 1.6 percent after a 1.5 percent fall this year and mine production edges up 0.2 percent after falling 1.6 percent. Refined usage is still forecast to grow 2 percent in 2027 after a slowdown this year that the group linked in part to solar manufacturing, but an expected decline in Chinese refined usage next year is the main reason the balance swings so far into surplus.
