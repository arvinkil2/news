**Correction:** An earlier version of this item carried incorrect ICSG figures. The correct forecast is a refined copper surplus of 104,000 tonnes in 2026, rising to 368,000 tonnes in 2027.

The International Copper Study Group expects refined copper production to rise 0.4 percent in 2026 and 3.4 percent in 2027, driven by smelter and refinery ramp-ups in Indonesia, India and the European Union, improved operating rates in Chile and increased output from scrap, after concentrate shortages constrained smelter output this year. Mine production is forecast to grow 1.3 percent in 2026 and 3.1 percent in 2027, helped by recovery at Indonesia's Grasberg and the DRC's Kamoa-Kakula mines. Refined usage is expected to grow more slowly, 1.5 percent in 2026 and 2.5 percent in 2027, with Asia the main source of growth and China accounting for 59 percent of global refined consumption.
