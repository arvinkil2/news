## Latest

Applied Digital widened its preferred equity funding line on November 24, signing a letter agreement with the investors under its April 30, 2025 Preferred Equity Purchase Agreement that lifts the aggregate commitment for its Series G convertible preferred stock from $2.0 billion to $2.5 billion, according to a Form 8-K filed the same day. After the increase, $1.035 billion of availability remains to be drawn under the agreement. The same filing furnished the company's two November 23 press releases, on the lease below and on the closing of a bridge loan facility by an indirect subsidiary.

Applied Digital has entered an 18-year lease agreement with a tier-one investment-grade hyperscaler for 150 megawatts of critical IT capacity, the Dallas-based AI data center developer announced on November 23. The lease provides approximately $5.4 billion in base-term contracted revenue, or approximately $12.7 billion if all renewal options are exercised over a total term of 33 years. The tenant was not named.

The agreement takes Applied Digital's total contracted base-term lease revenue above $41 billion, and to roughly $100 billion if all renewal options across its portfolio are exercised. It follows a 15-year, 210 MW lease worth about $5.2 billion that the company signed in June at its Delta Forge 2 site.

Delivery of the new capacity is expected to begin in the first half of 2028, subject to development milestones, utility arrangements and regulatory approvals. The company said it expects to complete the remaining project finance for Delta Forge 1 and Polaris Forge 3 in the first calendar quarter of 2027, followed by Delta Forge 2 in the second quarter, and it also agreed a $300 million senior secured bridge facility led by a global financial institution to keep Delta Forge 1 construction moving.

The structure is the current template for AI infrastructure: a developer with no investment-grade balance sheet of its own converts an investment-grade tenant's long lease into financeable collateral, and the risk sits in the two years of construction and project finance between signing and delivery. Applied Digital's shares rose sharply on the announcement, but the contracted revenue only becomes cash if the campuses are built, powered and handed over on schedule.
