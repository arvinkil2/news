Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 5.06 percent to 56,437, its lowest close since April 13, as the AI selloff deepened for a second session across Asia, with South Korea's Kospi down 5.21 percent and Taiwan's Taiex down 6.63 percent (see today's Asia stocks plunge story).

Silver miners fell 4.20 percent and gold miners 3.77 percent in the mining table, extending weekly losses to 14.43 percent and 12.02 percent. Copper miners fell 3.12 percent, down 16.52 percent on the week, as copper prices plunged about 6 percent this week to $12,471 a tonne on the London Metal Exchange, their lowest since April, on unwinding AI-demand and tariff bets (see today's Ivanhoe Electric story).

US technology names fell with the same trade on Thursday's close. Amazon fell 3.87 percent, Alphabet 3.46 percent, Nvidia 3.30 percent (down 12.16 percent on the week) and Meta 3.27 percent, while the S&P 500 fell 2.02 percent and the Nasdaq 100 fell 2.21 percent.

The pattern is a broadening risk-off move: what began as an Asian AI valuation selloff is now hitting miners, precious metals and US mega-cap technology together, while the long end of the Treasury curve stayed comparatively calm, with the 30-year yield easing 1.1 basis points to 5.40 percent. Nothing in this morning's reporting points to a single new catalyst beyond the selloff itself; the question for the day session is whether the selling stays concentrated in AI-linked names or spreads further into credit.
