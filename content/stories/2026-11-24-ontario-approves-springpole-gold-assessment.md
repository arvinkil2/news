First Mining Gold has received Ontario's environmental assessment approval for the Springpole project in northwestern Ontario, as it looks to finance a proposed $1.1 billion (C$1.6 billion) open pit operation about 400 km northeast of Thunder Bay. Environment Minister Todd McCarthy approved the assessment, the company announced on Monday. Ottawa approved the project on June 30, and further project permits and authorizations are still required.

National Bank Financial analyst Alex Terentiew called the approval a major de-risking milestone that materially improves visibility toward feasibility, financing and construction, and said that with formalized agreements in place with local First Nations communities, Springpole has secured the principal environmental and social approvals required to advance toward a development decision. Haywood Capital Markets analyst Marcus Giannini said the milestone places Springpole in rare company as one of only a handful of undeveloped gold projects of scale in Canada to have secured both federal and provincial environmental assessment approval.

First Mining shares jumped 7.5 percent to 57 Canadian cents on Monday morning in Toronto, valuing the company at about C$850 million. The company held C$81 million in cash and marketable securities at September 30 after raising gross proceeds of C$57.5 million in a September share sale. It is targeting completion of a feasibility study and project financing in 2027, ahead of a construction decision in 2028.

Part of the deposit lies beneath Springpole Lake, and First Mining plans to build two dykes and drain part of the lake to reach the ore. Under federal conditions it must establish at least one Indigenous environmental committee before construction. Cat Lake and Lac Seul First Nations conducted their own impact assessment before reaching an August benefits agreement with the company, and First Mining signed a separate agreement with Slate Falls Nation that month.

Springpole could produce 330,000 payable ounces of gold a year in its first five years and average 281,000 ounces over a 9.4 year mine life, the company estimated in a 2025 prefeasibility study. At a gold price of $3,100 per ounce, the project had an after-tax net present value of $2.1 billion at a 5 percent discount rate and a 41 percent internal rate of return, with initial capital costs estimated at $1.1 billion.
