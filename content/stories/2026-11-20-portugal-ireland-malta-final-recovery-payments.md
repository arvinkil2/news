Portugal has satisfactorily completed all 29 milestones and 67 targets behind its tenth and final payment request for 4.7 billion euros under the Recovery and Resilience Facility, the centrepiece of NextGenerationEU, covering social protection, affordable housing, healthcare, renewable energy and clean transport. Its flagship measures include a Single Social Benefit framework, 20,801 affordable housing solutions under the 1.º Direito programme, a nationwide zero-emission bus fleet of 860 vehicles and more than 2,200 new continued care, mental health and palliative care beds. The request, submitted on September 29, completes Portugal's 21.9 billion euro plan, financed by 16.3 billion euros in grants and 5.6 billion euros in loans, and would bring funds paid to 21.9 billion euros, 100 percent of the plan, with all 379 milestones and targets positively assessed.

Ireland satisfactorily completed all 14 milestones and 5 targets behind its fifth and final request for 225 million euros, covering public building energy efficiency, electrification of the Cork commuter rail area, research funding through the National Challenge Fund and a new anti-money laundering sanction regime. The Commission also found, however, that three milestones tied to annual carbon tax increases, one from the first payment request and two from the third, have been partly reversed, and it will start the procedure to reduce the fifth and final payment as a result. Ireland, which submitted the request on September 29, has two months to present its observations, and the Commission will take account of measures Ireland takes to remedy the reversal before it adopts the decision authorising disbursement.

Malta satisfactorily completed 26 of the 27 milestones and targets behind its fifth and final request for 61.7 million euros, which includes a new Blood, Tissue and Cell Centre, REPowerEU investment in the electricity distribution network and digital grants for small and medium-sized companies. The one milestone not fulfilled concerns a reform to strengthen the independence of specialised tribunals; if the Commission confirms its assessment after Malta's observations, which are due within two months, the amount corresponding to that milestone will be proportionally reduced from the 61.7 million euros. Malta submitted its final request on September 30. For all three countries, the Commission has sent its preliminary assessment to the Economic and Financial Committee, which has four weeks to give its opinion, and payment for the greenlit milestones can follow a positive opinion and a Commission payment decision, ahead of the facility's closure at the end of 2026.
