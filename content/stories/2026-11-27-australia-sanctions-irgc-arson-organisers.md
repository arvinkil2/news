Australia has imposed counter-terrorism financing sanctions on Zohair Hassib and Kazem Hamad, who Foreign Minister Penny Wong said used a complex chain of proxies to orchestrate the 2024 arson attacks on the Lewis Continental Kitchen in Sydney and the Adass Israel Synagogue in Melbourne on behalf of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. The two men, whose activities were referred to in ASIO's annual threat assessment in June, were named publicly for the first time on Friday, and it is now a criminal offence to use or deal with their assets or make assets available to them, with breaches carrying up to 10 years in prison.

The sanctions build on steps Australia has already taken over the attacks, including listing the IRGC as a state sponsor of terrorism, expelling the Iranian ambassador and three other diplomats, and suspending operations at the Australian embassy in Tehran. Australia now lists 102 persons and entities under counter-terrorism financing sanctions, alongside 30 terrorist organisations and one state sponsor of terrorism.
