## Latest

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on Thursday that two tankers exploded and caught fire after hitting naval mines north of Oman's Khasab port while attempting to cross the Strait of Hormuz on what the Guards' navy described as an unauthorized route, in a statement published by the semi-official Tasnim news agency and reported by Reuters. The statement named no vessels and gave no casualty figures, and no confirmation from UK Maritime Trade Operations, Oman or any shipping company had appeared by Thursday morning. It is the third consecutive day of such claims from Tehran, each resting solely on Iranian official statements.

Abu Dhabi has imposed restrictions on tanker movements near its main oil and gas export facilities. A notice from ADNOC's Petroleum Ports Authority prohibits tankers carrying crude oil, petroleum products, liquefied gases, chemicals and other hazardous cargoes from entering or waiting in waters under its jurisdiction unless they are conducting authorised business with its terminals, Splash247 reported. The rules apply regardless of flag, cover the Ruwais, Jebel Dhanna, Das, Zirku and Mubarraz terminals, require documentation at least 72 hours before arrival, and are initially valid for 90 days. Fujairah, the export and bunkering hub outside the strait, is under a separate port authority and is not covered.

UK Maritime Trade Operations issued Warning 222-26 on Wednesday, reporting that the master of a tanker had reported an explosion caused by an unknown projectile in close proximity to the tanker, 50 nautical miles west of Dubai, at 2130 UTC. The crew are safe and accounted for, no damage to the tanker was reported and there was no environmental impact, and UKMTO advised vessels to transit with caution while authorities investigate. The warning does not name the vessel or any attacker, and the location, in the southern Arabian Gulf, is separate from the Strait of Hormuz incidents claimed by Iran earlier in the day.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on Wednesday evening that a ship carrying liquefied natural gas struck a naval mine and caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz, in a claim carried by Iranian state media and reported by Reuters, which said it could not independently verify the report. Earlier on Wednesday, the semi-official Fars news agency, citing an Iranian Army statement, reported that an oil tanker had hit a mine and caught fire in a restricted zone in the strait, in addition to the two oil tankers the Guards Navy said struck mines on an unauthorized route. No vessel was named in any of the claims, and no casualty figures were given.

Iran's Army spokesperson Mohammad Akraminia said on Wednesday that the military had deployed new systems and upgraded existing ones to strengthen surveillance and control of the Strait of Hormuz, and warned that vessels passing through without coordinating with Iranian forces had faced greater risks and struck mines in recent weeks, Reuters reported. Tehran has told Washington it will not restore normal shipping through the strait until its demands are met, including the lifting of a US naval blockade of Iranian ports and an end to the war on all fronts; under a proposal conveyed through Qatari mediators, Tehran offered to reopen the waterway within 48 hours of Washington meeting its conditions.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy said two oil tankers exploded and caught fire after striking naval mines early on Wednesday while transiting what the force described as an unauthorized route through the Strait of Hormuz, in a statement carried by Iranian state media and reported by Reuters. The statement named no vessels and gave no casualty figures.

No independent confirmation of the Iranian claims, and no comment from the US Navy or shipping firms, was available at the time of writing. The claim follows a series of similar Guards statements about mine strikes on Hormuz shipping in recent weeks, each likewise sourced to Tehran.

Markets are pricing the route risk even while they discount the crude claims. Tanker freight rose 7.68 percent to 818.41 dollars at Tuesday's close in the estate market tables, up 26.33 percent on the week, while Brent crude fell 3.89 percent on surplus expectations ahead of the OPEC+ meeting on November 29.
