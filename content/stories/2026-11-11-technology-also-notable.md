An affiliate of Dallas-based Integra Mission Critical has registered a single data center building in Quanah, Hardeman County, Texas, at an estimated cost of 8 billion dollars, according to an October 27 filing with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation reported by Datacenter Dynamics and Baxtel. The registration, for a project named Clocktower NWB 05 and a facility called Building 5 Network at 4628 Four Corners Road, covers a single-story, 288,355 square foot data center with an office component, with construction scheduled from November 1, 2026 to December 31, 2028, and no tenant assigned. The registered owner, 19000-200 Holdings LLC, shares Integra's Dallas headquarters address. Integra's own project site describes the wider Quanah campus as 756 megawatts with 6.7 billion dollars of total capital investment, less than the cost the state filing lists for the one building; neither source explains the difference, and no power supplier or grid connection date has been named.

CISA added six vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities Catalog on November 10, based on evidence of active exploitation: flaws in Cisco BroadWorks, two in Barracuda Networks products, Openwave Messaging's Webtop Media Transcoder, Microsoft Office Outlook and Google Chromium. Under Binding Operational Directive 26-04, federal civilian agencies must prioritize rapid remediation of high-risk catalog entries on publicly exposed assets. The additions are routine in form but binding in effect for federal agencies and their contractors.

At its MAX event in Miami, Adobe added AI agents across Photoshop, Premiere, Illustrator and InDesign, expanded its Firefly creative tool, and released a local Model Context Protocol server that lets other chatbots, including ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, use Creative Cloud features, TechCrunch reported. Firefly gains a private-beta prototyping tool called Builder that moves into Figma's territory, an always-on agent, and a video assembly tool; Photoshop gains a relighting feature and Premiere a new color grading system and object removal for video. Adobe vice president Deepa Subramaniam said the company is still deciding which capabilities to expose through the protocol and will follow user feedback.

Anthropic launched Claude Meetings in beta on Team and Enterprise plans, initially with Google Meet, alongside Claude Whiteboards and Claude Sheets, TechCrunch reported. Claude joins a video call as a visible, identified participant, drafts the plan under discussion in real time, answers questions in the meeting chat, and leaves the notes and action items in claude.ai after the call; it will not join calls with external guests unless an administrator authorizes it and leaves automatically if one joins. Whiteboards turn a team brainstorm into an organized visual workspace on paid plans, Sheets builds collaboratively editable spreadsheets, and Claude Data, formerly Claude Dashboards, is now generally available. The launch follows OpenAI's workplace tools by a few weeks and extends the labs' contest with Microsoft and Google for the office suite.

Atomarine, a Cambridge, Massachusetts startup founded by MIT students Dimitris Koutentakis and Emile Germonpré, has raised 19 million dollars in a seed round led by Y Combinator, with Pantera Capital, Soma Capital, Seven Seven Six, Liquid 2 Ventures, Audacious Ventures and E14 Fund also taking part, Datacenter Dynamics reported (single-sourced). The company plans to mount small modular reactors on ships to power floating modular data center platforms of up to 150 megawatts, starting with natural gas generators because no SMR vendor has a commercial model or US regulatory approval; the founders target a first deployment in 2031 or 2032.
