Indonesia's bond selloff extended on Tuesday. The 10-year government bond yield climbed to a four-year high and the rupiah weakened toward a record low, Bloomberg reported, as elevated oil prices compounded the damage from MSCI's warning that Indonesia could be reclassified from emerging-market to standalone status over opaque share ownership and trading practices.

The move is the latest leg of a repricing that began with the MSCI warning. On Monday, October 19, the 10-year yield jumped to 7.4 percent from 7.169 percent the previous Friday, Reuters reported, while Jakarta stocks fell 3.6 percent after an 11.6 percent plunge, the worst two-day decline on record. By October 20 the yield was around 7.44 percent, up about 137 basis points since the end of 2025. Foreign investors have sold a net 4.98 billion dollars of Indonesian stocks this year, and Goldman Sachs has estimated a full MSCI removal could trigger a further 5.6 billion dollars of passive selling.

The pressure now sits with Bank Indonesia. The bank has already raised rates by a cumulative 100 basis points in May and June 2026, and economists are split on the next step: PermataBank's team describes market expectations shifting toward tightening, with a 25 basis point rise to 6.00 percent a proportionate response, against a consensus that still includes a hold at 5.75 percent. S&P Global Ratings analyst Rain Lin has said the MSCI warning could trigger more outflows but was unlikely to raise Indonesia's financing costs significantly on its own. Tuesday's four-year high in the 10-year yield is the market pricing the risk that he is wrong.
