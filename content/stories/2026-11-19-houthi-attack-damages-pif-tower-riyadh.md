## Latest

A tower in Riyadh's financial district was targeted in a terrorist attack on Tuesday, coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki Al-Malki said, adding that competent authorities were responding at the site. The statement did not name the tower, and Arab News reported no damage or casualty details and no claim of responsibility. The district was last hit on November 19, when a Houthi attack damaged the Public Investment Fund tower, and the capital has since seen repeated interceptions, most recently a Houthi drone destroyed over Riyadh on November 22.

The attacks on the capital entered a third night. Saudi air defences intercepted and destroyed three ballistic missiles launched by the Houthis toward Riyadh early on Saturday, coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki al-Maliki announced shortly after 2 a.m. local time, following a Civil Defense alert at 1:53 a.m. warning residents of an aerial threat. Two explosions were heard in the capital before Civil Defense lifted the alert, and no damage or casualties were reported from the interceptions themselves. The evening had already brought two ballistic missiles toward the Jazan region, which the coalition said were intercepted, with debris falling near Al-Shuqaiq, and across Friday the coalition counted five drones and three ballistic missiles intercepted over the kingdom. The coalition said it was also targeting a main Houthi supply depot in Sanaa and continuing operations against Houthi missile capabilities in Sanaa, Saada and Taiz and at Hodeidah port.

The fight widened to the Red Sea ports on Friday. The Saudi-led coalition urged civilians to evacuate areas around Hodeidah port and then said it had begun a targeted operation there, with spokesman Turki al-Malki saying the strikes hit operational capabilities at the port used, in the coalition's account, for smuggling and moving weapons and ballistic missiles, and that operations were continuing in Sanaa, Saada and Taiz. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree answered that Jeddah port and all Saudi ports on the Red Sea coast would become targets if the coalition struck Hodeidah, urged shipping companies to evacuate their ships from those ports immediately, warned airlines to stay out of Saudi airspace in the coming hours, and said the group would also target the power station at Shuqaiq in southern Saudi Arabia. Al Jazeera's correspondent in Sanaa described Friday's strikes as the heaviest witnessed since the conflict began, with both sides promising to hit energy infrastructure and airports. On the ground, the Houthis continue to advance on Aden, the government's de facto capital, and Taiz remains besieged and cut off from Aden.

Saudi Arabia said a Houthi attack hit a tower in the financial district of Riyadh on Thursday, causing material damage but no injuries. Images distributed by state media identified the building as the Public Investment Fund tower, the headquarters of the kingdom's sovereign wealth fund and the tallest skyscraper in the capital. The Civil Defence agency said the attack damaged the tower and nearby buildings, with damage reported after debris fell following the strikes.

The attacks continued into a second night. The Saudi-led coalition said its air defences intercepted and destroyed four ballistic missiles launched by the Houthis towards the southern region bordering Yemen, destroyed a drone headed for the same region early on Friday, and destroyed a ballistic missile launchpad used in the attempted attacks on Riyadh. Saudi Civil Defense has issued 15 public emergency alerts over the past two days, including three in the early hours of Friday, two of them for Riyadh. Saudi Arabia's Foreign Ministry condemned the attacks on civilian sites and said the kingdom reserves the right to take all necessary measures to defend its sovereignty. Pakistan condemned the tower attack as a grave violation of Saudi sovereignty.

The Houthis said on Wednesday they had targeted several sites in Saudi Arabia, including Riyadh's international airport and financial centre, a site in Jizan and the Khamis Mushait airbase in the south. The exchange ran both ways: a Saudi aircraft carried out air raids on Sanaa on Thursday, the Houthi-run Saba news agency reported, and the Houthi health ministry said attacks on the Yemeni capital killed 10 people over the past 48 hours. The World Health Organization put the death toll from the escalation that began in August at 2,260, with 10,519 wounded as of November 16, and UNICEF said more than 330,000 people have been displaced since the renewed fighting began, which has shattered the 2022 UN-brokered truce.

Oil prices edged higher on Friday as the attacks revived supply concerns. Brent crude futures rose 24 cents, or 0.3 percent, to $92.22 a barrel by 0118 GMT, and US West Texas Intermediate futures for December climbed 19 cents to $83.40 a barrel, though both benchmarks remained on course for a third straight weekly decline as crude shipments through the Strait of Hormuz returned to roughly pre-war levels. Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy said on Thursday that two oil tankers hit naval mines in the strait and were burning, a claim carried by Iranian state media and not independently confirmed. OPEC+ ministers meet on November 29 to decide their 2027 output policy.
