Latest, November 9: The Philippines' Department of Migrant Workers confirmed that the seafarer killed in the November 5 explosion was Filipino and that the other 21 Filipino crew members on board were unharmed, Marine Insight reported, citing the department and the manager's statement. The department said initial reports suggested a possible drone attack, while the manager cited a likely unidentified projectile strike on the port quarter; the cause remains unverified, the seafarer's name has been withheld, and the vessel is at Bahrain's Khalifa Bin Salman Port for investigation.

Earlier: The ship's manager, Greek firm Velos Tankers, said on Friday that the missing seafarer was found fatally injured on board, Reuters reported. There were no other injuries among the crew, who extinguished the fire caused by the explosion, and no pollution was reported. The unladen tanker has been taken to Bahrain's Khalifa Bin Salman Port, the extent of the hull damage is still being assessed, and an investigation will be launched, the manager said. The Reuters report names no party as responsible for the attack.

The Marshall Islands-flagged Velos Sapphire, a 74,998 dwt product tanker managed by Athens-based Velos Tankers, was struck about 50 nautical miles north of Madinat ash Shamal, Qatar. UK Maritime Trade Operations initially reported two unidentified projectiles hitting the tanker at 23:20 UTC, and the operator reported an explosion on the aft port side believed to have been caused by a missile. The International Maritime Organization identified the vessel and recorded damage, no pollution and one seafarer missing; a fire on board was extinguished, and the tanker, which had left Fujairah on November 3 for Sitra, Bahrain, continued towards Bahrain under its own power.

The Joint Maritime Information Center recorded four attacks in the strait in its latest 96-hour period, concentrated off Oman's Musandam peninsula between November 1 and 3, and two further attacks inside the Arabian Gulf, and still classifies the threat around Hormuz as severe. AIS monitoring counted nine commercial crossings on Thursday, a seven-day average of about nine a day against roughly 85 before the conflict.
