**Poland.** Bloomberg framed the Constitutional Tribunal standoff as the opening ground of the campaign for next November's parliamentary elections, and allies of Prime Minister Donald Tusk, including Deputy Prime Minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz, called over the weekend for a constitutional restart of the court after Friday's suspension of its president. The underlying events were covered and updated in place in this site's Poland coverage on October 31 and November 1; Monday adds positioning, not a new institutional step.

**New Zealand.** Bloomberg reported that New Zealand assets face headwinds as the election race tightens, with local markets pricing a closer contest than earlier in the cycle. The item is a market read on polling rather than a new result; the election authority's certified timetable remains the document to watch.

**South Carolina.** Al Jazeera reported a write-in campaign, dubbed Write-In Ralph, by South Carolina Republicans challenging President Trump's endorsed Senate pick in Tuesday's election. Write-in efforts rarely change outcomes, but the campaign is a measurable sign of intra-party dissent in a safe Republican state on the eve of the vote.

**Nigeria's Senate.** Premium Times' weekly roundup led on President Tinubu's fresh loan request before the Senate, alongside a bill on transferring convicted Nigerians abroad and Abuja tenancy rules. The loan request is the economically material item; its size and terms will be judged when the Senate takes it up, and it is flagged here rather than written up ahead of the document.

**Kenya.** Former deputy president Rigathi Gachagua threatened to leave Kenya for the United States if the opposition loses the 2027 election, in remarks reported by allAfrica on Monday. The statement is campaign rhetoric a year out, noted for the record.

**Singapore.** The Straits Times reported that Parliament will discuss changes to the haze law, with members asking about the impact on outdoor workers and vulnerable groups. Transboundary haze legislation is a recurring regional file; the bill's text, when tabled, is the operative document.

**European Parliament.** Members will meet representatives of the 2026 Sakharov Prize laureates in The Hague, the Parliament's press service announced. The meeting is ceremonial, but the prize's laureate selection is a standing signal of the Parliament's human rights priorities.
