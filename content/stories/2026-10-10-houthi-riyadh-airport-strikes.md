Germany's foreign minister condemned the attack and called it terrorism, per TASS (Russian state-affiliated; not independently corroborated by a Western outlet). Indonesian outlet Antara carried Pakistan's and Bangladesh's condemnations of the Houthi strike, and Nigeria's Vanguard repeated the Saudi toll of 12 killed and 309 injured. The Guardian opened a live blog tracking the crisis as the Houthis' warning to passengers kept Gulf aviation schedules throttled.

**Latest (Sunday, October 11):** The Houthis issued a new warning telling airlines not to use Saudi airports, Greek state broadcaster ERT reported, hardening the aviation threat after Saturday's deadly missile strike on King Khalid International Airport. The warning, also carried by TASS, lands on carriers already battered by the week: EASA's expanded airspace guidance over misidentification risk and FlightAware's count of 291 Riyadh cancellations in 24 hours have already throttled Gulf schedules.

**Latest (Sunday morning, October 11):** The United Nations joined the condemnation of Saturday's attack on King Khalid International Airport. Secretary-General Antonio Guterres said he was "deeply shocked" and strongly condemned what he called the Houthis' lethal attack, adding that attacks on civilians and civilian infrastructure violate international law and "must cease immediately." The UN's special envoy for Yemen, Hans Grundberg, called the strikes on civilian airports "a grave escalation" that threatens regional security and complicates peace efforts. Condemnations also came from Qatar (a "serious violation" of Saudi sovereignty), Egypt, Jordan (King Abdullah offered condolences and full solidarity), Oman (denouncing the attack and urging restraint), the Palestinian foreign ministry, Japan's embassy in Riyadh, and Canada's prime minister Mark Carney, who called the strikes "abhorrent." These reactions are carried by Al Jazeera's roundup, read in full, of official statements posted on X.

**Latest (Sunday, October 11):** The Saudi-led coalition vowed a "decisive response" to the Houthis on Sunday, carried as a France24 live blog headline and repeated across wire aggregations of AFP reporting; the coalition's spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki al-Maliki again described the strike as a "cowardly terrorist attack" and a "full-fledged war crime." Pakistan's prime minister Shehbaz Sharif extended full support to Saudi Arabia, invoking the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement and the kingdom's sovereignty; India's Ministry of External Affairs also condemned the attack, citing the 2.5 million Indian nationals in the kingdom. AFP reported health officials activated a rare mass call-up of doctors for the "serious emergency situation," and two doctors said several pilgrims headed to Mecca were among the wounded. Houthi-run al-Masirah TV said Saudi Arabia launched retaliatory strikes on Sanaa International Airport, areas near the Bab al-Mandeb Strait and several northern provinces; that is the Houthis' own outlet's account of the coalition's response and is uncorroborated. The Houthis separately said they were ready for "reciprocal measures" to keep airports, seaports and humanitarian facilities out of the confrontation. These items are cited from feed metadata and search-result aggregations of AFP reporting; the France24 live page itself could not be rendered by the fetcher.

That would put a back-channel running alongside his weighing of US airstrikes on the group, but neither outlet's account has been independently corroborated by a Western outlet, and the story's symmetric-skepticism rule applies: state-affiliated reporting of one party's own diplomacy is that party's narrative until corroborated.

**Latest (Saturday night, October 10):** Coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki Al-Maliki vowed a firm response to Saturday's attack on King Khalid International Airport, calling it a terrorist act and a "full-fledged war crime" under international humanitarian law, in a statement on X carried by the Saudi Press Agency. Trump, speaking at Joint Base Andrews, condemned the attack as "not acceptable" and said he would speak with Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman; Secretary of State Marco Rubio said an American citizen had been killed in the strikes. The coalition also announced it intercepted a Houthi ballistic missile in Al-Kharj governorate, and Yemen's government sent the UN a letter seeking condemnation of Wednesday's Houthi attack on Aden airport. FlightAware counted at least 291 cancellations at Riyadh airport over 24 hours, several international carriers suspended Saudi flights, and the EU Aviation Safety Agency expanded its Saudi airspace guidance, warning that missile and drone attacks and interception efforts raise the risk of civil aircraft being misidentified.

**Latest (Saturday night, October 10):** Saudi Arabia's General Authority of Civil Aviation confirmed 12 people were killed and 309 wounded in Saturday's attack on King Khalid International Airport, the first official consolidated casualty statement of the week, naming the dead as four Saudis plus nationals of Bangladesh, Palestine, Egypt, Jordan, Syria, Sudan and the United States. The coalition's spokesman, Maj. Gen. Turki Al-Maliki, said King Fahd International Airport in Dammam also came under attack, the first strike on the kingdom's main eastern airport since the Iran war began in February. KKIA remains temporarily suspended; Saudi missions abroad are assisting stranded travelers. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen condemned the attacks on civilian infrastructure, and Saudi envoy Abdulaziz Alwasil told the UN Security Council on Friday that Riyadh will not allow Yemen to become a launchpad for attacks or Red Sea shipping threats.

**Latest (Saturday night, October 10):** Al Jazeera confirmed Saudi Arabia's Civil Aviation Authority said several people were injured in the third attack this week, with video showing damage inside the terminal building. The Wall Street Journal, via TASS, reported two King Khalid International terminals took direct missile hits. Separately, Russian state-affiliated outlets reported a projectile fell near Dammam's international airport in eastern Saudi Arabia; uncorroborated. NYT and FT casualty figures cited by the same wires (12 dead, about 50 injured) remain unconfirmed by Saudi authorities.

**Latest (Saturday late evening, October 10):** EgyptAir cancelled its Riyadh flights after the airport attack, and the airport is expected to remain closed for another day, according to TASS. An extended closure would make Saturday's strike the most disruptive of the week, stranding schedules carriers had already rerouted on Friday. TASS is Russian state-affiliated and both items are cited from feed metadata, so treat them as uncorroborated until a carrier or Saudi authority confirms.

**Latest (Saturday evening, October 10):** Trump told reporters at the White House that the United States may decide "very quickly" to join airstrikes on the Houthi militia in Yemen after the fourth attack in a week on Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport. "We're going to look at it. We just found out about the recent attack (on the Saudi airport), so we'll make a decision," he said, adding "We move very quickly." The coalition backing Yemen's government said it had already launched "large-scale operations" against the Houthis on Friday, destroying more than 130 targets, as Yemeni government forces push offensives near the Bab al-Mandab Strait, which they seized last month and where the Houthis have previously attacked commercial vessels. Arab News reports four people killed and dozens injured in the week's airport strikes; Saudi authorities have not issued a consolidated casualty statement, so counts remain provisional.

Houthi-claimed missile strikes hit Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport on Thursday in two waves, killing three Saudi nationals including Saudia pilot Captain Hamoud Ali Alkalthami and destroying a parked Saudia aircraft that had no passengers aboard, with several more people of various nationalities wounded. The attacks forced more than 124 flight cancellations on Friday per FlightAware, drew suspensions from Air India, Lufthansa Group and Pakistan International Airlines, and prompted the EU Aviation Safety Agency to recommend airlines avoid additional sections of Saudi airspace. The Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed three Houthi rocket launch platforms and struck targets in Sanaa overnight, where Houthi officials reported six injured including a child.

**Latest (Saturday afternoon, October 10):** Saudi Arabia suspended operations at King Khalid International Airport after the attack, Bloomberg reported, the first official shutdown in the weeklong strike arc. The New York Times confirmed the airport was hit again after days of strikes, and The Guardian described the strike as a Houthi attack and reported dozens injured. Italy's ANSA, in Italian, reported a Houthi missile strike with five injured and the airport closed. Casualty figures remain inconsistent across outlets, from several injured per Reuters to dozens per The Guardian, and Saudi authorities had not issued a public statement as of Saturday afternoon Riyadh time. No group has claimed responsibility for the Saturday strike.

**Latest (Saturday midday, October 10):** The Associated Press, citing two regional officials speaking anonymously, reported another attack on the airport on Saturday: one official said the airport had been evacuated, another that air traffic had stopped, per Akhbrna's aggregation of the AP reporting. Deutsche Welle, reporting from the same incident, said witnesses and local officials confirmed the evacuation and blast but that there were no immediate reports of damage or injuries and no Saudi official statement yet; Reuters separately reported several people injured. Etihad, Qatar Airways and Kuwait Airways canceled Riyadh flights, with some cancellations extending into Monday. Germany's and Canada's embassies urged citizens to avoid the airport, while Spain's embassy referred to a "new attack" by the Houthis. No group has claimed the Saturday blast. Greek state broadcaster ERT, citing unnamed diplomatic and regional sources, reported a Houthi missile strike with dozens injured and at least five in critical condition; those casualty figures conflict with other outlets' reporting and remain uncorroborated.
