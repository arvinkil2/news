Grit Real Estate Income Group has entered into definitive agreements for two disposals that together break up the listed group. In the first, Grit sells its entire shareholding in and claims against Gateway Real Estate Africa (GREA) and Africa Property Development Managers (APDM) to the Government Employees Pension Fund of South Africa, represented by the Public Investment Corporation (PIC). The consideration is capped at $149,097,093, payable in cash on completion.

GREA is Grit's development platform, holding interests across seven African markets including Kenya, Mauritius, Ghana, Uganda, Mozambique, Nigeria and Ethiopia. Its portfolio includes data center projects and properties leased to the United States embassy programme. Grit is selling its directly held 48.08 percent stake in GREA and a 78.95 percent stake in APDM. PIC already holds 22.44 percent of Grit itself and the pension fund's existing economic interest in GREA is about 46.71 percent, so the sale is treated as a related-party transaction under the UK listing rules.

In the second transaction, Grit will dispose of substantially all of its remaining income-producing property interests, 12 assets across Mozambique, Zambia, Ghana, Senegal and South Africa, to HRT Africa Limited and HRT Aflite Ltd. That consideration totals about $179.23 million and consists principally of the buyers assuming about $143.93 million of property-level debt and about $35.30 million under a revolving credit facility, rather than cash.

Grit states plainly that the transactions are intended to address the group's debt and liquidity constraints. Completion of each deal remains subject to conditions including lender and regulatory approvals, neither transaction is conditional on the other, and the development platform sale carries a long-stop deadline of February 28, 2027, with an effective date currently expected on January 31, 2027. Grit's chief executive Bronwyn Knight told Bloomberg the Gateway sale must close by March. Grit has said the wider restructuring is likely to lead to its delisting from the London and Mauritius exchanges, where trading in its shares has been suspended.
