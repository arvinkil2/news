Tanker freight jumped 5.67 percent on Friday — up 32.75 percent on the week — as the Strait of Hormuz stayed under fire. UKMTO logged two projectile strikes on tankers Friday and a third on Sunday, plus a Saturday-night hit north of Musandam that left a vessel burning with its crew evacuated. The war-risk bid is the market's way of saying the corridor is open but not safe (see today's Hormuz story).

WTI fell 1.47 percent to $89.77, the mirror image: crude futures faded on the diplomacy calendar — the G7's 100-million-barrel release, Kpler's provisional data showing Hormuz crude flows back above pre-war levels, and Saudi Aramco's $3-a-barrel November price cut to Asia. Paper crude is trading the talks; physical freight is trading the strikes (see today's OPEC+ story).

Copper miners led the equity bounce at +3.01 percent, with BHP up 1.68 percent to $86.07 and Rio Tinto up 1.44 percent to $94.21 — a straight macro rebound as copper rose 1.37 percent on the war headlines. The copper miners' outperformance against the metal smells of short-covering after the prior week's growth-fear derisking; BHP and Rio's gains were metals-tape, not steel-fundamental (see today's miner equities story).

Silver rose 2.81 percent to $61.67, bouncing off last week's 6 percent drop — the haven bid reasserting itself as October Fed-hike pricing collapsed on the soft payrolls print. Gold added 0.46 percent to $4,181.50, still capped by the 5.28 percent 10-year and the 18-month-high dollar.

The lithium and battery complex gained 1.55 percent on M&A bid support: Titan's binding $333 million cash takeover of Global Lithium and Lynas's agreed A$968 million all-scrip acquisition of Meteoric Resources are repricing the developers (see today's mining stories).

Dry bulk freight was the demand-side tell, down 2.2 percent on Friday and 7.31 percent on the week — soft while tanker rates scream, a clean split between war-risk pricing and genuine demand worry.

The cross-market read is the two-tier market in one table: insurance and haven assets bid (tankers, silver, miners) while the dollar firms and crude fades on flow data. The physical war premium lives in freight; the paper diplomacy discount lives in barrels; and dry bulk's slide is the quiet vote that the war's demand destruction is real. Into the U.S. open, the test is whether diesel backwardation flattens on the G7 release — and whether the 4:00 p.m. USDA Crop Progress accelerates the corn unwind into Friday's WASDE.
