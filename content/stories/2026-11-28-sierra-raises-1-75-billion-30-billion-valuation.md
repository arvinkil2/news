Sierra said on November 23 that it has raised 1.75 billion dollars in a Series F round at a 30 billion dollar valuation, doubling the roughly 15 billion dollar valuation set by its previous round six months earlier. Greenoaks and Lightspeed Venture Partners led the round, and Sierra said its earlier lead investors, Benchmark, Sequoia, GV and Tiger Global, all increased their holdings.

The company, co-founded by former Salesforce co-chief executive Bret Taylor, who also chairs OpenAI's board, and former Google executive Clay Bavor, sells AI agents that handle customer service work for large companies by chat and voice. Sierra said on October 30 that its annual recurring revenue had passed 400 million dollars, more than doubling in five months, and that it serves almost half of the Fortune 50. At the new valuation, investors are paying about 75 times that run rate.

Sierra plans to use the money to build more agent tools and hire sales staff to win enterprise contracts. Taylor has previously predicted a correction in AI valuations within two years and argued Sierra intends to be among the survivors; the size and speed of this round, closed while public AI stocks were selling off, will be read as a test of that claim.

The round is the largest US venture financing of the week of November 22 to 28 and extends a pattern in which a small number of AI application companies with proven enterprise revenue are raising at prices that assume years of uninterrupted growth.
