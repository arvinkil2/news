The Nasdaq-100 fell 1.39 percent on Monday to close at 27,989.82, a close 10 percent below its most recent peak that puts the index in correction territory for the third time this year, after technology stocks fell for a third straight day. The S&P 500 eased 0.51 percent to 7,541.40, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 0.41 percent to 51,983.98, the only major index to finish higher.

MarketWatch, which called the October slide a tech wreck that continued on Monday, quoted Steve Sosnick, chief strategist at Interactive Brokers, saying after the closing bell that the main root of it is people questioning some of the assumptions they made about AI. FXStreet, citing Morgan Stanley, said the pullback is driven by the midterm elections, concerns over AI spending and a lack of liquidity, and that investors worry less about technology companies meeting forecasts than about spending eating into free cash flow.
