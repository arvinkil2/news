The United States and Japan said on Thursday that secure, resilient and diversified critical mineral supply chains are essential to both countries, and that responsible seabed mineral development can help diversify supplies, in a joint statement issued during a visit to Japan by a US interagency delegation. The two governments welcomed progress under their March 2026 memorandum of cooperation, including the working group's second meeting in Toyama on November 11 and 12, which discussed Japan's rare earth mud project near Minamitorishima Island and its polymetallic nodules project. Their nine priorities include fostering trusted industry partnerships across the seabed supply chain, securing processing capacity among trusted partners, sharing exploration and environmental assessment practices, coordinating research vessels and autonomous underwater vehicles, and promoting clear and predictable regulation to draw private investment.
