**Latest, October 30:** Evacuations have begun in Oleshky. Residents began leaving the town on Friday as an aid convoy arrived under the local ceasefire, in the first joint relief operation of its kind in more than four years of war, Reuters reported. Russia's human rights commissioner Yana Lantratova said groups of people were boarding evacuation buses, some wishing to go to Russia and others to Ukraine, and Ukraine's commissioner Dmytro Lubinets, appearing alongside her, said everyone in need of assistance would get it and that residents who wish to travel to Ukraine, or to Russia, would be given the opportunity. Prime Minister Sergiy Koretskyi told Ukraine's parliament that residents headed for Ukraine would travel through Russia to Belarus before entering the Kyiv and Chernihiv regions, Interfax Ukraine reported. Lantratova said food, hygiene products, medical supplies and fuel were being delivered to the town, and that a sapper and one other person were killed during the demining that preceded the convoy. It was not clear how many people were in each group, or how many residents had decided to stay.

The United Nations set out its position the same day. Tom Fletcher, the UN's emergency relief coordinator, said each day the ceasefire holds means hope for civilians in the city and that those wishing to leave must be able to do so safely and voluntarily, UN News reported. The ceasefire, brokered by First Lady Melania Trump and announced by the White House on October 23, is due to end on the evening of November 1. UN spokesperson Stéphane Dujarric said the UN had played no role in the evacuation itself and was providing humanitarian aid to evacuees on the receiving end, as they reach the Ukrainian side of the frontline. Elsewhere, two humanitarian vehicles came under drone attack on Thursday, the UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs said: a clearly marked civilian bus in the Zaporizhzhia region was struck, killing a woman and injuring passengers and the driver, and an armoured evacuation vehicle in Sloviansk, in the Donetsk region, was hit, injuring two evacuees. OCHA also said 190,000 consumers in the Dnipro region were still without electricity on Friday morning after attacks on energy infrastructure.

**Latest, October 27:** The silence regime around Oleshky has taken effect. Russia's Defence Ministry said Russian forces ceased fire on the Kherson direction from 04:00 Moscow time on Tuesday, October 27 (01:00 GMT), introducing the regime in Oleshky to prepare the humanitarian operation, including safe delivery of essential supplies and engineering reconnaissance around the town, Vedomosti reported in Russian. Al Jazeera, with AP, reported the localised ceasefire is due to last through Saturday, or longer, and that negotiators involved in the mission said doctors, medicines, food and water would be taken into Oleshky before evacuation corridors are opened, with up to 2,000 people still in the town, a figure it attributed to the United Nations. Both accounts rest on the Russian Defence Ministry's announcement of the start; neither reports independent confirmation from the ground that strikes have stopped, and no Ukrainian statement on the ceasefire taking effect had been reported at this update.

**Latest, October 26 (afternoon):** Ukraine’s General Staff said Ukraine will introduce a silence regime in the Oleshky area from 03:00 Kyiv time on Tuesday, October 27, to carry out the humanitarian operation and help the civilian population, NV reported. The General Staff said the regime applies exclusively to delivering humanitarian aid to residents still in the city and to preparing free movement of civilians out of Oleshky, and it asked people not to spread information about the routes, times and places of movement of people and humanitarian convoys. The stated start, 03:00 in Kyiv, is the same instant as the 04:00 Moscow time start the Russian General Staff announced on October 23, so the two militaries’ timetables now agree on when the truce begins; the October 29 date in the earlier accounts refers to the aid and evacuation operation that follows. This is the Ukrainian military’s own statement, reported by a Ukrainian outlet, and is carried on that basis.

**Latest, October 26:** On the eve of the truce, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Russia is open to further local humanitarian ceasefires of the Oleshky kind, telling reporters that for humanitarian purposes it is absolutely possible, and that the Oleshky arrangement could set a precedent for other areas, AFP reported. He said he hoped Kyiv would respect the agreements reached with the direct involvement of the American negotiators led by Melania Trump. At the same time he said the decision to resume trilateral talks next month implies no restrictions on combat operations, and that Russian forces continue massive strikes on Ukraine's military industrial complex. President Volodymyr Zelensky said the American side proposed the ceasefire starting on October 27, that it must last several days for humanitarian missions to reach the town, and that Russia must clear the roads of mines for the evacuation to proceed. The start date accounts still differ between the two capitals, as recorded below: the Russian silence regime from 04:00 Moscow time on October 27, and the Ukrainian ombudsman's operation dated October 29. Peskov's remarks are the Kremlin's account, carried by AFP and Italian agency reporting; the wider war claims in them are not independently verified here.

**Latest:** European Parliament Vice-President Pina Picierno has agreed to end the hunger strike she began on October 21 over Oleshky, Ukraine’s human rights ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets said, as reported by Ukrainska Pravda. Lubinets said he spoke with Picierno on Friday, urged her to stop because the strike posed a serious threat to her life and health, and told her about the local ceasefire agreements, which provide for demining the road to Oleshky, delivering food, water, medicines and equipment for the hospital, and ensuring safe movement and evacuation of civilians. He promised to inform her regularly about implementation of the agreements.

**Latest:** Both sides have now confirmed the agreement. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov told journalists that Russia welcomes Melania Trump's efforts and that she made a substantial contribution to agreeing the humanitarian operation, TASS reported, and Chief of the General Staff Valery Gerasimov said Russian forces will cease fire on the Kherson direction and observe a silence regime from 04:00 Moscow time on October 27, with engineering reconnaissance organised and safe movement of the humanitarian column into the city ensured. Ukraine's human rights ombudsman, Dmytro Lubinets, confirmed a humanitarian operation and ceasefire in Oleshky on October 29 in three parts, a silence regime, delivery of humanitarian cargo, and free movement of civilians out of the city, and said the operation would proceed gradually and is maximally complex to carry out, Ukrainska Pravda reported.

The White House announced on Friday that First Lady Melania Trump had secured a local ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine in Oleshky, the Russian-occupied city on the east bank of the Dnipro River opposite Kherson, with both sides to suspend military action there from October 29 so aid can enter and civilians can leave through a designated safe passage, the BBC and Al Jazeera reported. Melania Trump said in a White House statement that the situation in Oleshky was dire, with residents living with limited electricity, mined roads and regular military attacks, and the White House estimated that 1,700 to 2,000 of the city's pre-war 24,000 residents remain, including about 50 children. The first lady said she initiated the talks and that she and a representative worked directly with Russian and Ukrainian leadership on the terms, that civilian representatives from both countries would visit residents at home to assess what help they need, and that residents who wish to leave would be taken to their preferred destination in Russia or Ukraine. Neither government had commented on the announcement as of those first reports; both confirmed it later in the day, as recorded above.
