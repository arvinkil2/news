Appian Capital Advisory has deployed about 70 percent of the International Finance Corporation's initial $100 million commitment under the mining investment vehicle the two launched in 2025, Appian chief executive Michael Scherb told Reuters, and a World Bank Group spokesperson confirmed on Tuesday that about 70 percent had been allocated across three investments. The wider partnership carries a total commitment of up to $1 billion, and the vehicle is the IFC's first dedicated emerging markets metals and minerals fund and its first with a mining focused private equity investor.

The capital has gone into Asante Gold's gold mines in Ghana, Omico Copper's copper project in Namibia and Atlantic Nickel's nickel copper cobalt operation in Brazil, which includes the Santa Rita mine in Bahia, the fund's first investment. Appian expects to deploy the remaining balance by the end of 2026 from a pipeline in Africa and Latin America, and is exploring whether a similar structure could be repeated with the European Bank for Reconstruction and Development and other public lenders.

The speed matters for host governments and competing financiers. Appian, which says it has helped bring 12 mines into production, is pairing development finance standards, including IFC environmental and social performance requirements, with private equity execution timelines. For emerging market miners, that combination is becoming an alternative to both traditional project finance and Chinese state backed capital, and its expansion will be watched in exactly the jurisdictions, Ghana and Namibia among them, where mining codes and royalty regimes are being rewritten this year.
