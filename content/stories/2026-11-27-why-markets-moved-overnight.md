Silver miners rose 4.84 percent to 65.45 dollars and gold miners rose 3.53 percent to 73.89 dollars, riding the metals surge in today's commodities reporting: silver futures jumped about 10 percent against Wednesday settlements and gold rose about 4 percent, with no confirmed single trigger and thin holiday trade doing part of the work (see today's silver story).

Nvidia fell 3.91 percent to 149.30 dollars, Amazon fell 3.12 percent to 244.43 dollars and Alphabet fell 2.13 percent to 273.10 dollars. Today's technology reporting puts the three in the same megacap slide that followed Tuesday's chip-led drop, with no new company-specific driver reported this morning. Meta moved the other way, up 3.4 percent to 609.89 dollars; no clear single driver appears in this morning's reporting.

The Nikkei 225 and the Hang Seng each rose 2.11 percent, to 58,711 and 22,695 points. For Hong Kong and mainland shares, today's reporting names the driver directly: Beijing's State Council pledged a bigger market-stabilising role for state-backed investors, and mainland stocks posted their best session in two years (see today's China market support story). No clear single driver for the Nikkei appears in this morning's reporting.

The cross-market read is selective risk-taking rather than a broad risk-on move. Miners and Asian equities were bid, helped by the metals surge and Beijing's policy floor, while the largest US technology names mostly fell, which left the S&P 500 down 0.25 percent and the Nasdaq 100 down 0.64 percent at the last close. Treasury yields then rose at Friday's reopen as equities rebounded, a move covered in today's rates reporting.
