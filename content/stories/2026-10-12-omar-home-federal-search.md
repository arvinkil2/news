**Latest (19:53 ET, Oct 14):** The raid reached Omar's phone and her wider family. Federal authorities seized a cell phone belonging to Omar during Monday's search of her Washington home, Fox News Digital reported on Wednesday, and investigators have issued more than a dozen subpoenas to her family members since the raid. The probe is examining whether Omar's family lied about her age when she immigrated to the United States in 1995, including whether the age on her application differed from her actual birth date, and whether statements on her late father's entry filings were untruthful. Omar's husband, Tim Mynett, told Fox this week that she arrived at 12 as a child war refugee and that the couple have no concerns. Omar has denied wrongdoing, and her office called the raid a pointless use of federal power against a political opponent. A separate review of her federal financial disclosures is unrelated to the raid, officials noted in the report.

Homeland Security Investigations agents raided the Washington-area home of Rep. Ilhan Omar, D-Minn., in the early hours of Monday under a search warrant secured by the Justice Department, Fox News Digital reported in an exclusive citing a source familiar with the matter. A DOJ spokesperson confirmed that HSI "executed a judicially authorized search warrant" as part of "an ongoing federal investigation," and multiple agents wearing HSI jackets were seen outside the home.

The probe centers on a criminal fraud investigation into immigration paperwork filed by Omar's late father when she was a child, according to two sources close to the investigation. Border czar Tom Homan had said in mid-September that Omar was subject to an ongoing "criminal" investigation. Fox notes the story is developing and no charges have been announced.
