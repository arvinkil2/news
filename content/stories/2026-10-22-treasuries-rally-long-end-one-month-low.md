Treasuries climbed across the curve on Thursday, pushing long-end yields to their lowest in a month, as renewed doubts about the durability of the artificial intelligence trade drove investors toward havens, Bloomberg reported. Early trading had the 10-year yield down about 7 basis points to 5.08 percent and the 30-year down about 6 basis points to 5.38 percent, with the two-year yield down about 5 basis points to 4.77 percent, according to the Jin Shi market flash carrying the same levels. By midday the fall had deepened in this site's market tables: the five-year yield was down about 16 basis points to 4.81 percent, the 10-year down about 15 basis points to 4.99 percent and the 30-year down about 12 basis points to 5.32 percent.

The move reverses the prior close. This site's market tables for Wednesday, October 21 put the five-year yield at 4.97 percent, up 3.7 basis points on the day, the 10-year at 5.15 percent, up 2.9 basis points, and the 30-year at 5.44 percent, up 1.4 basis points, with the dollar index softer at 101.61, down 0.17 percent, and USD/JPY at 156.93, down 0.85 percent. Thursday's rally therefore retraces the week's drift higher rather than extending it, and even after the fall the 30-year yield remains within its recent range, near levels last seen consistently in 2002.

The driver matters for how long it lasts. This is a haven bid out of equities, the same AI valuation doubt that took Seoul's Kospi down 3.52 percent on Thursday, not a repricing of the inflation path: producer prices are running at 6.3 percent year on year and the Federal Reserve meets on October 27 to 28 with markets expecting a hold and the December meeting live. A rally built on equity nerves can unwind with the next earnings print, and the curve goes into the FOMC week with the long end bid but the policy path unchanged.

The close confirmed the move and enlarged it. This site's market tables for Thursday put the five-year yield at 4.77 percent, down 20.4 basis points on the day, the 10-year at 4.95 percent, down 19.3 basis points, and the 30-year at 5.29 percent, down 15.2 basis points. The Seoul Economic Daily, citing Koscom Check data, put the 10-year at 4.953 percent, its first close below 5 percent since September 23 and its largest single-day decline since March 10, 2023, with the two-year yield down 18.8 basis points to 4.635 percent. The dollar index rose 0.13 percent to 101.74 even as yields fell, and USD/JPY fell 0.99 percent to 156.57, a combination that marks the move as a duration bid on equity risk, with the yen also bid, rather than a dollar or inflation repricing. The curve goes into the October 27 to 28 FOMC meeting with the long end at a one-month low and the policy path, on this evidence, unchanged.

Friday gave back a small part of the move and no more. This site's market tables for Friday, October 23 put the five-year yield at 4.80 percent, up 3.7 basis points on the day, the 10-year at 4.99 percent, up 3.5 basis points, and the 30-year at 5.32 percent, up 2.8 basis points. Dow Jones Data Talk, on Tradeweb closing prices at 3 p.m. ET, put the 10-year at 4.987 percent, down 0.110 percentage point on the week for a third consecutive weekly decline and the largest three-week fall since the week ending April 4, 2025, with Friday's rise the largest one-day gain since Monday, October 19. The driver flipped with the calendar: Brent crude settled at 116.72 dollars a barrel on Friday, up 3.14 percent on the day and 6.24 percent on the week in this site's tables, its highest close since April 29 according to Aju Press, and the energy move revived the inflation concerns that Thursday's haven bid had set aside. The dollar index rose 0.2 percent to 101.81. The week therefore closes with the 10-year below 5 percent but no longer falling, which keeps the rally's character intact: a risk move that oil can interrupt, heading into the FOMC week.
