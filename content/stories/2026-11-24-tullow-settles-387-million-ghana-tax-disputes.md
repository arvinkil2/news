Tullow Oil has reached agreement with the Government of Ghana to settle two long-running tax disputes involving principal assessments totalling $387 million, the London-listed producer announced on November 23. The first dispute covers a $196.5 million assessment on business interruption insurance proceeds, on which an International Chamber of Commerce tribunal ruled in Ghana's favour in September 2026. The second covers a $190.5 million assessment from the disallowance of loan interest deductions. Both assessments carried penalties of 100 percent, so the disputed exposure was materially larger than the principal sum.

Under the settlement, Tullow will pay $112 million in cash upfront, settle approximately $91 million through the assignment of certain receivables and a deduction for previously unutilised business interruption insurance premiums, and pay a further $40 million in 2027 and $44.5 million in 2028. The 2027 and 2028 payments can be deferred if annual average oil prices are below $90 per barrel, with a final payment deadline in 2032, and the package also includes future revenues from gas produced at the offshore Jubilee field. The government has approved the agreement, which remains subject to parliamentary approval.

Tullow cut its full-year 2026 free cash flow guidance to $160 million to $210 million, from $170 million to $250 million, reflecting the settlement alongside production performance and two October cargoes realised above $100 per barrel. Chief Executive Ian Perks said the agreement lets the company proceed with its refinancing plans and support investment in the Jubilee and TEN fields. Tullow shares rose about 20 percent on the announcement.
