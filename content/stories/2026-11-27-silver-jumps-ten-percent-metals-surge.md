Friday's settlements confirmed the scale of the metals rebound. COMEX gold futures settled at 3,681.10 dollars an ounce, up 2.44 percent, and COMEX silver futures settled at 43.985 dollars, up 5.35 percent, while spot gold closed near 3,653 dollars, up 2.45 percent on the day, and spot silver closed near 43.62 dollars, up 5.32 percent. Silver settled 7.76 percent higher on the front contract measure used by The Edge wrap, and finished the week up 6.74 percent against gold's 3.66 percent weekly gain, taking the gold silver ratio down to 83.6 from 85.9.

The intraday trade had been larger still. December silver stood at 44.09 dollars late in the London morning, up 10.07 percent from Wednesday's settlement, January platinum added 8.43 percent and December palladium rose 8.13 percent, all in thin post Thanksgiving liquidity. Part of the intraday premium faded into the settlement, which is the usual pattern for holiday sessions, but the weekly direction held across every measure.

Context matters for reading the size of the move. Spot gold had touched 3,445.89 dollars on Wednesday, its lowest level since August 2025, after a week in which it fell about 9 percent, so Friday's trade is a rebound from depressed levels rather than a breakout to new highs. Market participants attributed the recovery to bargain hunting and short covering after the scale of the prior correction, and analysts cautioned that oversold conditions alone do not mark a bottom.

Copper staged its own version of the same trade in London, surging as much as 6.5 percent on the LME in its biggest intraday gain since January; that move is covered in today's separate copper story. Whether the precious metals rebound holds will be tested when full US liquidity returns on Monday and by Friday's official exchange data on positioning.
