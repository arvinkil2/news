Silver led a broad metals surge on Friday. December silver on COMEX stood at 44.09 dollars an ounce late in the London morning, up 10.07 percent from Wednesday's settlement, and India Infoline reported an intraday high of 44.235 dollars, up 10.4 percent. December gold rose 3.99 percent to 3,677 dollars, while February gold futures were reported up 4 percent at 3,708.40 dollars.

The rest of the complex moved with it. January platinum added 8.43 percent to 1,347.10 dollars, December palladium rose 8.13 percent to 900.50 dollars on an older quote, and December copper gained 4.76 percent to 5.59 dollars a pound, with March copper reported up 5.1 percent. The Edge, citing Reuters, attributed gold's rise to a pause in the dollar's advance and calmer equity markets, and noted that wire coverage offered no explanation for the scale of the gains in silver, platinum or palladium.

Context matters for reading the size of the move. Gold had fallen to its lowest level in more than a year on Wednesday, near 3,425 dollars on some measures, so Friday's trade is a rebound from depressed levels rather than a breakout to new highs. Trading is also thin: US markets were closed on Thursday for Thanksgiving and reopen only for a shortened session on Friday, which can amplify intraday swings in both directions.

The estate market table, which carries the previous completed session, points the same way on direction: silver up 11.34 percent to 44.60 dollars, gold up 4.82 percent to 3,706.40 dollars and copper up 6.49 percent to 5.68 dollars. Whether the surge holds will be decided by Friday's official settlements and by whether follow through buying appears when full US liquidity returns on Monday.
