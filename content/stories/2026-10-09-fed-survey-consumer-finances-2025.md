The Federal Reserve on Friday released the 2025 Survey of Consumer Finances, its triennial portrait of American family finances. Real median family income rose 7 percent between the 2022 and 2025 surveys to $82,200, while real mean family income fell 6 percent to $145,200, with families at the lower ends of the income and net worth distributions seeing modest gains and those at the upper ends seeing declines. Real median net worth rose 2 percent to $215,900 and real mean net worth rose 7 percent to $1.24 million.

Stock market participation slipped slightly, to 56 percent of families from 58 percent, but conditional on holding stock, median holdings grew 36 percent to $77,400. Homeownership held at 66 percent. The share of families with debt payments exceeding 40 percent of income rose from 6.5 to 8.6 percent, a level last seen in the 2013 survey.
