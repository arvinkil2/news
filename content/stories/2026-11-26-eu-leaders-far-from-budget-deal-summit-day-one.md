Latest: The summit formally ended late on Thursday night after a final plenary session, with no agreement on the budget and no decision on the proposed 45 billion euro loan for Ukraine in 2027, both deferred to the European Council that opens on December 17. Leaders said the budget talks had not brought the camps closer, and several net-contributor governments had declined to take part in Council President Antonio Costa's small-group sessions during the day.

Earlier: Leaders dropped the planned Friday session and reconvened late on Thursday evening instead, with a further round possible around midnight, after Dutch Prime Minister Rob Jetten said they had made less progress than hoped and that Council President Antonio Costa would need to improvise a new budget proposal. Chancellor Friedrich Merz said the day had brought new movement toward reducing the size of the proposed budget, and that leaders would try to reach agreement at a two-day summit starting December 17. No budget or Ukraine financing agreement had been reached by Thursday evening.

EU leaders closed the first day of the extraordinary Brussels summit on Thursday with no agreement on the 2028 to 2034 long-term budget or on financing for Ukraine, and Council President Antonio Costa's compromise of 1.78 trillion euros, about 204 billion euros below the Commission's initial proposal, did not bridge the gaps. Chancellor Friedrich Merz said the budget was still too high and that Europe must not take on more debt, Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni said leaders were light-years away from a solution, and Hungary's Peter Magyar put the chance of a deal on Thursday at virtually zero, with Bulgaria, the Czech Republic, Hungary and Slovakia opposing funds for military aid to Ukraine. Costa aims for agreement at a further summit on December 17 to 18.
