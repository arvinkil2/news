Chile's copper production is heading for its lowest level in 23 years, with output expected to fall 6.6 percent to 5.034 million tonnes in 2026, a loss of 354,000 tonnes from last year, according to estimates by mining industry analyst Juan José Pardo of Plusmining reported by Mining.com. Production fell 12.7 percent year on year to 366,000 tonnes in August, reflecting weaker ore grades, operational disruptions and adverse weather, with Escondida and Spence leading the declines and El Teniente still contending with the aftermath of a 2025 rockburst.

Compounding the production problems, sulfuric acid imports reached a record 3.97 million tonnes in 2025, supplying roughly half of national consumption, the Chilean Copper Commission (Cochilco) said on Tuesday, while domestic acid production fell to its lowest since 2002 as smelters ran at about 60 percent of capacity. Spot sulfuric acid prices in Chile climbed from about $190 per tonne in late February to $485 by mid-June before retreating to around $340 in early November, raising leaching costs sharply for the operations that produce about a fifth of Chilean copper.
