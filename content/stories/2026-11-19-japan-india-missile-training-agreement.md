Japan and India agreed on Thursday, at the fourth meeting of their foreign and defence ministers in Tokyo, that Japan's Self-Defense Forces may conduct long-range missile firing training in India, the Asahi Shimbun reported. Defence Minister Shinjiro Koizumi said Japan would speed up the practical arrangements so the forces could use an Indian firing range during 2027, noting that Japan has little wide open land suited to such training; the Self-Defense Forces already train with live fire in the United States and are arranging to use a test site in Australia.

The ministers, Koizumi and Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi for Japan and Rajnath Singh and Subrahmanyam Jaishankar for India, also discussed exporting to India the Unicorn communications antenna fitted to Japan's Mogami-class frigates, and agreed their diplomats would hold tabletop exercises and consultations on protecting their own nationals overseas. It was the first such 2+2 meeting since August 2024.
