Brookfield Renewable Partners has rewritten the approval test for its corporate simplification after turnout, not opposition, threatened the plan. The partnership said on November 27 that about 62 percent of its units had been voted, with 99.7 percent of votes cast in favour, short of the original requirement for approval by holders of two-thirds of all eligible units.

The general partner has amended the limited partnership agreement, as the agreement permits, so the plan of arrangement now requires two-thirds of votes cast by unitholders. Unitholders no longer vote separately on the partnership agreement amendment, and the company said all other substantive terms, including the tax treatment and the expected benefits, are unchanged. Holders who have already voted do not need to vote again.

Under the plan, outstanding limited partnership units, other than preferred units, exchange one for one into shares of a new listed Canadian corporation, Brookfield Renewable Partners Inc. Shareholders of Brookfield Renewable Corporation have already approved the matching exchange of their class A shares into the same company, also one for one.

The proxy deadline for registered unitholders is now 5:00 p.m. Toronto time on December 7, with the virtual unitholders meeting on December 9. Brookfield declared a quarterly distribution of $0.392 per unit, payable December 21 to holders of record on December 8, and said it expects the simplification to complete by the end of 2026, after those distributions are paid. The distribution reinvestment plan terminates with the change of structure, with a substantially similar plan expected at the new corporation.

The simplification replaces two listed routes into the same renewable power business, a Bermuda limited partnership and a Canadian corporation, with one corporate security. That structure has historically complicated index inclusion, tax reporting and institutional mandates; the vote mechanics, rather than investor opposition, were the last obstacle.
