Chicago grains held their ground on Monday after Friday's strong close. Soybeans for January delivery rose 0.7 percent to 12.28 and three-quarter dollars a bushel, supported by stronger soymeal demand that lifted meal to its highest in nearly a month, while March wheat settled up 0.6 percent at 5.95 and a half dollars and March corn rose 0.2 percent to 4.49 and three-quarter dollars, according to Dow Jones reporting of the settlements. Reuters attributed part of the support to a weaker dollar and to concern over Black Sea supply while the Russia-Ukraine war continues.

The United States Department of Agriculture reported corn inspected for export at 1.19 million tonnes in the week ended November 19, up from 869,714 tonnes the week before but below the 1.7 million tonnes of a year earlier. Soybean inspections fell to 1.56 million tonnes from 1.73 million, still well above the year-earlier 810,283 tonnes. Wheat inspections were 344,886 tonnes, down on the week and the year, but above the 250,000 to 300,000 tonnes trade expectations cited in market reporting. The USDA also reported a sale of 110,000 tonnes of white wheat to unknown destinations for the 2026-27 marketing year.

The season-to-date picture is less strong than the daily price action. Farm Progress reporting of the USDA data put corn shipments for 2026-27 at 490.7 million bushels, down 21 percent from the same period in 2025-26, though still the third-strongest pace on record for the date, and wheat export commitments off 31 percent from last year. DTN put funds net short just over 90,000 corn contracts and over 90,000 Chicago wheat contracts entering the week, a position that amplifies any bullish surprise.

In the estate market tables, the front wheat series rose 3.34 percent to 596.2 cents a bushel and corn rose 3.74 percent to 450.5 cents at Monday's close, with soybeans up 0.33 percent to 1,232.8 cents. United States markets close Thursday for Thanksgiving and trade an abbreviated session Friday, and December corn first notice day is November 30, so positioning and delivery mechanics share the stage with export data this week.
