**Latest:** The RBI delivered the consensus 25bp hike on Wednesday, raising the repo rate to 5.50 percent, and changed its stance from neutral to "calibrated tightening" by a 4-2 vote, the first hike since February 2023. The Monetary Policy Committee raised its FY27 inflation forecast to 5.2 percent from 5.0 percent and its GDP growth forecast to 7.1 percent from 6.7 percent. Governor Sanjay Malhotra said rate cuts are off the table in the near term; the next move can only be a hike or a pause. The market's 50bp tail risk did not materialize. Bond markets turned bearish after the decision, with the 10-year G-Sec yield hardening about 5bp to roughly 7.25 percent, and the rupee fell 22 paise to 96.57 against the dollar. Meanwhile a Reuters poll of 41 economists published Thursday puts September CPI inflation, due shortly, at a median 5.4 percent, up from 4.82 percent in August, with forecasts ranging from 4.9 to 5.9 percent; respondents pointed to broadening energy-price pressure, with India's crude import price averaging $116.09 a barrel in September.

The market's hawkish tail is growing ahead of Wednesday's Reserve Bank of India decision. The 1-month OIS rate hit 5.55 percent, its highest since April 2, implying significant probability of a larger-than-consensus move. State Street's Krishna Bhimavarapu assigns a 33 percent chance to a 50bp hike, while bankers adjusting for the MIBOR basis put it near 25 percent. Most economists still expect 25bp to 5.50 percent.

The RBI's own operations are adding to the hawkish read. The central bank conducted an overnight variable rate reverse repo auction on October 6 for a notified 2.50 lakh crore rupees, drawing 1.92 lakh crore in bids at a 5.24 percent cut-off. The Hindu BusinessLine characterized the enlarged quantum as priming the market for a hike a day ahead of the decision. The RBI has now announced an even bigger overnight VRRR for October 7, the morning of the decision itself: a notified 3 lakh crore rupees, one-day tenor, reversal on October 8.

The rupee has hit a two-month low as Asian currencies eased against a firm dollar. J.P. Morgan's Sajjid Chinoy cautioned that 50bp would be an overreaction, arguing for 25bp, but the swaps market is hedging against the central bank validating the region's more aggressive tightening rhythm after Indonesia's 100bp of hikes over May and June.
