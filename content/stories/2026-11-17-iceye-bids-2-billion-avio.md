Finnish radar satellite operator Iceye has made a €2 billion cash offer, about $2.2 billion, for Italian rocket maker Avio, offering €41 a share through a wholly owned Italian subsidiary with the aim of delisting the company. The price is a 21.3 percent premium to Avio's closing price on Monday and a 34.8 percent premium to its three month volume weighted average price, and Iceye said it would invest €1 billion in Avio in Italy over five years to expand the customer base of its Vega rocket. Avio shares climbed more than 15 percent to around €39.5 on the news.

Avio's board met on Tuesday and said it had started assessing the bid, and the chief executive of Leonardo, the Italian state controlled defence group that owns 18 percent of Avio, said he was pleasantly surprised by the bid and would consider it. Finland's defence minister, Antti Hakkanen, backed the offer, and Iceye chief executive Rafal Modrzewski said the company had consulted the parties involved, including governments, before bidding and expected the transaction to take a few more months to close. An Avio spokesman said United States government approval would be required for a takeover, as well as Italian government approval.
