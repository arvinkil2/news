The Food and Drug Administration on Friday issued a complete response letter refusing to approve deramiocel, Capricor Therapeutics' allogeneic cardiosphere-derived cell therapy for Duchenne muscular dystrophy, saying the evidence does not meet the standard required for approval. The letter cites serious data integrity violations in the conduct of Study HOPE-3, including unauthorized post hoc changes to the statistical analysis plan and violations of study blinding procedures, which the agency said render the data unreliable and incapable of post hoc remediation; HOPE-3 had also failed its pre-specified primary and secondary endpoints.

The decision followed a July 29, 2026 advisory committee meeting and a major amendment in which Capricor added 24-month open-label extension data and changed its proposed indication to upper limb impairment rather than cardiomyopathy, which the FDA said it reviewed before concluding the standard was still not met. The agency also issued a Regulatory Responsibility Letter dated November 20, 2026 over the inspection findings behind the data integrity concerns. Capricor shares fell 72 percent in extended trading, according to Reuters.

Update, November 22: Capricor said the complete response letter states that the FDA does not consider the data sufficient to establish substantial evidence of effectiveness and cites certain other clinical deficiencies, which the company did not describe. Chief executive Linda Marban said deramiocel has been studied in two randomized, double blind, placebo controlled trials, HOPE-2 and HOPE-3, both of which the company says met their primary endpoints, and that the company stands behind the rigor of its programme and the integrity of its data. The company said HOPE-3 slowed decline on the Performance of the Upper Limb measure by 54 percent versus placebo at 12 months. Capricor held cash, cash equivalents, restricted cash and marketable securities of approximately $198.5 million as of September 30, 2026, which it believes funds planned operations for at least the next 12 months. The company is reviewing the letter, says its operating expenses are under review, and will discuss next steps on a conference call on Monday, November 23 at 8:30 a.m. Eastern. This is the second complete response letter for deramiocel, after a first rejection in July 2025.
