Biogen's diranersen, an antisense therapy developed with Ionis Pharmaceuticals that silences the gene producing tau protein, slowed clinical decline by 38 percent at two years (Week 96) on the Clinical Dementia Rating, Sum of Boxes scale in the Phase 2 CELIA study of early Alzheimer's disease, a 1.09 point treatment difference. The effect, at the 60 milligram twice-yearly spinal injection dose, grew from the 26 percent slowing reported at 18 months, with greater slowing across five of six measures of cognition, function and disease progression than at the earlier readout.

The two-year analysis comes from the study's open-label extension, in which the comparison group had received no doses or a single dose of the drug, and higher doses produced weaker results at 18 months, slowing decline by 9 to 14 percent. Biogen said it is discussing the outline of a Phase 3 trial with regulators, with results still several years away.
