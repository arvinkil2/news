TP-Link, which may be the top provider of wireless routers for US homes and small businesses, is not allowed to sell its latest Wi-Fi routers in the United States. The Trump administration imposed a sweeping ban on foreign-made consumer routers in March 2026, and TP-Link has not been able to obtain an exemption.

The rules prevent the import or sale of all new router models that are manufactured, assembled, designed or developed outside the US. Permission to import and sell new products runs through a waiver review process involving the Federal Communications Commission, the Department of Defense and the Department of Homeland Security, and those reviews, formally called conditional approvals, are conducted in secrecy. Some competitors have obtained exemptions. TP-Link's routers approved before the March ban remain on sale, but its newly released Wi-Fi 8 routers, including the Archer 8 Ultra it opened for preorders elsewhere in September, cannot be imported or sold.

TP-Link was founded in 1996 in China and has since split into two companies, one in the US, and states it has split from and no longer has any affiliation with the company serving the mainland Chinese market. It has pledged to assemble all new routers in the US and earlier this year announced plans to begin assembling routers in Mississippi as early as 2027. Ars Technica reports that convincing officials it is fully under US control is likely to be a difficult task.

The Pentagon declined Ars Technica's request for comment on TP-Link's exemption request, and the FCC and Department of Homeland Security did not provide comment. The ban itself was a dramatic expansion of the FCC's Covered List, which under this administration has added entire product categories regardless of whether a particular vendor has been identified as a risk.
