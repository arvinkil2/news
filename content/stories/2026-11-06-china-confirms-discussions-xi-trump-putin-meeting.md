Latest: President Donald Trump said on Sunday that he expects to meet Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping next week, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen on November 18 and 19. "One of the meetings, I believe, will be with President Putin and President Xi and it will be interesting," Trump told reporters before leaving Florida for Washington, in remarks carried by Reuters. "We are discussing many issues, among them denuclearization, but many issues will be on the table. We are trying to end the war in Iran."

The Kremlin says the meeting is now being prepared, not only discussed. Presidential aide Yuri Ushakov said the organization and preparation of a trilateral meeting on the APEC sidelines has already begun, and spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Moscow, Washington and Beijing are already submitting proposals for the agenda of a possible meeting, RIA Novosti reported. In a CBS 60 Minutes interview, Trump also said he believes negotiations to end the Russia Ukraine war are "very close to reaching an agreement" and that a deal would produce a "Ukraine with a somewhat different form", without saying what he meant.

China confirmed for the first time on November 6 that discussions were underway on a meeting between Xi Jinping, Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin, the Associated Press reported. Foreign ministry spokesperson Mao Ning said at a press briefing in Beijing that China is in communication with the relevant parties regarding a meeting between the three heads of state.

The meeting would take place this month on the sidelines of the APEC leaders' summit in Shenzhen, which China hosts this year. Trump had said in October that he was open to trilateral arms control talks with Xi and Putin centred on denuclearization, and the Wall Street Journal has reported that Putin proposed a trilateral meeting to discuss ending the Ukraine war.
