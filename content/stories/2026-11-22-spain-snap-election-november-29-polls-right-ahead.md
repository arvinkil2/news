Spain votes on Sunday, November 29, in a snap general election called by Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez on October 5 after the Congress of Deputies rejected two government housing decrees. Almost 39 million Spaniards are eligible to elect all 350 members of Congress and the elected part of the Senate, according to the National Statistics Institute figure of 38,728,356 voters cited by La Cotorra.

A prime minister needs an absolute majority of 176 of the 350 Congress seats on the first investiture vote. Most polls have the conservative People's Party and the far right Vox past that line together. The 40dB tracking poll for El Pais and Cadena SER put the right at 192 seats on November 16, with the PP at 31.5 percent, the Socialists at 30.3 percent and Vox at 18.0 percent, while Politico's poll average has the Socialists pulling even with the PP on votes.

The polls carried a warning from 2023, when the PP and Vox finished with 170 seats combined and Sanchez assembled a majority 116 days after the vote. Sanchez stays on as acting prime minister until Congress confirms a successor. The new Congress meets on December 23, after which the King consults the parties and proposes a candidate.

Housing is the campaign's central issue, the question on which the legislature fell. Publication of opinion polls is banned from November 24 to 28, so the final week runs without fresh public numbers.
