Latest: Three more tankers have exploded and are burning in the Strait of Hormuz, the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps navy said in a statement carried by TASS on Sunday. The statement said the three tankers were on a route in the strait not authorised by Iran, that they exploded from the first hours of Sunday morning and are engulfed in strong fire, and that smoke from the burning tankers has covered the sky over the strait. The IRGC attributed the explosions to the tankers switching off their navigation and identification systems and to actions of the American army, and warned that vessels violating Iran's passage rules on unauthorised routes with American assistance face the same fate. The statement named no vessel, owner, flag or cargo and gave no casualty or damage figures, and no confirmation from UKMTO, the United States or shipping sources accompanied it; the claim is Tehran's account alone and is uncorroborated.

Earlier: Two more oil tankers struck mines in the area of the Strait of Hormuz while following a route not agreed with Iran, Iranian television reported, citing the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, Interfax reported. According to the IRGC, both vessels sustained serious damage and stopped moving. The report named no vessels, gave no position beyond the strait area and mentioned no casualties, and it stated that no confirmation of the claim had been received from other sources. The claim follows the Iranian forces' claims of November 5, carried below, that five tankers and a tugboat had caught fire in the strait, which likewise named no vessels and were never confirmed by shipping authorities.

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said on Thursday that two oil tankers and a tugboat caught fire in separate incidents in the Strait of Hormuz after attempting to use routes it called unauthorized, Reuters reported. One tanker struck a mine while crossing a passage south of the strait, a supertanker caught fire while exiting the route in the eastern part of the waterway, and a tugboat that approached it to help put out the fire also caught fire.

The report named no vessels and cited no confirmation from UKMTO, the United States or shipping sources, and oil had not reacted early on Thursday, with Brent holding around $97 a barrel on hopes of a diplomatic path with Iran, according to investingLive's Asia-Pacific wrap, which said the claims could not be independently verified.

Update: The claim had no market support through Thursday morning. Brent was at 95.25 dollars a barrel in the site market tables, down 17.09 percent on the week, as traders kept pricing a diplomatic path on Iran rather than a wider Hormuz closure, and tanker freight fell 17.17 percent to 691.6 dollars. No confirmation of the fires had emerged from UKMTO, the United States or shipping sources in the reporting read for this update, and the claim remains the IRGC's account alone.

Update: Tehran is also saying some traffic moves freely. Ahmad Hayat-Moghaddam, vice chairman of the Iranian parliament's National Security and Foreign Policy Committee, told the Iranian Students News Agency that Iran has exempted India, China, Iraq and Qatar from its transit restrictions in the strait, that ships from those countries pass freely, and that the United States does not interfere with them, in reporting carried by OilPrice. He said India's exemption was meant to facilitate medicine imports and China's to secure Beijing's political support. The statement is an Iranian official's account, unconfirmed by the exempted governments or by shipping sources in the reporting read, and is graded accordingly.

Update: The Iranian army later said three more tankers struck naval mines and caught fire in waters east of the strait, one early on Thursday and two at around noon, in statements carried by the official IRNA news agency and reported by Al Jazeera. That lifts the total claimed by Iranian forces on Thursday to five tankers and a tugboat. No vessel was named in the army statements and no confirmation came from the United States. Separately, UKMTO said a tanker was struck by two projectiles about 50 nautical miles north of Madinat ash Shamal, Qatar, on Wednesday at 23:20 GMT; it did not identify the vessel or its flag, and there was no claim of responsibility.
