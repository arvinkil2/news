Latest: Pakistan had negotiated safe passage for the Al Mayeda's cargo with Iran before the attack, and Iranian authorities were understood to have given the transit a green light just hours before the ship came under fire near the Strait of Hormuz on Friday, Bloomberg reported on Sunday, citing people with direct knowledge of the matter who asked not to be named. The Marshall Islands-flagged vessel was sailing toward the waterway loaded with liquefied natural gas from Qatar when it was hit. Neither the attacker nor any Iranian response to the report has been identified.

Qatar's state news agency reported on Sunday that the Al Mayeda, a liquefied natural gas carrier belonging to the Qatar Gas Transport Company, was struck by unidentified objects while transiting Gulf waters on Friday evening, AFP reported. No crew member was injured and there was no environmental impact, and the company said the ship was in a safe condition and operational. No claim of responsibility for the attack was reported, and the strike follows Thursday's limpet mine attack on the tanker Louise 1 in the Strait of Hormuz.
