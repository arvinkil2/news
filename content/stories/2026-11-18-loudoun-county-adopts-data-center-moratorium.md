Loudoun County's Board of Supervisors voted 7-1, with one abstention, to adopt a resolution pausing Board consideration of legislative applications for data centers and substations for a period not to exceed 12 months, Datacenter Dynamics reported. The county's own action item states the resolution is effective September 15, 2026, that paused applications will be held in queue after Planning Commission review rather than becoming inactive, and that the pause lasts until the county adopts its Phase 2 data center standards, expected in July 2027, with grid-reliability substations excepted.

The county document counts 33 active legislative applications: 10 data centers, 13 substations and 10 combined data center and substation applications. County staff plan to meter applications to about five per board meeting once hearings resume, and expect four to six months of meetings to clear the backlog after September 2027. Loudoun has 245 data centers in operation and under development, and its 2027 budget projects data center revenue of 1.3 billion dollars, 40 percent of the total budget, according to a county fact sheet cited by Datacenter Dynamics.
