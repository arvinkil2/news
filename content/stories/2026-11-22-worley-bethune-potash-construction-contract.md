K+S Potash Canada has awarded Worley the construction contract for the Compaction and Power Distribution project at the Bethune potash mine in Saskatchewan. Under the two-year contract, Worley will deliver field construction services for the compaction building and its associated power distribution infrastructure, including installation of structural, piping, architectural and mechanical equipment, and electrical, instrumentation and control works.

The project is the single largest scope in K+S's long-term Ramp-Up Program at Bethune, which is designed to raise the mine's production capacity to approximately four million tonnes per annum by around 2040, close to double its current output. Worley's ASX release describes the compaction and power scope as fundamental to expanding the mining and processing infrastructure the growth depends on. The contract value was not disclosed.

The work will be delivered in partnership with George Gordon Developments Ltd, the development arm of the George Gordon First Nation, which both companies frame as a route to long-term socio-economic opportunities for First Nation communities. K+S had named Worley and Graham Construction as preferred construction partners for the first major ramp-up scopes in April 2026, with combined early scopes valued at more than C$100 million and site activity expected to run into 2028.

Bethune, near Moose Jaw, is Saskatchewan's first greenfield potash mine in more than 40 years and produces two grades of potassium chloride. The ramp-up is a decade-plus programme of sequential projects rather than a single build, so individual contract awards like this one are the practical measure of its pace.
