Bangladesh has seen at least six deaths and dozens of injuries in violent protests, arson attacks, bomb blasts and shootings reported in several cities since November 14, after former prime minister Sheikh Hasina announced she would return from exile in India in December and the government rejected her request for a new passport and national identity card. Hasina's Awami League called a nationwide strike on Thursday, and authorities deployed 97 platoons of the Border Guard Bangladesh across seven districts, including the Awami League strongholds of Gopalganj and Faridpur.

Police say dozens of crude bomb blasts and vehicle arson attacks have occurred in at least 28 districts over ten days of demonstrations, and the ancestral home of former interim leader Muhammad Yunus in Chattogram was set on fire on Wednesday night, with his family members upstairs escaping unharmed. Grameen Bank, the institution Yunus founded, said at least 37 of its branches and offices have been targeted since November 10, and police detained former education minister Nurul Islam Nahid in Dhaka on Thursday over deaths during the 2024 protests that ended Hasina's rule.
