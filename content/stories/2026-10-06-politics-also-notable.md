**G20 trade ministers reach steel and food consensus in Milwaukee.** USTR Ambassador Jamieson Greer hosted the G20 Trade Ministerial in Milwaukee last week, chairing the Global Forum on Steel Excess Capacity to a "Milwaukee Framework" for joint action against overcapacity. Ministers also reached consensus condemning the weaponization of food through coercive trade actions, and the US, Mexico and Argentina issued a separate statement on eliminating forced labor in global supply chains (status: announced; USTR release, October 5).

**Parliament approves 115 million euro AGILE defence innovation programme.** MEPs voted 536 to 101 to create AGILE, a fast-track defence-innovation instrument for SMEs, start-ups and scale-ups developing emerging and disruptive defence tech, with a four-month time-to-grant and dedicated matchmaking with major defence primes. The legislation needs formal Council endorsement before publication, with operation beginning in early 2027 (status: passed Parliament; Council pending; EP release, October 6).

**Former BND chief August Hanning arrested on espionage and treason suspicions.** Hanning, 80, who led Germany's foreign intelligence service from 1998 to 2005, was detained October 6 on orders of the Federal Public Prosecutor General on suspicion of treasonous espionage, state-secret betrayal and attempted high treason. Investigators allege a former chief of staff, Manfred D., passed him about 2,000 classified documents between 2010 and 2022 in exchange for payment, some used in Hanning's private consulting work; the foreign service allegedly involved has not been named, though WDR/NDR/Süddeutsche Zeitung report it is not Russia (status: reported; Reuters/Bild, DW-reported investigation, October 6).

**Badenoch rules out a deal with Reform UK.** Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch said October 6 she would "rather have another election" than do a deal with Reform UK, closing off post-election pact speculation as the Tories discuss paths back to winning (status: rhetoric; BBC, October 6).

**Can-Am Spyder caught in the tariff war.** The New York Times profiled how the US-Canada tariff fight has ensnared BRP's Can-Am Spyder, a case study in how tariff exposure is hitting cross-border manufactured goods and supply chains (status: reported; NYT, October 6).

**Hoekstra speaks at pre-COP31 leaders' plenary.** Climate Commissioner Wopke Hoekstra delivered remarks at the leaders' plenary of the pre-COP31 meeting (status: announced; EC press release, October 6).

**Commission proposes Standardisation Regulation to speed up EU standards.** The Commission adopted a proposal to accelerate standards development and improve EU competitiveness, with an accompanying Q&A and factsheet (status: proposed; EC press release, October 5).

**Bessent's Iraq meeting and Treasury's Syria banking roundtable.** Treasury's October 2 releases included a readout of Secretary Bessent's meeting with Iraq's Foreign Minister and a readout of a Treasury co-hosted Syrian banking roundtable, the continuing financial-diplomacy track on Iraq sanctions enforcement and Syria's banking reintegration (status: announced; Treasury releases, October 2).
