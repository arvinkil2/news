**Latest.** South Korean content, gaming and travel shares rallied sharply on Thursday, the first trading session after the conclusion of the talks. Studio Dragon, the drama producer behind Crash Landing on You, rose 29.96 percent, CJ ENM, which backed the film Parasite, rose 26 percent, travel agency Hana Tour rose 18 percent and film distributor Showbox gained 9 percent, while gaming names Krafton advanced 2.69 percent and Shift Up 6.57 percent. Analysts cited by CNBC called the agreement a meaningful step towards reopening the Chinese market after nearly a decade of curbs, with Hana Securities estimating Studio Dragon's operating profit could rise by as much as 50 percent if Chinese streaming platforms resume airing Korean dramas it has already sold. The agreement has yet to be formally signed or take effect, and it does not explicitly address a resumption of K-pop concerts, according to NH Investment and Securities cited by CNBC.

South Korea and China declared the substantial conclusion of the services and investment follow-up negotiations under their free trade agreement at a bilateral summit held in Shenzhen on Wednesday on the sidelines of the APEC summit, South Korea's Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy said. Substantial conclusion means the negotiations are closed, with some technical text still to be finished through working-level discussions. The talks were launched in March 2018 as a follow-up to the original agreement, which took effect in December 2015, and reached this point after about eight years and eight months, including a stall of more than four years and five formal rounds this year alone, from the thirteenth round in Beijing in January to the seventeenth in Seoul in October.

On content, China promised most-favoured-nation treatment for Korean film and games, meaning Korean content will not be treated less favourably than third-country content, and agreed to apply a non-discrimination principle to the pre-approval screening that Korean games and films need for distribution in China, including game publishing licences. The ministry said this is the first time China has put either commitment into a trade agreement. Co-production, previously limited to film, will extend to drama, animation, variety programmes and documentaries, and opening will rise in tourism, legal services and construction, with South Korea also promising a higher level of opening than the original agreement in legal services, distribution and audiovisual fields within its domestic law.

On investment, the two sides barred performance requirements that condition investment, including forced technology transfer, widened investor-state dispute settlement protection to investments at the pre-establishment stage, moved market access from the original positive list to a negative list, and added a ratchet clause preventing liberalised rules from being tightened again. Trade Minister Kim Jung-kwan said the conclusion establishes a stable institutional foundation for South Korean companies to expand into the Chinese market in sectors including content, tourism, finance and distribution.

The imbalance the follow-up was meant to address is large. Goods trade between the two countries runs at about 270 billion dollars a year, 272.7 billion dollars in 2025 on the ministry's figures, while services trade is about 38.8 billion dollars; China takes 20.7 percent of South Korea's goods trade but only 12.4 percent of its services trade. The Korea Chamber of Commerce and Industry welcomed the conclusion on Wednesday, saying it expects cooperation to expand from goods into services and investment and new business opportunities to open for Korean companies. The ministry will now hold legal reviews with China to finalise the text, before the formal signing and entry-into-force procedures, so the conclusion is not yet market opening in force.
