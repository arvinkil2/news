The pass-through from the disruption to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz is now showing up in agricultural harvests, International Fund for Agricultural Development President Alvaro Lario told Reuters in Bangkok on Thursday. Disrupted fertilizer shipments and higher fuel and transport costs have led to more cautious planting and reduced fertilizer use, and agricultural commodity prices rose 13 percent in the third quarter, with further increases expected in the fourth quarter and early next year. Countries reliant on imported fertilizer, fuel and food are most exposed, Lario said, pointing to Southeast Asia's fuel dependence and the Gulf region's reliance on food imports.
