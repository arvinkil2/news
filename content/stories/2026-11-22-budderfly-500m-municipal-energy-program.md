Budderfly, a Connecticut based energy as a service company, will make $500 million in project capital available to small and mid sized US municipalities under its Modernizing American Cities program, financing, deploying, owning and operating energy systems that towns repay over time from the savings achieved rather than from capital budgets or new debt.

The program targets municipalities of about 10,000 to 30,000 residents and provides up to $5 million in upfront capital per town. Christian Edwards, Budderfly's vice president of business development and strategy, told Utility Dive the $500 million could cover about 100 clients over the next 12 to 18 months. Contracts run 15 years at a locked in rate based on the customer's historical usage; Budderfly becomes the utility's customer of record, pays the bill and takes a share of the savings, working toward a 30 percent bill savings goal with savings beyond that split evenly with the municipality.

Projects can combine HVAC and lighting upgrades, LED lighting, smart thermostats, solar, battery storage and electric vehicle charging. Schneider Electric supplies the energy management and automation technology, and Budderfly plans to aggregate the sites into a 150 megawatt virtual power plant.

The model has one running example. In Cadillac, Michigan, Budderfly is investing about $4.4 million across seven municipal facilities, including city hall and the wastewater treatment plant, with solar, storage and microgrid capability to keep essential services running during outages, under a 15 year agreement in which the company assumes the performance risk. Budderfly says it has invested more than $378 million in facility upgrades over the past decade across more than 9,000 commercial customer locations.
