December COMEX silver closed at $61.455 on Monday, up $1.48 (+2.5 percent) from Friday's $59.977, and December COMEX copper closed at $6.6415/lb, up 2.3 percent from Friday's $6.492. December COMEX gold closed at $4,171/oz, up about 0.2-0.3 percent from Friday's $4,162.30, fading from higher morning levels. All three settled off their intraday extremes.

The macro setup carried over from the morning: September payrolls of +29k versus roughly 90k expected pushed October Fed-hike pricing to about 18 percent, but the 10-year Treasury at 5.28 percent and the dollar index at 102.25 kept a ceiling on precious metals. Bloomberg ran a video segment Monday asking whether a gold relief rally is on the horizon; no new data in that segment was verified.

The divergence matters. Silver and copper outperforming gold points to an industrial-risk bid alongside haven demand, and to partly technical or ETF-driven flows in silver, for which no fresh positioning data exist today. The next CFTC Commitments of Traders release is Friday Oct 9 (week ended Oct 6); all current positioning reads are stale. Morning-edition macro references (10Y, DXY, hike pricing) were not re-verified at the evening close and should be treated as carryover.
