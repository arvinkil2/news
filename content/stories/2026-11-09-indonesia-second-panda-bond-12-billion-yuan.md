**Latest.** The sale priced on Tuesday, November 10 at 10 billion yuan, up from the inaugural July issue of 7 billion yuan, with total orders of about 17.26 billion yuan, a bid-to-cover ratio of 1.726 times. The three year part took 5.7 billion yuan at a 1.96 percent coupon, the five year 3.7 billion yuan at 2.15 percent and the seven year 600 million yuan at 2.50 percent, all rated AAA with a stable outlook by China Lianhe Credit Rating, with settlement scheduled for November 17. Suminto, the finance ministry's director general of financing and risk management, said the government will maintain its presence in the panda bond market and is considering the instrument for the 2027 state budget, with the timing and size of any next issue to be set by market conditions and the debt management strategy; the bonds complement rather than replace domestic and conventional international issuance.

Indonesia has begun marketing its second yuan-denominated bond in China's domestic debt market, with a target of raising as much as 12 billion yuan (1.8 billion dollars), according to an announcement cited by Bloomberg. The offering would have three, five and seven year parts. The sale proceeds even as Southeast Asia's largest economy grapples with the threat of a double downgrade from MSCI.
