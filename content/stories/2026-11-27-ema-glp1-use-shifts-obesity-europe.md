The European Medicines Agency has published results from a large real-world study showing that the use of GLP-1 medicines in Europe has changed substantially over the past decade. The study covers more than 1.6 million people who started a GLP-1 medicine between 2015 and 2025, drawn from 12 healthcare databases in ten European countries.

Medicines in the class were first authorised for type 2 diabetes. Four are now also authorised for weight management in people living with obesity or overweight with related conditions. From 2022, obesity became the most commonly recorded indication for starting treatment in several of the populations studied, a shift the agency attributes largely to rapid uptake of semaglutide and tirzepatide medicines. Semaglutide medicines were the most used overall, although patterns varied by country and over time.

The study also speaks to the off-label debate around the class. Across most data sources, fewer than 14 percent of new users had no recorded diagnosis of type 2 diabetes or obesity. Median age at treatment start ranged from 52 to 63 years across the databases, and more than half of patients were women in nine of the 12 databases.

Prescribing fluctuated in some countries in line with the shortages EMA has reported since 2022, with semaglutide use showing temporary declines or spikes during supply disruption. The agency said other regulators in the International Coalition of Medicines Regulatory Authorities are using the same DARWIN EU protocol for similar analyses, and that further studies are planned on possible sex-specific differences in adverse reactions and on cardio-metabolic disease burden.
