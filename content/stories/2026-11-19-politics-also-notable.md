**Kahana takes the speaker's chair, 66 to 52.** The Knesset voted late on Tuesday to elect Matan Kahana of Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party as speaker, replacing Amir Ohana of Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud, after a debate and filibuster that ran more than six hours. Likud's candidate Ofir Katz received 52 votes. Kahana was backed by Eisenkot's four-party bloc, which holds 48 seats, together with the two Arab parties, Ra'am and the Joint List, which hold 19 between them. "I will be a Knesset speaker for everyone, irrespective of race, religion or gender," Kahana said on taking the chair.

The vote follows the October 27 election, in which Netanyahu's bloc won 53 of 120 seats and lost its majority, while Eisenkot's bloc took 48. President Isaac Herzog has tasked Eisenkot with forming the next government, and he has up to about five more weeks to secure a coalition; until a government is sworn in, Netanyahu remains prime minister. The speakership matters because the speaker, with the presidium, sets the Knesset's legislative agenda. Eisenkot's bloc has pledged to begin advancing bills to repeal key parts of the outgoing government's judicial overhaul, and Kahana is expected to start that process this week.
