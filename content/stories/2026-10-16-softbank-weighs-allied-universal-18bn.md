SoftBank Group is considering an acquisition of Allied Universal, the US security services provider, in a deal that could value the company at about 18 billion dollars, Bloomberg News reported on Friday, citing people familiar with the matter. Allied Universal supplies security staffing to businesses, venues and major events and has a growing presence in the data centre industry, supporting both the development and the operation of facilities.

The interest fits SoftBank founder Masayoshi Son's deepening focus on AI infrastructure and on AI powered robots across industries: with Allied, SoftBank would look to eventually replace some security staff with robots, though humanoid security workers are likely to take more time, the report said. SoftBank declined to comment and Allied Universal did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

A valuation point the first reports compressed: the 18 billion dollars is the possible equity value of Allied Universal. Including roughly 12 billion dollars of publicly traded debt, in loans and bonds, the total transaction value would be close to 30 billion dollars. The two figures describe the same deal at different levels of the capital structure and should not be reported as rival bids.

Allied is backed by Warburg Pincus and La Caisse, the Canadian pension manager, whose consent any sale would need. SoftBank's American depositary receipts fell about 9.5 to 9.9 percent in New York on October 16 as the report circulated, a market verdict that a labour heavy services business sits awkwardly against the group's AI infrastructure spending, and one person cited in the reporting said the humanoid security technology is not there yet.
