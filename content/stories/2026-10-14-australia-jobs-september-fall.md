Australia's labour market cooled more than expected in September. Employment fell by 39,300 to 14,782,700 in seasonally adjusted terms, the Australian Bureau of Statistics said on Thursday, against a consensus forecast for a gain of about 20,000. The drop was the largest monthly fall since February 2025 and reversed a downwardly revised August gain of 29,300. Part-time employment fell 73,700 to 4,550,200, more than offsetting a 34,400 rise in full-time employment to 10,232,400.

The unemployment rate was 4.7 percent, up 0.1 points on the bureau's seasonally adjusted headline and unchanged once August's upward revision from 4.6 percent is counted. It is the highest level since October 2021 and 0.6 points above where it stood at the start of the year. The number of unemployed rose 8,900 to 732,000, participation fell 0.2 points to 66.8 percent, the employment to population ratio fell 0.3 points to 63.6 percent, and underemployment edged up to 6.3 percent from 6.2 percent. Monthly hours worked rose 0.1 percent despite the fall in headcount.

Markets moved quickly to price out further tightening. Reuters reported that the implied chance of the Reserve Bank of Australia holding the cash rate at 4.6 percent on November 3 rose to nearly 90 percent, from 66 percent before the release, while the chance of a December hike fell to about 32 percent, with another increase no longer fully priced in. The Australian dollar fell 0.3 percent to 0.6953 US dollars in the immediate reaction, and three year government bond futures rallied 11 ticks to 95.19, a five week high, helped by a global rally in Treasuries. The morning market table in Toronto showed the Australian dollar down 0.88 percent at 0.6924.

The Reserve Bank has raised rates four times in 2026 to 4.6 percent, while headline inflation picked up to 4.0 percent in August and underlying inflation held at 3.6 percent, both above the 2 to 3 percent target band. AAP reported that unemployment is already above the 4.5 percent the RBA forecast for the December quarter, and that HSBC's Paul Bloxham judged the labour market to be loosening faster than the bank expected, while warning that the quarterly CPI due later in October will be the main factor in the November decision. The RBA has also urged caution in reading the monthly figures because of Australian Bureau of Statistics methodology changes, a caveat analysts repeated on Thursday.

The composition of the fall is what makes it a rates story rather than a recession signal. Firms cut part-time roles while still adding full-time staff, a pattern Moody's Analytics described as typical of an early hiring downturn, and hours worked still rose slightly. That combination argues for a central bank that can wait: the cooling it sought is arriving through the flexible margin of the labour market first, and the November 3 decision now hinges on whether the quarterly inflation print, not the jobs data, forces its hand.
