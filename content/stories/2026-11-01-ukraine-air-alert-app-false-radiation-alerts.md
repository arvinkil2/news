**Latest, November 2:** The alert hacks now run in both directions. Russia's Ministry of Civil Defence, Emergencies and Disaster Relief said on Monday morning that its warning app had come under a massive cyberattack and had wrongly warned users of imminent drone attacks, and that the alerts spread false information; the ministry named no suspects. Media reports cited by Al Jazeera said the false Russian messages claimed nuclear warheads mounted on Ukrainian drones had been launched on all regions of Russia and told people to stock up urgently on food for three weeks and hide. Ukraine's Center for Strategic Communications blamed Russia for Sunday night's Ukrainian incident, pointing to the use of the Russian language in one of the fake messages. Neither side's attribution is independently corroborated, and Al Jazeera reported this appears to be the first time either country's official alert app has been hacked.

**Latest, November 1:** Ukraine's app is partially back. Its developers said they are gradually bringing it back into operation after the cyberattack: the map again shows the yellow and red danger levels, yellow for drone danger and red for massive drone or missile danger, and push notifications about air alerts are arriving again. The display of targets on the map remains temporarily suspended. The team said it is putting additional protection in place to prevent a repeat and repeated that the app does not store or use personal data, so there is no threat to users on that account. The partial restoration follows the team's confirmation of the hack and its suspension of the app's operation earlier in the night.

Ukraine's Air Alert app, the air raid warning application supported by the Ministry of Digital Transformation, sent a rapid series of false alerts on Sunday evening, beginning with a radiation threat declared in Kyiv at about 22:47 local time, followed within two minutes by messages in Russian and Ukrainian warning of a nuclear alert, a chemical attack on Kyiv and street fighting in the capital, each then cancelled by individual all-clear messages. The Ukrainian Air Force said on Telegram that the app had probably been subjected to hostile interference, stated there was no radiation threat and urged people to stay calm, while the Vinnytsia regional governor, Natalya Zabolotna, called it a cyberattack and warned users not to click a link in a message. A Ukrainian intelligence body said a radiation hazard signal had also been played over loudspeaker devices in the Lviv region and that there was no radiation hazard, and no official statement attributed the interference to a named attacker.
