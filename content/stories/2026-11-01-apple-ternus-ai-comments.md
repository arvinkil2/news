Apple's new chief executive has given his first clear statement of the company's AI doctrine, two days before its earnings. John Ternus, the longtime hardware executive who became chief executive at the beginning of September, told Wired's Steven Levy that he likes where Apple is at on AI, that the company is absolutely going to leverage AI in its new products, and that it does not want to ship AI for AI's sake. When AI is done right, he said, people will not have to think about whether it was AI or traditional software that made something happen; what matters is whether Apple built something people love.

The context is a two year narrative that Apple is behind. Its more powerful Siri, delayed repeatedly, finally launched this summer, and the company has spent the AI capex race largely on the sidelines while Microsoft, Google, Amazon and Meta committed sums that this week drew a market penalty. Ternus acknowledged that everyone has an opinion on Apple's position and insisted the internal view is comfortable. Pressed on whether agents could make apps unnecessary and reshape Apple's ecosystem, he answered that it could make life really easy, but that creating products is a deeply human and personal thing the company loves to do.

The remarks land at a pointed moment. Apple reports fourth quarter results on Monday, November 2, into a market that has just spent a week repricing the cost of AI ambition at its rivals. Ternus is signalling that Apple will not answer with a spending number of its own: its defence is the integrated product, with AI invisible inside it. Whether investors accept that framing, with the stock down 1.23 percent on Friday at 344.63 dollars while the sector fell harder, is the first test of his tenure's story.
