**US: Trump says he may fire Guilfoyle.** President Trump told reporters on Monday that he was considering firing US Ambassador to Greece Kimberly Guilfoyle, saying his administration was looking into the allegations about her and was not happy, Bloomberg and MyJoyOnline reported, in reports opened and read in full. Earlier Monday, Senate Majority Whip John Barrasso called reports about Guilfoyle's finances "very troubling," The Hill reported, a rare public wobble from the Senate GOP leadership toward a Trump appointee. The underlying reports about her finances were not readable from here (The Hill is blocked for direct fetch), so treat that part as a headline-level development.

**Finance/diplomacy: bankers gather in Hong Kong before Trump-Xi summit.** Wall Street and global bank chiefs are convening in Hong Kong ahead of a Trump-Xi summit, Bloomberg reported Monday, a scheduling signal that a leader-level US-China meeting is taking concrete shape. The summit itself has no announced date in the reporting seen; treat as single-sourced.

**Nigeria 2027: the campaign machinery churns.** APC chairman Nentawe Yilwatda said the party's internal polls show President Tinubu will win the 2027 presidential election, per Vanguard and Premium Times. Meanwhile the Socio-Economic Rights and Accountability Project sued the Central Bank of Nigeria over its failure to account for $6.23 million in "missing" election funds and 1.63 trillion naira in other public funds, Vanguard reported Sunday. In Oyo state, Governor Seyi Makinde's chief of staff resigned months before the general election, and Makinde said Tinubu chose Adebayo Adelabu as minister over his own nominee, exposing intra-party friction.

**Zambia: the election petition died on procedure.** Zambia defeated the challenge to its 2026 general election without ever arguing the merits of the case, allAfrica reported Monday, a procedural ruling that leaves the opposition's rejection of President Hichilema's "free and fair" claim legally unanswered but politically alive.

**EU: methane reporting templates go to consultation.** The European Commission's DG Energy launched a call for feedback on methane emissions reporting templates on Monday, a primary publication. The consultation sits under the EU's methane regulation and will shape compliance costs for gas importers and domestic operators.

**Sahel: displacement nears four million.** The UN is alarmed as the number of displaced people in the Central Sahel reaches nearly four million, allAfrica reported, underscoring the humanitarian spillover of the region's insurgencies.

**Ghana: Holy See agreement heads to Parliament.** A Ghana-Holy See agreement went to Parliament after cabinet approval, MyJoyOnline reported Sunday, the next step in formalising the bilateral framework.

**Korea/US: Seoul demands commercial viability for Alaska LNG.** Korean officials told Alaska state legislators that any Korean investment in Alaska LNG must be commercially viable, Yonhap reported Sunday (in Korean). The condition puts a market test on one of Washington's flagship energy-export pitches to Seoul.
