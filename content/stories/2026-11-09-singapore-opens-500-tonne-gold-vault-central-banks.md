The Monetary Authority of Singapore rolled out its gold vaulting service on Tuesday, offering foreign central banks, sovereign entities and multilateral organisations storage in the national currency vault, which can hold up to 500 tonnes with room to expand. MAS chairman Gan Kim Yong, who is also deputy prime minister, said Singapore does not aim to replace other gold centres and is offering an additional option as demand for gold as a reserve asset grows. Alongside the launch, MAS and the finance ministry introduced an enhanced GST framework under which services between institutional participants, including vaulting, refining, recasting, assaying and broking, will not be charged GST from April 2027, and MAS will offer gold custody accounts to select Singapore based bullion banks.

Singapore's over the counter gold clearing system, being prepared by the Singapore Exchange with six banks, is expected to be ready by the end of this year, with live trading and market operations scheduled to begin in the first quarter of 2027.
