## Latest

Iranian figures have gone further than denial, claiming the attack was a false-flag operation by Ukraine. Mohammad Marandi, a former adviser to Iran's nuclear negotiating team, said on X that the attack was likely carried out by Ukraine to demonstrate its value to President Donald Trump, and the IRGC-affiliated Fars News Agency cited a security source claiming Ukrainian operatives had deployed drones across the Middle East. Iran's embassy in Rome warned against false-flag operations aimed at drawing Europe into the Gulf conflict. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi told his Italian and Kuwaiti counterparts the incident looked suspicious and offered Iranian cooperation in an investigation. These claims are uncorroborated; Ukraine has publicly deployed drone specialists to Gulf states to help defend against Iranian attacks, and no evidence linking Ukraine to this strike has been presented.

Iran has denied carrying out the attack. Abolfazl Shekarchi, spokesperson for Iran's General Staff, rejected the accusations categorically, saying Iranian armed forces conducted no military operation in the region, according to Prensa Latina citing Iranian state television. He said the accusations were meant to cover up attempts to generate regional insecurity.

Saudi Arabia led Arab condemnation of the attack, calling on Iran to halt its attacks immediately and holding Tehran responsible for the consequences of continued escalation. The United Arab Emirates, Bahrain, Qatar and Jordan also condemned the strike as a violation of Kuwait's sovereignty, and Gulf Cooperation Council Secretary-General Jasem Al-Budaiwi called it a dangerous escalation and reaffirmed Kuwait's right to defend itself. Bahrain urged the UN Security Council to hold Iran accountable and to secure reparations.

Two Italians have been killed in an Iranian drone attack in Kuwait, a source close to the matter told Reuters on Thursday, adding that the victims were technicians for the German defence group Rheinmetall. Italy's ANSA news agency reported that the two men were among the four injured people Kuwait's military had announced earlier in the day. Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani said drones from Iran struck Kuwaiti military sites, among them an air defence radar station where the two Rheinmetall Italia technicians were working, and that an explosion killed both men.

Kuwait's defense ministry said at least four people were injured on Thursday after Iranian drones targeting a vital facility were intercepted. In the ministry's account, falling shrapnel from the interceptions injured four people, who were taken to hospital, and the incident caused unspecified material damage. The statement did not name the facility. Kuwait's Defense Ministry later said the falling debris killed the two Italian technicians at an air defence surveillance station and injured a Kuwaiti and a Pakistani national.
