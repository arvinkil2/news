A Suffolk County jury on Friday awarded 6 million dollars in damages to David Sabatini, the former MIT professor and Whitehead Institute scientist whose career imploded after a younger colleague accused him of inappropriate sexual conduct in his Cambridge research lab. The jury directed Kristin Knouse, who had brought her own suit against Sabatini, to pay the 6 million dollars plus interest, finding that she had defamed him and interfered with his ability to make a living.

The jury of seven women and six men rejected the account at the centre of Knouse's claim, that Sabatini had subjected her to a coercive relationship and fostered a hostile and sexualized workplace environment in his lab at the Whitehead Institute. STAT, carrying reporting by Boston Globe journalists, called the verdict a stark rejection of a high profile MeToo claim, and the New York Times independently reported the same 6 million dollar outcome.

The scope of the verdict needs care. A civil jury finding on defamation and interference is a finding about what Knouse said and did and what it cost Sabatini. It is not a court reversal of the Whitehead Institute's own investigation or of MIT's separate policy findings, and neither institution was on trial in this case.

For the research sector the case remains a governance landmark either way. It began with an institutional investigation that ended a star scientist's tenure, drew in competing accounts of lab culture that the trial aired in detail, and now ends, at least at trial level, with the accuser ordered to pay the accused. Universities and institutes rewriting misconduct procedures will read the verdict as a warning about process in both directions.
