China's National Medical Products Administration has accepted for review HUTCHMED's New Drug Application for the combination of Orpathys (savolitinib) and osimertinib, AstraZeneca's Tagrisso, in previously untreated, locally advanced or metastatic non-small cell lung cancer patients whose tumours carry EGFR-sensitizing mutations and MET overexpression. HUTCHMED announced the acceptance on November 26 in a release furnished to the SEC on Form 6-K.

The application rests on SANOVO, a blinded, randomized Phase 3 trial in 326 treatment-naive patients in China. In the intention-to-treat population, the combination delivered median progression-free survival of 24.9 months against 13.8 months for osimertinib alone, a hazard ratio of 0.56 that amounts to a 44 percent reduction in the risk of disease progression or death. Overall survival also favoured the combination, with a hazard ratio of 0.48 and median survival of 44.5 months against 34.9 months. The company said the safety profile was consistent with the known profiles of each medicine, with no new safety concerns.

The combination is already approved in China for a later treatment setting, patients with MET amplification whose disease progressed on an EGFR inhibitor, based on the SACHI Phase 3 trial. The new application would move dual EGFR and MET blockade to the start of treatment for the MET-overexpression subgroup, replacing osimertinib alone as the first therapy those patients receive.

Orpathys is jointly developed by HUTCHMED and AstraZeneca and commercialized by AstraZeneca. Regulatory processes for the combination after prior EGFR therapy are also underway in the United States, Europe and Japan, supported by the global Phase 3 SAFFRON trial. HUTCHMED did not give a target date for the NMPA decision.
