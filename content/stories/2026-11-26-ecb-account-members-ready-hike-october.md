A large number of European Central Bank Governing Council members could have supported raising interest rates at the October 28 to 29 meeting, the account published on Thursday shows, because incoming data since September had confirmed the case for further tightening and a hike then would simply have frontloaded tightening they judged necessary. All members still agreed to hold the three key rates unchanged, with the deposit facility rate at 2.50 percent, on the condition that communication conveyed that another hike would likely be necessary unless the inflation outlook improved significantly, and that the pause did not reflect diminished concern.

The account records headline inflation rising to 3.8 percent in September from 3.2 percent in August, energy inflation at 18.9 percent, and market inflation fixings peaking at 4.5 percent for January 2027, with no material signs yet of second-round effects and longer-term expectations still around 2 percent. Members who favoured waiting pointed to the December Eurosystem staff projections, wage negotiation outcomes and two further inflation releases as the evidence that would settle the timing, and Reuters reported that most policymakers have since signalled a 25 basis point hike on December 17.
