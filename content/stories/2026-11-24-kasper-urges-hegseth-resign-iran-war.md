Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth's former chief of staff has called on him to resign over his handling of the Pentagon and the war with Iran. "Pete's ability to be effective has been constrained and very soon he'll have lost any ability to be effective," Joe Kasper, who worked as Hegseth's top aide for the first three months of the Trump administration, told Politico in an hourlong interview. "For that reason and others it's time for Pete to call it quits."

Kasper, an Air Force veteran, criticised Hegseth for firing top military leaders, including the Army's top general, Gen. Randy George, and two other senior officers nine months ago, and Maj. Gen. Brett Sylvia, the Pentagon's top military planner, who was fired in October over alleged leaks about the Iran war and the Russia Ukraine war. Kasper said there was no evidence that officials put on administrative leave last year and subsequently fired, among them senior adviser Dan Caldwell, deputy chief of staff Darin Selnick and Colin Carroll, had leaked information to the media. He called Hegseth's handling of the Iran war a "s--- show" and said Hegseth was "all about Pete rather than the institution and the men and women who support it."

He also accused Hegseth of failing to work with Congress, warning that this might make it difficult for the Pentagon to get adequate funding at a time when Hegseth and the Trump administration have called for a 1.5 trillion dollar defense budget, which would be the highest ever. "He's not just burned bridges up on the Hill," Kasper said. "He's napalmed them. He's going to be suffocated, and it's not like it's going to be a slow suffocation. It's going to be a quick asphyxiation."

A senior Pentagon official told Politico it was not surprising that a "disgruntled former staffer, someone who could barely survive ninety days in the building, is now peddling made up gossip to the media." The official added: "Secretary Hegseth is laser focused on restoring lethality to the force and supporting our war fighters, while Mr. Kasper is more focused on rewriting history to cover for his own failed tenure."

Hegseth has faced mounting criticism in recent weeks, including over lethal strikes on purported drug boats in Latin America carried out without congressional approval, his communications mishap on the Signal app, and a classified Pentagon memo restricting routine professional and social engagements between US service members and Canadian troops. He survived Rep. Thomas Massie's impeachment resolution after the House voted 211 to 204 on November 10 to refer it to the Judiciary Committee; the resolution accused Hegseth of dismantling Pentagon civilian harm mitigation offices ahead of a deadly bombing at an Iranian elementary school. Hegseth has acknowledged the threat of impeachment in a social media video, arguing it would be "all about the politics of personal destruction" and "won't work with me." President Donald Trump has remained supportive of Hegseth. Status: reported (a resignation call by a former aide; no change of office has been announced).
