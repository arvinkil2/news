Spanish infrastructure group ACS is considering strategic options for Hochtief, its listed German subsidiary, including acquiring the shares it does not already own or merging the two companies, according to people familiar with the matter cited by Bloomberg on November 6. As part of any deal, ACS would then consider a listing in New York of Turner Construction, Hochtief's United States building arm.

Neither ACS nor Hochtief has confirmed that any of the options will be pursued, and no terms, timetable or valuation have been disclosed. Hochtief shares rose sharply on the report, reversing earlier losses.

The deliberations surface alongside strong trading at the German group. Hochtief reported operating net profit of 751.6 million euros for the first nine months of 2026, up 39.8 percent year on year, which sharpens the question of what the minority stake is worth under each route: a takeover turns on the price offered to remaining shareholders, a merger on the exchange ratio between the two listed groups, and a Turner listing on whether the United States business commands a higher value on its own than inside the group.

The next fixed point is ACS's capital markets day on November 12, where investors will look for any statement on the Hochtief stake and the group's strategic priorities.
