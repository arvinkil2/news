Botswana will take a 5 percent stake, free of charge, in mines developed on prospecting concessions held by its newly formed state exploration company, Minerals and Energy Minister Bogolo Kenewendo said in an interview in Gaborone reported by Bloomberg on November 25. The free-carry stakes will be transferred to the state by Exploration Investment Company Botswana, the exploration vehicle set up in February, which is promoting exploration for copper, gold, platinum, chrome, iron ore and nickel.

The 5 percent comes in addition to the state's existing right to purchase up to 24 percent of a project when a mining licence is issued. Kenewendo said the government cannot always afford to buy. "Government can express interest by paying for 24 percent, but I think, and fortunately the president agrees, that the government of Botswana shouldn't always be put in a corner to buy, especially in these tightened fiscal situations," she said. "We can have that 5 percent free carry in our heads as a minimum and reserve the right to negotiate more."

The move extends a regional pattern of host states converting fiscal weakness into equity participation rather than higher royalties: the state takes a carried interest it does not have to fund, and investors price a larger government share of future mines into new exploration decisions.
