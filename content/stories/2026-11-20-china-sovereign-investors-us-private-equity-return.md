Chinese state-backed investors are exploring a return to US private equity, with China Investment Corporation pressing fund managers to build sanction safeguards into new allocations, including cash-out mechanisms backed by promissory notes, sidecar vehicles and excuse provisions that would let a sovereign investor exit without forcing a manager to sell illiquid assets. The channel shut for new commitments at the peak of last year's US-China trade tensions, when Beijing pressure led state-backed investors to pull back, after years in which CIC and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange backed groups including Global Infrastructure Partners, Thoma Bravo, Vista Equity Partners, Carlyle and Blackstone. Fund advisers say President Trump's state visit to China this year has improved CIC's willingness to invest again, but no commitments have been announced and fresh disputes, including over US semiconductor sales to China, could still delay a broader return.
