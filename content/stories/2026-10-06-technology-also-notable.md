TeraWulf's Kentucky campus is on track to reach 1 gigawatt in 2029, the company said, another gigawatt-scale node in the AI buildout's geography of cheap power (Datacenter Dynamics, October 6). Nscale hired Meta executive Justin Osofsky as chief operating officer, a talent move that says the AI-cloud builders are professionalizing for the next phase of growth (Datacenter Dynamics, October 6). The same logic runs through the day's smaller buildout items: Axe Compute took full ownership of an AI cluster in Columbus, Georgia, and forum Markets is partnering with Edge Node on a US compute rollout (Datacenter Dynamics, October 6).

Quantum hardware keeps collecting university partnerships as commercial evidence thins out: D-Wave is working with the University of Arkansas on supply-chain quantum applications, and Quantinuum partnered with the University of Western Australia to widen quantum computing access (Datacenter Dynamics, October 6). Japan's Rapidus is standing up a CORE initiative to support semiconductor design and manufacturing, a reminder that the 2-nanometer race is also a domestic-industrial-policy race (Datacenter Dynamics, October 6). On the cooling front, HF Sinclair launched a fluid line for direct-to-chip environments, chasing the thermal market the AI buildout created (Datacenter Dynamics, October 6).

Uber is spending $2.3 billion to get into catering, its latest push to turn the delivery network into something bigger than rides and food (TechCrunch, October 6). Anthropic is giving startups a free year of Claude Team plus $1,000 in token credits, a distribution play that buys future enterprise seats at the price of free compute today (TechCrunch, October 6). Both are land-grab moves, and both assume the customer acquisition math still works at this scale.

The security desk was busy. The FBI confirmed multiple arrests connected to the ShinyHunters hacking campaign, the long-running data-extortion operation that has rattled corporate targets (The Register, October 5). Atlassian warned of a critical file-access flaw in its datacenter products, and Citrix NetScaler 0-days drew more bad reports (The Register, October 6 and 5). Debian's latest kernel security update shipped with 1,313 reasons to patch, a security researcher claims to have found a KVM guest-host escape, the Asos app shipped a data-leak threat instead of fast fashion, Denmark's ID register spilled more people's details than the country has residents, and school-software provider Bromcom was bitten by a legacy sign-on service (The Register, October 5-6). Separately, a VMware survey found licensing costs are driving 90 percent of users to explore alternatives, the clearest market signal yet that Broadcom's pricing has a real churn problem (Ars Technica, October 6).

On the consumer side: the Emmys move from broadcast TV to Prime Video in 2027, another flagship property leaving linear television (TechCrunch, October 6). TikTok rolled out an AI shopping assistant with one-click checkout, Pinterest turned beauty pins into AI action plans, and Bluesky is offering users their own domains on the open web (TechCrunch, October 6 and 5). MIT Technology Review published its 2026 list of 10 climate tech companies to watch, spanning batteries, nuclear and cement (October 6). And Telus with AST SpaceMobile completed a direct-to-device test in Canada, a satellite-connectivity datapoint for the same networks the Open Cosmos raise is aimed at (Datacenter Dynamics, October 6).
