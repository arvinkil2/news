Latest: China's Ministry of Finance began selling the yuan-denominated green sovereign bonds in Canada on Monday, November 9, according to Bloomberg, with initial price guidance of around 1.8 percent for the three-year notes and around 1.9 percent for the five-year notes. The notes may be priced later on Monday, and Royal Bank of Canada is a joint bookrunner on the sale, Bloomberg reported, citing people familiar with the matter. The report gave no total size for the offering; the ministry's November 5 announcement capped the Canada issuance at 4 billion yuan.

Earlier: China's Ministry of Finance announced on November 5 that, with State Council approval, it will issue renminbi green sovereign bonds in Canada in the week of November 9, 2026, in a size not exceeding 4 billion yuan, with the detailed issuance arrangements to be announced before the sale. Reuters put the size at up to 4 billion yuan, about $599.76 million. The sale would be China's first yuan sovereign issuance in Canada, adding a North American venue to a green sovereign programme previously placed in European markets.

China is also considering selling sovereign bonds denominated in dollars and in offshore yuan as early as this month, according to people familiar with the matter cited by Bloomberg, as Beijing seeks to deepen its presence in international debt markets. Officials at the Ministry of Finance have been working with investment banks on the possible sales, the people said.

The discussions are ongoing and the details could still change, and the report gave no size for either sale.
