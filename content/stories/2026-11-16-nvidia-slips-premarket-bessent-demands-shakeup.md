**Latest.** President Donald Trump told reporters on Monday, before leaving for an Asia trip, that he "would not mind" if Jensen Huang returned as Nvidia's chief executive, while repeating that he would support former Apple chief executive Tim Cook as Huang's successor. Trump said Huang had "learned a big lesson," said he did not know the woman at the centre of the scandal was a suspected spy, and said national security "was not put at risk." Nvidia reports fiscal third quarter results on Tuesday, November 17.

Nvidia was down about 2.7 percent in premarket trading on Monday as of 8:07 AM ET, CNBC reported, extending a slide that erased hundreds of billions of dollars in market value last week after the board announced on Thursday that Jensen Huang had resigned as chief executive. Longtime finance chief Colette Kress is interim chief executive while the board considers internal and external candidates for the permanent role.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on Sunday that Nvidia needs a dramatic shakeup, that the board should look outside the company for its next chief executive, and that other executives should face scrutiny over compliance with US export controls. An online petition calling for Huang's reinstatement had passed roughly 180,000 signatures by Monday morning, with Elon Musk saying he had signed it, and US lawmakers are pushing for comprehensive investigations into the scandal.
