Extreme weather events are posing significant risks to international oil and natural gas shipping, with the potential to damage critical infrastructure, disrupt major trade routes and threaten energy security, according to a new IEA report published on Wednesday. The agency said that in 2024 around 40 percent of global crude oil supply, 30 percent of petroleum products and 15 percent of natural gas supply travelled by water for at least part of their journey, and that international fuel shipping is projected to increase by more than 15 percent by 2035 under its Stated Policies Scenario, with LNG shipping growing particularly strongly.

Ports handling oil and gas tankers face hazards including tropical cyclones, flooding and extreme heat, with virtually all tanker ports in Japan, Korea and China exposed to a tropical cyclone at least once in 500 years on average and more than 100 ports in Europe, 24 percent, facing river or coastal flooding, the report found. Tanker ports average around 1.5 days of downtime a year from extreme weather, though major events can interrupt operations for weeks or months, and disruptions at chokepoints such as the Luzon Strait and the Turkish Straits can force longer routes or halt flows where alternatives are unavailable. The IEA called for weather risks to be integrated into national energy security planning, alongside diversified supply chains, reinforced port infrastructure and better early warning systems.
