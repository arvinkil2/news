The Moscow District Arbitration Court, sitting in cassation, upheld the lower courts' decisions ordering Euroclear to pay 18.2 trillion rubles to the Bank of Russia and rejected the depository's complaint, according to the court file reported by Interfax. The cassation court had refused on September 30 to suspend enforcement, an appeals court rejected Euroclear's challenge on July 16, and the first-instance court granted the central bank's claim in full on May 15, with immediate execution ordered on May 26.

The Bank of Russia sued Euroclear in Moscow on December 12, 2025, putting its losses across all asset types at €200.1 billion (18.173 trillion rubles at the official rate on December 1, 2025) and tying the claim to the freezing of its reserves and to European Commission plans to immobilise the funds indefinitely and use the assets for transfer to third parties. Roughly $300 billion of Russian reserves were frozen by the EU, the US and other countries in spring 2022.
