Treasury yields drifted lower again on Tuesday, extending the previous week's rally rather than reversing it. In the latest market snapshot, the 5-year yield stood at 4.71 percent, down 1.6 basis points on the day and 18.8 basis points on the week. The 10-year was at 4.90 percent, down 1.4 basis points on the day and 19.9 on the week, and the 30-year was at 5.29 percent, down 0.2 basis points on the day and 16.6 on the week.

The moves were small compared with Friday's sharp fall, and their shape mattered more than their size. The belly of the curve led again, as it did on Monday, which keeps the move reading as a growth and policy repricing concentrated in intermediate maturities rather than a term premium event at the long end, where the 30-year has barely budged for two sessions.

What decides whether the rally holds is now a data question. The Bureau of Economic Analysis publishes the second estimate of third-quarter GDP and the October personal income and outlays report, including the PCE price index, at 08:30 EST today. Those are the last tier-one US prints before the Federal Open Market Committee meets on December 8 to 9, and a yield curve that has already moved 20 basis points in a week has less room to absorb a hot inflation reading without giving some of it back.
