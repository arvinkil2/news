Mexican authorities captured Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, known as El Chapo Isidro, in Guasave, Sinaloa overnight, the Associated Press reported, saying the government used stealth and surprise, set a perimeter around the building where he was located, and entered a home to arrest him after its forces were attacked with guns and explosives. Meza Flores was one of the FBI's 10 most wanted fugitives and a top leader of the Beltran Leyva criminal organisation, wanted for trafficking drugs to the United States and illegally carrying firearms, and Mexico's government said in an early Saturday report that intelligence from the Trump administration helped authorities capture him, without giving details of the coordination.

The capture set off a burst of violence in Guasave, where videos and photos showed civilians fleeing gunfire at a baseball game and cars and gas stations burning. Security Secretary Omar Garcia Harfuch wrote on X on Saturday morning that armed forces would remain deployed in Sinaloa to protect civilians, calling the detentions an important step in pacifying the state. The takedown comes as President Claudia Sheinbaum faces pressure from President Donald Trump to crack down on cartels, and follows the February killing of El Mencho, Mexico's most powerful cartel leader, which set off similar violence.
