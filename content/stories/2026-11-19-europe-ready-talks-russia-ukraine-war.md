European leaders at the Berlin summit of the Coalition of the Willing, Ukraine's allies, said on Thursday that they were ready for negotiations with Russia on ending the war. British prime minister Andy Burnham said Europe had a clear, consistent and united negotiating position, and German chancellor Friedrich Merz said Europe was willing to enter serious discussions on European security with Russia, provided a peace agreement with Ukraine came first and the conditions were acceptable to Europe and Ukraine. The United States envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff attended the summit, and Kushner said Moscow was now comfortable with Ukraine receiving security guarantees modelled on the collective defence idea in NATO's Article Five.

Putin, speaking at a press conference after the Shenzhen trilateral with Donald Trump and Xi Jinping, said Russia was not against European participation and had invited the Europeans to Moscow through diplomatic channels to determine their positions, after which Russia would decide in what format to continue the dialogue with Ukraine. No date was given for the Moscow visit and no format for talks was announced. President Volodymyr Zelensky, who attended the Berlin summit, said Putin had always said no. The statements came on a day when drones struck several buildings in Kyiv's historic centre, killing one person in a residential building in the Podilskyi neighbourhood and damaging the Dutch and Austrian embassies.
