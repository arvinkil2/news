Meta is reconsidering the deadline for its climate pledge. In its annual sustainability report, published on Thursday, the Facebook and Instagram owner said it is re-evaluating its timelines, sequencing and interim milestones, including the 2030 timeframe for its net zero emissions goal, according to Al Jazeera reporting with AFP. The statement, attributed to Rachel Peterson, Meta's vice president of data centres, and Blair Swedeen, its global head of net zero and sustainability, said the company had set the goal expecting steady growth and falling clean energy costs, and that the reality had been far more complex. Meta has not abandoned the net zero goal itself.

The pressure is the AI buildout. Meta reported that its net carbon-equivalent emissions rose 45 percent in 2025 to 11.8 million tonnes, and the executives said delivering the company's vision requires building the infrastructure that powers AI. The 2030 goal, set in 2020, covers emissions across Meta's entire value chain, including purchased goods and services and capital goods such as equipment and buildings. AFP noted the wider context: the International Energy Agency estimates data centres used about 415 terawatt hours of electricity globally in 2024, about 1.5 percent of supply, a figure that could nearly double by 2030.
