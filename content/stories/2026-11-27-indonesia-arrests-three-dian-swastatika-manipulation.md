Indonesian police have arrested three people over alleged manipulation of shares in PT Dian Swastatika Sentosa, a coal mining and energy company whose trading prompted MSCI to propose a two-step downgrade of Indonesia's market status. Suhasto, the National Police director of special economic crimes, said in a televised briefing that 11 parties used offshore securities accounts to inflate the stock's trading volume, at one point executing roughly 5,800 transactions in less than a minute, and gained as much as 10 trillion rupiah, about $544 million, as the share price rose about 210 percent over 34 trading days in 2024.

One suspect, identified only by the initial R, was arrested on Friday and is accused of using overseas accounts to stabilise trading volume; two others already in custody are accused of maintaining volume domestically. Police said they had questioned 61 witnesses and searched two locations, in an investigation that followed a report filed by the Financial Services Authority on November 5. Dian Swastatika entered MSCI indexes in August 2025, MSCI cut its effective weighting within days over free float concerns, and on October 15 MSCI opened a formal consultation on reclassifying Indonesia as a standalone market.
