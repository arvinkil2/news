Landmark laws to end the leasehold system in England and Wales enter Parliament this week. The Commonhold and Leasehold Reform Bill caps ground rents at 250 pounds a year in 2028, reducing them to zero after 40 years, a change the government says cuts bills for up to one million leaseholders, and it bans leasehold for most new flats and homes, making a modernised commonhold tenure the standard instead.

The bill also lowers the threshold for existing blocks to convert to commonhold from unanimous agreement to 50 percent support, scraps marriage value on lease extensions, allows 990 year extensions, and ends powers that put a home at risk over relatively small unpaid charges, replacing them with a court led process. It further protects up to 1.75 million freehold homes on managed estates from enforcement over unpaid maintenance fees.
