Ontario plans to overhaul mining approvals beyond its fast track program for four major projects, proposing to eliminate or streamline half of the key permits and authorizations required to develop mines. Energy and Mines Minister Stephen Lecce said Tuesday the government would introduce legislation next week to accelerate exploration and approvals for new and expanding mines, while introducing tougher enforcement measures and new rules aimed at preventing companies from indefinitely holding idle mining leases.

The changes would cover new and expanding mines, as well as early and advanced exploration. Companies would be able to obtain a single exploration permit instead of several, while the validity of early exploration plans would be extended to three years from two. The reforms build on Ontario's One Project, One Process framework, launched in October 2025 to halve government review times for major mining developments. Four projects have been designated so far: Frontier Lithium's PAK lithium project, Kinross Gold's Great Bear gold project, Canada Nickel's Crawford nickel project and Mayfair Gold's Fenn-Gib gold project.

Lecce also announced a use it or lose it policy intended to prevent companies from indefinitely holding mining leases without advancing exploration or development. Only 15 percent of Ontario's land with high mineral potential is currently available for new staking, he said, and fewer than half of Ontario's mining leases have exploration plans or permits. Under the proposed changes, idle leases would not be renewed, allowing other companies to stake the land. Lecce did not specify how idle leases would be defined or whether exemptions would apply.

The legislation would also consolidate oversight of mine tailings storage facilities under the Ministry of Energy and Mines, and introduce a graduated enforcement system including field orders, tickets and administrative monetary penalties. Lecce stopped short of committing Ontario to minimum price guarantees for critical minerals, pointing instead to Ottawa's work with G7 partners on a buyers' club. He said Ontario plans to release a new minerals strategy in March 2027, and that further investments under the province's existing $500 million Critical Minerals Processing Fund will be announced in the coming weeks and months.
