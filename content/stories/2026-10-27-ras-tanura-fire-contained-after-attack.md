**Latest, October 28:** Yemen's Houthis said on Tuesday that they had attacked the Ras Tanura refinery with a ballistic missile, a claim reported by gCaptain on Tuesday and carried by CNBC on Wednesday at the top of its oil market report. CNBC stated no damage and no casualties from the attack, and no Saudi statement on a missile strike on the refinery appeared in the reporting read. Oil rose on Wednesday, with Brent crude futures up 1.50 dollars, or 1.32 percent, to 115.59 dollars a barrel and West Texas Intermediate up 1.47 dollars, or 1.47 percent, to 101.37 dollars, CNBC reported.

Saudi air defences intercepted two drones near the Ras Tanura oil facility in the Eastern Province, the defence ministry said in a statement carried by the Saudi Press Agency, and debris from the interceptions sparked a fire at a parked vehicle outside the facility perimeter. The fire was contained, there were no injuries, the facility itself was not damaged and petroleum supplies were not disrupted, the statement said, as reported by Reuters.

Bloomberg had earlier reported a fire near the hub contained following an attack. Ras Tanura, on the kingdom's Gulf coast, is one of Saudi Arabia's main oil export and refining centres, and it has been targeted before in the current regional war.
