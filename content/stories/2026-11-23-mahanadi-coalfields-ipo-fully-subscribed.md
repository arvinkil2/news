The initial public offering of Mahanadi Coalfields, a unit of state-owned Coal India, was fully subscribed on the third and final day of bidding on November 23, led by large institutional investors, exchange data showed. The sale sought a valuation of about $9.6 billion for a company that accounts for roughly a fifth of India's coal production.

The offer was entirely an offer for sale: Coal India planned to sell about 662 million shares, a 10 percent stake, and is expected to raise about $963 million at the upper end of the price band of 129 to 136 rupees a share. Anchor investors took a portion before the public offer, leaving about 493 million shares for other investors, and bids reached 736.3 million shares by mid-afternoon on the final day.

Qualified institutional buyers bid nearly five times their portion, while non-institutional investors subscribed 0.65 times and retail investors 0.43 times their portions, a split that shows the deal cleared on institutional rather than household demand. Overall subscription was about 1.47 times.

Mahanadi is the third Coal India unit to go public this year as the government pursues its divestment programme. The company itself receives no proceeds from the sale, so the listing changes its shareholder register and disclosure obligations rather than its balance sheet.
