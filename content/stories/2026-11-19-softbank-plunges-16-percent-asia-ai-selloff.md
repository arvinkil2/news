**Latest.** The regional index has now fallen far enough to set weekly records. The MSCI Asia-Pacific ex-Japan index tumbled 3.5 percent on Friday to its weakest level since early April and is set to lose about 8.6 percent this week, its worst weekly performance since the Covid crisis of early 2020, Investing.com reported. Hong Kong's Hang Seng fell 2.1 percent on Friday and is set to enter a bear market, down more than 20 percent from its January high. Taiwan's Weighted Index fell 5.6 percent to a seven-month low and South Korea's Kospi fell 4.4 percent to a nine-month low, while China's CSI 300 lost 2.2 percent and Singapore's Straits Times Index fell 1.8 percent. Alibaba was the one large name to rise, up more than 2 percent in Hong Kong on stronger than expected September quarter revenue, against a 4.6 percent fall in Baidu. The report also tied part of the pressure in Tokyo to Japan's October consumer price data, which kept inflation above the Bank of Japan's 2 percent target and added to expectations of a December rate increase; that release is covered separately on this site.

The Friday selloff was steepest in Taipei, where the Taiex plunged nearly 6 percent in morning trading, Al Jazeera reported, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng Index fell as much as 2 percent. Rajat Agarwal, an Asia equity strategist at Societe Generale, told the outlet the selling reflected concern that AI-led growth is slowing and that foreigners were not buying, leaving it unclear how long domestic investors would hold, particularly in South Korea, where the drawdown had been more severe.

SoftBank Group shares plunged 16.16 percent in Tokyo on Friday, CNBC reported, leading a broad selloff in Asian technology stocks a day after Anthropic's debut on the Nasdaq. Japan's Nikkei Stock Average fell 4.7 percent, with memory chipmaker Kioxia Holdings down 12.6 percent and chip-testing equipment maker Advantest down 12.3 percent, Dow Jones reported, while Japanese semiconductor names Tokyo Electron, Lasertec and Renesas Electronics fell 7.19 percent, 6.75 percent and 6.71 percent respectively, according to CNBC. South Korea's Kospi also fell 4.7 percent, with SK Hynix down 6.3 percent and Samsung Electronics down 4.8 percent, and Australia's S&P/ASX 200 was down 0.9 percent.

The selling followed Anthropic's first day of trading in New York, in which the Claude maker raised about $81 billion in the largest IPO on record and closed 17 percent above its $83 offer price at $97.17, as covered on this site. Commerzbank Research said the listing appeared to trigger rotation out of existing AI-related names as investors redeployed capital into the new issue, and OCBC strategists said investors continued to unwind crowded positions as the record IPO sharpened scrutiny of stretched AI valuations and financing risks. Short-dated Asian and US government bonds rose as the equity selloff bolstered safe-haven demand, Dow Jones reported.
