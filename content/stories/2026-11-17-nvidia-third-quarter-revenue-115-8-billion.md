Nvidia reported revenue of $115.8 billion for its third quarter of fiscal 2027, ended October 25, up 20 percent from the previous quarter and up 103 percent from a year ago, in its first results since Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive last week. The figure came in ahead of the $108 billion the company had expected. Data Center revenue was $107.6 billion, up 21 percent on the quarter and 110 percent from a year ago, and Edge Computing revenue was $8.2 billion, up 14 percent on the quarter and 42 percent from a year ago.

Profitability held near record levels. GAAP gross margin was 74.8 percent and non-GAAP gross margin 74.9 percent. GAAP net income was $65.3 billion, with GAAP earnings per diluted share of $2.70 and non-GAAP earnings per diluted share of $2.68.

The outlook is the number the market was waiting on. Nvidia expects fourth quarter revenue of $130.0 billion, plus or minus 3 percent, in a quarter that includes an additional week, against an average analyst estimate of $125.25 billion according to LSEG data cited by Reuters. The company said it is not assuming any Data Center compute revenue from China in the outlook. It expects GAAP and non-GAAP gross margins between 71.0 and 72.0 percent for the quarter. Shares of the company, valued at roughly $3.8 trillion, were up about 2 percent in extended trading, Reuters reported, after falling about 17 percent since Huang's resignation erased more than $1 trillion of market value.

The product story is the Vera Rubin ramp. Nvidia said its Vera Rubin NVL72 platform has shipped in the fastest product ramp in the company's history, with racks running at CoreWeave, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, Oracle Cloud Infrastructure and Nebius, and coming online soon at SpaceXSI. Interim chief executive Colette Kress, who also remains chief financial officer, said Vera Rubin is off to an explosive start and the roadmap is on track. The release also announced an agreement to acquire Hugging Face, the open AI model platform, to scale it.

Capital returns stepped up through the crisis. The board approved a $150 billion increase to the share repurchase authorization in September, the company returned $28.1 billion to shareholders in the quarter through buybacks and dividends, and about $227.3 billion remained under the authorization at quarter end. The next quarterly dividend of $0.25 per share is payable December 23 to shareholders of record on December 2.

The results land while the leadership question stays open. On the earnings call, board chair Harvey Jones said the board does not intend to reinstate Huang as chief executive and declined to take questions on his resignation or the ongoing investigation, a stance set out in the separate leadership coverage on this site.
