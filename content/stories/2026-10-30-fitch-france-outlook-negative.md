Fitch Ratings revised France's outlook to negative from stable on Friday, affirming the A+ long-term rating, and said the change reflects a deterioration since its August review: weaker growth, higher borrowing costs and a wider deficit, compounded by political fragmentation that is undermining the prospects for debt stabilisation. Fitch now sees France's debt rising to around 124 percent of GDP by 2028, from 115.7 percent in 2025, with the deficit at 5.5 percent of GDP in 2027, and said the policy debate ahead of the presidential election has not materially strengthened its confidence in durable deficit reduction. The action lands on the day INSEE reported a 0.1 percent third quarter contraction for France and a week after Moody's kept the sovereign at Aa3 with a negative outlook; Fitch said it expects to review the rating by the end of February 2027 at the latest, while the large, wealthy and diversified economy, strong institutions and euro area membership continue to support the rating itself.
