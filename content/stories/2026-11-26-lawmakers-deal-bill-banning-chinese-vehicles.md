The Connected Vehicle Security Act, sponsored by Senators Bernie Moreno of Ohio and Elissa Slotkin of Michigan, would ban US sales of cars made with components from adversaries such as China and Russia, and its backers aim to pass it by voice vote in December with House colleagues fast tracking the measure in that chamber, Politico reported. The Alliance for Automotive Innovation, whose members include Mercedes, Volvo, GM, Ford and Stellantis, told Politico it is "very supportive" of the bill.

Under the deal, neither Mercedes nor Volvo would be blocked from selling cars in the United States: the bill adds flexibility for companies with silent shareholders in place of the original test based on more than 15 percent Chinese ownership, and negotiators kept batteries off the list of covered components, leaving the focus on battery management systems, which should leave Ford's licensing partnership with CATL unaffected. A sunset provision for the ban was also agreed.
