Leaders of the French-speaking world opened their biennial summit in Phnom Penh on Sunday with the election of the bloc's next secretary-general overshadowed by the conflict in eastern Congo. The Organisation internationale de la Francophonie, a confederation of 90 states and governments with 53 full members, meets through Monday in the Cambodian capital, and leaders are due to choose on Monday who leads the organisation for the next four years.

DR Congo moved first. On Saturday, Kinshasa announced it was withdrawing its candidate, Juliana Lumumba, the 71-year-old daughter of independence leader Patrice Lumumba, and throwing its weight behind Romanian former prime minister Dacian Ciolos, in its words to allow consensus around a unifying candidacy. Rwanda's foreign minister responded that the incumbent, Rwanda's Louise Mushikiwabo, who has led the organisation since 2019 and is seeking a third four-year term, would remain in the race.

The dispute is a proxy for the war in eastern Congo. Congo, the United States and United Nations experts accuse Rwanda of backing the M23, one of about 100 armed groups fighting in the mineral-rich east; Rwanda's government consistently denies backing armed groups in Congo. Kinshasa has threatened to leave the organisation if Mushikiwabo is re-elected. With a population above 110 million, Congo is the most populous country where French is an official language, and its departure would be a major blow to the bloc.

French President Emmanuel Macron, attending in person and handing the summit chairmanship to Cambodia, called for unity in his opening speech and said enlargement of the bloc requires consensus to preserve the union. His office said most members agreed on the need for consensus on the leadership question and that he would take the matter up with his African counterparts. France is the largest contributor to the organisation's budget, which gives Paris weight in the outcome even as its influence in Francophone Africa has receded.

The stakes extend beyond one appointment. Mali, Niger and Burkina Faso announced their withdrawals from the organisation in March 2025 after their memberships were suspended over military coups, and a Congolese walkout would remove the bloc's biggest African member at a moment when France is trying to rebuild its standing on the continent. About 20 heads of government are attending, including Canada's Mark Carney, Vietnam's To Lam, Rwanda's Paul Kagame and Congo's Felix Tshisekedi. The vote on Monday will show whether the compromise candidacy holds or the bloc elects its leader over the objection of its largest French-speaking state.
