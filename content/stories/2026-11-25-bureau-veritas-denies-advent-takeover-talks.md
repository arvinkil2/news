Bureau Veritas said on November 25 that it is not engaged in discussions with Advent International about a potential takeover, responding to a Bloomberg report that the US buyout firm was weighing a bid for the French testing, inspection and certification group. "Bureau Veritas is not engaged in discussions with Advent and does not comment on market rumours," a company spokesperson said. The shares had risen 7.5 percent on the report before the denial.

Bloomberg reported on November 24, citing people familiar with the matter, that Advent was working with advisers on a possible offer and might seek to carve out some Bureau Veritas assets as part of any deal. The talks were described as early stage and may not lead to a transaction. Advent declined to comment. Bureau Veritas carries a market value of about $14.76 billion, and a buyout at that scale would be one of the largest of a European company this year.

Advent has fresh capital to deploy: it raised $30 billion for its latest buyout and mid-market funds in October. Bureau Veritas reported third-quarter revenue growth of 10.1 percent year on year to 1.57 billion euros.

The denial narrows the claim to what the evidence supports: a sponsor studying a target is not a negotiation. What remains material is the direction of travel. Testing and certification groups, with contracted inspection revenues and fragmented regional competitors to roll up, sit squarely in the buyout industry's current target set, and Bureau Veritas is now a named name in that screen whether or not Advent ever bids.
