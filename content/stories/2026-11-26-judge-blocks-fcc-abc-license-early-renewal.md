A US judge on Wednesday barred the Federal Communications Commission from putting the licenses of eight Walt Disney owned ABC television stations up for early review. US District Judge Loren AliKhan granted Disney's request for a preliminary injunction, which bars the FCC from holding a hearing on the licenses, a step that could have led to their revocation and forced the stations off the air.

"This is not a close case," AliKhan wrote, saying the FCC appeared to be carrying out a retaliatory campaign driven by President Donald Trump that threatened the broadcaster's free speech rights. ABC has a "weighty interest in vindicating their right to be free of retaliation for protected speech," she said, and FCC Chair Brendan Carr, whom Trump named to the position in January 2025, "has taken President Trump's rhetoric as his marching orders." The judge also rejected the FCC's effort to have Disney's First Amendment lawsuit dismissed, finding that ABC and Disney had presented substantial evidence that the early renewal proceedings were motivated, at least in part, by retaliation for protected speech.

The dispute began in April, when Carr ordered reviews of the eight stations' licenses a day after Trump urged ABC to fire late night host Jimmy Kimmel, even though the renewals were not due to be considered until October 2028 at the earliest. The FCC had not ordered an early review in more than 50 years before April. In an April 28 letter, the chief of the FCC's video division, David J. Brown, directed Disney's ABC to file license renewals for all of its licensed television stations within 30 days, by May 28, 2026. Disney and ABC sued in August, arguing that the administration was trying to punish the network over broadcast content in violation of the Constitution.

An FCC spokesperson said on Wednesday that the granting of the injunction was "wrong on the facts and wrong on the law, as controlling Supreme Court precedent makes clear," and added: "We will continue to use our lawful authority to hold broadcasters accountable to their public interest obligations." A spokesperson for ABC News said the decision was "an important victory for the First Amendment," and Democratic FCC Commissioner Anna Gomez said the ruling "brings badly needed accountability to a commission and an administration that have politicized the machinery of government in a sustained campaign of censorship and control."

Broadcast stations need FCC licenses to use the public airwaves, and while revocations are extremely rare, critics said the risk of losing a license could chill a broadcaster's coverage. The FCC is also investigating ABC's daytime talk show The View, after saying it may be subject to federal rules requiring broadcasters to provide equal time opportunities to political candidates; Disney has said the program invited no political candidates to appear ahead of the November midterm elections as a result, citing the FCC's pressure campaign. Status: preliminary injunction granted November 25, 2026; the underlying suit continues and the ruling can be appealed.
