Gold Fields Ghana is seeking to invest $7.5 billion in the Tarkwa mine over the next 20 to 21 years as it works to renew leases due to expire in April 2027. Senior vice president and managing director Elliot Twum announced the plan in an interview with Channel One TV on Monday, saying continued investment and the use of technology would be essential to maintaining the mine's productivity and efficiency.

Twum outlined the company's contribution to Ghana's economy, saying it had spent $4.6 billion on local procurement and procurement involving host communities during the past five years, and that 99 percent of its workforce is Ghanaian. Every dollar generated from the operation returns $0.74 into the local Ghanaian economy, he said, a figure covering statutory payments, payments to the state, local and host community procurement, salaries and other in-country expenditure.

The remarks come amid pressure from traditional authorities in the Wassa enclave, who have accused the company of failing to deliver development benefits matching the scale of Tarkwa and are demanding that government hand the mine to a different investor when the leases expire. The Wassa Professionals Network has called for host communities to receive a 10 percent free carried equity stake and 2 percent of gross mineral sales revenue under a proposal it calls the Wassa Reset, and Twum said Gold Fields recognised the right of host communities to demand greater benefits from mining activities.

The network cited Gold Fields' own reserve declaration showing Tarkwa's managed mineral reserves rising from 4.3 million ounces in 2024 to 7.4 million ounces, indicating a mine life of about 17 years with further potential from resources, underground development and exploration. The renewal decision now sits with the government, and the terms set at Tarkwa will be read across Ghana's wider gold sector.
