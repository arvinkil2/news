Vietnam's National Assembly approved a revised land law on Saturday that lets authorities issue land expropriation decisions for some special projects before compensation plans are approved, provided no resettlement is required and the plans have been publicly disclosed. The provision covers nationally important projects, urgent public investment projects and cases where more than 75 percent of affected land users agree to the expropriation, and five of the assembly's 479 deputies voted against while eight did not cast a vote. The law takes effect on July 1, 2027 and adds safeguards including stronger resettlement protections, livelihood support for up to three years and expanded vocational training, while compensation disputes have long delayed infrastructure and industrial projects after complaints that payments fell below market value.
