Chilean fixed income investors are rotating into inflation linked notes as higher oil prices work through fuel costs and inflation expectations, Bloomberg reported on Monday. The shift follows a central bank meeting on October 27 at which the Banco Central de Chile held its policy rate at 4.5 percent, as every forecast in Bloomberg's survey expected, but warned that risks to inflation had increased significantly.

The statement moved the market even without a rate change. Swap rates rose by up to 6.6 basis points on the hawkish tone, Chilean reporting said, and pricing has since flipped from expecting tightening to begin in about a year to assigning high odds to a 25 basis point increase within six months. Scotiabank's Chile team projects a first hike on December 15, with a possible second in January 2027.

The inflation arithmetic explains the hedging. Headline inflation was 4.1 percent in September from a year earlier, two year expectations have crept above the bank's 3 percent target, and forwards in the UF, Chile's inflation linked unit, imply consumer prices topping out at 5.4 percent in February before easing below 4 percent in April. State fuel company Enap has raised pump prices five weeks running, with diesel up about 6.7 percent in the latest adjustment, and the finance ministry is spending about 80 million dollars a week cushioning pump prices.

Buying linkers rather than simply shortening duration is a hedge against the price level, not a bet on the policy path. It leaves investors exposed if the bank hikes faster than priced, but protected if oil keeps feeding the index, which is precisely the split outcome the October statement set up.
