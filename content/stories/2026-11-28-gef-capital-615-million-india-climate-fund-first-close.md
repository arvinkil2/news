GEF Capital Partners announced the first close of its fourth India-focused climate fund at $615 million, about 56.8 billion rupees, on November 26 at the Indian Venture and Alternate Capital Association's Climate Capital Summit in Mumbai. The close exceeds the fund's initial target and is roughly 40 percent larger than its predecessor, a $440 million vehicle, making it the firm's largest raise to date.

The first close drew pension funds, sovereign investors, foundations and family offices, primarily from Europe, Japan, the United Kingdom, Singapore and the United States. The fund will back mid-market, growth-stage companies in India and South Asia across energy transition, electric mobility, resource efficiency and sustainable industrial development.

GEF plans to write cheques of $30 million to $60 million into nine to twelve companies, with deployment expected to begin next year. Its earlier India investments include ReNew Power, Indian Energy Exchange, Premier Energies, Sterlite Electric, Sahyadri Farms, Arya.ag and Yulu Bikes.

The raise is a data point on where climate capital is going: the limited partner base is global and institutional, the strategy is growth equity rather than venture, and the destination is Indian industrial decarbonisation, where power, mobility and supply chain assets can absorb cheques of this size.
