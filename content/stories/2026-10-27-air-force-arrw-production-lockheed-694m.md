The US Air Force awarded Lockheed Martin a $694 million contract in late September to produce and field the AGM-183A Air-Launched Rapid Response Weapon, or ARRW, according to an announcement on Monday. The contract, placed under the Defense Department's Enterprise-Wide Agile Acquisition Contract, formally moves the boost-glide weapon out of prototyping and flight testing and into production, and the service withheld how many missiles it covers and when deliveries begin, citing operational security.

ARRW uses a solid-fuel rocket booster to accelerate past Mach 5 before releasing a maneuvering glide vehicle, and is meant to strike time-sensitive, heavily defended targets from standoff range, initially from B-52 bombers. Secretary of the Air Force Troy Meink called the move a turning point in the advanced weapons roadmap. The production decision reverses the service's 2023 move to halt ARRW purchases after unsuccessful tests, when it shifted focus to its other hypersonic programme, the Hypersonic Attack Cruise Missile.
