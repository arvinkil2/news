Estonia will close every road crossing on its border with Russia from Tuesday, December 1. The checkpoints at Narva, Koidula, Luhamaa and Saatse, including the Narva foot crossings over the Friendship Bridge to Ivangorod, will shut to vehicles and pedestrians alike, initially for six months, after which the government will review the decision. Rail freight will continue under strict controls.

The government took the decision on November 20, after an attempted arson attack on Threod Systems, an Estonian company in Viimsi that makes reconnaissance and strike drones. Estonia's internal security service says Russian military intelligence, the GRU, commissioned the attack, the second sabotage attempt on an Estonian defence company this year, and eight suspects were detained. Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna confirmed the closure in Baku on Friday, saying Estonia was not happy about it but needed to respond and to secure Europe as well.

The effects are already visible. Lux Express has suspended its Riga to St Petersburg and Tallinn to St Petersburg bus services from December 1, with the last departures on November 30, while keeping its routes inside the European Union. Lithuania's border service, by contrast, said it sees no need to close its own Russian crossings for now, so from Tuesday the three Baltic states will run three different border regimes with Russia.

For residents of the border city of Narva, many of whom cross to Ivangorod routinely, the closure ends daily overland movement entirely; no humanitarian exceptions had been published in the reporting read.
