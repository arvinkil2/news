Oil ended the week lower for a third straight week. Brent settled at 89.95 dollars a barrel on Friday in the estate market tables, down 2.21 percent on the day and 6.15 percent on the week. WTI closed at 83.41 dollars, up 0.24 percent on the day but down 5.82 percent on the week.

The week's trading captured the tension. On Friday morning Brent rose 79 cents, or 0.9 percent, to 92.77 dollars after Houthi missiles hit Riyadh and Iran's Revolutionary Guards said two tankers had struck naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz and were burning, a claim Reuters reported could not be independently verified. Saudi civil defence said the Riyadh attack damaged several floors of the kingdom's tallest skyscraper, headquarters of its sovereign wealth fund, with no injuries reported.

By the close, surplus expectations had won. Crude shipments through Hormuz have returned to roughly pre-war levels, according to Reuters reporting, and a Joint Organizations Data Initiative report showed Saudi crude exports rose nearly 21 percent in September from August to 5.38 million barrels a day, the highest since February.

The International Energy Agency put the cumulative cost of the conflict at 2.8 billion barrels of oil and 60 billion cubic metres of gas supply lost from the Gulf. OPEC+ ministers meet on November 29 to decide 2027 output policy, with prices down about a fifth from their highs and tanker freight, up 20.54 percent on the week in the market tables, still pricing the shipping risk that crude itself is discounting.
