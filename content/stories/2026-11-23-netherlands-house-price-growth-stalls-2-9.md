Dutch house price growth has stopped accelerating. Statistics Netherlands and the Cadastre reported that prices of existing owner occupied homes were 2.9 percent higher in October than a year earlier, exactly the September rate, and that the year on year increase has been slowing almost continuously since the end of 2024. Prices rose 0.5 percent on the month.

Volumes moved the other way. The Cadastre recorded 20,376 housing transactions in October, a year on year decrease of nearly 7 percent, although the first ten months of the year still show 196,787 homes changing owners, up 2 percent on the same period last year. The average transaction price was 508,793 euros, a figure CBS cautions does not adjust for the quality mix of homes sold.

Prices remain far above the last peak. October prices stood 17.9 percent higher than at the previous peak in July 2022, after the 2022 to 2023 correction reversed from June 2023 onward.

For the rates picture, the Netherlands is a useful euro area tell: a high priced housing stock, decelerating price growth and now falling transactions, with no sign of a price correction. That combination argues for financial stability vigilance rather than rate relief driven by housing stress.
