Latest: S&P Global Ratings cut BMW's long- and short-term issuer credit ratings to A-/A-2 from A/A-1 on November 5 and lowered the senior unsecured issue rating to A- from A, with a negative outlook. S&P said BMW brand sales volumes in China fell 15 percent year on year in the first nine months of 2026 and its premium segment share there dropped to about 22 percent from about 26 percent in 2023, a structural shift it expects to continue as domestic brands press their advantage in new-energy vehicles, pricing and dealer reach. The agency forecasts adjusted free operating cash flow after capital spending of about 1.7 billion euros in 2026 and about 1 billion euros in 2027, against 3.3 billion euros in 2025, and said a further downgrade could follow if the China share losses and margin pressure do not stabilise.

Earlier: BMW cut its 2026 automotive EBIT margin forecast to a corridor of -0.5 percent to 0.5 percent, from 1 percent to 3 percent, and its automotive return on capital employed forecast to -1 percent to 1 percent, from 1 percent to 5 percent, while leaving automotive free cash flow guidance unchanged at above 2.5 billion euros. The board expanded and intensified its portfolio and structural measures, which BMW said will have a non-operating effect on automotive earnings in the fourth quarter even though operating performance stayed solid in the third quarter; the restructuring includes about 8,000 job cuts, and deliveries guidance moved to a moderate decrease from a slight decrease. To counter weak demand in China, BMW has added factory shifts to speed the production ramp of its Neue Klasse models and will phase out earlier vehicle and battery generations sooner.
