OFAC published a notice in Monday's Federal Register updating the identifying information of entries on its sanctions lists. The action, dated November 4, applies secondary sanctions information to names associated with the IRAN-EO13902 programme, the executive order covering Iran's construction, mining, manufacturing and textiles sectors. It is an identifier update rather than a new designation round, but it widens the documented exposure of the named parties to secondary sanctions, and the full updated list sits on OFAC's recent actions page. A further OFAC update on Monday made one change to the Iran list: Volta Shipping Services LLC, a Dubai company linked to Mohammad Hossein Shamkhani and designated under the same executive order, now also trades under the name Volta Container Line FZCO, and no new names were designated.

Qatar's prime minister, Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani, is due at the White House on Monday, the next step in the mediation channel through which Iran has sent Washington a nuclear proposal and through which President Trump spoke with Qatar's emir on Friday about de-escalation. The visit follows a weekend in which Qatar denied reports that the US and Iran had failed to reach a deal, and its outcome is the first concrete test of the diplomatic track behind Trump's Sunday remarks on strikes, covered in full in today's Iran story.

The Russia Ukraine US trilateral talks open in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday, November 10, running to November 11, the first trilateral round since Geneva in February. On the eve of the talks, US officials told the BBC that Moscow and Kyiv are privately showing a little flexibility on who would control Ukraine's eastern regions after the war, while neither side has moved in public: Moscow still demands that Ukraine relinquish the parts of Donetsk it holds, and Kyiv still wants the contact line frozen. One US official said the Donetsk and territorial questions will not be solved until the postwar security architecture is known, and Jared Kushner, who leads the US delegation with Steve Witkoff, said the talks will focus on the postwar security concerns of both sides and could at most build momentum toward the next phase. The framework under discussion includes security consultations, crisis communications hotlines and reciprocal de-escalation measures, and officials floated reactivating the Nato-Russia Council, paused since 2022. Russia's delegation is led by Kremlin aide Vladimir Medinsky, with deputy foreign ministers Sergei Ryabkov and Mikhail Galuzin, spy chief Igor Kostyukov and General Yury Zhigarlovsky; Ukraine sends Foreign Intelligence Service head Rustem Umerov, chief of staff Kyrylo Budanov and MP Davyd Arakhamiya. The talks begin while Kyiv's services are still only partially restored after the November 6 to 7 strikes on nuclear substations, a file this site updated on Sunday.
