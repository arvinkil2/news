**Latest:** SpaceX's own securities filing, published on Monday, contradicts the account of a closed $40 billion debt deal that this story originally carried. In a Form 8-K filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission on October 26, SpaceX disclosed that on October 24 it entered into a binding commitment letter to enter into a master finance lease with an unnamed entity managed by Apollo Global Management. Under the contemplated lease, the lessor will acquire graphics processing unit pods and related equipment and lease them to SpaceX for use at data centres selected by SpaceX, with the equipment delivered in tranches, each tranche subject to a separate lease schedule with a term of approximately five to five and one-quarter years. Rent under each schedule is payable quarterly in advance and consists of an amount equal to one twentieth of the financed cost of the tranche, together with an amount calculated at a fixed rate per annum on the outstanding lease balance and certain ongoing costs. SpaceX expects aggregate principal payments under the lease schedules to total approximately $40 billion. The filing states that the fixed rate will be determined at pricing, which will occur before SpaceX enters into the master finance lease and had not occurred as of the commitment letter, and that the master lease itself has not been executed: entry into it is subject to certain closing conditions and is expected before the end of 2026, with the lease schedules expected to commence in the first half of 2027 upon delivery of the equipment. The filing describes no tranches, no senior or subordinated split, no A2 tranche, no bonds and no 144A issuance, names no buyer, lender or other participant, and does not describe how the lessor will fund the equipment purchases.

The filing is the first primary document in the record, and it does not corroborate the terms reported over the weekend. Elon Musk said on Sunday that SpaceX had closed $40 billion in debt financing to buy graphics processing units for its artificial intelligence buildout, writing that the company could do so because it is investment grade and has Starlink and launch revenue behind it, and describing the package as primarily callable debt for GPU purchases arriving in the first and second quarters of 2027. Bloomberg reported on Monday, citing people familiar with the matter, that Pimco had purchased $4 billion of the package as part of an A2 tranche sitting between the more junior and more senior parts of the structure, with the debt to be issued as 144A bonds; the interest rate was not disclosed and neither Pimco nor SpaceX confirmed the purchase on the record. The Financial Times had reported in early October, via Reuters, a structure of about $10 billion in bank loans and $30 billion in investment grade debt, and Semafor reported indicative yields of about 7.75 percent on the safer tranche and about 8.3 percent on the riskiest. None of those terms appears in the filing, and the instrument it describes, a finance lease whose rate is not yet set, has no tranches or bonds at all. The reported debt terms accordingly stand as attributed press accounts, evidence grade C, unconfirmed and inconsistent with the filed instrument.

Musk paired his Sunday announcement with a warning about the rest of the market. He said debt markets have in recent weeks almost entirely shut off access to money for companies without substantial revenue independent of AI data centres, and predicted widespread failures and consolidation among the more than 200 neocloud firms that rent out GPU capacity. That is his assessment, not a market finding. CNBC put the financing against the scale of SpaceX's AI segment, which it calls Super Intelligence: third quarter revenue of $7.56 billion, up ninefold from $813 million a year earlier, making it the company's largest business unit on the latest earnings release.

What happens next is set by the filing rather than the announcement: a pricing at which the lease's fixed rate is set, execution of the master lease before year end if the closing conditions are met, and first deliveries in the first half of 2027. No filing by Apollo or any other party describing how the lessor will fund the equipment purchases had been published as of Monday evening.
