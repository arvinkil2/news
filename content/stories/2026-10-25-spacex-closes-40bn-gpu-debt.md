**Latest:** Pimco has purchased $4 billion of the SpaceX package, Bloomberg reported on Monday, citing people familiar with the matter, the first named buyer and the first tranche detail to surface since Musk announced the close. Pimco is buying part of the A2 tranche, a slice that sits between the more junior and more senior parts of the package, one of the people said, and the debt is to be issued as 144A bonds, privately placed securities that can be traded among institutional buyers. Bloomberg described the overall deal as roughly $40 billion of equipment financing signed with lenders led by Apollo Global Management, to fund purchases of Nvidia graphics processing units and related equipment for SpaceX's artificial intelligence operations. The interest rate on the debt was not disclosed, and neither Pimco nor SpaceX confirmed the purchase on the record in the report.

Elon Musk said on Sunday that SpaceX has closed $40 billion in debt financing to buy graphics processing units for its artificial intelligence buildout, writing that the company could do so because it is investment grade and has Starlink and launch revenue behind it. He described the package as primarily callable debt for GPU purchases arriving in the first and second quarters of 2027, and said it is not a major factor this quarter.

The announcement moves the financing from reported to claimed closed. On October 6, Reuters reported, citing the Financial Times, that SpaceX planned to raise $40 billion, led by Apollo Global Management, to purchase Nvidia AI chips, structured as about $10 billion in bank loans and $30 billion in investment grade debt, with Pimco among a small group of lenders in talks and closing then expected in 2027. SpaceX, Apollo and Nvidia did not comment at the time, and no full lender list, pricing or maturity for the closed package has been published since; the Pimco allocation reported above is the first individual purchase to surface.

Musk paired the announcement with a warning about the rest of the market. He said debt markets have in recent weeks almost entirely shut off access to money for companies without substantial revenue independent of AI data centres, and predicted widespread failures and consolidation among the more than 200 neocloud firms that rent out GPU capacity. That is his assessment, not a market finding, but it states the two tier logic of the deal plainly: the same chip purchases are financeable at $40 billion when they sit on Starlink subscription and launch cash flows, and increasingly hard to finance when the borrower has nothing but the compute itself.

CNBC put the borrowing against the scale of SpaceX's AI segment, which it calls Super Intelligence: third quarter revenue of $7.56 billion, up ninefold from $813 million a year earlier, making it the company's largest business unit on the latest earnings release.

SpaceX has not published the terms of the closed financing, and no bank or bondholder group has confirmed its participation. The size, purpose and structure rest on the October reporting; the fact of closing rests on Musk alone.
