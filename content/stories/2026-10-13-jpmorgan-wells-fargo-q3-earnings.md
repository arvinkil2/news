Latest: Goldman Sachs reported third-quarter net revenues of $18.68 billion, up 23% year on year, and net earnings of $5.29 billion, or $17.01 per diluted share, for an annualized return on equity of 18.7%. Global Banking and Markets revenue rose 32% as advisory, equity and debt underwriting all benefited from heavier merger, IPO and investment-grade issuance activity, and equities revenue surged 72% on financing and derivatives. Asset and wealth management revenue rose 14%, while Platform Solutions revenue fell sharply on markdowns in the Apple Card portfolio and the provision for credit losses rose to $368 million on wholesale loan impairments.

Citigroup's net income rose 40% year on year to $5.245 billion as revenue climbed 8% to $23.845 billion, which chief executive Jane Fraser called the bank's strongest third-quarter revenue in a decade, and credit provisions fell 6% to $2.586 billion. Reported earnings of about $2.85 a share topped consensus near $2.65, and the bank said it now expects to exceed its full-year profitability target. Together with the two earlier prints, the morning leaves dealmaking and equities, not consumer credit, as the quarter's swing factor.

JPMorgan Chase reported third-quarter net income of $15.3 billion, up 6% from a year earlier, on reported revenue of $50.2 billion, with net interest income up 13% to $27.1 billion and equity markets revenue up 49%, but adjusted earnings per share of $5.75 missed consensus of about $5.93 after a $658 million net investment securities loss in the corporate segment and a fixed-income trading result that fell short of estimates. Wells Fargo's net income rose to $5.95 billion, or $1.87 per share, slightly ahead of consensus, helped by loan growth and a 5% rise in net interest income to $12.52 billion, though revenue of $21.87 billion missed estimates and the shares were down 1.5% in premarket trading. Goldman Sachs and Citigroup report later on Tuesday, with the KBW bank index already down more than 12% from its August highs as investors weigh whether high Treasury yields help or hurt the largest lenders.
