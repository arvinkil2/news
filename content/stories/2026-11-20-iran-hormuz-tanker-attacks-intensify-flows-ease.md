**Latest.** President Donald Trump said on Sunday that he is inclined to order military strikes against Iran after the new year if the two countries fail to reach an agreement, dismissing the ongoing talks with Tehran as meaningless. In exclusive comments to Fox News, Trump accused Iranian negotiators of stalling while escalating hostilities, calling them "professional tap dancers" who only talk. He predicted tit-for-tat strikes between American and Iranian forces will continue, dared Tehran to challenge the US naval blockade of Iranian ports, and, asked about renewed anti-government demonstrations in several Iranian cities, told protesters to keep protesting. No Iranian response was reported in the Fox account.


**Latest.** Iran said on Sunday that tankers had again struck naval mines east of the Strait of Hormuz. The Iranian Army's naval forces said two oil tankers that entered a prohibited zone east of the strait before noon on Sunday, with their identification and navigation systems switched off, hit a sea mine and caught fire, and warned that owners and operators of ships sailing prohibited areas with systems off would bear responsibility for any damage. The Revolutionary Guards' navy separately said two supertankers using unauthorized routes north of Oman's Ganem Island, east of the strait, hit mines, exploded and were stopped, and said the strait is entirely under its control and surveillance. Anadolu Agency's headline counted four tankers, but its report does not establish whether the two statements describe four vessels or the same two twice, and neither statement names a vessel or reports casualties. UK Maritime Trade Operations said only that it had received a report of an incident involving a tanker and military forces in the Gulf of Oman, that authorities were aware and investigations were ongoing, and it confirmed no mine, fire or explosion. The Iranian claims are uncorroborated by any independent source.

**Latest.** UK Maritime Trade Operations, in its weekly security overview published on Friday, counted 263 vessel transits through the Strait of Hormuz in the seven days to November 18, down from 308 the previous week, leaving traffic about 65 percent below pre-war levels. UKMTO reported attacks on six tankers during the week, four in the strait and two in the Persian Gulf; two of the vessels needed assistance, none was reported sunk, and the agency has now recorded 106 incidents in the strait since the conflict began on February 28. The Joint Maritime Information Center assessed the threat level in the strait as severe.

**Latest.** Two more tanker incidents were reported near Khasab on Saturday. A tanker sailing outbound through the Strait of Hormuz was hit on its stern by an unidentified projectile about 11 nautical miles north of Khasab, UK Maritime Trade Operations said, and UKMTO later reported a second incident in which a tanker's engine room was hit by an unidentified projectile 17 nautical miles east of Khasab. Reuters, reporting the UKMTO statements, said it was not immediately clear whether the two reports involved the same vessel. No casualties were reported in either incident and the environmental impact remained unknown. The reports follow a similar attack near Khasab on Friday that caused a fire on board the vessel struck. Iran's navy said on Saturday that an oil tanker caught fire after hitting a naval mine in a restricted area east of the strait; Reuters said it could not independently verify the Iranian account, or establish whether it referred to either of the incidents UKMTO reported.

Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, said Tehran is ready to hold expert-level talks with the United States on reopening the Strait of Hormuz, and could shorten its proposed two-day reopening timetable to as little as two hours, provided Washington first states clearly that it wants an agreement, takes confidence-building steps, and meets the seven requirements Iran has submitted, which include lifting the latest US restrictive measures. Araghchi said the earlier Islamabad memorandum of understanding is no longer under discussion, though its principles could still ground a deal, and that Iran has had no US response since President Trump said Washington does not need an agreement with Tehran. The remarks, in an interview with an Iranian newspaper the report does not name, reached this site through a single chain, Sputnik/RIA Novosti reporting carried by Antara, and are uncorroborated; they are Iran's stated position, not a confirmed change in US policy.

A tanker sailing outbound through the Strait of Hormuz was struck by an unknown projectile eight nautical miles north of Khasab, in Oman's Musandam region on the southern coast of the waterway, UK Maritime Trade Operations said on Friday. The vessel's company security officer reported a fire on board; no casualties or environmental impact were reported, and authorities were investigating. The tanker was not named and no attacker was identified. Iranian media said a missile had hit Iran's Qeshm Island, on the northern side of the strait; that report carries no detail on the missile's origin, target or damage, no official statement from any government, and it is uncorroborated.

Iran's attacks on oil tankers crossing the Strait of Hormuz increased in October and have continued at the higher tempo this month, at roughly one UKMTO-reported incident a day and about 14 incidents so far in November, according to data compiled by Bloomberg, with at least two seafarers missing after attacks this week and maritime security officials reporting repeat attempts on the same ship and sightings of limpet mines on vessels. The seven-day moving average of oil transits through Hormuz and net ship-to-ship transfers in the Gulf of Oman eased to about 10 million barrels a day by November 10, from around 13.4 million at the start of the month, Kpler data show, even as Brent futures have slid about a fifth this month. Kuwait has written to the International Maritime Organization about attacks on eight of its tankers in recent weeks, calling them a clear breach of international law and demanding Iran stop them.

Chinese buying tells the other half of the recovery. China's crude oil imports from Persian Gulf producers, excluding Iran, rose 42.4 percent in October from September to 19.7 million tons, the highest level since the war began and the fourth consecutive monthly increase, on South China Morning Post calculations from Chinese customs data released on Friday, with Gulf producers supplying almost half of China's total term supplies and volumes approaching pre-war levels as Hormuz flows rebounded and Gulf producers cut prices, including Aramco's $2 a barrel cut to the December Arab Light price for Asia.
