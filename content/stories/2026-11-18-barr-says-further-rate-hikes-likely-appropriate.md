Federal Reserve Governor Michael Barr said on Wednesday he is likely to be on board with tighter monetary policy in an economy still beset by inflation pressures. "If the economy evolves as I currently forecast, I expect it will be appropriate to raise rates further," Barr said in the text of remarks prepared for delivery before a conference at the New York Fed, adding that inflation is too high and is making it hard for American families to make ends meet.

Barr also said the economy is otherwise doing well, with low unemployment, solid consumer spending and robust business investment. Reuters noted the Federal Open Market Committee meets on December 8 and 9 and is widely expected to raise the federal funds target range by a quarter percentage point, to between 4 percent and 4.25 percent.
