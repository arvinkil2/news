The European Commission on Wednesday proposed a Quantum Act to turn Europe's quantum research into products and services, strengthen supply chains and prepare digital systems for post-quantum cryptography, organised around three pillars: research excellence, bringing quantum technology into use, and keeping systems and supplies secure. The Act would create a network of leading research institutes, targeted Grand Challenge funding competitions and pan-European quantum infrastructure, overseen by a new Quantum Strategy Board and a European Quantum Alliance.

The proposal also allows for a Sovereign Tech Equity Fund, an investment vehicle providing equity and similar financing for technologies the Commission calls essential to the Union's technological sovereignty and economic security, to be established with the European Investment Bank Group and member states. Reuters reported the fund ambition at about 50 billion euros, a figure the release itself does not state. The European Parliament and the Council will now consider the proposal, which supports the Quantum Europe Strategy goal of global quantum leadership by 2030.
