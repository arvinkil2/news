**Latest, Monday in Sydney:** NextDC chief executive Craig Scroggie told the UBS Australasia conference on Monday that the company will stop work on all projects without customer contracts, saying anything without a customer commitment the company has to deliver on is no longer a priority, and that the top task is winning community backing for the M3 Melbourne project. He did not say how many projects the pause covers; the company's 2026 annual report listed 11 centres in development or planning. Work on M3, suspended after power delays and community backlash forced last month's earnings cut and a 42 percent share fall, will not restart until the company can show it has solved the problem and earned back trust, Scroggie said, describing the backlash as reflective of global unease about artificial intelligence and the data centres that embody it. Goodman and Digital Realty finalised their A$715 million purchase of a 9.9 percent stake last week and said they have no current plans for a takeover offer but reserve the right to make one; NextDC's share price has since recovered the ground lost in the October writedown.

**Latest, Thursday in Sydney:** NextDC shares surged nearly 43 percent to A$9.34 on Thursday, as the S&P/ASX 200 rose 1.8 percent, after the stake purchase completed. NextDC said a trust controlled equally by Goodman and Digital Realty conducted an aftermarket bookbuild on Wednesday to buy an up to 9.9 percent stake at A$9.50 a share, a 45.5 percent premium to Wednesday's close, and that its board considers the purchase an opportunistic move by the two buyers. Goodman rose 2 percent in Sydney and Digital Realty rose 2.6 percent in New York trade on Wednesday. NextDC shares had lost more than 40 percent this year, most of it in late October after the company paused future developments and cut revenue forecasts, wiping about A$3 billion from its value in a day.

Goodman Group and Digital Realty have jointly offered A$715 million for a 9.9 percent stake in NextDC, at A$9.50 a share, a 45.5 percent premium to the valuation when trading ceased on Wednesday. The offer was revealed in a term sheet submitted to the Australian Stock Exchange after trading finished, led by JP Morgan, and the two bidders say they will not make a full takeover bid or seek a controlling stake, while leaving open that this could change in the future.

NextDC's shares have fallen as much as 45 percent in recent weeks after delays to electricity network upgrade work at its M3 Melbourne facility forced it to defer A$140 million of customer revenue and pause new development projects and land acquisitions. The company operates or is developing 20 data centres across Australia, with further sites planned or under evaluation in Japan, Thailand, Malaysia and Singapore, and raised A$500 million of debt financing in July.
