The European Union has offered to build a data center for Moldova, a proposal made by enlargement commissioner Marta Kos during a visit to the country last week and reported by Europa Libera. The facility would help protect the personal data of Moldovan citizens from cyberattacks, after an attack in October 2026 exposed the data of an estimated 450,000 people, and would be constructed with the assistance of Sweden if accepted. No details have been released on timing, cost, location or capacity.

Prime Minister Vasile Tofan welcomed the offer, saying Moldova is advancing the preparatory work for a secure data center. The proposal was made at the first meeting of the High-Level Digital Connectivity Platform in Chișinău, where the European Commission said up to €623 million ($689 million) pledged by nations and private sector partners would go into digital connectivity between Europe and Central Asia, including data center infrastructure in the Black Sea region.
