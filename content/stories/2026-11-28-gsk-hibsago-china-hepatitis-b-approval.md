China's National Medical Products Administration has approved Hibsago (bepirovirsen), GSK announced on November 26. The approval covers use with nucleos(t)ide analogue therapy to achieve functional cure of chronic hepatitis B in non-cirrhotic adults who have been on such therapy for at least six months, with HBV DNA below 90 IU/mL and hepatitis B surface antigen at or below 3,000 IU/mL. It was granted under Breakthrough Therapy and Priority Review designations.

Bepirovirsen is a first-in-class antisense oligonucleotide, licensed by GSK from Ionis Pharmaceuticals, designed to inhibit production of the virus's genetic components and viral proteins, including surface antigen, so the immune system can regain control. Functional cure means viral DNA and surface antigen stay undetectable for at least 24 weeks after all treatment stops.

In pooled results from the B-Well 1 and B-Well 2 Phase 3 trials, conducted in 29 countries, six months of bepirovirsen treatment produced a functional cure rate of 19 percent (233 of 1,220 patients) against 0 of 614 on placebo. In patients with surface antigen at or below 1,000 IU/mL, the rate was 26 percent (200 of 768) against 0 of 393. GSK said standard care alone typically achieves a functional cure rate below 1 percent after one year. The most frequent adverse events were injection site redness, local pain and a temporary rise in a liver enzyme.

Chronic hepatitis B affects an estimated 75 million people in China and is the leading cause of liver cancer there, according to GSK. China's viral hepatitis action plan targets at least 80 percent diagnosis and treatment rates by 2030.

GSK will work with Chia Tai Tianqing Pharmaceutical Group, a subsidiary of SBP Group with access to more than 5,000 medical centres, to accelerate the launch in mainland China. Hibsago is also approved in Japan and the United States, GSK said. The company gave no China price or launch date.
