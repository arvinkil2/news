Semiconductors were the day's largest move. The chip index fell 8.57 percent to 469.81 dollars, Broadcom fell 5.77 percent to 325.14 dollars and Nvidia fell 4.21 percent to 204.72 dollars, with the broader technology sector down 4.85 percent and the Nasdaq 100 down 3.26 percent to 28,449 points. The selling is the second day of the repricing set off by Alphabet's third quarter results, in which record capital spending of 51.4 billion dollars pushed free cash flow to negative 9.07 billion dollars (see today's Alphabet story). Alphabet itself fell a further 1.09 percent to 280.65 dollars after Thursday's 12.64 percent drop. No story in today's edition reports company specific news for Broadcom or the chip index; the move reads as the Alphabet repricing working through the suppliers who receive the spending.

Precious metals extended Thursday's fall. Silver futures dropped 4.03 percent to 49.26 dollars an ounce and silver miners fell 5.48 percent, while gold futures fell 2.45 percent to 3,905.90 dollars and gold miners fell 5.20 percent. The driver in today's coverage is the policy path: September PCE inflation rose to 3.6 percent on the year and third quarter GDP grew 3.4 percent, a combination that keeps a December Federal Reserve hike priced as the base case risk (see today's PCE and GDP stories). The commodities roundup had already recorded gold slipping below 4,000 dollars this morning; by midday it was below 3,910 dollars.

Uranium miners fell 5.93 percent to 33.61 dollars, the second largest drop in the tables. No story in today's edition explains that move, and none is offered here.

Copper miners fell 4.89 percent to 72.22 dollars, following copper itself down 0.98 percent to 6.48 dollars a pound. Today's copper story sets out why the price and the physical market disagree: nearly 70 percent of visible exchange stocks sit in COMEX warehouses in the United States while inventories in London and Shanghai run below their ten year average (see today's copper stockpiles story). The wider mining complex fell with the metals, with BHP down 3.18 percent and Rio Tinto down 1.76 percent, against an iron ore price heading for its lowest month end close since 2022 (see today's iron ore story).

Crude moved the other way. WTI rose 2.26 percent to 104.31 dollars a barrel and Brent rose 1.57 percent to 113.83 dollars, as banks raised year end forecasts on a tightening physical market, with ANZ lifting its Brent forecast to 105 dollars and UBS at 110 dollars (see today's oil forecasts story). Chevron and ExxonMobil both reported third quarter profit jumps on refining margins this morning, with Chevron's downstream earnings more than quadrupling from a year earlier (see today's Chevron and ExxonMobil story).

Treasury yields fell for a second session. The five year yield dropped 5.8 basis points to 4.74 percent and the ten year fell 4.2 basis points to 4.95 percent, extending Thursday's rally, which today's Treasury yields story attributes to safe haven demand as technology shares fell rather than to any softening in December hike pricing. The morning's employment cost data gave the rally no policy cover: compensation costs rose 0.8 percent in the third quarter and the annual rate held at 3.4 percent (see today's employment costs story).

The midday read is risk off in equities and metals, risk on in duration and crude. Investors are selling the assets whose prices rest on future AI cash flows and on a Fed that stays put, and buying the two things this week's data still support: government bonds as a hedge against the equity fall, and oil, where the constraint is physical and present rather than projected.
