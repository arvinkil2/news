The Uganda People's Defence Forces will begin withdrawing from Lubero town in North Kivu province, eastern Democratic Republic of Congo, within the next two weeks, the army said in a statement on Friday. Security in the town will pass to the Congolese armed forces, the FARDC. Ugandan troops have operated in Lubero since January 2025, pursuing remnants of the Allied Democratic Forces, the Islamic State-affiliated group that fled there under pressure from joint operations in North Kivu and Ituri.

The statement, signed by Colonel Chris Magezi, the acting director of defence public information, gave no completion date, no troop numbers and no reason for the withdrawal. Army spokespeople said the joint campaign, Operation Shujaa, continues elsewhere in DR Congo and that the withdrawal applies only to Lubero town.

The timing is difficult to separate from the diplomatic row. Days earlier, on November 24, Kinshasa summoned Uganda's top diplomat over social media posts by Uganda's military chief, General Muhoozi Kainerugaba, the son of President Yoweri Museveni. Hours after the withdrawal statement, Magezi issued a second statement saying Uganda fully supports a peaceful resolution of the conflict in eastern DR Congo among the armed groups and the Congolese government. The army did not say whether the two events are linked.
