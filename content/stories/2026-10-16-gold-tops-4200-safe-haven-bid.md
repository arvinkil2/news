Gold closed above 4,200 dollars an ounce in the latest session in the site tables, settling at 4,203.70 dollars, up 0.81 percent, with silver up 2.04 percent to 57.99 dollars and platinum group sentiment firm alongside. The precious complex rose on a day when the energy complex fell, WTI down 2.31 percent, which separates this move from a general commodity bid.

The drivers are the standing ones, now compounding: central bank buying that has run for three years, a dollar index down 0.28 percent to 101.71, real yields that fell with the Treasury rally, and a war economy in which the haven trade no longer needs a fresh escalation to attract money. Silver's bigger move, even after a 4.41 percent weekly fall, marks the retail and speculative participation that gold's steadier grind does not show.

The miners confirmed the session: gold miners rose 4.34 percent and silver miners 3.48 percent in the mining tables, the strongest groups of the day, catching up with a metal price that had left them behind earlier in the month. The question the complex now poses is not direction but distribution: at these levels, every producer hedge, every central bank pause and every ETF outflow moves a market priced for a one way consensus.
