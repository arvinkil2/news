**PwC: sell the AI, not the ERP upgrade.** PwC says IT departments struggling to fund ERP migrations should make the expected benefits of AI part of the pitch, arguing that a clean core of modern, lightly customized applications makes deploying AI agents easier. The consultancy's Jennifer Colapietro told The Register that AI value is now a key business case justification for moving to SAP S/4HANA or Oracle Fusion. A survey of SAP users last year found 95 percent said building a positive case for migration required substantial effort or was genuinely challenging. Kingfisher, which owns B&Q, is the counterexample in the same report: it kept its older SAP system on third-party support from Rimini Street and is building its AI strategy around it, ahead of mainstream ECC maintenance ending in December 2027.

**FAA modernization, by the numbers.** The Ars Technica explainer on modernizing the US national airspace system without emergency ground stops, and Nextgov's account of the November 13 nationwide ground stop, put figures on the program: Congress appropriated $12.5 billion in July 2025 for the Brand New Air Traffic Control System, the FAA had replaced 2,560 of 5,170 old copper wires with fiber optic cable as of May 2026, and the phase 2 automation layer carries an FAA estimate of about $10.2 billion excluding facilities, with no start date. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy has asked Congress for a further $50 billion. The November 13 stop, blamed on legacy equipment overloaded during upgrade work, lasted more than four hours.

**TeraWulf goes vertical in Kentucky.** TeraWulf has started vertical construction at its Justified Data Campus in Hawesville, Kentucky, with first structural steel up on the first building and steel delivered for the second. The 480MW campus sits on a 790-acre former aluminum processing site acquired from Century Aluminum, Anthropic is the customer, Google acts as financial backstop, Fluor is the construction partner, and the first facility is due online in 2027.

**Signal blocks screenshots against AI agents.** Signal announced on Friday that it will restrict screenshots by default on Android and iOS to stop operating system AI agents from reading private chats, extending the Windows protection it added after Microsoft's Recall feature. President Meredith Whittaker called OS-level agentic AI an existential threat to Signal's ability to guarantee privacy, while conceding the remedy is incomplete because agents can reach app data by other routes. Users can turn the protection off, which Signal says breaks its privacy guarantees.

**CyrusOne KEP tops out in Osaka.** CyrusOne and Kansai Electric Power have topped out the steel structure of their first Japanese data center, OSK1 in Kansai, which will offer 48MW of IT capacity across 11,560 square metres. Ground was broken in September 2024 and the first phase is due to be operational by the first quarter of 2028.
