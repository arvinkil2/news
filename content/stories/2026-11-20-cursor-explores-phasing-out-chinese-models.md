Cursor told the House Committee on Homeland Security and the House Select Committee on China that it plans to explore phasing out models developed in the People's Republic of China because of geopolitical risk, according to the committees' report, which followed a months-long investigation into the adoption of Chinese AI models in the American tech stack and was reviewed by CNBC ahead of publication on Friday. Cursor's coding model Composer 2 was built on top of Kimi K2.5, from the Chinese lab Moonshot AI. SpaceX completed its acquisition of Cursor in August, at an implied equity value of $60 billion.

The report found that cost, customization and near-frontier performance were driving adoption of Chinese models, with the United States lacking a competitive open-weight alternative at the price and performance the market demands. It recommends a national American open-weight initiative and extending an existing ban on DeepSeek in federal civilian systems into a prohibition on PRC-developed AI models in federal information systems and in the performance of federal contracts. Airbnb and DoorDash also engaged with the investigation about their use of Chinese models; neither Cursor nor SpaceX responded to CNBC's request for comment, and Airbnb declined to comment.
