Tajikistan has formally notified the United Nations of its decision to withdraw from the International Criminal Court, becoming the fourth country to begin the process since the United States urged members to leave the court in July, after Venezuela, Chad and Naoero, formerly Nauru. The withdrawal from the Rome Statute is due to take effect on November 20, 2027, a year after the decision, which Tajikistan's government said it took on November 20 during a visit by Russian President Vladimir Putin. The court said it regrets the decision, which it said undermines the collective pursuit of justice.

The move follows a finding by the court in March that Tajikistan had failed to comply with its request to arrest Putin, who is wanted over alleged war crimes in Ukraine, during his visit in 2025. The Trump administration has sanctioned ICC judges and prosecutors and said in July it would seek to disable the court's operations, which it portrays as a threat to US sovereignty. Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger also notified the UN of their withdrawals in June, before the US campaign began, denouncing the court as an instrument of neocolonial repression; all seven departing states remain among the court's 125 members until their exits take effect.
