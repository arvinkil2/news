**Latest, October 28:** The Budget also carried a further 10 billion pounds of economic and military support for Ukraine, which HM Treasury called the largest ever package of UK financial backing for the country and which takes total UK commitments to 35 billion pounds. The package is 7 billion pounds of economic support made available over three years, including up to 5.5 billion pounds of World Bank guarantees and a 500 million pound grant contribution to the World Bank International Development Association crisis facility, a 3 billion pound increase in UK Export Finance capacity for Ukraine, bringing its total to 6.5 billion pounds, and 500 million pounds of additional military support for Ukraine's air defences this winter. It is in addition to the 3 billion pounds a year already committed for as long as it takes. Prime Minister Andy Burnham and Chancellor John Healey told President Zelenskyy of the decision this morning.

**Latest, October 28:** Chancellor John Healey has delivered his first Budget, and the fiscal rules are met with a smaller buffer. Healey told the Commons that the Office for Budget Responsibility puts headroom against the stability rule at 15 billion pounds in 2029-30, rising to 22 billion pounds in 2030-31, against 24 billion pounds of headroom at the March forecast. He said he had chosen to use some of the headroom established last year to absorb the impact of the Iran war, and that borrowing falls from 4.2 percent of GDP this year to 3.4 percent next year, 2.9 percent in 2028-29, 2.1 percent in 2029-30 and 1.8 percent in 2030-31. The OBR forecasts growth of 1.2 percent this year and 1.0 percent next year, then 1.6 percent in 2028, 1.8 percent in 2029, 1.7 percent in 2030 and 1.6 percent in 2031, with debt at 83.2 percent of GDP this year and 83.8 percent next year before falling to 81.7 percent by the end of the forecast.

The main revenue measure is narrower than lenders had feared. The surcharge on bank profits rises from 3 percent to 6 percent, partially reversing the cut from 8 percent made in 2023, and Healey said the rise is temporary and will last 15 months. Shares in the large British banks rallied on Wednesday after the increase came in smaller than feared, Reuters reported. Other new levies fall on drivers, property and gambling: a pay per mile duty for electric cars from April 2028, at 3p a mile for electric vehicles and 1.5p a mile for plug-in hybrids, a surcharge on homes worth more than 2 million pounds from April 2028, remote gaming duty rising from 21 percent to 40 percent, and remote general betting duty rising from 15 percent to 25 percent from April 2027.

Household support is the spending centrepiece. About 20 million households in council tax bands A to D will receive a 100 pound payment, the cut in VAT on electricity bills will be maintained until July next year, the planned fuel duty rises have been cancelled with rates frozen, the state pension rises 4.8 percent in April in line with earnings, and the minimum wage rises 3.9 percent. The abolition of standing charges on gas bills, announced by the government this morning, is covered in a separate story on this site.

Britain's Budget today is a rates event before it is a political one. Chancellor John Healey delivers his first Budget, alongside the Office for Budget Responsibility's updated outlook and the Debt Management Office's revised borrowing plan, to a gilt market that has already marked its conditions: headroom against the fiscal rules in double digits, credibly assembled, and no surprise increase in long dated supply.

The starting position leaves little slack. Borrowing between April and September reached 97.8 billion pounds against a forecast of 91.8 billion, and higher yields and the energy shock have eaten into the Chancellor's buffer since the spring, with economists estimating what remains at anywhere between 8.5 billion and 16 billion pounds. A poll of all 15 primary dealers expects 2026/27 gilt issuance to rise by 3.4 billion pounds to 249.6 billion, with roughly 42.7 percent in short dated gilts. The DMO has kept long dated supply in check, and analysts warn that any surprise increase at the long end would hit the 30 year gilt immediately.

Revenue is expected to come from narrow bases, because income tax, National Insurance and VAT rises have been ruled out. Around 5 billion pounds of tax rises on online retailers, gambling, bank profits and the tax gap is expected, and reports before the statement suggested the bank corporation tax surcharge could rise from 3 percent to 8 percent, a measure Broadstone estimated could raise more than 2 billion pounds a year, while warning that lenders could respond by tightening credit. Bloomberg reported on Wednesday that the bank tax hike was set to be less severe than lenders had feared; the confirmed figure comes with the statement.

Sterling went into the day softer, at 1.3284 against the dollar, down 0.46 percent on the day and 0.67 percent on the week in this site's tables. The interaction the market is pricing is with Threadneedle Street: inflation jumped to 3.5 percent in September, Bank Rate stands at 3.75 percent, and markets are braced for a possible hike on November 5. If the OBR judges that the cost of living package pushes inflation higher, the Bank of England is more likely to tighten, raising the government's own debt costs at the same moment.
