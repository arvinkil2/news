**Latest (Tuesday, October 13):** Bumble confirmed the deal on Tuesday, agreeing to be acquired by Ithaca Holdings for $1.3 billion and taken private, above the roughly $1 billion valuation reported in Monday's talks. The agreement followed a strategic review by an independent committee of Bumble's board; founder Whitney Wolfe Herd, who returned as chief executive in March 2025, did not take part in the board's discussions or vote, and said she will stay actively involved in the business alongside Ithaca. Bumble's second-quarter revenue fell 15.2 percent year on year to $211 million, with paying users down 16.4 percent to about 3.2 million.

Bumble is in advanced talks to be taken private by Ithaca Holdings, a holding company for technology businesses, in a deal valuing the Austin, Texas based dating app operator at roughly $1 billion, according to a source familiar with the matter cited by Reuters on Monday. Shares surged about 50 percent to $3.81 following the report, against a market value of roughly $389 million at Friday’s close, and neither company responded to requests for comment.

The talks follow a slide in the business Wolfe Herd founded in 2014: total paying users fell 16.4 percent in the second quarter to 3.2 million, and 2025 revenue declined nearly 10 percent to about $966 million. Wolfe Herd returned as chief executive in March 2025 and has been adding AI powered features as the wider dating sector grapples with weaker engagement among younger users.
