The European Commission's Autumn 2026 Economic Forecast raised EU growth to 1.4 percent in 2026, higher than projected in spring after a stronger than expected first three quarters, and held it at 1.4 percent in 2027 and 1.5 percent in 2028, with euro area growth at 1.2 percent in both 2026 and 2027. Inflation moved the other way: euro area inflation is now put at 3.2 percent in 2026 and 3.1 percent in 2027, which Commissioner Valdis Dombrovskis said is 0.8 percentage points higher than projected in spring, and EU inflation at 3.3 percent and 3.2 percent. Headline inflation is expected to peak above 4 percent between late 2026 and early 2027 before easing, and the decline to the ECB's 2 percent target is delayed to 2028, when euro area inflation is forecast at 2.1 percent.

The Commission traced the inflation overshoot to energy: euro area inflation reached 4.0 percent in October as retail energy prices surged almost 21 percent year on year, Brent crude exceeded 100 dollars in October, and EU gas storage stood at 75 percent of capacity at the end of October, the lowest level for the season since records began in 2011. Food inflation, which began rising in October, is set to accelerate on delayed pass-through of energy and fertiliser costs and on crop damage from the summer's extreme weather. The fiscal position deteriorates through the forecast: the average EU deficit rises from 3.1 percent of GDP in 2025 to 3.6 percent in 2026, 3.7 percent in 2027 and 3.8 percent in 2028, on defence spending, rising interest costs and measures shielding households and firms from energy prices, while EU debt climbs from 82.6 percent of GDP to 85.6 percent by 2028. Unemployment is set to stay just above 6 percent, close to its record low, with 800,000 additional jobs expected in 2026.
