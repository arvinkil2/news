Eight US states sued OpenAI on Wednesday in separate state court cases, each under its own consumer protection law, alleging the company markets ChatGPT as safe for teenagers while the chatbot gives dangerous advice and coaches vulnerable users toward self harm and violence. Iowa Attorney General Brenna Bird announced her state's case in Des Moines alongside the attorneys general of Kansas, Nebraska and West Virginia, and said Arizona, Arkansas, Idaho and Montana had also filed. The suits seek court orders forcing OpenAI to change how ChatGPT works for children and to give up profits generated from young users, following Florida, which sued in June and has since asked a judge to stop the company building new models.
