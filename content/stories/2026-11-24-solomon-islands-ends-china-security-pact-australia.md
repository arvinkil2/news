The Solomon Islands has informed China it will terminate the security agreement the two countries signed in 2022, the Sydney Morning Herald reported on Tuesday, ahead of Prime Minister Matthew Wale's visit to Australia. Wale's government recently told Beijing it would not renew the five year security pact when it expires in April, the newspaper reported, citing unnamed sources from the Solomon Islands.

Australia and the Solomon Islands are expected to sign a new comprehensive treaty that would cement Australia as the Pacific nation's top security partner when Wale meets Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese on Wednesday. The Herald reported that China's policing presence in the Solomons is expected to be scaled back significantly as a result of the decision, which had been closely guarded to avoid angering Beijing. Australia's foreign ministry and the Solomon Islands embassy in Canberra did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

The Solomon Islands occupies a strategic position 1,600 kilometres northeast of Australia, and the country's deepening ties with China were among voters' concerns in the election in May that led to Wale becoming prime minister. Wale, who was elected in May, survived a no confidence motion in September that was widely believed to have been linked to China's efforts to scuttle negotiations on a comprehensive treaty with Australia. He had vowed during a visit to Australia in June that he would review the security agreement with China and promised a reset in the relationship with Australia. Former prime minister Manasseh Sogavare, who signed the 2022 pact, warned Wale against scrapping the agreement earlier on Tuesday.

The 2022 agreement, a leaked copy of which states that it remains valid for five years and would automatically extend for another five years without six months' written notice from either side, allows the Solomons to ask China to send police and military personnel to help maintain social order and allows Chinese naval vessels to make stopovers in the country. Its signing in 2022 prompted Australia to broker its own security agreements with Tuvalu, Nauru, Papua New Guinea, Vanuatu and Fiji. Australia and the Solomon Islands pledged in July to deepen bilateral ties and jointly criticised China's test of a ballistic missile launched from a nuclear powered submarine in the Pacific.

China said it has grown its ties with the Solomon Islands based on mutual respect and equality, and that it does not engage in geopolitical manoeuvring or target any third party in its cooperation with Pacific Island nations. "The international community should jointly support the sustainable development of Pacific Island nations, prioritising their autonomous development, maintaining openness and inclusiveness, and ensuring that relevant cooperation is not directed against any third party," foreign ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun said at a regular news briefing.
