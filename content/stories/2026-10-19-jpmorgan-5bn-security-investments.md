JPMorgan Chase has put 5 billion dollars into startup investments in the first year of its security and resiliency initiative, halfway to the 10 billion dollar direct investment goal it set for a decade, CNBC reported on Monday. Most of the stakes have not been disclosed; the two named are Hadrian, which builds automated factories for defence, aerospace and maritime parts and raised 1.4 billion dollars in an August round JPMorgan helped lead, and Castelion, a hypersonic missile maker founded by former SpaceX engineers that raised 1 billion dollars the same month. The direct investments sit inside a wider plan to finance and facilitate 1.5 trillion dollars over 10 years for industries tied to national security, of which the bank says it handled more than 230 billion dollars in the first year.
