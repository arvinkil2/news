**Latest (Tuesday 14:28 ET):** Kenya's ninth Ebola case is a 24 year old man who was already in quarantine when he fell ill and was isolated at the first sign of illness, the Ministry of Health said on Tuesday. He is a close family member of Kenya's first patient, who travelled from Congo through Uganda and died in Nairobi on October 5. Director General Patrick Amoth said all nine cases belong to one cluster, with no evidence of transmission outside the group; eight patients are stable and responding to treatment, 134 of 135 contacts have been traced and quarantined, and most contacts who stayed well through the 21 day monitoring period can return home from October 25. Kenya has imposed no restrictions on travel, trade or public gatherings.

**Latest (Sunday 21:47 ET):** Al Jazeera reported that the eighth patient worked as a patient attendant, cleaning utensils and linens, and was likely exposed to Kenya's first Ebola case, according to a Ministry of Health source; he developed symptoms on Saturday and was quarantined before testing positive. The account fills the exposure gap left by the ministry's earlier statements and, if correct, keeps the Kenyan cluster tied to the first patient's known contacts rather than to untraced community transmission.

**Latest (Sunday 15:47 ET):** Kenya's confirmed Ebola cases rose to eight on Sunday after one more person tested positive, Director General for Health Patrick Amoth said on Citizen TV. The patient is a 49 year old man who is not a direct health care worker; he developed fever, headache and muscle pain, was one of 38 people tested on Sunday, and has been isolated. The ministry has not provided his exposure history or said whether he was among the contacts already being monitored. Amoth maintained that the risk to the general public remains low and that there is no evidence of community transmission, and the ministry has warned that more cases could emerge among quarantined contacts before the 21 day monitoring period ends.

Kenya's health ministry said on Saturday that four more people have tested positive for the Ebola Bundibugyo virus, taking the country's confirmed total to seven, with three of the new cases health workers who treated the first patient and the fourth a relative of that patient, who died on October 5 after falling ill in the Democratic Republic of Congo and flying to Nairobi. The cases are the first known to have been passed on inside Kenya, and they are linked to the Congo outbreak, which has recorded more than 9,000 cases and more than 4,300 deaths since it was detected in May. The ministry said the relative had been in quarantine since October 6, that 128 of the first patient's 130 contacts had been traced and quarantined, and that there is no evidence the virus is spreading in the community.

Update, October 18: the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention has reaffirmed its support for Kenya's response, in a statement issued from Addis Ababa on October 17, and called on member states to strengthen cross border surveillance and information sharing. The agency said it is working with the Kenyan government on surveillance, technical coordination, risk communication and community engagement, commended the speed and transparency of the response, and confirmed all four new patients are receiving supportive care at a designated treatment facility. It reported no change to the case count of seven and no evidence of community transmission.
