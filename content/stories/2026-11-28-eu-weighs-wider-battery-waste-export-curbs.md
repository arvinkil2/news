The European Union is weighing tighter export controls on battery waste to stop critical minerals from flowing to China, the Financial Times reported on November 27. The measures under consideration would build on the reclassification of black mass and waste lithium batteries as hazardous waste, which from December 9 bars their export for recovery to countries outside the OECD, China included, under the EU Waste Shipment Regulation.

Black mass, the powder left after spent batteries are shredded, carries lithium, nickel, cobalt, manganese and graphite, and a substantial share of Europe's output is still shipped to Asia for final recovery, leaving EU recycling capacity underused. The Commission has announced no new proposal, and officials are reportedly concerned that traders could route material through OECD countries to get around the December ban.
