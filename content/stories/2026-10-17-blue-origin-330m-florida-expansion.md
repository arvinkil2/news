Blue Origin will spend 330 million dollars expanding its Florida production and integration facilities to support a heavier lift version of its New Glenn rocket, the New Glenn 9x4, with completion targeted for summer 2027, operations chief Ian Richardson said in a company post reported by Reuters. The expansion would allow production of up to 150 upper stages a year.

The company has invested more than 3 billion dollars to date in the Florida complex, which spans about 31 acres and employs some 5,000 people, and plans additional launch and landing pads. The stated goal is infrastructure that supports multiple flights per week by the end of the decade.

The spending follows a hard year. New Glenn has been grounded since an explosion in May badly damaged its only launchpad days before a planned launch of Amazon satellites, and its April flight put an AST SpaceMobile satellite in the wrong orbit. Return to flight is expected early next year.

Against SpaceX's launch dominance, Blue Origin's constraint is industrial, not conceptual: one pad, one production line, three flights since the 2025 debut. The 330 million dollars is aimed squarely at that bottleneck.
