Japan and Saudi Arabia agreed on Friday to expand their joint crude oil stockpiling arrangement, with Japan lending the kingdom temporary use of an additional 16 million barrels of storage at its national stockpiling facilities, on top of the 8 million barrels Saudi Arabia already holds there. Japan also intends to buy up to 8 million barrels of Saudi crude for its national petroleum reserve, subject to favourable market conditions and internal procedures, and Saudi Arabia has already begun refilling the tanks in Japan, according to the joint statement carried by the Saudi Press Agency.

The agreement follows a November 9 virtual meeting between Saudi Energy Minister Prince Abdulaziz bin Salman and Japan's Minister of Economy, Trade and Industry Ryosei Akazawa. Japan released oil from its national reserves last month as part of the International Energy Agency's coordinated drawdown, in which member countries agreed to release a record 400 million barrels, and under the stockpiling arrangement Tokyo holds preferential rights to buy the Saudi crude stored in Japan during a supply emergency.
