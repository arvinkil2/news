Europe's mayors took their energy bill to the summit on Friday, meeting EU leaders in Brussels under the Covenant of Mayors banner to press for affordable energy and climate resilience funding for cities. The meeting ran alongside the European Council, a piece of choreography that put municipal leaders inside the building while heads of government argued about competitiveness and energy prices upstairs.

The mayors' ask is concrete: money and rules that let cities retrofit housing, electrify heating and protect vulnerable residents from the energy prices the Hormuz closure keeps elevated. Cities sign the cheques for district heating support and social housing energy costs, which makes them the first political surface the energy shock touches.

The Commission's energy directorate publicised the meeting for a reason. The summit conclusions' language on energy prices and competitiveness, and the grids package the IEA backed this week in the commodities file, both depend on municipal delivery. Brussels can legislate the market; mayors operate it.
