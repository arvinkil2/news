The Democratic Republic of Congo will receive $965 million in mining sector investment, organizers announced at the opening of the Makutano Mining Forum in Kinshasa on November 24. Of the total, $700 million is earmarked for financing in the Congolese mining sector, with financial institutions committing to support the sector and its entire value chain. The remaining $265 million is being provided through the International Finance Corporation, the private sector arm of the World Bank Group, to support private businesses in the country.

The financing will support small and medium sized enterprises, mining subcontracting, local processing of resources and trade finance, according to the announcement. Details on the individual financial institutions involved, the specific projects to receive funding, disbursement timelines and financing conditions were not provided.

President Felix Tshisekedi used the opening ceremony to reaffirm Congo's aim to move beyond extracting resources to creating added value through local processing. The announcement lands days after Congo reimposed its cobalt export ban as prices slid, a separate measure aimed at supporting prices for a metal the country dominates. Together the two moves frame Kinshasa's current strategy: restrict raw exports where it has market power, and channel finance toward the domestic processing and services economy around the mines.
