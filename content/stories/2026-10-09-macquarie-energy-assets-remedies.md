The Competition and Markets Authority said on October 9 that there are reasonable grounds for believing the undertakings offered by Macquarie Asset Management and Energy Assets Group, or a modified version of them, might be accepted under the Enterprise Act 2002. The remedy Macquarie proposed is to sell EAG's business providing non-domestic, non-smart gas meter reading services to commercial customers to a CMA-approved purchaser who can maintain it as a viable competitor.

The CMA's phase 1 investigation found the merger between Macquarie-controlled National Gas Metering and EAG, currently the largest supplier of these services, would materially increase concentration in an already concentrated market. The combined share is very high, and the regulator names only two other main competitors: Stark, in which Macquarie holds a minority interest, and SMS. The CMA will consider the undertakings in more detail within 40 working days, seeking third-party feedback and vetting potential buyers; if satisfied, it will conditionally clear the deal, otherwise it can still refer it for a phase 2 investigation.

The deal itself dates to February: Macquarie announced earlier this year it had agreed to buy Scotland-based EAG from its consortium of owners, which includes Swiss Life, Asterion Industrial Partners and EDF Invest. EAG owns and operates smart-meter and last-mile utility connection infrastructure, while Macquarie's UK utility holdings already include Last Mile Infrastructure, National Gas, Cadent and Southern Water. The October 9 releases draw a line under the September 25 SLC decision and the October 2 undertakings deadline: remedies were offered in time, so the phase 2 referral is suspended while the CMA tests whether the divestment works.
