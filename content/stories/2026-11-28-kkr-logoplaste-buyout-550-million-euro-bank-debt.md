Banks have lined up about 550 million euros, or $603.5 million, of loans to finance KKR's acquisition of Logoplaste, the Portuguese rigid plastic packaging producer, according to people familiar with the situation. Barclays, Banco Santander, Rabobank, UniCredit and Goldman Sachs are among the arrangers, and Logoplaste also plans a new 150 million euro revolving credit facility.

KKR agreed earlier this month to buy Logoplaste from the Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan, its majority owner, in a transaction that could value the company at more than 1.2 billion euros. Logoplaste carries about 770 million euros equivalent of existing term loans maturing in 2028, which the new capital structure has to address.

Logoplaste makes rigid plastic packaging on or near the plants of large consumer goods customers, a contracted model that lenders treat as infrastructure-like. The financing is a bank syndication rather than a private credit deal, continuing the pattern of banks winning back larger European buyout financings this month.

For the seller, the exit closes a holding bought at a reported valuation near 1.4 billion euros in 2021. A sale above 1.2 billion euros would still leave Ontario Teachers short of that mark, a reminder that pandemic-era packaging valuations were set when rates and resin economics were very different.
