**Latest, October 29 (afternoon):** The count is nearly complete and the deadlock is settled in shape if not in outcome. At about 99 percent of votes counted, Deutsche Welle's live coverage puts Netanyahu's Likud led bloc on 53 seats, the bloc led by Gadi Eisenkot on 48 and the Arab parties on 19, a record that beats their previous high of 15, with neither main bloc near the 61 seats needed to govern and difficult coalition talks ahead. The fraud dispute has also moved against Likud: Israeli police said there is no evidence to support the party's claims of large scale foreign involvement led by Qatar and Turkey, a senior Central Elections Committee official denied the allegations, and the committee's acting director general, Dean Livne, wrote that the elections were conducted lawfully and that there was no basis for allegations of systematic violations of election integrity. Qatar categorically denied interfering in the election or funding activities to influence it, with foreign ministry spokesman Majed Al Ansari calling the accusations a disinformation campaign. Ra'am leader Mansour Abbas welcomed any investigation and said his party had nothing to do with interference. A final tally is expected by October 30, and official finalized results are due on November 4, after which President Herzog receives the results and begins coalition consultations.

**Latest, October 29:** The count has moved to the absentee and double envelope ballots, the soldiers' and overseas votes that were always scheduled for today, with coalition talks set to begin in parallel, The Guardian's live coverage reported. Arab News reported that Netanyahu is set to remain caretaker prime minister after the inconclusive election, and Bloomberg described the phase ahead as protracted horse-trading between blocs neither of which can reach 61 seats without partners that have pledged not to sit together. Likud's fraud allegations, carried in full below, remain allegations: no certified result is due until November 4, and no new seat total beyond yesterday's roughly 90 percent count was established this morning.

**Latest, October 28:** Likud has alleged that the election was stolen. In a statement shared by Netanyahu on X, the party said: “The elections in the State of Israel were stolen by Arab parties that support terrorism and the destruction of the Jewish state, through serious criminal offenses involving bribery, threats, violence, fraud, and the use of prohibited software to mobilize voters.” Likud said it is forwarding hundreds of witness accounts and pieces of evidence to the police, the Shin Bet and the Central Elections Committee, citing envelopes containing cash alongside Ra’am ballot papers, threats and violence against polling officials, multiple voters entering booths together, voting under duplicate identities, and discrepancies between ballot envelopes and voter totals at polling stations in Arab communities. The party demanded immediate investigations and prosecution. These are allegations, not findings: the committee said it had received Likud’s request and that it is under review, and it has found “no indications of systematic tampering with ballot slips” while it examines individual complaints, Clash Report reported. Police confirmed three arrests in the Negev and the seizure of more than 24,000 shekels in cash alongside Ra’am ballot papers, with no link established to senior party officials, and Likud submitted about 775 complaints on election day. Mansour Abbas had dismissed Netanyahu’s earlier election day allegations as “lies” and “incitement.”

The count behind the dispute has also moved. With about 85 to 93 percent of ballots counted, partial results put Netanyahu’s bloc on about 53 seats and the Arab parties on about 19, the Joint List on about 12 and Ra’am on about seven, above their previous record of 15 seats in 2020, with Likud the largest party on 26 to 27 seats. The Central Elections Committee has corrected its estimate of double envelope ballots, including soldiers’ votes, to 430,000 to 480,000, from about 600,000 earlier, and those ballots are counted on October 29. President Trump said he would see if there is a way he can help form a unity government in Israel, Israel RealTime reported.

Latest count: with 84.66 percent of ballots counted, the Central Elections Committee figures carried by The Petek's live count put Netanyahu's bloc on 54 seats, the bloc led by Eisenkot on 49 and the Arab parties on 17, still leaving both main blocs short of the 61 seats needed to govern. At 77.6 percent counted, All Israel News put the Joint List on 11 seats and Ra'am on 6 within the Arab parties' 17, with Likud the largest party on 27 and Yashar on 22, and turnout at 77.7 percent. The remaining ballots include the double envelope votes, which are counted on October 29, and official results are due on November 4.

Latest count: with 72.4 percent of votes counted, the Central Elections Committee's partial results, as reported by the Times of Israel, project Netanyahu's bloc on 54 seats, the Eisenkot led bloc on 48 and the predominantly Arab parties on 18, split between the Joint List on 12 and Ra'am on 6. Likud remains the largest single party on 27 seats and Yashar second on 21. An earlier snapshot at 41.9 percent counted had put the blocs on 57 and 47, so Netanyahu's projected total has narrowed as the count has advanced, and the special votes still to come can move it further in either direction.

Partial results in Israel's parliamentary election leave both blocs short of the 61 seats needed to form a government in the 120 seat Knesset. Deutsche Welle's live coverage of the count put Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc on about 56 seats after around two thirds of votes had been counted, the bloc led by former army chief Gadi Eisenkot on about 48, and the Arab parties on about 16, after an earlier snapshot at 57.3 percent counted had Likud as the largest single party on 27 seats and Eisenkot's Yashar on 21. A record 650,000 special votes, cast outside assigned polling stations and worth roughly 14 seats, are counted later and could still change the picture.

Netanyahu stopped short of declaring victory, said Likud was the largest party and called for a strong right wing government, rejecting any government that relies on Arab parties. Eisenkot said voters had chosen change and voted against Netanyahu continuing as prime minister, and pledged to work to form a government. On the latest figures, Eisenkot's bloc and the Arab parties together would hold a majority, and Mansour Abbas, whose Ra'am party was part of the coalition that briefly unseated Netanyahu in 2021, has said he wants to join the next government, while several opposition parties have pledged not to work with Arab majority lists. Turnout reached more than 77 percent, the BBC reported.
