Exit polls in Israel's parliamentary election put Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc on 59 seats and the bloc led by former army chief Gadi Eisenkot on 57 in the 120 seat Knesset, both short of the 61 seats needed to form a government, the BBC reported, with the Arab party Ra'am projected to win the remaining four seats. Netanyahu said the exit polls showed his Likud was the largest party and that he would form a strong right wing government, and warned against any coalition relying on Arab parties, while Eisenkot said change was still possible and pointed to Arab voter turnout as a factor in the result.

Neither leader can reach a majority on these numbers without Ra'am, whose leader Mansour Abbas has said the party would consider supporting a bloc only in return for major improvements in Arab communities, which puts the Arab parties in the position of potential kingmakers as the count continues.
