Latest count: with 72.4 percent of votes counted, the Central Elections Committee's partial results, as reported by the Times of Israel, project Netanyahu's bloc on 54 seats, the Eisenkot led bloc on 48 and the predominantly Arab parties on 18, split between the Joint List on 12 and Ra'am on 6. Likud remains the largest single party on 27 seats and Yashar second on 21. An earlier snapshot at 41.9 percent counted had put the blocs on 57 and 47, so Netanyahu's projected total has narrowed as the count has advanced, and the special votes still to come can move it further in either direction.

Partial results in Israel's parliamentary election leave both blocs short of the 61 seats needed to form a government in the 120 seat Knesset. Deutsche Welle's live coverage of the count put Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc on about 56 seats after around two thirds of votes had been counted, the bloc led by former army chief Gadi Eisenkot on about 48, and the Arab parties on about 16, after an earlier snapshot at 57.3 percent counted had Likud as the largest single party on 27 seats and Eisenkot's Yashar on 21. A record 650,000 special votes, cast outside assigned polling stations and worth roughly 14 seats, are counted later and could still change the picture.

Netanyahu stopped short of declaring victory, said Likud was the largest party and called for a strong right wing government, rejecting any government that relies on Arab parties. Eisenkot said voters had chosen change and voted against Netanyahu continuing as prime minister, and pledged to work to form a government. On the latest figures, Eisenkot's bloc and the Arab parties together would hold a majority, and Mansour Abbas, whose Ra'am party was part of the coalition that briefly unseated Netanyahu in 2021, has said he wants to join the next government, while several opposition parties have pledged not to work with Arab majority lists. Turnout reached more than 77 percent, the BBC reported.
