Erdene Resource Development has published a preliminary economic assessment for its wholly owned Zuun Mod molybdenum copper project in southwestern Mongolia, putting the build cost at 687 million dollars and the after tax net present value at 745 million dollars at an 8 percent discount rate. The proposed open pit would produce an average of 19.8 million pounds of molybdenum and 27.9 million pounds of copper a year over 34 years, with payback in the sixth production year. The assessment is preliminary: inferred resources account for about 74 percent of the proposed plant feed, and the study does not establish reserves.

The company's release puts all-in sustaining costs at 13.89 dollars a pound of molybdenum, with a molybdenum break-even near 18.50 dollars a pound, and notes the project sits about 280 kilometres from a railhead on the Mongolia-China border, a location the company expects to draw long term offtake interest from Asian buyers. At spot prices of 33 dollars a pound for molybdenum and 6.50 dollars for copper, Erdene estimates the after tax value rises to about 2.0 billion dollars with a 27.8 percent internal rate of return.

Erdene is seeking a partner for Zuun Mod, which would become a second development centre in its Khundii Minerals District alongside the Bayan Khundii gold mine, now in commercial production. Under an accelerated schedule the company sees production possible by 2031. Its shares rose 1.8 percent on Thursday to 5.71 Canadian dollars, valuing the company at about 374 million Canadian dollars.
