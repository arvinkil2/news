Silver miners are struggling to close a persistent supply gap. Global silver mine production is expected to remain virtually unchanged this year despite a sixth consecutive annual market deficit. Demand is forecast to exceed supply by 35 million ounces in 2026, bringing the cumulative shortfall since 2021 to about 746 million ounces, against projected annual mine output of 834 million ounces, according to figures from the Silver Institute and Metals Focus. Primary silver mines accounted for just 26.1 percent of global mine supply in 2025, the lowest proportion in the data series, with most silver produced as a byproduct of copper, lead, zinc and gold operations whose output decisions barely respond to the silver price. Deutsche Bank expects weakening industrial demand, including thrifting in solar applications, could tip the market into surplus as early as 2027.

In Canada, the First Nations Major Projects Coalition has entered a C$5.6 million (about US$3.9 million) multi year partnership with the BHP Foundation to strengthen how First Nations negotiate and structure their involvement in major projects. Funding through March 2031 will expand the independent advice and practical tools available to the coalition's more than 200 members. A study commissioned by the coalition found that upcoming natural resource projects in Canada represent a potential Indigenous equity opportunity of more than $50 billion.
