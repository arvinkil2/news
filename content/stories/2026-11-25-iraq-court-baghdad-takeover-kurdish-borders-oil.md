Iraq's Federal Supreme Court ruled on Wednesday that the federal government can deploy troops to the country's international borders, including in the semi-autonomous Kurdish region, and that federal authorities can oversee the agencies operating at all border crossings, coordinating with regional and provincial governments on customs. The court also said the federal oil ministry can oversee and audit oil and gas fields, with Baghdad sharing responsibility for managing production with regional governments and producing provinces, according to state television.

The ruling came a day after Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi ordered federal forces to take over Kurdish-run border crossings and demanded authority over oil fields controlled by Kurdish security forces, in an effort to curb oil smuggling. The Kurdistan Regional Government rejected what it called military language and threats, called the move an assault on its constitutional rights, and said it was working with federal authorities against corruption and smuggling. The federal government did not say how or when the takeover would be implemented.
