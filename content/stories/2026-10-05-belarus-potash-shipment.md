The Belarusian Potash Company loaded 30,000 tonnes of granular potash at Russia's Bronka port near St Petersburg on September 15, with ship-tracking data showing arrival at New Orleans expected on October 14. It is the first Belarusian potash to reach the US in four years, after Washington sanctioned the sector in 2021 over Alexander Lukashenko's crackdown on pro-democracy protests.

Washington began easing the sanctions in December as part of an arrangement tied to the release of political prisoners. Between 2017 and 2021, Belarus exported an average of 635,000 tonnes of potash a year to the US, according to Argus data. Analysts are skeptical the Belarusian volumes will move prices. StoneX fertilizer analyst Josh Linville said he doubts the shipments will play a major role in US supply or lower what farmers pay.

The Financial Times confirmed on October 4 that the vessel was Marshall Islands-flagged and loaded granular muriate of potash, noting US potash prices sit at record highs, which gives the cargo commercial logic beyond symbolism. Chinese import data show Belarusian potash moving elsewhere again: China imported 1.0 million tonnes in January to August, its first intake since November 2025. Whether this becomes a flow or a one-off is the open question: sanctions remain the gating issue, no follow-on cargoes have been reported, and 30,000 tonnes is modest against global potash trade. Fertilizer buyers and potash producers will read the October 14 arrival as a signal of US sanctions posture.
