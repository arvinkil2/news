Soybeans and corn closed higher for a third session on Wednesday in pre-holiday trade, while wheat again failed to follow. January soybeans closed up 10 cents at 12.44 dollars a bushel and March soybeans up 7 and three-quarter cents at 12.45 dollars. December corn closed up 5 and three-quarter cents at 4.39 and three-quarter dollars, and March corn up 6 and a quarter cents at 4.56 and a half dollars. March Chicago wheat closed down 1 and three-quarter cents at 5.87 and a half dollars, March Kansas City wheat down the same at 6.30 and a half dollars, and March Minneapolis wheat was effectively flat at 6.465 dollars.

The soybean market spent the morning lower on tariff headlines before recovering. DTN lead analyst Rhett Montgomery said early detail eased concern: US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer described the new tariffs as targeted rather than blanket, and the reciprocal tariff reductions in the US-China trade truce still appear slated to proceed, with China already over two-thirds of the way to its truce goal in known soybean commitments for 2026-27. A USDA flash sale of 132,000 tonnes of soybeans to China, reported Wednesday, added support, and Grain Central said further large Chinese purchases beyond the 25 million tonne pledge look likely, citing AgResource.

Meal remains the complex's leader. January soybean meal futures have risen more than 30 dollars a ton through November on strong demand and lower US reserves than first thought, and board crush premiums remain above average, estimated at 2.78 dollars gross. The other side of the crush is weak: soybean oil is at a nine-month low as expanding crush adds supply, and that dragged ICE January canola down 1.90 Canadian dollars to 771.30 Canadian dollars a tonne, with palm oil also lower.

Corn's support is increasingly a Ukraine story. Grain Central reported that only 422,170 tonnes of Ukrainian corn had shipped by November 23 against 860,000 tonnes contracted for the month, because the seaports that normally handle about 90 percent of corn exports have been effectively blocked since late July. The Ukrainian farmers' union UAC now sees 2026-27 exports of 12 to 14 million tonnes rather than 15 million, and says contract defaults have begun. In the US, ethanol production averaged 1.129 million barrels a day last week, up 3,000 barrels a day, and ethanol stocks rose 345,000 barrels to 25.3 million barrels, the highest since May 1.

Wheat's problem is demand, not supply news. DTN said the market is waiting for a positive demand cue, with SovEcon estimating Russian November exports at just 2.7 million tonnes, and Paris milling wheat lower for a third time in two weeks. Grain Central noted the offsetting shift: US hard red winter wheat is now competitive into destinations that recently looked out of reach, French wheat still sets the world price, and EU soft wheat exports reached 11.40 million tonnes by November 22, up 5 percent year on year. US markets are closed Thursday for Thanksgiving, with a half session on Friday.
