The Department of War and Lockheed Martin unveiled the framework on Thursday. It covers 850 US aircraft over 10 years, with associated initial spares, 10 years of modification kits and sustainment, and international customer orders across Lots 21 to 30, at a continued production rate of 156 aircraft a year. Lockheed said the framework is expected to save taxpayers billions of dollars over the program's life through long term pricing and volume discounts, and that it will support 317,000 US jobs and about $79 billion in annual economic activity over the next decade.

The agreement is a framework, not a signed contract. If Congress grants multiyear authority and the associated appropriations in the coming months, the F-35 Joint Program Office and Lockheed will finalise contract terms, pricing and schedules through a formal contract award, and the Pentagon statement carried no value for the deal. Bloomberg described the planned purchase as the biggest in the program's quarter century history. More than 1,375 F-35s are currently fielded worldwide, according to Lockheed.
