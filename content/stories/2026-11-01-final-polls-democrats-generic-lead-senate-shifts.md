The last public evidence before Tuesday points one way. A generic ballot poll reported by The Hill gives Democrats a 10 point lead over Republicans for Congress. Sabato's Crystal Ball has moved two Senate races, Minnesota and Louisiana, toward the Democrats in its final adjustments, and Bloomberg reported Florida and Nebraska shifting left in the homestretch as well, Nebraska on the strength of Dan Osborn's independent candidacy rather than a Democratic nominee.

Ratings shifts this late are rarely about new candidate news. They aggregate the generic environment, early voting patterns and the final public polls, and when several independent forecasters move the same direction in the same week it usually means the late data is breaking consistently. The Republican side's own voices have supplied the matching quote this cycle: Senator Ted Cruz warned his party could lose eight Senate seats and get swept in Texas, a warning carried in yesterday's edition.

The caution is the usual one, and it is real. Generic ballot leads do not distribute evenly across districts, Senate races turn on state level candidate quality, and the 10 point figure is one poll's topline without published internals in the reporting read. What the combined picture does establish is that no late evidence in the pool points toward a Republican surge; every final week indicator in this edition's pool leans the same way.

Status: reported polling and forecaster ratings, snapshots rather than results. The count starts Tuesday.
