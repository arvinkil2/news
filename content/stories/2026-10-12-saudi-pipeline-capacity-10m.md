Saudi Arabia is looking at ways to expand its oil pipeline capacity to destinations outside the Persian Gulf to 10 million barrels a day within two to three years, Energy Minister Prince Abdulaziz bin Salman told the WPC Energy Congress in Riyadh on Monday. The plan combines upgraded pumping stations on the East-West pipeline, which was moving 5.8 million barrels a day as of October 6 after a September drone attack knocked it offline, with repurposing a gas pipeline to carry crude: about 1.2 million barrels a day within seven to eight weeks, potentially rising to 3 million with further investment.

The repurposed line would revert to domestic gas service after May, ahead of summer demand, and Aramco is installing dual-use pumping and compression so the pipeline can carry oil or gas as circumstances require. The minister said the East-West pipeline's wartime role, carrying crude from the eastern producing region to Yanbu on the Red Sea and bypassing the Strait of Hormuz, had more than recovered its cost.
