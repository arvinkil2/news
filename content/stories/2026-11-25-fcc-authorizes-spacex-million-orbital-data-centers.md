The Federal Communications Commission said on Wednesday it has granted SpaceX authorization to deploy one million orbital compute modules, which it calls the Starmind constellation, to process data in space for use on Earth, describing it as the first authorization in US history for a constellation of data centers in space. The system would rely mainly on optical inter-satellite links, backed by radiofrequency links in the 18.8 to 19.3 GHz downlink and 28.6 to 29.1 GHz uplink bands for telemetry, tracking and command, and the FCC said it operates consistent with the agency's orbital debris rules.

Chairman Brendan Carr said the action "opens a bold new frontier for the computing infrastructure needed to power America's leadership and growth," and the commission said it is continuing to review additional orbital data center proposals from SpaceX's competitors and intends to decide each expeditiously.
