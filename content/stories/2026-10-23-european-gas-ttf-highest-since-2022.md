**Latest, October 26:** The front-month Dutch TTF price rose 2.1 percent early on Monday to as much as 95.73 euros per megawatt-hour, about 107.40 dollars and more than 30 dollars per million British thermal units, its highest since the autumn of 2022, OilPrice reported. The move extends a 7 percent gain last week, as several EU countries began withdrawing gas from storage amid lower temperatures and Qatari LNG cargoes, slowed to a trickle over the past seven months, show no sign of returning. EU storage sites were 74.62 percent full as of October 25, per Gas Infrastructure Europe, well below the five-year average of about 86 percent for this time of year. Saxo Bank's Ole Hansen put European inventories at around 845 terawatt-hours, approximately 100 terawatt-hours below the level a year earlier, a shortfall he said equals the energy carried by more than 80 large LNG carriers.

The front-month Dutch TTF price jumped 3 percent at the open in Amsterdam on Friday to 92.30 euros per megawatt-hour, about 103.40 dollars and more than 30 dollars per million British thermal units, its highest since December 2022, after settling above 90 euros on Thursday. A second snapshot at 0630 GMT put TTF at 91.28 euros, up 1.37 percent, confirming the level if not the exact print. The move came as Middle East escalation continues to restrict Qatari LNG flows, some European countries have started drawing on storage as temperatures drop, and Brent crude hit a five-month high.

The buffer is thin. EU storage sites were 74.62 percent full as of October 22, per Gas Infrastructure Europe data, against a five-year average of about 86 percent for this point in the year, and ING strategists Warren Patterson and Ewa Manthey wrote that storage appears to have peaked at just under 75 percent full, with LNG flows likely to be largely absent for at least much of the northern hemisphere winter. Anadolu, citing shipping data, put Middle Eastern LNG shipments around 80 percent below year-earlier levels, which raises competition between European and Asian buyers for the cargoes that remain.

The squeeze is not only European. US natural gas rose 12.09 percent to 3.13 dollars per million British thermal units in the latest market tables, the largest one-day move in the commodity complex, after trading near 2.79 dollars earlier in the week. No single US supply headline in this morning's reporting explains a move of that size on its own; it arrived alongside the European spike, colder forecasts and the broader energy risk premium, and it unwinds most of the autumn slide in a single session.
