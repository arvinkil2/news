**Update, November 1:** The shelving is now corroborated across several outlets, and the shape of the opposition is clearer. Fierce Network, TheStreet and Datacenter Dynamics have all carried the Bloomberg account: Deutsche Telekom opted against a formal bid for the time being after pushback from its works council, whose employee representatives hold half the seats on the supervisory board, and from activist investor Elliott Investment Management, which took a stake in September and pressed the company to drop the plan and favour shareholder returns such as larger buybacks instead. The combination, first reported in April as a possible joint holding company structure, was valued at roughly 300 billion dollars in Semafor's July reporting, which also said talks had fizzled over the summer as T-Mobile US executives cooled on it over shareholder and regulatory concerns. Deutsche Telekom already owns more than half of T-Mobile US, so the pause changes nothing about control; it removes the near term prospect of the largest public merger ever attempted. Separately, an October 26 SEC filing recorded former T-Mobile chief executive Mike Sievert's resignation from the board. Deutsche Telekom reports third quarter results on November 5.

Deutsche Telekom has opted against submitting a formal bid for the time being to combine with T-Mobile US, its American arm, Bloomberg reported on October 28, 2026, describing the planned combination as what would be the largest-ever public merger. The decision followed, in part, pushback from the German company's works council, according to people familiar with the matter cited by Bloomberg, who declined to be identified because the discussions are confidential. No bid terms or figures were reported in the portion of the article that could be read, and neither company had issued a statement on the decision at this update.
