**Latest (Saturday night, October 10):** The figures are now in conflict. A direct read of the New York Times piece behind its login gate (preview paragraphs only) describes a larger and possibly separate revision: 86 deaths and 234 injuries, up from 39 killed and 131 wounded, announced Friday by chief Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell after a "comprehensive review" that found dozens of service members wounded in the opening days had not been properly recorded. Per the preview, 34 of the 47 newly confirmed deaths occurred on June 22, when Iranian ballistic missiles struck Al Udeid Air Base in Qatar during the opening hours of the American bombing campaign, with the other 13 in subsequent missile and drone attacks; the revised figures include troops who died of wounds and traumatic brain injuries from strikes the Pentagon had previously described as causing no American casualties. That clashes with the DCAS-based 21-dead figure reported by the Washington Post, The Hill and Task & Purpose, and with the 865-wounded DCAS count. Whether the two tallies cover different operations or the same count described differently is not yet clear from the visible preview; treat both as reported pending the full Pentagon statement.

The Pentagon updated its Defense Casualty Analysis System late Friday to list 21 US troop deaths tied to operations against Iran, adding two noncombat deaths that had gone uncounted: Army National Guard Maj. Sorffly Davius, 46, who died of a medical emergency in Kuwait on March 6, and an unidentified Navy sailor who died by suicide in September. The database also lists 865 wounded, and a separate entry for Sgt. Devin A. Seibel, killed in a May training accident in Iraq, brings the broader Middle East death count to 22. The revision follows the Washington Post's September reporting, based on six US officials, that actual losses exceeded the public figures, which Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth had dismissed as "a complete lie." The Pentagon added a new "pending adjudication" category to its casualty reporting; House Armed Services Democrats Adam Smith and Chrissy Houlahan called the correction an embarrassment and demanded audits.
