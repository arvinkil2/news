**Latest.** Japan's Financial Services Agency has issued a reporting order to Rakuten Securities under the Financial Instruments and Exchange Act, requiring the company to report its analysis of how the unauthorized access happened and the measures it will take to prevent a repeat, the Asahi Shimbun reported. The agency acted because of the scale of the information that may have leaked and because it included sensitive data such as customers' financial asset holdings and annual income; ordering a report from a financial institution that suffered unauthorized access is unusual, and the agency will consider administrative action, including a business improvement order, if the report shows deficiencies in how the company manages customer information.

Rakuten Securities said on Tuesday that unauthorized access to its systems may have leaked up to 4.47 million customer records, covering names, addresses, telephone numbers, employers, the bank account numbers customers use for withdrawals, and their self-declared annual income and financial assets. The company said passwords and PINs were not leaked, though up to 11.25 million account login IDs may also have been exposed. Rakuten detected suspicious access on November 16 and found the information may have leaked externally on November 11; it is contacting affected customers individually and has apologized.
