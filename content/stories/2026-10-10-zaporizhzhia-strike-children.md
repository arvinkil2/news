**Latest (Saturday morning):** The death toll from the Zaporizhzhia strike rose to at least 15, including three children, with at least 16 wounded and more people believed to be missing under the rubble, President Zelensky and emergency services said. Five glide bombs hit residential buildings in the south-eastern city; acting Mayor Regina Kharchenko said one apartment block was mostly destroyed, with the ground floor hit and the floors above collapsing. More than 80 rescue workers were searching the rubble, regularly stopping to listen for survivors.

Zelensky said 13 other regions were also hit overnight and vowed a response. Tying the attack to Washington's new posture, he wrote that "Russia interprets any easing of pressure as a signal that it can supposedly continue the war," adding that Russia "must feel its losses." He had already criticized Trump's diesel deal as "an investment in a war that must be ended, not prolonged." The strike came hours after the deal's announcement.

Russian aerial bombs struck homes in Zaporizhzhia on Saturday, killing at least eight people including three children, according to regional officials cited by the Associated Press. At least 16 people were wounded, and authorities warned the toll could rise as rescue and identification work continued. The strike coincided with the opening of US-Ukrainian-European talks in Miami aimed at finding a path to end the war.

The attack caps a week of heavy civilian casualties. On Thursday, at least 33 people were killed in another Russian glide bomb attack on two civilian buses in Kramatorsk, and on Wednesday 22 people including five children died when a Russian missile hit an apartment block in Pryluky, east of Kyiv. The UN says the number of civilian casualties in Ukraine jumped 55 percent in the first eight months of the year.
