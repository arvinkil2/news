Indonesia's 2026 wildfires caused an estimated 340 trillion rupiah ($18.6 billion) in losses of physical assets and disrupted economic activity, equal to about 2.7 percent of GDP, with health costs from wildfire-related respiratory illness potentially adding a further 225 trillion rupiah ($12.3 billion), according to research by the Jakarta-based Center of Economic and Law Studies (CELIOS) presented on Friday. The fires, across Borneo, Sumatra and Papua, had burned 2.88 million hectares this year by the Nusantara Atlas monitor's count, and haze from them made hundreds of thousands of Indonesians ill and drifted as far as Malaysia and Singapore. Researchers have said a strong El Niño, deforestation and peatland drainage for mining and plantations supercharged this year's fire season.
