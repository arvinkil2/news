Japan will include research and survey funding in next year's budget bill to assess introducing nuclear-powered submarines, and the ruling Liberal Democratic Party has been discussing writing a plan to review future introduction into the three security documents that carry Japan's core defence strategy and are due for revision within the year, Yonhap reported, citing Tokyo Shimbun and other Japanese media. Kyodo News reported separately that the government has decided the revised documents will state explicitly that, while the Three Non-Nuclear Principles are maintained, the exceptional introduction of nuclear weapons will be permitted in an emergency, a move linked to port calls by United States submarines carrying the sea-launched nuclear cruise missile now in development.

One nuclear-powered submarine is estimated to cost about 1 trillion yen in development and maintenance, at least three boats would be needed to sustain an operational rotation, and Tokyo Shimbun said the government and ruling parties assume project work would begin within five years from fiscal 2027. A cautious view inside the LDP, citing nuclear proliferation and regional arms race concerns, means the policy could still be revised during coordination between the government and the ruling parties.
