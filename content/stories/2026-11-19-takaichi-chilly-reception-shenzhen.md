Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi received a deliberately low-key welcome at the APEC summit in Shenzhen, CNBC reported: she was met on the tarmac on Wednesday by a retired Chinese diplomat and Japan's own ambassador rather than a serving Chinese official, and she was placed in the back row, off to the side, in the leaders' group photo, while Xi Jinping greeted Donald Trump personally. No formal meeting between Takaichi and Xi has been publicly scheduled during her visit, the first to China by a Japanese prime minister since Shinzo Abe's trip in December 2019.

Analysts quoted by CNBC read the protocol as a downgrade tied to Takaichi's November 2025 remarks that a Taiwan Strait crisis could threaten Japan's survival, which Beijing has demanded she retract. The strained reception points to China's restrictions on rare earth and other dual-use exports to Japan, in place for the past year, remaining in force rather than easing.
