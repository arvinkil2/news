Tanker freight rose 9.92 percent to 879.30 dollars, the largest move in the midday tables, while dry bulk freight fell 2.50 percent to 12.67 dollars, so the move is specific to tankers. No story in today's reporting names a cause for the freight index itself. Today's shipping reporting documents the risk around it: Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps claimed seven vessels were hit in the Gulf, a claim with no independent confirmation and no vessel named (see today's story, "Iran's Guards claim seven vessels hit in the Gulf"), and the oil reporting counted at least 18 attacks on vessels in two weeks, with Iran targeting tankers beyond Hormuz into the Gulf of Oman (see today's story, "Oil jumps after Shaybah strike as inventories tighten").

Meta fell 6.89 percent to 649.47 dollars and is down 11.84 percent on the week, the largest fall among the large technology names in the tables. Today's reporting gives the context rather than a Meta-specific account: Meta reported strong third quarter results before the open but gave no specific capital spending guidance for next year, with its free cash flow turning negative, and Seoul analysts tied that gap to the doubts about AI profitability that drove the Korean market lower (see today's story, "Kospi closes down 3.5 percent as chip selloff deepens").

Semiconductors fell 5.75 percent to 481.02 dollars, with Nvidia down 3.83 percent to 213.48 dollars and Broadcom down 3.83 percent to 324.86 dollars. The driver in today's reporting is the same AI valuation doubt that took the Kospi down 3.52 percent in a chip selloff led by Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, and that pushed Treasury yields lower in a haven bid (see today's stories, "Kospi closes down 3.5 percent as chip selloff deepens" and "Treasuries rally as AI doubts push long end lower"). Nvidia's own company news today ran the other way: it is paying to expand supply chain capacity, with substrate capacity set to rise two and a half times in three years (see today's story on Nvidia's supply chain capacity advances).

The miners fell with the metals they produce. Silver miners fell 5.94 percent to 76.57 dollars, copper miners fell 5.73 percent to 73.55 dollars and gold miners fell 5.63 percent to 81.84 dollars, with BHP down 3.22 percent and Rio Tinto down 2.85 percent. Today's commodities reporting explains the metal side of that fall: silver and gold fell as the oil shock was priced through inflation and rates, and copper fell as London Metal Exchange stocks rebuilt (see today's stories, "Gold and silver fall as oil stokes inflation fears" and "Copper falls as LME stocks rebuild, nickel jumps").

Silver fell 4.24 percent to 54.99 dollars an ounce and gold fell 2.19 percent to 4,059.20 dollars, both extending the morning's losses. Today's commodities reporting explains the direction: higher crude is lifting inflation and rate expectations, which makes non-yielding metal more expensive to hold, and that pressure has outweighed the geopolitical bid for three sessions in India as well (see today's story, "Gold and silver fall as oil stokes inflation fears"). The midday fall came even as Treasury yields dropped sharply, so the metals are not trading as havens in this session.

Amazon fell 3.84 percent to 252.29 dollars. No story in today's reporting explains Amazon's move specifically. It fell with the wider technology sector, down 2.89 percent in the tables, on the same AI valuation doubts documented in today's Kospi and Treasuries reporting, but no Amazon-specific driver is claimed here.

Treasury yields fell across the curve at midday, the five-year down about 16 basis points to 4.81 percent, the 10-year down about 15 basis points to 4.99 percent and the 30-year down about 12 basis points to 5.32 percent, taking the long end to a one-month low. Today's rates reporting attributes the rally to a haven bid driven by equity risk, the AI valuation doubts in particular, rather than to any repricing of the inflation path, and notes that a rally built on equity nerves can unwind before the Federal Reserve's October 27 to 28 meeting (see today's story, "Treasuries rally as AI doubts push long end lower").

The cross-market read is risk-off in growth assets, with the commodity shock still priced separately. The S&P 500 fell 1.05 percent and the Nasdaq 100 fell 2.27 percent, miners and semiconductors fell 5 percent or more, and yields fell 12 to 16 basis points as investors bought Treasuries. Against that, Brent crude rose 2.81 percent to 111.88 dollars and tanker freight rose 9.92 percent, because the Gulf risk is physical: production assets and tankers, not sentiment. The dollar index barely moved, down 0.10 percent to 101.68, and precious metals fell with equities rather than against them, so the day's split is between a haven bid for government bonds and a supply premium in energy and shipping, with almost everything else sold.
