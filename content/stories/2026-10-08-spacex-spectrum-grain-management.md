**Latest (1:47pm ET Friday):** The market is treating the Grain deal as a genuine carrier threat, and the price tag leaked. The Wall Street Journal reported SpaceX will pay about $8 billion for the spectrum, per Datacenter Dynamics, and Friday's session brought real damage: Reuters reported T-Mobile, Verizon and AT&T fell 5 to 6 percent in premarket trading, CNBC reported Verizon was heading for its worst day since 2002, and SpaceX itself rose 3.6 percent. The selloff crossed the Atlantic: the STOXX Europe 600 Telecommunications index fell 3.3 percent to its lowest since February, Deutsche Telekom fell 8 percent, Vodafone 4.5 percent, Orange 3.4 percent and Telefonica 3.3 percent, per Reuters; Finimize put the US session moves at T-Mobile down 9.1 percent, AT&T 7.4 percent and Verizon 5.9 percent. Bloomberg separately reported Verizon and AT&T bonds weakened on SpaceX's wireless plans. The one bright spot was tower owners: American Tower, Crown Castle and SBA Communications rose 5 to 6 percent as investors bet SpaceX's wireless ambitions will drive demand for towers, rooftops and small cells, with a Morgan Stanley note cited by Reuters making the case; Finimize put those tower moves at 7.6 to 12.6 percent. Morgan Stanley analysts also said the Grain transaction is a clear sign SpaceX will be a more aggressive spectrum acquirer, with any threat to established carriers materializing gradually, starting in rural markets. Background color from Datacenter Dynamics: Grain only bought the 800 MHz airwaves in August as part of a $2.9 billion swap with T-Mobile (which took Grain's 600 MHz frequencies), and the spectrum had originally been earmarked for EchoStar-owned Dish before that deal fell through because Dish could not afford it.

SpaceX announced Thursday an agreement to buy a nationwide portfolio of up to 14 MHz of paired spectrum in the 800 MHz band from Grain Management, subject to FCC approval, with no purchase price disclosed. The company said the spectrum fills a key technical gap paving the way for Starlink Mobile "to become a major mobile carrier in the US," combining satellite and ground-based coverage once towers are deployed, in direct competition with AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile. The carriers' shares tumbled in extended trading on the news while SpaceX rose about 3 percent after hours, per CNBC, and Elon Musk called the deal a "very big deal" on X.
