Canadian rare earth recycler Cyclic Materials has selected a site in Germany for its first European magnet recovery facility. The privately held company signed a land reservation agreement for a facility at the Dow ValuePark in Schkopau, near Leipzig, it said on Thursday. The plant would lay the groundwork for a European rare earth recovery network with the potential to meet as much as 15 percent of the European Union's rare earth needs.

Cyclic, backed by Microsoft, Amazon and BMW, plans to process 25,000 tonnes of magnet bearing scrap annually at the German facility, feeding a central chemical processing hub where recovered material would be refined into rare earth oxides. Annual output could reach 750 tonnes of magnet concentrate, alongside 14,000 tonnes of steel, 4,600 tonnes of aluminium and 3,000 tonnes of copper, and the non rare earth metals account for 40 to 50 percent of revenue, according to Reuters. The German plant is the first of five planned for Europe and is slated to begin operations in 2028, chief executive Ahmad Ghahreman told Reuters.

The expansion comes as European demand for rare earth magnets is projected to more than double over the next decade, while China accounts for 90 percent of global processed rare earths. Cyclic opened its first commercial scale magnet recovery facility in Mesa, Arizona, in September, and is building an integrated recycling campus in South Carolina. Unlike producers such as MP Materials, which has secured minimum price guarantees from the US government, Cyclic says its production costs allow it to operate without similar support.
