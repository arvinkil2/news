The Conference Board's consumer confidence index rose 7.5 points to 91.7 in October, up from a revised 84.2 in September and back to June levels, the board said on Tuesday. The present situation index rose 8.6 points to 120.4 and the expectations index recovered 6.9 points to 72.6, reversing only part of three consecutive monthly declines. Economists polled by The Wall Street Journal had expected a reading of 81.

The board's senior economist Fergus McCormick said the recovery was in line with retail gasoline prices easing from September to October, but that confidence remained on its long-term downward trend and consumers planned less spending on services and durable goods over the next six months. Average 12-month inflation expectations eased to 5.8 percent, still elevated, and write-in responses continued to cite prices and the cost of food and groceries.
