The market goes into the new week treating a December Federal Reserve rate increase as the base case. Futures pricing put the chance of a quarter point hike at the December 8 to 9 meeting at about 77 percent after Friday's employment report, according to CME FedWatch data reported by HedgeWeekly, and Reuters reporting put the figure at about 78 percent on Friday afternoon, up from 66.9 percent the day before. A December move would lift the target range from 3.75 to 4.00 percent to 4.00 to 4.25 percent.

The repricing followed the October payrolls beat, 165,000 jobs against forecasts near 65,000 with unemployment at 4.1 percent, covered on this site on November 6, and the split among officials that followed it, covered on November 7. What Friday's trading did not do was run away with the long end: the 10 year Treasury yield closed at 4.86 percent, up 2.2 basis points on the day but down 7.0 basis points on the week, the five year at 4.65 percent, up 4.0 basis points on the day and down 7.3 on the week, and the 30 year at 5.26 percent. The dollar index rose 0.74 percent on Friday to 102.3. The week's haven bid is still in the market even as the policy pricing hardens.

Monday morning brought no break in that pricing. Treasury yields edged lower, with falling oil prices steering direction for now, Dow Jones Newswires reported from Tradeweb data: the two year yield eased 0.2 basis points to 4.519 percent and the 10 year fell 0.4 basis points to 4.854 percent, while money markets priced a 78 percent probability of a 25 basis point December hike, according to LSEG. The stabilization follows Friday's employment driven rise in yields.

By Monday's close the pricing had hardened further. December hike odds rose to 84.2 percent and the two year yield rose 5 basis points to 4.56 percent, flattening the two to ten year spread to 32 basis points, FXStreet reported on Tuesday morning. This site's market tables put Monday's closes at 4.66 percent on the five year, up 1.1 basis points, 4.86 percent on the 10 year, up 0.5 basis points, and 5.26 percent on the 30 year, down 0.4 basis points, even as equities rallied hard, the S and P 500 up 2.35 percent and the Nasdaq 100 up 3.4 percent. Early Tuesday, Governor Christopher Waller then gave the pricing an official voice, saying he leans toward a 25 basis point December increase even if October inflation improves, and that a core CPI reading around 0.3 percent or higher today should put a 50 basis point move on the table, in a speech covered in full in today's Waller story.

Two tests arrive before the meeting, and the first lands today. The Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes the October consumer price index at 8:30 a.m. Eastern on Tuesday November 10, the last full inflation read the FOMC will have in hand alongside the November employment report due December 4. Consensus looks for a 0.3 percent monthly rise in headline prices, cooling from September's 0.6 percent, and 0.2 percent on core, down from 0.3 percent, with the Cleveland Fed nowcast softer at 0.24 percent headline and 0.21 percent core. This print carries a quirk that raises the weight on the monthly figures: because the Bureau could not collect prices during the October 2025 federal shutdown, no official year over year rates will be published for this release. A hot print would validate the priced hike and the officials arguing for one; a soft print is the one development that could pull pricing back toward the two thirds level of a week ago.

Supply runs in parallel. The Treasury's November refunding, announced November 4, sells 125 billion dollars of securities this week to refund about 97 billion dollars of privately held notes and bonds maturing November 15 and raise about 28 billion dollars of new cash. The first leg, Monday's 58 billion dollar three year note, stopped at 4.627 percent with a small tail and soft foreign demand, covered in today's three year auction story. Still to come are the 42 billion dollar 10 year note on Tuesday November 10 and the 25 billion dollar 30 year bond on Thursday November 12, all at 1:00 p.m. Eastern and settling November 16. The cash bond market is closed on Wednesday for Veterans Day, which compresses the week's trading around the auctions. With the 10 year at 4.86 percent and December priced above four in five, the remaining auctions will show whether duration demand at these levels is as deep as the week's rally implied. New York Fed President John Williams speaks on Thursday.
