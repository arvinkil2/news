**Latest (result, early Tuesday):** Alberta voters have rejected the separation path by roughly three to one. In the unofficial count reported by Elections Alberta, with 25.34 percent of voting places reporting, 815,392 voters, or 76.29 percent, chose for Alberta to remain a province of Canada, against 253,425 votes, or 23.71 percent, for beginning the legal process toward a binding separation referendum. Bloomberg called the result for the remain side, a win for Prime Minister Mark Carney, who campaigned in the province in the final week. The count ran late: several voting places opened late on Monday and stayed open longer, and one Edmonton location was permitted to vote until 10 p.m. Mountain Time, delaying the unofficial tally until every location had closed. The margin is wider than the final polls, which put remain between 68 and 70 percent, and it clears the test Premier Danielle Smith set in advance, since she has said she will not hold another separation referendum if the remain side wins. The other nine questions are still being counted, and Elections Alberta will announce the official results on October 29.

Albertans vote today in a ten-question provincial referendum whose final question asks whether Alberta should remain a province of Canada or whether the provincial government should begin the legal process required to hold a binding referendum on separation. Polls are open from 9 a.m. to 8 p.m. Mountain Time at assigned voting places, and election officers will count the separation question first when polls close. The other nine questions cover provincial control of immigration, eligibility for provincially funded services, proof of citizenship for voting and constitutional changes touching the courts, the Senate and federal programs. Under the order setting the vote, none of the ten results is binding.

Turnout is the first result. Preliminary figures reported this morning put advance and special ballots at 1,356,873, or 43.1 percent of eligible voters, before referendum day voting began, and Elections Alberta recorded 174,532 ballots on the first advance day alone. Final polls pointed the same way: Leger, polling October 2 to 4, found 70 percent for remaining in Canada against 22 percent for starting the separation process, and the Writ's tracker, updated October 17, stood at 68 percent to 26 percent.

The politics therefore turn on the margin, not the outcome the polls predict. Prime Minister Mark Carney has called the vote a dangerous bluff and compared it to Brexit. Premier Danielle Smith, whose government put the question after a court challenge halted a citizen-led petition for a direct independence vote, has said she will not hold another separation referendum if the remain side wins. A narrow remain result would keep the movement credible; a lopsided one pushes the argument back into ordinary federal-provincial bargaining over energy and fiscal policy, which is where Smith has said she wants a stronger Alberta inside Canada. The separation result is expected first, with the full count running into Tuesday.
