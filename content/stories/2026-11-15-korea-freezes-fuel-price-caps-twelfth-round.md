South Korea will keep its caps on refiners' petroleum product supply prices unchanged for a 12th round, the Ministry of Trade and Industry said on November 13, with gasoline capped at 1,784 won a litre, diesel at 1,773 won and kerosene at 1,380 won for four weeks from midnight on November 14. It is the fifth consecutive freeze since a cut in late June, and the system, introduced on March 13, has now run for more than eight months.

The government said crude import costs remain high and lifting the controls could raise household costs, and it is preparing funding on the assumption the caps run into next year: about 1.4 trillion won ($950 million) in next year's budget proposal is earmarked for compensating refiners' fourth-quarter losses, with the loss amounts to be disclosed in early December. The Korea Development Institute estimated the caps lowered consumer price inflation by an average of about 0.6 percentage points a month from March to October.
