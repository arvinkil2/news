Analog Devices reported fiscal fourth-quarter results on November 24 for the quarter ended October 31, 2026. Revenue was 4.373 billion dollars, up 42 percent from 3.076 billion a year earlier and above the 4.3 billion dollar midpoint of the company's own guidance. Net income was 1.65 billion dollars, or 3.40 dollars a share, against 787.7 million dollars, or 1.60 dollars, a year earlier. Adjusted earnings were 4.06 dollars a share, above analyst estimates of 3.90 dollars and above the top of the company's guided range.

Growth was led by the two largest end markets. Industrial revenue was 2.19 billion dollars, up 55 percent, and communications revenue was 707 million dollars, up 81 percent, with data-center demand a named driver. Automotive revenue was 1.04 billion dollars, up 21 percent. For the full fiscal year, revenue was 15.18 billion dollars, up 38 percent, and free cash flow reached 5.45 billion dollars.

The outlook is where the quarter turned. For the first quarter of fiscal 2027, Analog Devices guided revenue to 4.4 billion dollars, plus or minus 150 million, roughly in line with analyst expectations, but adjusted earnings to 3.82 dollars a share, plus or minus 20 cents, below the 4.06 dollars just delivered, on an implied adjusted operating margin of about 50.5 percent against 52.3 percent in the fourth quarter. The board declared a quarterly dividend of 1.10 dollars a share, payable December 21 to holders of record on December 7.

The shares fell sharply on the print during Tuesday's chip-led selloff, which this site covered in its market stories. The result itself is a record; the debate the guidance opens is whether analog margins have peaked even as orders keep growing, a question that matters for the whole industrial semiconductor complex heading into 2027.
