**Latest, Tuesday October 13:** OPEC's monthly oil market report showed the wider OPEC+ group pumping 39.07 million barrels a day in September, up about 900,000 barrels a day from August, as Saudi Arabia told the group it produced 8.372 million barrels a day, more than 2 million barrels a day above August, after Aramco restored Gulf loadings disrupted by the attack on the East-West pipeline. Russian output fell 38,000 barrels a day as Ukrainian strikes on refineries continued. The same report cut the 2026 demand growth forecast for a sixth straight month, to 180,000 barrels a day, below both the September estimate and the 400,000 barrel a day figure Al Ghais cited on Monday, and raised 2027 growth to 2.55 million barrels a day, taking demand to an average of 108.3 million barrels a day on the need to rebuild inventories depleted by the Iran war.

OPEC Secretary General Haitham Al Ghais told the WPC Energy Congress in Riyadh on Monday that the group now expects world oil demand to grow by 400,000 barrels a day this year, down from the 1.4 million barrels a day it projected at the start of 2026, a revision he described as in line with how the year has developed. Al Ghais said the demand deferred from 2026 is expected to show up in 2027 as pent-up consumption.

The cut is a statement about the world economy, not about supply: it arrives with oil prices elevated by the Hormuz disruption and the attacks on Saudi infrastructure, so the market is carrying a war premium on top of a weakening consumption outlook.

Update, Tuesday October 13: two qualifications sharpen the headline. OPEC had already cut its 2026 growth estimate to 380,000 barrels a day in September, so Monday's 400,000 barrel a day figure is a slight increase from last month's forecast, even though it is a 1 million barrel a day reduction from the outlook at the start of the year. And the International Energy Agency expects a much deeper demand hit: a 2.5 million barrel a day decline in global consumption in 2026, followed by a 2.6 million barrel a day rebound in 2027. The gap between the OPEC and IEA demand paths is now the central uncertainty in the 2027 balance.
