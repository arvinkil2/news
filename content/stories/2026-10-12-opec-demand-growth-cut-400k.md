OPEC Secretary General Haitham Al Ghais told the WPC Energy Congress in Riyadh on Monday that the group now expects world oil demand to grow by 400,000 barrels a day this year, down from the 1.4 million barrels a day it projected at the start of 2026, a revision he described as in line with how the year has developed. Al Ghais said the demand deferred from 2026 is expected to show up in 2027 as pent-up consumption.

The cut is a statement about the world economy, not about supply: it arrives with oil prices elevated by the Hormuz disruption and the attacks on Saudi infrastructure, so the market is carrying a war premium on top of a weakening consumption outlook.
