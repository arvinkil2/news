South Korea will introduce a cornerstone investor system for initial public offerings on November 13, after a Cabinet meeting approved the amendment to the enforcement decree of the Financial Investment Services and Capital Markets Act, the Financial Services Commission said on November 3. Cornerstone investors receive shares in advance from the institutional tranche, capped at 20 percent of that tranche for KOSPI listings and 30 percent for KOSDAQ listings, and must hold 50 percent of the allocation for six months, 30 percent for eight months and the remaining 20 percent for ten months, with participating institutions required to hold equity capital or entrusted assets of at least 20 times their subscription.

A preliminary demand survey starts at the same time, letting underwriters gauge institutional prices and volumes before a securities registration statement is filed and before the indicative price range is set. The commission said securing medium and long term institutional investors in advance should cushion the share price declines caused by heavy selling immediately after listing, and the rules bar cornerstone contracts with institutions that hold a stake in the issuer.
