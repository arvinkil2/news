In a trial of 61 patients recently diagnosed with advanced pancreatic cancer that had spread to other parts of the body, the KRAS inhibitor pill daraxonrasib left cancer undetectable on CT scans in about one in 12 patients, stopped tumours growing in 90 percent, and shrank or cleared tumours in just over 60 percent, with a median follow-up of 18 months. One year after starting treatment 76.6 percent of patients were alive, against the 40 to 45 percent usually alive a year on chemotherapy alone, and the presenting researcher, Dr Wungki Park of Memorial Sloan Kettering, reported a near doubling in the duration of disease control. Cancer Research UK called the results the biggest leap forward in decades for the disease, while noting that more trials are needed before the treatment can be assessed for routine use and that the pill cannot yet be considered a cure.
