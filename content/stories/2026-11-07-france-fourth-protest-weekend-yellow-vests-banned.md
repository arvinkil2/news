France is bracing for a fourth weekend of mass demonstrations on Saturday, as the student-led protest wave over the cost of living and the state of the country's schools enters a second month. Students and labor unions called an authorized march through central Paris on Saturday afternoon, while the Yellow Vests declined to join it and called instead for "mass disobedience" at the Place de la Concorde, near parliament and the Elysee Palace, in an event the authorities have banned.

Interior Minister Laurent Nunez said 70,000 police and security forces will be deployed across the country over the weekend. The deployment follows a nationwide strike on Thursday that brought more than 600 arrests, and rioting on October 17 that caused more than one billion euros of damage to public and private property. Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu is scheduled to meet trade and employee unions on Monday, and an inter-union statement on Friday said the protests will end only when the government answers demands on wages, pensions and school funding that unions have put forward for several weeks.
