Namibia's ambition to develop a domestic nuclear industry is gaining momentum as the government seeks to address a contradiction at the centre of its economy: exporting uranium used to generate electricity in other countries while importing a substantial share of its own power. President Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah met the Atomic Energy Board of Namibia, led by chairperson Shitaleni Herman, at State House in Windhoek on Monday, and Herman briefed her on the board's work, the Nuclear Science and Technology Conference held in Swakopmund from November 18 to 20, and the Nuclear Industry Strategy.

The nuclear programme gained political momentum in April 2025, when Nandi-Ndaitwah called for Namibia to explore domestic nuclear power generation during a visit to the Rossing uranium mine. Cabinet approved the Nuclear Industry Strategy in September 2025, and Prime Minister Elijah Ngurare launched it in Swakopmund last week, saying it is time for Namibia to take its rightful place in the value chains of the nuclear industry, not merely as a supplier of raw uranium ore.

According to World Bank trade data cited in the report, Namibia exported uranium ores and concentrates worth approximately N$24.5 billion in 2025, with China the largest buyer, followed by France and the United States. The Sixth National Development Plan identifies uranium mining as a significant contributor, accounting for 3.2 percent of GDP in 2024, and the sector generated N$15.53 billion in turnover in 2023, including N$10.27 billion in wages, salaries, royalties and local procurement.

Namibia's participation remains concentrated in mining and uranium concentrate production rather than the advanced stages of the fuel cycle. The national plan identifies uranium conversion, enrichment, nuclear fuel fabrication and power generation as opportunities for domestic value addition, and estimates conversion alone could attract investment of up to N$20 billion under favourable market, financial and technical conditions. Uranium miners in the estate's market tables rose 5.9 percent on Monday, in line with the wider mining rebound.
