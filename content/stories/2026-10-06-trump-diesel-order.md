President Trump signed an executive order Monday temporarily allowing red-dyed diesel, normally reserved for tractors, construction equipment and other off-road machinery, to be used in highway vehicles, Bloomberg reported. The order waives the off-road requirement and defers the 24.4-cents-per-gallon federal excise tax through the end of the year, with the IRS directed to suspend applicable penalties for highway use of dyed fuel through December 31. The administration estimates truckers could save more than $100 per refill.

The mechanics are messy. Most truck stops do not sell dyed diesel, so drivers have nowhere to buy it, and state laws still ban dyed fuel on roads in most states, which an executive order cannot override. The federal tax is deferred rather than eliminated, and many states charge their own sales tax on dyed diesel. GasBuddy's Patrick De Haan said the order does nothing for diesel supply, so futures and crack spreads should keep trading on Middle East flows, low inventories and emergency stock releases rather than tax policy.

The diesel file stays the active one in refined products. AAA put average retail diesel at $6.32 a gallon Monday, up 77 percent this year. The G7 plan floated October 2 to release up to 100 million barrels of diesel and crude faces market skepticism given the US Strategic Petroleum Reserve sits at 283 million barrels, the lowest since October 1982 per EIA data. The EIA's Winter Fuels Outlook puts heating-oil bills up 21 percent this winter, and the agency's weekly Petroleum Status report lands Wednesday at 10:30am ET.
