US import prices rose 1.1 percent in October, matching September's advance, as nonfuel import prices rose 0.8 percent and fuel import prices rose 3.6 percent on higher prices for petroleum and natural gas, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported. Import prices for capital goods rose 1.5 percent, the largest monthly advance since that index was first published in December 1988, led by computers, peripherals and semiconductors, and prices for imports from China rose 1.4 percent, the largest monthly rise since January 2004.

US export prices rose 1.1 percent in October after rising 2.4 percent in September, with agricultural export prices up 1.2 percent and nonagricultural export prices up 1.1 percent.
