Lithuania's government has said it could unilaterally revoke the legal status of the Taiwanese Representative Office in Vilnius if Taiwan refuses to change the office's name. Prime Minister Mindaugas Sinkevičius said on Wednesday that Lithuania had that right, and President Gitanas Nausėda, speaking to journalists in Brussels on Thursday, said the change could be made by agreement or unilaterally, with legal options available, while adding that relations with Taiwan should be preserved while the issue is resolved. The Taiwanese Representative Office told the ELTA news agency that Taiwan's government does not plan to change the name, because it was agreed by both sides.

Nausėda also said Taiwanese investment in Lithuania had fallen well short of expectations, putting it at about five million euros over several years. The office opened in late 2021 under the name "Taiwanese," after which China downgraded diplomatic representation in Lithuania and imposed economic sanctions; the current government's programme calls for normalising relations with Beijing to the level maintained by other EU member states.
