For the first time in more than two decades, no international observers will watch a US federal election at the host government's invitation. The OSCE's election arm, ODIHR, and its Parliamentary Assembly announced on September 23 that Washington had not invited them to observe the 2026 midterm congressional elections. ODIHR has assessed American elections since 2002, under presidents of both parties; its missions have no enforcement power and do not rule on legitimacy, instead evaluating the electoral environment against OSCE standards and recommending changes.

The administration gave two explanations. A State Department spokesperson said the OSCE should focus its limited resources on elections where it can have the greatest impact. White House spokeswoman Lauren Bis said US elections would be administered, conducted with the participation of, and observed by American citizens. Supporters of the decision note that party and candidate poll watchers will be present, and the Department of Justice has announced plans to deploy its own monitors, though critics respond that a domestic federal deployment is not a substitute for independent international observation.

The OSCE's reply was institutional rather than heated. It called the decision regrettable, particularly given long standing cooperation, and said it is not in line with the 1990 Copenhagen Document commitments under which participating states agreed to invite observers. Parliamentary Assembly President Pere Joan Pons called it a significant departure from an established democratic practice, and Senator Jeanne Shaheen called it deeply antithetical to the spirit of freedom, democracy and transparency. Critics also noted the company the decision keeps: Russia and Belarus have likewise refused or restricted OSCE observation in recent years.

Nothing about the vote itself changes on November 3. What changes is the precedent available to every government the United States next urges to admit observers, and the loss of the outside assessment American officials have cited for 24 years when defending their own elections' credibility. Status: decision stated by the OSCE on September 23, no invitation issued since.
