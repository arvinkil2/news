Argentina's Senate gave initial approval on Thursday to the electoral reform backed by President Javier Milei, voting 43 in favor and 29 against, a wider margin than the government had expected. The bill now passes to the Chamber of Deputies, whose approval would make it law.

The reform would suspend the PASO primaries (Primarias, Abiertas, Simultáneas y Obligatorias) for the 2027 elections, introduce a Ficha Limpia rule barring people with confirmed criminal convictions from candidacy, abolish the separate election of Argentine representatives to the Mercosur Parliament, and make the presidential debate optional rather than mandatory. It would also tighten party rules, requiring national parties to be present in 10 districts, double the current threshold, raise caps on private campaign contributions, and end free campaign advertising in audiovisual media. Congress suspended the primaries once before, for 2025; without this law they would return in 2027.
