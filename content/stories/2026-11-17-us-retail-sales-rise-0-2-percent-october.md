US retail and food services sales rose 0.2 percent in October to $748.2 billion, the Census Bureau reported on Tuesday, slowing from September's gain of 0.8 percent, which was unrevised as a rate though the September level was revised to $746.6 billion. Sales were 6.8 percent above October 2025, and total sales for the August through October period were 6.4 percent above the same period a year ago. The bureau notes that the 90 percent confidence interval on October's monthly change includes zero, so the increase is not statistically distinguishable from flat, and the figures are adjusted for seasonal variation, holidays and trading days but not for price changes.

The detail was firmer than the headline. Motor vehicle and parts dealers' sales fell, down 1.6 percent on the month on the reported category figures, while sales excluding autos rose 0.6 percent. Retail sales excluding automobiles, gasoline, building materials and food services, the control group that corresponds most closely with the consumer spending component of GDP, rose 0.6 percent in October after an upwardly revised 0.5 percent rise in September, ahead of the 0.4 percent economists polled by Reuters had forecast; the headline 0.2 percent matched the Reuters consensus.
