President Donald Trump is directing federal contractors and subcontractors to consult with his administration about inquiries they receive from Congress and to withhold any communications that the White House considers privileged, under an executive order issued late on Friday, the Associated Press reported. Companies must also coordinate with the White House and federal agencies on appropriate responses to such requests.

Non-compliance carries commercial penalties. The White House warns that contractors that fail to comply could have their contracts terminated and their future work with the federal government suspended. The order applies to all contractors and subcontractors, a universe of tens of thousands of businesses.

The order is the administration's latest step to resist the oversight expected from Democrats when they take over Congress in January, after winning both chambers in the November 3 midterms. Earlier in the week, White House counsel Will Scharf told federal agencies they must seek approval from his office and the White House Office of Legislative Affairs before agreeing to appear before Congress.

Democrats condemned the move. Incoming House oversight chair Robert Garcia called it part of a blatant attack on Congress's constitutional right to conduct oversight, and Representative Jamie Raskin called it totally unprecedented and blatantly unlawful, Politico reported. The White House framed the requirements in a fact sheet as protecting executive privilege from congressional overreach. The order is announced, not yet tested in court, and its legality and enforceability remain contested.
