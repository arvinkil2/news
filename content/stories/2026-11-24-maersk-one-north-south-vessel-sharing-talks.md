Maersk and Ocean Network Express (ONE) are working to develop a vessel-sharing agreement covering the north-south trades globally, rather than lane by lane, The Loadstar reported, citing sources in Asia. If confirmed, it would be the first such global pact: the two carriers' existing groupings, Maersk's Gemini Cooperation and ONE's Premier Alliance, cover east-west trades only, while north-south services remain a patchwork of small agreements.

The logic rests on ONE's fleet, the report said: 36 percent of ONE's operated capacity sits in ships of 10,000 to 15,100 teu, against 14 percent at Maersk, and most of its orderbook is in the same bracket, the size north-south trades are moving toward. On Far East to west coast South America, Linerlytica puts Maersk at 16.1 percent of weekly capacity and ONE at 9 percent, together about a quarter of the trade. Neither carrier had formally responded to The Loadstar's approach.
