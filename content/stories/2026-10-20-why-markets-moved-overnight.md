Tanker freight fell 7.61 percent to 805.00 dollars, the largest move in the tables, and is down 19.42 percent on the week. Today's reporting does not treat the proxy's fall as a spot rate collapse: the Canadian oil story puts spot VLCC earnings at a record 805,000 dollars a day, and more than 40 percent of the world VLCC fleet is in or near the Persian Gulf (see today's dark fleet tanker story and the Canadian oil discount story).

WTI crude fell 5.45 percent to 98.01 dollars a barrel. Much of the drop is contract mechanics rather than a fresh supply shock: today's Brent story notes the November WTI contract expires on Tuesday and the more active December contract traded at 98.93 dollars early in the day, while the sanctions waiver trade described in the dark fleet story continues to promise more effective Atlantic Basin supply (see today's Brent story).

Dry bulk freight fell 3.78 percent to 12.46 dollars. No story in today's reporting explains the move, and no clear single driver is claimed here.

United States natural gas fell 2.91 percent to 2.97 dollars per million British thermal units, extending a weekly fall of 9.56 percent. The move runs against the international gas market in today's reporting, where European prices rose above 90 euros a megawatt hour and Asian spot LNG topped 30 dollars for the first time since 2022, and no story in today's files explains the United States decline (see today's Asia LNG story).

Meta fell 2.00 percent to 691.36 dollars and is down 9.33 percent on the week. No Meta specific story appears in today's reporting; the closest context is the Kospi story, where renewed concern over AI buildout financing and profitability weighed on Asian technology shares ahead of earnings (see today's Kospi story).

BHP fell 1.99 percent to 81.57 dollars. Today's BHP story was two sided, reporting a first block cave resource at Olympic Dam alongside a 16 percent year on year fall in Copper South Australia production after a conveyor failure, and today's reporting names no single catalyst for the share fall (see today's Olympic Dam story).

Tesla rose 1.75 percent to 374.49 dollars. No story in today's reporting explains the move, and no clear single driver is claimed here.

Rare earth miners rose 1.69 percent to 54.26 dollars. Today's rare earth reporting is company specific, the Aclara story on the doubled value of its Penco project in Chile, and does not explain the index move (see today's Aclara story).

The cross-market read at midday is an energy led reversal inside a steadier equity market. Crude and freight gave back part of the Gulf shock premium, with Brent down a smaller 0.74 percent to 108.74 dollars, while Treasury yields eased only slightly, the 10 year down 1.3 basis points to 5.12 percent, and the dollar index slipped 0.22 percent. United States equities leaned risk on, the Russell 2000 up 1.46 percent, the Dow Jones up 1.01 percent and the S&P 500 up 0.42 percent, even as technology stayed mixed and Asian shares had fallen earlier, which reads as position adjusting around the energy shock rather than a broad change in the growth outlook.
