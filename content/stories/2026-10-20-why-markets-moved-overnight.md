Amazon rose 4.98 percent to 262.45 dollars, the largest single move in the tables. No clear single driver appears in this morning's reporting; the technology brief reads Monday's technology gains as a partial retracement of the previous week's selloff, and Amazon moved with that rebound.

Tesla rose 3.76 percent to 368.06 dollars, the second largest gain. As with Amazon, this morning's reporting names no Tesla specific catalyst, and the move sits inside the broader technology recovery rather than on company news.

Dry bulk freight rose 3.19 percent to 12.95 dollars. This morning's commodities reporting records the settlement without a matching single catalyst, so no clear single driver is claimed here.

Tanker freight fell 3.07 percent to 871.34 dollars, and is down 13.73 percent on the week. The commodities brief treats the proxy's fall as an index composition effect rather than a rate collapse: reported spot VLCC earnings remain at record levels near 805,000 dollars a day, with more than 40 percent of the world VLCC fleet in or near the Persian Gulf (see today's dark fleet tanker story).

WTI crude fell 2.92 percent to 100.63 dollars a barrel even as Brent rose. The split is the waiver trade: Washington's move to let sanctioned dark fleet tankers be sold and refurbished into legitimate carriers, and the continuing Russia sanctions waiver, promise more effective supply into the Atlantic Basin, while Brent carries the Gulf risk premium (see today's Brent story and the dark fleet story).

Broadcom rose 1.78 percent to 334.59 dollars and the semiconductor index rose 1.73 percent to 501.93 dollars. Both are rebounds within a down week: semiconductors remain 7.88 percent lower over five sessions, and this morning's technology reporting ties the firmer tone in part to reported Chinese technology orders for Nvidia chips and the partial retracement of the chip selloff.

Brent crude rose 1.62 percent to 111.33 dollars a barrel, its highest in this table's window, on the Gulf security premium: 18 attacks on shipping in two weeks and Hormuz flows running at about 8.5 million barrels a day (see today's Brent story).

The cross-market read is two trades at once, not one. Risk appetite returned to the technology names that sold off last week, while energy priced scarcity in the Gulf and relief in the Atlantic at the same time, and the one energy price not participating in the shock, United States natural gas at 3.01 dollars, fell again. Miner equities weakened across the complex despite copper rising, a reminder that Monday's bid was selective rather than broad.
