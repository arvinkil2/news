Tanker freight fell 7.61 percent to 805.04 dollars, the largest move in the tables, and is down 19.42 percent on the week. Today's reporting does not treat the proxy's fall as a spot rate collapse: the Canadian oil story puts spot VLCC earnings at a record 805,000 dollars a day, and more than 40 percent of the world VLCC fleet is in or near the Persian Gulf (see today's dark fleet tanker story and the Canadian oil discount story).

WTI crude fell 6.07 percent to 97.37 dollars a barrel. Much of the drop is contract mechanics rather than a fresh supply shock: today's Brent story notes the November WTI contract expires on Tuesday and the more active December contract traded at 98.93 dollars early in the day, while the sanctions waiver trade described in the dark fleet story continues to promise more effective Atlantic Basin supply (see today's Brent story).

Dry bulk freight fell 3.67 percent to 12.48 dollars. No story in today's reporting explains the move, and no clear single driver is claimed here.

United States natural gas fell 2.55 percent to 2.98 dollars per million British thermal units, extending a weekly fall of 9.23 percent. The move runs against the international gas market in today's reporting, where European prices rose above 90 euros a megawatt hour and Asian spot LNG topped 30 dollars for the first time since 2022, and no story in today's files explains the United States decline (see today's Asia LNG story).

BHP fell 2.03 percent to 81.53 dollars. Today's BHP story was two sided, reporting a first block cave resource at Olympic Dam alongside a 16 percent year on year fall in Copper South Australia production after a conveyor failure, and today's reporting names no single catalyst for the share fall (see today's Olympic Dam story).

Meta fell 1.98 percent to 691.44 dollars and is down 9.32 percent on the week. No Meta specific story appears in today's reporting; the closest context is the Kospi story, where renewed concern over AI buildout financing and profitability weighed on Asian technology shares ahead of earnings (see today's Kospi story).

Silver rose 1.84 percent to 58.02 dollars an ounce, reversing the early slide to 56.69 dollars recorded in today's gold story. No story in today's reporting explains the reversal (see today's gold story).

The Russell 2000 rose 1.75 percent to 2,775.9 points, the strongest United States index move in the tables, ahead of the Dow Jones at 0.96 percent and the S&P 500 at 0.43 percent. No story in today's reporting explains the small cap outperformance.

The cross-market read at midday is an energy led reversal inside a steadier equity market. Crude and freight gave back part of the Gulf shock premium, with Brent down a smaller 1.19 percent to 108.25 dollars, while Treasury yields eased only slightly, the 10 year down 1.7 basis points to 5.09 percent, and the dollar index slipped 0.24 percent. Equities leaned risk on in the United States, led by small caps, even as technology stayed mixed and Asian shares had fallen earlier, which reads as position adjusting around the energy shock rather than a broad change in the growth outlook.
