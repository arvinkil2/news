The Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye has loosened its macroprudential loan growth limits for an eight week period, raising the cap on general purpose loans to consumers from 3 percent to 3.5 percent and the cap on vehicle loans to consumers from 3 percent to 3.5 percent. Turkish lira loans to small and medium enterprises may now grow 5.5 percent, up from 5 percent, and Turkish lira loans to larger enterprises 2.5 percent, up from 2 percent.

The bank also decided to pay remuneration in Turkish lira on the required reserves that banks and financing companies hold against their non-deposit liabilities with maturities longer than one year, in both lira and foreign currencies. It said the changes to the reserve requirement framework are intended to support macrofinancial stability and the monetary transmission mechanism.
