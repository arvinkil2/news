**Latest (Monday, October 12):** Sunday's tanker strikes now have vessel details and a casualty toll. Argus reported, citing ship-tracking data and India's shipping minister, that one of Sunday's targets was a Suezmax loaded with Iraqi crude that had sailed October 8; it was hit by an unknown projectile that started a fire, and two sailors were still missing at the time of writing, per OilPrice. The other vessel was a product carrier hit by a drone, an attack UKMTO had already reported. India's foreign ministry reiterated the need for immediate de-escalation, saying attacks on commercial vessels and seafarers must cease and that freedom of navigation through international waterways must be upheld under international law. The IRGC Navy, via Iranian state media, warned that any ship within the strait's limits that turns off its navigation and identification systems would be treated as hostile and dealt with severely, escalating the dark-transit crackdown the IRGC has run for weeks. The vessel identities and the missing-crew report have not been independently corroborated; the IRGC statement is a conflict-party claim.

**Latest (Sunday, October 11, evening):** Iran publicly graded Washington's answer on the diplomatic track as a fail. Security chief Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of the Supreme National Security Council, told state media the US response to Tehran's seven conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz was "insufficient," per Reuters via gCaptain, which was opened and read in full. Rezaei said Iran presented the proposal during the UN General Assembly offering to restore normal maritime passage within seven days if the conditions were met, the US replied through Qatari intermediaries, and Washington accepted some conditions while leaving others unclear. "We presented seven conditions to the Americans and clearly stated that the Strait of Hormuz would not be opened to them, and no negotiations would take place, until these conditions are met," he said, adding that Iran is "no longer in the process of responding to the Americans." The White House did not immediately respond to Reuters. Tehran said late last month it had received a US response to its latest proposal to resurrect the collapsed ceasefire in the Gulf, days after Trump said he had rejected it. Rezaei's account is Iran's narrative of the exchange via state media; the US version is not yet public.

**Latest (Sunday, October 11):** Both sides hardened their positions on Sunday. The IRGC said Saturday night that an unnamed crude supertanker struck a naval mine while transiting via an unauthorized route with its navigation systems off, suffering a major fire, and warned other tankers of a similar fate for ignoring the strait's "security and rules," per Al Jazeera, which was opened and read in full. The claim is uncorroborated and the vessel is unidentified. On Iranian state television, army chief of staff Habibollah Sayyari said Iran had struck blows against enemy vessels and would strike them again if anything else happened against Iran. On the US side, CENTCOM released footage early Sunday of a fighter jet bombing the stern of a Panama-flagged cargo vessel in the Gulf of Oman to enforce the blockade, disabling its propulsion. CENTCOM says that since the blockade was reimposed in mid-July, US forces have disabled four commercial vessels, turned back 135 ships and destroyed 10 tankers linked to Iran, and that Washington says Tehran has exported no oil through the blockade line. The physical toll on flows is now measurable: Kpler data cited by CNBC showed crude transiting the strait in the week ended October 7 averaged 8.5 million barrels a day, roughly 40 percent below normal levels, after the most intense week of ship attacks since the war began. As the price of reopening the strait, Iran has demanded a return to the collapsed June memorandum of understanding plus US backing of a finalized Iran-Oman deal designating temporary coordinated entry and exit routes, with no breakthrough expected before November's US midterms. On the nuclear track, Vice President JD Vance said last week Iran must make a "meaningful" reduction in enrichment capacity to end the war, a possible softening of the long-stated zero-enrichment demand, while Tehran said it will not abandon enrichment.

**Latest (Sunday, October 11):** UKMTO reported a second tanker strike on Sunday: the company security officer of another outbound tanker reported the vessel struck on its port side by an unidentified projectile. UKMTO said the vessel sustained damage but all crew were safe, with no environmental impact reported, per gCaptain, which was opened and read in full. Neither vessel was identified and UKMTO attributed responsibility for neither attack. gCaptain also noted UKMTO's Saturday advisories: several shipmasters near Ras Al Khaimah reported VHF broadcasts directing them to leave their anchorages, and the US-led Joint Maritime Information Center warned Thursday that attacks had become more frequent.

**Latest (Sunday, October 11):** Israeli media reported preparations for a possible resumption of war on Iran ahead of Israel's Knesset elections on October 27, with the public broadcaster reporting that Israel is bracing for the chance Trump orders a restart of the war and Channel 13 saying Trump and Netanyahu held "urgent discussions" on a possible new attack. One scenario reported is a US "green light" for a solo Israeli strike; separately, Walla reported the US had asked Israel to strike Tehran independently before the idea was dropped after IDF chief Eyal Zamir warned it would delay Israel's elections. Israeli outlet Maariv, citing a military official, said no preemptive strike is currently being prepared, Iran has not crossed the "red line" Israel set, and some parties have an interest in heating up the front - while noting Israeli forces are at maximum alert. On Thursday, Trump said Washington has "useful talks" with Tehran and does not intend to attack Iran before November's US midterms. Al Jazeera Arabic (opened and read in full, in Arabic) relayed all of these Israeli-media claims; none has been corroborated by named US or Israeli officials beyond the outlets' own sourcing.

**Latest:** Overnight, Iran's IRGC claimed through official Iranian media that its forces struck the LPG carrier NV Sunshine south of the Strait of Hormuz for attempting to transit via an "illegal route," and asserted Iranian "control of the administration of the strait." The claim is uncorroborated and it is unclear whether the vessel named is the same one UKMTO reported struck Saturday.

United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations said Saturday it received a report that the master of an outbound tanker was struck on the port side by an unknown projectile within the Strait of Hormuz at 7:20 PM UTC, causing a fire on board. The crew were reported safe; damage and environmental impact were unknown, and vessels were advised to transit with caution. In a separate advisory the same day, UKMTO said several ship captains near Ras Al Khaimah reported being ordered by VHF broadcast to move from their anchorages. Russian state outlet RT separately reported a tanker ablaze after shelling in the strait. The incident is distinct from Saturday's UAE Coast Guard rescue of 22 crew from a burning tanker in Gulf waters and from the IRGC's claim that a supertanker hit a mine in the strait.
