Copper gave back all of Monday's artificial intelligence fuelled gains on Tuesday as investors who sold metals during the recent AI rout showed little appetite to return. Comex copper for December delivery fell 3.5 percent to $5.437 a pound, or $11,985 a tonne, in morning trade in New York. A settlement at that level would be the lowest since March 24. The contract had settled at $5.634 on Monday, up 2.7 percent but well off the session high of $5.787 reached after Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive.

Tuesday's drop leaves copper 21 percent below its record settlement of $6.889 on September 9, technically a bear market. The metal is down 3 percent this year and 16 percent in November. Three month copper on the London Metal Exchange fell 3.5 percent to $12,054 a tonne in official trading after briefly ducking below $12,000, Reuters reported; it had jumped as much as 5.3 percent on Monday.

Miners reversed with the metal. Freeport-McMoRan fell 5 percent at the New York open, cutting its market value to $68 billion, and Southern Copper lost 4 percent to $117 billion. Teck Resources dropped 4 percent to $25 billion, while BHP slipped 2 percent to $174 billion and Rio Tinto 2 percent to $125 billion. The five heavyweights shed about $15 billion in the first minutes of trading, after adding more than $25 billion on Monday. In London, Antofagasta dropped 7 percent, Anglo American almost 6 percent and Glencore 4 percent, while Poland's KGHM fell 7 percent. In Toronto, First Quantum Minerals lost 6 percent, Lundin Mining and Hudbay Minerals 5 percent each and Ivanhoe Mines 4 percent. At the close, the Kilambi News market tables showed copper miners down 4.27 percent on the day, rare earth miners down 2.76 percent, silver miners down 2.73 percent, uranium miners down 2.71 percent and Rio Tinto down 2.56 percent, with gold miners down 1.96 percent.

The physical market remains tight, but financial flows have overpowered it, BMO Capital Markets wrote after attending Asia Copper Week in Shanghai. Visible exchange inventories cover only about two days of consumption, and the Yangshan premium for imports into China climbed from under $100 a tonne to around $500 in a month. Those premiums, and a narrowing gap between New York and London prices, have prompted traders to book an estimated 30,000 to 100,000 tonnes of US cathode for direct shipment to China, a sign, BMO said, that traders are losing confidence in a tariff decision that would keep US stockpiles sealed off from the rest of the world.

Mine supply is not rescuing the bulls either. Global copper mine production fell 1 percent year on year in September and is down 1 percent over the first nine months of 2026, the International Copper Study Group said in its latest monthly bulletin. Output from Chile slumped 15 percent and Peru 9 percent in the month, more than offsetting gains in Indonesia, China and the Democratic Republic of Congo. BMO expects the selloff to extend until Washington signals its tariff intentions or prices fall far enough to hit supply, potentially in the $10,000 to $11,000 a tonne range, while standing by its structurally bullish medium term view.
