Pan Gongsheng told the International Monetary and Financial Committee in Bangkok on October 16 to 17 that trade friction and protectionism drag on global growth by disrupting supply chains, pushing up inflation and unsettling market expectations, and that countries should strengthen policy coordination, firmly support the multilateral trading system and resist protectionist measures in all forms, the People's Bank of China said. He urged members that have not completed domestic approval of the 16th general review of quotas to do so quickly, and called for the 17th review to agree a new quota formula on a clear timetable so that quota shares shift in a meaningful way.

Pan also asked the IMF to strengthen its surveillance of fiscal risks in the major developed economies and their spillovers, and to give financial factors such as capital flows more weight in its analysis of global imbalances.
