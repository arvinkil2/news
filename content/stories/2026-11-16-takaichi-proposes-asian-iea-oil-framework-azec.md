**Latest.** The Asia Zero Emission Community leaders, co-chaired by Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi and Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr., adopted their joint statement in Manila on Tuesday and renewed the group's action plan for the next decade with economic and energy resilience added as a new pillar, the Prime Minister's Office of Japan said. Reflecting that outcome, Japan and the International Energy Agency issued a joint statement the same day on the establishment of the IEA-Japan Oil Security Hub for Asia. Takaichi told the meeting Japan would create an Asia Oil Security Task Force to write rules for emergency release and transfer of oil, with the Tokyo hub serving its core functions, and proposed stockpiling cooperation through idle tanks in Japan coordinated with the Philippines-led joint stockpiling scheme. She also set out the POWERR Gulf plan for cooperation between Middle East producers and Asian consumers, and the leaders announced a co-chairs' statement with India and South Korea, attending as invited guests, to strengthen the framework.

Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi was expected to propose, at the Asia Zero Emission Community summit in Manila on Tuesday, the creation of a new framework under which countries cooperate on procuring oil in a crisis, as a measure to strengthen the resilience of energy supply, Yonhap reported, citing Kyodo News. Japanese reporting had described the plan as an Asian version of the International Energy Agency, alongside a plan under which Japan would stockpile oil for Southeast Asian countries inside Japan in return for a fee. AZEC is a regional grouping launched in 2023 under Japan's leadership, with 11 participating countries including Australia and Indonesia.
