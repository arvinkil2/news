Angola plans to pay about $180 million (C$258 million) for a 10 percent stake in De Beers, the head of state diamond miner Endiama said on Friday, as African producers seek greater control of the industry. Endiama chief executive José Manuel Augusto Ganga Júnior disclosed the amount, according to Reuters; how the investment would be structured was not detailed. On a simple proportional basis, $180 million for 10 percent implies an equity value of $1.8 billion for De Beers, although that calculation does not establish an agreed transaction valuation without knowing what the investment covers.

Anglo American owns 85 percent of De Beers and put the business up for sale in 2024 as part of a broader restructuring, amid falling diamond prices and growing competition from lab grown stones. Botswana holds the remaining 15 percent, backs the takeover proposal from the Gareth Penny led Global Diamond Consortium, and is seeking to double its own holding to 30 percent. President Duma Boko announced on November 9 that Botswana had selected the consortium as its preferred bidder for Anglo's controlling interest, and Anglo said it was still working with the governments and the consortium towards binding transaction documents. Rapaport News reported on November 10, citing a source, that the consortium's offer included $750 million up front and $250 million in deferred payments to Anglo, with a $500 million capital injection into De Beers under discussion; those terms cannot be compared directly with Angola's proposed investment.

In a November 12 statement, Angola's mining ministry outlined its participation in the proposed acquisition alongside the consortium, Botswana, Namibia and Oman, without disclosing the ownership allocation. The ministry said the shareholding would give Angola a voice in De Beers' strategy and help develop domestic diamond cutting, polishing and marketing, and Ganga Júnior said the investment remained worthwhile despite the downturn in gem prices. De Beers already explores in Angola with Endiama; the partners announced a new kimberlite field discovery in August 2025. Ganga Júnior spoke after the inauguration of Angola's first gold refinery at Viana in Luanda, built and financed by Endiama with a design capacity of up to 25 kilograms of gold a day, according to the government.
