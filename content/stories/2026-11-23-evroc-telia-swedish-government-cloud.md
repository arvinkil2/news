Swedish cloud company evroc and Telia Cygate, the secure infrastructure arm of Telia, have presented Government Cloud, which they describe as the first sovereign national cloud for Sweden's public sector. The design splits the stack in two: evroc builds, owns and controls every layer of the software platform, so no part of it answers to a jurisdiction outside Sweden, while Telia Cygate provides the physical foundation, classified data centre sites staffed by security-cleared personnel and connected to Telia's national fibre network. Telia is part of Sweden's national total defence structure, which the partners cite as part of the offer for the state's most critical operations.

Sweden is the first deployment of a repeatable model: in each future country, a national operating partner would run the evroc platform locally inside a sovereign, classified environment. The Swedish service is expected to be available in the first half of 2027, sold through Telia Cygate's framework agreement with Kammarkollegiet, the Legal, Financial and Administrative Services Agency, and other approved procurement routes.

The launch is commercial evidence for the European sovereignty thesis that also runs through today's Airbus story: governments and large companies are now paying for infrastructure whose control, not just whose data location, sits inside their own jurisdiction.
