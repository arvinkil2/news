Tudor Gold's (TSXV: TUD) Goldstorm deposit in northwestern British Columbia could yield a $2.3 billion (C$3.2 billion) post tax net present value at $1.54 billion in initial costs, the first economic assessment of its Treaty Creek project shows. The Goldstorm mine, about 930 km northwest of Vancouver, could produce 4.68 million ounces of gold over 28 years with a 20 percent internal rate of return.

The study, prepared by Fuse Advisors, assumed $3,670 per ounce gold, $50 per ounce silver and $5 per pound copper. At those prices the mine would pay back its initial costs in 3.7 years. Gold output would average 202,300 ounces annually from years three through 12, with a life of mine average of 165,600 ounces a year. Over its life the mine would also yield 22.1 million ounces of silver and 209,900 tonnes of copper, and all in sustaining costs would average $1,358 per ounce of gold after credits for both metals.

Goldstorm's November 2025 resource estimate outlined 912.3 million indicated tonnes grading 0.85 gram gold, 5.07 grams silver and 0.15 percent copper, amounting to 24.9 million ounces of gold, 148.7 million ounces of silver and 3.05 billion pounds of copper. Tudor has yet to declare reserves. Tudor owns 80 percent of Treaty Creek and covers exploration costs for its 20 percent partner, Teuton Resources, until it gives notice of production.

The proposed mill and some underground workings overlap with a provincial licence of occupation held by Seabridge Gold for its neighbouring KSM project, and the study notes Tudor may have to move or redesign those facilities unless the companies reach an agreement or the province changes the tenure. Tudor shares fell 4.9 percent to C$0.58 in Toronto on Tuesday, valuing the company at C$240 million.
