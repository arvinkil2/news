**Latest.** Indonesian police removed 24 body bags from the destroyed factory on Sunday and suspended the search overnight, with operations scheduled to resume on Monday morning, Banten police chief Hendra Kurniawan told Metro TV. The factory said in a statement that of the 65 workers involved, 24 had returned home, 4 were receiving medical treatment, 29 remained missing and the whereabouts of another 8 had yet to be confirmed; its chief executive Steven Erwin Wijaya said about 300 people were present for Saturday’s shift. Police disaster victim identification official Wahyu Hidayati said the fire’s intensity had left the recovered victims unidentifiable by visual inspection, and that DNA samples from relatives and the missing workers’ belongings would be collected, a process that could take two to three weeks. Police said the number of dead was not immediately clear, because one body bag could contain parts of more than one person, and parts of the building remained at risk of collapse.

Rescuers in Indonesia were searching on Sunday for 36 workers still missing after a fire tore through the Fonusa Agung Mulia confectionery factory in the Serang district, around 50 kilometres west of Jakarta, on Saturday afternoon. The later wire updates put the toll at three dead and four injured, while firefighters worked on smouldering hotspots and parts of the plant were still burning on Sunday, complicating the search.

Banten rescue official Rizky Dwianto said the joint search and rescue team was making maximum effort to locate the victims, and the local disaster agency said the cause of the fire is under investigation. Indonesian agencies gave differing counts of the missing earlier in the search, so the toll remains subject to verification.
