The Federal Reserve Board on Thursday announced an enforcement action against American Express Company, saying the firm failed to sufficiently detect and report certain suspicious activity related to money laundering. The Board also identified significant deficiencies in how the firm's enterprise-wide anti-money-laundering program was implemented, in particular at its subsidiary national bank.

**Latest:** The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, the primary federal regulator of that bank, hit American Express National Bank with a cease-and-desist order and a $350 million civil money penalty for deficiencies in its Bank Secrecy Act and anti-money-laundering compliance program. The OCC found the bank failed to tailor its BSA/AML risk assessment to its actual business, focusing on risks in its relatively narrow demand-deposit products while underweighting its dominant credit and charge-card business. The agency also cited inadequate resources, staff lacking sufficient expertise, systemic internal control gaps, weak independent testing, and weak training. According to the OCC, systemic breakdowns in suspicious-activity monitoring led to the bank failing to timely identify and report roughly $13 billion of suspected trade-based money laundering activity over the past decade.
