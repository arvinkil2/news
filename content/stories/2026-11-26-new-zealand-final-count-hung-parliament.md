## Latest

Luxon said on Friday afternoon that National will begin formal negotiations with New Zealand First, Act and Opportunity, cautioning that the discussions would be challenging and could take some time. Opportunity leader Qiulae Wong confirmed she would speak with Luxon, but said she would not strike a deal if New Zealand First leader Winston Peters serves as foreign affairs minister, and that her party's bottom lines, including climate policy and capping political donations, are non-negotiable. The special votes changed three electorate seats: Labour took Wairarapa from National by 81 votes and Hamilton East by 173 votes, and the Greens took Wellington Bays from Labour. National can apply for a recount by December 3, and winning back one seat would restore a slim coalition majority without Opportunity.

New Zealand's official final count, released after election authorities counted the special votes including ballots cast from abroad, left neither bloc with a majority: Prime Minister Christopher Luxon's three-party centre-right coalition won 65 seats, one short of the 66 needed in the 130-seat parliament, and the Labour-led left bloc won 58, with National taking 40 seats, Labour 36, the Greens 17, Act 13, New Zealand First 12, Opportunity 7 and Te Pati Maori 5. The result reverses the preliminary count from the November 7 election, which had given the coalition a slim two-seat majority.

The new Opportunity Party, entering parliament for the first time under leader Qiulae Wong, now decides who governs, and Wong has said she is willing to speak with either side. Luxon said negotiations with the party would be challenging and could take some time, and analysts cited by Deutsche Welle said a failure to reach a deal could send New Zealanders back to the polls.
