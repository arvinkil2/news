New Zealand's official final count, released after election authorities counted 400,000 special votes including ballots cast from abroad, left neither bloc with a majority: Prime Minister Christopher Luxon's three-party centre-right coalition won 65 seats, two short of a majority, and the Labour-led left bloc won 58, with National taking 40 seats and Labour 36. The result reverses the preliminary count from the November 7 election, which had given the coalition a slim two-seat majority.

The new Opportunity Party, entering parliament for the first time under leader Qiulae Wong, now decides who governs, and Wong has said she is willing to speak with either side. Luxon said negotiations with the party would be challenging and could take some time, and analysts cited by Deutsche Welle said a failure to reach a deal could send New Zealanders back to the polls.
