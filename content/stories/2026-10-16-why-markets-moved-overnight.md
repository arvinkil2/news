The Nikkei 225 fell 4.9 percent to 64,426 points, down 8.01 percent on the week. No story in today's edition explains the Japanese index fall directly. The closest context in today's reporting is the chip selloff story, which covers a global derating of semiconductor shares through the week, and Japan's own items today are a Treasury bill auction and an antidumping probe, neither of which is an equity driver.

Natural gas fell 3.58 percent to 3.12 dollars per million British thermal units. No story in today's edition explains the US gas move. Today's gas reporting is European and Asian: benchmark European prices near their highest since December 2022 with storage at 74 percent, in today's Europe gas story, and South Korea planning to lean on coal and nuclear power this winter to cut liquefied natural gas use, in today's South Korea story. A falling US price against that tight international picture is a regional split, and today's files give it no single cause.

Broadcom fell 3.09 percent to 333.70 dollars, down 7.7 percent on the week, the largest fall among the large US technology names in the tables. Today's chip selloff story covers this move: the derating is running on positioning and the price of AI financing, not on demand, and today's TSMC story records the counter-evidence, a guidance raise to revenue growth of close to 40 percent for 2026 from the contract chipmaker that sees Broadcom's order book months ahead.

Meta fell 2.46 percent to 718.61 dollars. No story in today's edition gives a Meta specific reason for the fall. Meta's only item today is a product release, the Muse Spark Open open weight model, in today's technology edition, and the share price moved with the wider technology decline rather than on anything in that reporting.

Dry bulk freight rose 2.33 percent to 12.53 dollars, a rebound inside a weekly fall of 9.76 percent. No story in today's edition explains the daily rise. The weekly direction is still down, and today's mining reporting, including the Pilgangoora lithium expansion decision covered this morning, continues to show producers committing capital on long term cost curves rather than on spot freight rates.

Semiconductors fell 2.10 percent to 503.90 dollars, down 9.92 percent on the week. Today's chip selloff story explains the move and its limits: Nvidia was almost flat at midday in the fresh tables, up 0.18 percent, after falling in the previous session, which points to selling concentrated in the wider basket rather than a fresh break in the largest name. The story's read stands: two days of selling through record TSMC earnings is a pricing of the AI trade itself, not of its order book.

The technology sector fell 1.98 percent to 185.45 dollars, down 6.71 percent on the week, with Microsoft down 1.90 percent and Alphabet down 1.66 percent at midday. That breadth is weaker than the selective rotation in this morning's chip story, where Microsoft had risen in the previous session while chips drifted lower. By midday the selling had spread from chips to the large software names.

Brent crude rose 1.92 percent to 107.95 dollars a barrel, up 3.08 percent on the week, while WTI crude fell 0.59 percent to 97.39 dollars. Today's commodities reporting supports the Brent direction: attacks on ships in the Strait of Hormuz have reached a new high, in today's Hormuz story, and US crude inventories drew 8.6 million barrels, in today's inventories story. The same edition records the offsets that cap the US benchmark, with Aramco offering extra spot cargoes to Europe and tanker freight down 1.87 percent at midday, in today's Aramco and oil shipping stories.

The cross market read at midday is risk reduction in shares, not in oil or bonds. The Nikkei is down 4.9 percent, the Nasdaq 100 down 1.54 percent and European indexes are higher, with the DAX up 0.95 percent, so the selling is concentrated in Asia and in US technology rather than general. Against that, Brent is up, gold is up 0.38 percent to 4,186 dollars an ounce, the 10 year Treasury yield is steady at 5.11 percent, up 1.9 basis points, and the dollar index is flat at 101.9. Markets are cutting the crowded AI position and paying more for physical oil, while leaving the duration trade that rallied on Thursday largely untouched.
