Osisko Metals (TSX: OM) has outlined an underground deposit containing 7.4 billion pounds of copper at its Gaspé project in eastern Quebec, at nearly twice the grade of its measured and indicated open pit resources. The first underground estimate for Porphyry Mountain, 1.1 km northeast of the centre of the old Copper Mountain pit, puts inferred resources at 706 million tonnes grading 0.48 percent copper and 0.055 percent molybdenum, Osisko said Monday in a resource update released after markets closed. The estimate, effective October 30, draws on drilling from 1994 to 2019 and new assays of some of the old core.

Measured and indicated resources in the pit now total 2.15 billion tonnes grading 0.25 percent copper for 11.6 billion pounds of the metal. In April, the estimate stood at 1.83 billion tonnes of 0.27 percent copper for 10.8 billion pounds of contained metal. Osisko lowered the reporting cut off to 0.12 percent copper equivalent from 0.16 percent, bringing more lower grade material into the pit estimate. A further inferred 265 million tonnes at 0.38 percent copper contain 2.2 billion pounds in the pit.

Osisko is working to reopen the former Noranda mine at Murdochville, about 825 km northeast of Montreal. The company modelled the deeper deposit for block caving, which uses gravity to draw broken rock into tunnels below, but has yet to demonstrate the economics of mining it. National Bank Financial analyst Rabi Nizami said the underground resource was larger than anticipated; he had expected about 4 billion pounds of copper equivalent metal underground, and noted the open pit remains the focus of development for the next several years.

Results from this year's 70,000 metre drill program are due to feed into the next resource update, planned for April. Osisko shares rose 8.7 percent to $1.12 on Monday in Toronto before the update's release, valuing the company at about C$1 billion.
