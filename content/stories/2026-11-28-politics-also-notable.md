OPEC+ meets on Sunday with quotas expected to roll over. The 42nd OPEC and non-OPEC Ministerial Meeting opens Sunday, November 29 at 1300 GMT, with the current production agreement expiring on December 31. Delegates have told Reuters and Bloomberg that ministers agreed in principle to leave targets unchanged for the first quarter of 2027, but the harder question, converting the first independent capacity audit by DeGolyer and MacNaughton into 2027 baselines, is unresolved: Iraq disputes its assessed capacity of about 4.9 million barrels a day and is pressing for recognition of a higher figure, and Kazakhstan has also sought an upward revision. Brent settled at 95.44 dollars a barrel on Friday. The commodities beat carries the market detail; the decision itself lands Sunday.

Taiwan's nuclear referendum count runs into Saturday night. The referendum on ending the nuclear-free homeland policy, proposed by the KMT and approved by the Legislative Yuan 59 to 51 in August, passes only if yes votes outnumber no votes and reach at least a quarter of the 20,003,835 eligible voters, a threshold of 5,000,959 yes votes. Yesterday's story set out the background; the count was expected to finish later than the local election results on Saturday evening, and any restart decision still requires regulator and Taipower safety approvals over years, not the vote alone.

Brussels clears Deutsche Boerse's largest ever deal. The European Commission on November 27 unconditionally approved Deutsche Boerse's acquisition of Allfunds under the EU Merger Regulation, concluding after a Phase I review that the transaction raises no competition concerns in the European Economic Area. The deal, agreed on January 21 at 8.80 euros per Allfunds share and valuing the fund platform at about 5.3 billion euros, still needs further supervisory clearances and an English court's sanction of the scheme of arrangement; Deutsche Boerse now expects completion in the first quarter of 2027. This was the politics beat's single-source gap-check flag, corroborated here by the Commission release and the company's own disclosure.

Lakenheath drone incident: no new official statement. The story published earlier today on the reported drone swarm at RAF Lakenheath stands as written; neither the UK Ministry of Defence nor the US Air Force had added to their November 13 statements in the reporting read this morning, and the reported Iranian link remains uncorroborated, graded C in that file.
