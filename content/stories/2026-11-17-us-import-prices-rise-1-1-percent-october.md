US import prices increased 1.1 percent in October, matching September's advance, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on Tuesday, with higher nonfuel prices driving the rise. Nonfuel import prices rose 0.8 percent, fuel import prices rose 3.6 percent on higher petroleum and natural gas prices, and import prices for capital goods jumped 1.5 percent, the largest monthly advance since that index was first published on a monthly basis in December 1988, led by computers, peripherals and semiconductors. Import prices from China rose 1.4 percent, the largest monthly increase since that series began in January 2004.

Prices for US exports also advanced 1.1 percent in October after a 2.4 percent rise in September. Agricultural export prices rose 1.2 percent and have not fallen on a monthly basis since December 2025, while nonagricultural export prices rose 1.1 percent on higher prices for industrial supplies, consumer goods and automotive products.
