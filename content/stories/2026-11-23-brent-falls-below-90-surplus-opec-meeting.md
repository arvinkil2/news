Brent crude fell 4.05 percent to 88.82 dollars a barrel in the latest session recorded in the estate market tables, down 3.92 percent on the week, while WTI fell a smaller 1.62 percent to 82.62 dollars, widening the Brent premium over WTI to 6.20 dollars. The fall extends a slide that has taken Brent down more than 20 percent from its late October high near 118 dollars, through three straight weekly declines.

The drivers have not changed, but they are compounding. Crude shipments through the Strait of Hormuz have recovered to roughly pre-war levels even as attacks on tankers continue, US crude inventories posted a larger than expected build of 3.4 million barrels in the EIA report of November 19 for the week ended November 13, and the International Energy Agency expects the market to move into surplus in the fourth quarter as Middle East exports recover faster than demand. Reporting on Friday's earlier session recorded Brent settling at 90.33 dollars, down 5.2 percent on that week, on the same combination of stock builds, surplus forecasts and recovering Gulf flows.

The curve still disagrees with the flat price. January Brent settled at 92.57 dollars on Friday against 89.95 dollars for February, a backwardation of 2.62 dollars that says prompt barrels remain scarce even as the forward market prices next year's surplus. That tension is the story of the oil market this month: the physical market is tight enough to pay up for immediate delivery, and the paper market is selling the surplus it expects in 2027.

Everything now runs through Sunday, November 29, when OPEC+ ministers meet to set the group's 2027 production framework, including the fate of a layer of cuts of roughly 2 million barrels a day that covers most members only until the end of 2026. The January Brent contract expires the next day, November 30. If the group lets the cut layer lapse as Gulf output normalises, the surplus the agencies forecast gets larger; if it extends restraint, a market this short of length could reprice higher quickly.
