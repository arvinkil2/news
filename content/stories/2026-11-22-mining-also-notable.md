**Skouries filter presses running.** Eldorado Gold has the first two of six Metso tailings filter presses operational at its Skouries copper-gold mine in northern Greece, with commissioning of the remaining four due to complete in November. Four presses are required for commercial production on sulphide ore and five for nameplate throughput, and the company is targeting commercial production in the fourth quarter. The filtration plant is the pacing item for the mine's start-up, so the November completion is the milestone to watch.

**Saudi mining revenues climb.** Saudi Arabia's Operating Revenues Index rose 12.2 percent year on year in September, according to the General Authority for Statistics, with manufacturing revenues up 15 percent and mining and quarrying among the fastest growing activities. The print is a revenue index rather than a production measure, but it keeps mining on the list of sectors the kingdom's diversification programme can point to with current data.

**Malawi formalises gold buying at Karonga.** Malawi's mining minister Thoko Tembo has opened a gold trading centre at Karonga, the sixth under the Reserve Bank of Malawi's Export Development Fund, which buys artisanal gold to keep it out of smuggling channels. Officials said the fund had purchased 2.3 tonnes of gold valued at K1.13 trillion as of November 2026, and estimated smuggling costs the country about $700 million a year in lost foreign exchange. Traditional leaders at the opening pressed the government to pair the buying centres with enforcement against illegal mining in the district.

**Operators weigh Chinese equipment.** A Harbour survey of more than 100 mine operators and contractors, published ahead of bauma CHINA 2026 in Shanghai, found faster adoption of Chinese mining equipment in surface coal and iron ore, with continuing concerns about software integration, lifecycle support and spare parts logistics outside China. The confidence gap, rather than headline specifications, is what procurement teams say will decide how fast Chinese fleets spread in export markets.
