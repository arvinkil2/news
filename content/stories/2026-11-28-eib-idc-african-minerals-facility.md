The European Investment Bank and South Africa's Industrial Development Corporation have agreed to jointly provide up to 300 million euros, about $327 million, to advance African critical minerals projects, linking investment in local processing and industry with Europe's efforts to diversify its supplies.

Announced on Thursday, the facility is expected to support around 900 million euros in total investment, according to the EIB. It is consistent with South Africa's 2025 Critical Minerals Strategy, which aims to process more minerals locally and expand the country's role in supplying global industries. "Ultimately, this facility is about turning our mineral wealth into factories, skills and jobs," IDC chief executive Mmakgoshi Lekhethe said in a statement.

Eligible activities range from extraction and processing to refining, recycling and substitution, along with supporting energy, transport and digital infrastructure. Projects in South Africa and other sub-Saharan African countries must contribute to local industrial development and demonstrate a clear connection to European value chains, the EIB said. By enabling investment in strategic minerals and related industries, the financing "will contribute to economic growth, value creation and greater supply chain resilience for both Africa and Europe," EIB vice-president Ambroise Fayolle said.

The facility is the EIB's first dedicated critical minerals financing operation outside the European Union under the strategic initiative it launched in 2025. The announcement did not identify project recipients or disclose individual loan allocations, and projects are expected to meet international environmental and social standards. For developers, the funding could help address financing needs that extend beyond the boundaries of a mine or a processing plant, a major obstacle to developing the continent's mineral resources.

The deal follows a November 2025 accord between the European Union and South Africa on sustainable minerals and metals value chains, signed alongside a clean trade and investment agreement. The EIB has already backed preparatory work elsewhere in Africa, offering Andrada Mining and EcoGraf up to 2 million euros each in technical assistance in February for lithium work in Namibia and graphite development in Tanzania. On Wednesday the bank also agreed to invest about 400 million euros in Polish copper miner KGHM, reinforcing its parallel push to secure production within Europe.
