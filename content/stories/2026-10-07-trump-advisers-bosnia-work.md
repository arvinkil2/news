President Donald Trump on Wednesday distanced himself from two of his top political advisers, James Blair and Chris LaCivita, writing that if their outside work presents a conflict, "I will terminate them, immediately," CNN reported.

## Latest

Retiring Sen. Thom Tillis (R-N.C.) condemned the arrangement in a CNN interview Wednesday evening, calling Republika Srpska a "destabilizing force" and urging the advisers to cut ties. "These guys are not hurting for money. They don't need to be doing that," Tillis told CNN's Kasie Hunt. "They shouldn't be doing it, and if the reports are true, they should get out of that relationship very, very quickly." Tillis, who co-chairs the Senate NATO Observer Group, added: "This is about knowing your customer, and this customer is bad news for that region... It's a horrible look." He said he would leave any firing decision to Trump.

The Hill also added detail on the underlying New York Times report: LaCivita and Blair were each paid hundreds of thousands of dollars through a Washington-based firm hired by the Republika Srpska government to advise on voting issues ahead of Sunday's elections — two sources told the Times the work was for the government, not the dominant nationalist party, though the two are heavily intertwined. Blair, the former White House deputy chief of staff who left in April to run the MAGA Inc. super PAC, and LaCivita, Trump's 2024 co-campaign manager, are leading Trump's midterm advertising campaign. Trump called the pair "outside consultants" on Wednesday and repeated he would terminate them if their work conflicted with the White House.

## Earlier

The warning came after the New York Times reported that Blair, who leads Trump's outside political operation, and LaCivita, Trump's 2024 co-campaign manager, traveled to the Balkans last week for paid work on behalf of a pro-Russian government in Bosnia's Republika Srpska, ahead of elections there on Sunday.

The revelation lands less than 30 days before the midterm elections, with Blair and LaCivita leading a big-money midterm advertising campaign for the Trump political operation. The New York Post characterized the work as advising a pro-Russia Bosnia party, adding to pressure on the operation over conflicts between its domestic campaign role and foreign clients.
