The Food and Drug Administration has approved Hepcludex (bulevirtide), made by Gilead Sciences Inc., to treat adults with chronic hepatitis D virus infection and compensated liver disease. The drug is given as a 2 milligram injection under the skin once a day, and the agency called it the first and only FDA approved treatment option for hepatitis D, which it described as the most serious form of viral hepatitis and a cause of faster progression to cirrhosis, liver failure and death than other forms. The virus can infect only people who carry hepatitis B, and the FDA estimates that at least 12 percent of hepatitis B patients worldwide also have hepatitis D.

The approval rests on three trials of the drug given once or twice daily. At the approved 2 milligram dose, about 50 percent of patients met the combined measure of an undetectable virus level or a large fall in virus level together with normalisation of the liver enzyme ALT at week 48. The most common adverse reactions were headache, reactions at the injection site, raised eosinophil counts, itching, fatigue, joint pain, upper respiratory tract infection, mouth and throat pain and influenza like illness, and the drug must not be used by patients with a hypersensitivity to it. Acting FDA Commissioner Kyle Diamantas said the approval gives patients a new option where effective treatment has been limited, and Michael Davis, director of the Center for Drug Evaluation and Research, said it advances treatment for a serious disease that has had significant unmet need.
