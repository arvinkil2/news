Monday's close took back much of Friday's rebound. Silver fell 3.25 percent to 41.74 dollars an ounce, copper fell 2.07 percent to 5.51 dollars a pound and gold fell 0.79 percent to 3,622.40 dollars, leaving the three metals down 11.72 percent, 7.79 percent and 6.26 percent respectively on the week in the estate market tables.

Early Tuesday trade extended the pattern. The Edge's 08:28 GMT wrap put January platinum down 3.46 percent, the largest early move in the complex, with December silver at 41.775 dollars, down 3.24 percent against Monday's settlement, December copper at 5.4770 dollars, down 2.79 percent, and December gold at 3,610.70 dollars, down 1.11 percent. Platinum, palladium and copper were all below Friday's settlements, erasing Monday's intraday gains.

The weekly context remains a broad liquidation. Kalkine's review of last week put silver down 16.39 percent, gold down 9.54 percent and copper down 9.35 percent, with zinc and lead also lower. Friday's bounce, gold up 3.16 percent and silver up 3.67 percent, was attributed in yesterday's edition to oversold readings after that selloff rather than to any change in physical balances.

That distinction still holds. Nothing in this window loosens the physical copper position described on November 20, record Yangshan premiums and Shanghai stocks at their lowest since December 2023. Price is being set by positioning and rates, not by the physical market, which is the divergence to watch if the liquidation continues into the November 29 OPEC+ meeting and the December Federal Reserve decision.
