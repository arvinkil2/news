ZIM Integrated Shipping Services reported third quarter net income of $830 million, up from $123 million a year earlier, on revenue up 53 percent to $2.71 billion. Average freight rates climbed 55 percent year over year to $2,480 per TEU while cargo volume rose only 4 percent to 962,000 TEUs, so pricing did the work. Adjusted EBITDA rose 124 percent to $1.33 billion, and ZIM raised its full year guidance to adjusted EBITDA of $3.3 billion to $3.5 billion and adjusted EBIT of $2 billion to $2.2 billion. The board declared a dividend of $2.01 per share. Hapag-Lloyd's proposed acquisition of ZIM at $35 per share in cash remains subject to regulatory approvals, including Israel's consent under its special state share, and is now targeted to close in the first quarter of 2027.

In the market tables, tanker freight ended the week 20.54 percent higher while dry bulk freight fell 2.49 percent on the day and 6.26 percent on the week, a split that matches ZIM's account of port congestion and disrupted shipping passages supporting rates on the routes that remain constrained.
