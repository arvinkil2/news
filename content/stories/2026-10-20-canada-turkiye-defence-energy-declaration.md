Canada and Turkiye are expected to sign a joint declaration during Prime Minister Mark Carney's visit this week that deepens cooperation in defence, energy, the economy and social and diplomatic ties, and sets up committees in each area to draft action plans, a person briefed on the matter said in a Reuters report carried by Arab News. Carney meets President Tayyip Erdogan in Ankara on Thursday and Turkish business leaders in Istanbul on Friday, accompanied by Foreign Minister Anita Anand and Energy Minister Tim Hodgson, in what Ottawa describes as an effort to reduce trade dependence on the United States. The energy agenda is expected to cover a working group on a possible CANDU bid for a planned nuclear plant on Turkiye's Black Sea coast, potential Turkish purchases of Canadian liquefied natural gas and critical minerals, while defence talks focus on drones and naval cooperation.
