**Latest:** Prime Minister Mark Carney and President Recep Tayyip Erdogan announced the launch of a Canada-Turkiye Strategic Partnership in Ankara on Thursday, backed by a three-year action plan. The joint statement said the two sides will work toward the conclusion of a Canada-Turkiye free trade agreement, with the first round of trade talks to be held in Ankara next month, and Erdogan told reporters the two countries had agreed to begin the negotiations before the end of the year. He put the aim at raising bilateral trade from about 3 billion dollars to 5 billion dollars initially and then to 10 billion dollars.

The action plan rests on four pillars named in the statement: trade and investment, defence and security, energy, and ties between people. On trade, the priority sectors named are energy supply, defence production, aerospace and artificial intelligence. On energy, the two countries' energy ministers will work on liquefied natural gas, nuclear energy and critical minerals, the file that had a possible CANDU bid for a planned Turkish nuclear plant on the Black Sea coast on its agenda ahead of the visit. On defence and security, the leaders said they intend to deepen cooperation and to work together in support of peace and security in the Euro-Atlantic region and beyond, including Ukraine, the South Caucasus and the Middle East, in particular Gaza.

The outcome matches the declaration this site previewed on October 20, when a person briefed on the matter said the two governments were expected to sign a joint declaration deepening cooperation on defence, energy, the economy and diplomatic ties during Carney's visit, with committees in each area to draft action plans. Carney's trip is the first dedicated bilateral visit to Turkiye by a Canadian prime minister. He is accompanied by Foreign Minister Anita Anand and Energy Minister Tim Hodgson, and travels on to Istanbul on Friday to meet Turkish business leaders.
