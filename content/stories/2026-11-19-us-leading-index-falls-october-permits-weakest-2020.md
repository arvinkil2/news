The Conference Board's Leading Economic Index for the United States fell 0.3 percent in October to 99.3, after being unchanged in September, the Board said on Thursday. The decline was mostly fueled by fewer building permits, which were at their weakest level since May 2020 and fell across almost every category and region, and consumer expectations for business conditions, average hours worked in manufacturing, and manufacturers' new orders for nondefense capital goods were also negative contributors.

The index still grew 0.1 percent between April and October, a slight reversal from its 0.6 percent decline over the previous six months, and the coincident index was unchanged in October at 115.1. The Conference Board expects real GDP to grow 2.3 percent in 2026 and 2.1 percent in 2027, and said the six-month growth rate points to continued expansion, but increasingly investment-led with softer household demand.
