Japan scrambled fighter jets from the Air Self-Defense Force's Western Air Defense Force early on November 17 after two suspected Russian aircraft entered Japanese airspace northwest of Okinoshima, an island in Fukuoka Prefecture, at about 4:07 a.m. local time, according to Japan's Defense Ministry. The first aircraft stayed about three minutes, a second entered the same area at about 4:09 a.m. and also left after roughly three minutes, and the jets issued notifications and warnings before the aircraft crossed the Tsushima Strait and continued into the Pacific.

Defense Minister Shinjiro Koizumi said Tokyo lodged a strong protest with Moscow and demanded measures to prevent further violations, calling the incidents a grave infringement of Japan's sovereignty and entirely unacceptable. The incursion came as Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi warned of growing instability in the international order at the summit meetings in Manila and pledged a more proactive Japanese role in regional security.
