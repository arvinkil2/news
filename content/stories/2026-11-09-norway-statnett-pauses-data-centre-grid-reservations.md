Norway's grid operator Statnett has temporarily halted the reservation of connections for new data centres, a move that acts as a moratorium on new grid-connected developments until regulations are in place. Projects already connected or already grid-approved are unaffected, and Statnett said the halt is expected to be lifted no later than August 1, 2027.

The government is preparing a licensing scheme that is expected to favour data centres serving defence, police and emergency services, health services and other critical infrastructure such as the mobile network and the financial system. "We want data centers in Norway, but we can't have all types of data centers," Digitalization Minister Torgeir Micaelsen said. "We must therefore control the growth and ensure that Norway gets the data centers we actually need, those that provide the greatest societal benefit."

The scale of demand explains the intervention. About 800 megawatts of data centres are currently connected in Norway, 4.2 gigawatts are reserved and 9.1 gigawatts sit in the capacity queue, with a further 18 gigawatts requested, against national peak demand of about 25 gigawatts. Energy Minister Terje Aasland said Statnett had received connection applications for 26,000 megawatts this year, more than the country's current capacity, with data centres accounting for 75 percent of requests. A Norwegian Technology Council report last month said foreign companies own 90 percent of data centre capacity in Norway and that the facilities provide few jobs beyond construction.
