The Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency approved linvoseltamab, sold as Lynozyfic, on November 27, the agency announced. The approval covers adults with multiple myeloma whose cancer has worsened after at least three previous treatments, including an immunomodulatory agent, a proteasome inhibitor and an anti-CD38 antibody. The marketing authorisation was granted to Regeneron UK Limited through the International Recognition Procedure.

Linvoseltamab is a bispecific antibody that binds BCMA on myeloma cells and CD3 on T cells, bringing the two together so the T cells kill the cancer cells. It is given on its own in this population.

In the main study of 117 patients with relapsed or refractory disease after at least three prior therapies, about 71 percent responded to treatment (83 of 117), and about 50 percent had a complete response (58 of 117). Responses lasted an average of 29 months, according to the MHRA.

The drug is already authorised in the European Union and the United States, where the FDA granted accelerated approval in July 2025 for patients after at least four prior lines of therapy. The UK decision aligns the British label more closely with the European position of three prior therapies.

Julian Beach, the MHRA's Executive Director of Operations, said the agency was pleased to approve linvoseltamab as an option for patients whose cancer has worsened despite at least three previous treatments, and that its safety will remain under close review. The Summary of Product Characteristics and patient leaflet will be published on the MHRA Products website.
