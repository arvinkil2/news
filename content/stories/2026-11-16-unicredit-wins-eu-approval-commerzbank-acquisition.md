UniCredit has won unconditional European Union antitrust approval for its acquisition of Commerzbank, the European Commission said on Monday, clearing the biggest regulatory hurdle between the Italian bank and control of Germany's second largest lender.

The decision imposes no conditions or remedies on the deal. It follows the European Central Bank's green light last month, when the ECB authorised UniCredit on October 15 to raise its stake in Commerzbank above 30 percent. UniCredit has built a position of close to 50 percent of Commerzbank's voting rights through an exchange offer that concluded in July and through financial derivatives, and chief executive Andrea Orcel said on November 9 that all remaining regulatory approvals would come before the end of November, sooner than anticipated.

"Europe needs stronger banks that can compete, grow and support our businesses and families. Cross-border mergers can help make this happen, allowing banks to operate more efficiently and better withstand economic shocks," Teresa Ribera, the Commissioner in charge of competition, said. "But growth must not come at the expense of customers. Our approval of UniCredit's acquisition of Commerzbank shows that it is possible to support a stronger European banking sector while safeguarding competition and choice," she added.

The outcome matches what was expected going in. Reuters reported on November 9, citing three people with direct knowledge of the matter, that the Commission was set to clear the takeover without conditions by its November 16 deadline. Orcel has said UniCredit would seek an extraordinary general meeting of Commerzbank shareholders immediately after the remaining approvals land, to begin changing the German bank's governance structure.
