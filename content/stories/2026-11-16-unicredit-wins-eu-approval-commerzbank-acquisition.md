Latest: UniCredit said on Thursday that it has received the outstanding approvals from Poland's financial supervision authority, the KNF, for the completion of its takeover offer for Commerzbank, and that all regulatory clearances required for settlement have now been obtained and all offer conditions fulfilled. The voluntary public exchange offer, launched on May 5, 2026 for all Commerzbank shares not directly held by UniCredit, will now settle: the new UniCredit shares for the tendered Commerzbank shares are expected to be credited at custodian banks by December 7, 2026, no later than the tenth business day after the announcement. UniCredit has also asked Commerzbank to convene an extraordinary general meeting to elect new shareholder representatives to the supervisory board.

Previously: UniCredit and the German government have reached agreement on the big sticking points in the Italian bank’s takeover of Commerzbank, Reuters reported on Wednesday. In a letter dated November 18 and seen by Reuters, chief executive Andrea Orcel told Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil he was prepared to commit to a series of binding principles. Commerzbank’s German headquarters will remain in Frankfurt as a listed entity, a central German demand, and Berlin has given up its insistence on naming two supervisory board members. UniCredit also pledged to put German nationals on its own board of directors.

Klingbeil said Germany supports a swift process and an orderly transition of control, and that UniCredit had made clear commitments to Germany as a business location, to the rights of Commerzbank employees and to continued dialogue with the works council. ORF, reporting the same agreement in German, added that Commerzbank is to expand its business with mid-sized companies and that the two sides agreed an orderly transition in the supervisory board. The German government holds 13.3 percent of Commerzbank from its financial crisis bailout, and UniCredit has built a stake nearing 50 percent. A combination would create a bank with more than 1.3 trillion euros, or 1.43 trillion dollars, in assets. Neither report published a takeover price, an offer timetable or a completion date.

UniCredit has won unconditional European Union antitrust approval for its acquisition of Commerzbank, the European Commission said on Monday, clearing the biggest regulatory hurdle between the Italian bank and control of Germany's second largest lender.

The decision imposes no conditions or remedies on the deal. It follows the European Central Bank's green light last month, when the ECB authorised UniCredit on October 15 to raise its stake in Commerzbank above 30 percent. UniCredit has built a position of close to 50 percent of Commerzbank's voting rights through an exchange offer that concluded in July and through financial derivatives, and chief executive Andrea Orcel said on November 9 that all remaining regulatory approvals would come before the end of November, sooner than anticipated.

"Europe needs stronger banks that can compete, grow and support our businesses and families. Cross-border mergers can help make this happen, allowing banks to operate more efficiently and better withstand economic shocks," Teresa Ribera, the Commissioner in charge of competition, said. "But growth must not come at the expense of customers. Our approval of UniCredit's acquisition of Commerzbank shows that it is possible to support a stronger European banking sector while safeguarding competition and choice," she added.

The outcome matches what was expected going in. Reuters reported on November 9, citing three people with direct knowledge of the matter, that the Commission was set to clear the takeover without conditions by its November 16 deadline. Orcel has said UniCredit would seek an extraordinary general meeting of Commerzbank shareholders immediately after the remaining approvals land, to begin changing the German bank's governance structure.
