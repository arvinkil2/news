HealthSpan Partners, an affiliate of the nonprofit hospital system Bon Secours Mercy Health, will take Surgery Partners private in a deal valued at about $5.7 billion including debt, the companies said on October 26, in terms reported by Reuters and independently by STAT. Shareholders other than Bain Capital will receive $23.50 per share, a premium of 49 percent to the last close, and Surgery Partners shares rose 46 percent on the announcement. The deal is expected to close in the first quarter of 2027.

Bain Capital, which has been a shareholder since 2017 and owns 39 percent of Surgery Partners, will back the transaction and retain a major ownership stake after closing, rather than selling out. The structure ends a two year strategic review in which Surgery Partners drew interest from several buyers, including a Bain bid to take the company private last year that was rebuffed.

Surgery Partners operates more than 200 outpatient surgery centers, short stay hospitals and related services across 30 states, with about 5,000 affiliated physicians. Adding it expands Bon Secours Mercy Health well beyond its more than 45 hospitals and makes the Catholic system one of the largest ambulatory surgery operators in the country. Reuters noted the deal follows Ascension's $3.9 billion purchase of the surgical firm AmSurg earlier this year, part of a wider push by hospital systems into outpatient surgery, where volumes are migrating away from inpatient settings.

Mizuho analyst Ann Hynes told Reuters there is some market overlap in Ohio and Kentucky but the transaction substantially broadens the system's national footprint, so meaningful Federal Trade Commission antitrust risk is not expected, and Bain retaining its position should help the deal close.
