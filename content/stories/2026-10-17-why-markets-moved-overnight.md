Broadcom fell 4.52 percent on Friday, the largest single move in the tables. Today's story "Chip selloff deepens as Broadcom leads technology lower" reads the fall as part of a broad repricing of the AI complex on financing and valuation, set against TSMC guidance earlier in the week that pointed to demand growth of close to 40 percent for the full year.

The semiconductor index fell 4.14 percent on Friday, taking its weekly loss to 11.8 percent. The same chip selloff story covers the index move and makes the same point: no company specific announcement in today's reporting explains the sector fall, and the argument in the market is about the cost of building AI capacity, not about end demand.

Brent crude rose 3.72 percent to 109.86 dollars a barrel, its highest close since May after touching 110 dollars intraday. Today's commodities reporting and Friday's Brent coverage on this site attribute the bid to the Hormuz supply risk premium that built through the week, and the midday check found the settlement unchanged.

WTI crude rose 3.51 percent to 101.41 dollars a barrel, up 10.41 percent on the week, the largest weekly gain in the commodity tables. It is the US leg of the same supply move as Brent, covered in the same commodities reporting, rather than a separate development.

Meta fell 3.72 percent on Friday. No story in today's reporting gives a company specific cause for the fall: today's chip selloff story places Meta inside the broader technology decline, and today's separate Meta story, on revised business terms claiming AI scraping rights, is a regulatory story and is not presented in the reporting as the driver of the share move.

Natural gas fell 3.71 percent to 3.12 dollars per million Btu. Today's story "Gas build misses forecasts but futures fall on mild weather" ties the fall to Friday's trading after the weekly storage report: a 67 billion cubic foot build took stocks to 3,567 billion cubic feet, below the five year average build, but mild weather forecasts through late October outweighed the tighter stock position.

The broader technology sector fell 3.02 percent on Friday and 7.7 percent on the week. That move is the sector level version of the chip story above, with Microsoft down 1.93 percent, Alphabet down 2.47 percent and Nvidia down 1.43 percent in the same session, as reported in today's chip selloff story.

Rare earth miners fell 2.71 percent on Friday, taking their weekly loss to 11.05 percent, the worst in the mining tables. Today's story "Growth metal miners slide on week as metals hold" frames the mining falls as a position unwind in the growth metals complex: uranium miners fell 8.92 percent on the week and the lithium and battery basket 8.15 percent, while gold itself fell only 0.83 percent on the week to 4,181.10 dollars.

The cross market read at midday is unchanged from the close, because there has been no new session to change it. It is a split tape rather than a general risk off move: energy was bid on physical supply risk, growth and AI exposed equities were sold, Treasury yields were steady on Friday after falling 12 to 16 basis points on the week (10 year 5.10 percent, up 0.9 basis points on the day, in today's story "Treasury yields steady Friday, down sharply on week"), and the dollar index eased 0.16 percent to 101.84. Gold held at 4,181.10 dollars, up 0.26 percent on Friday, in today's story "Silver ends week down six percent as gold holds gains", while silver closed the week down 6.06 percent.
