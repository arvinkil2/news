Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh, opening the Fed's Productivity and Jobs Task Force conference in California, called superintelligence an entirely new force in economic life and said Board staff have begun evaluating scenarios for its impact, stressing they are illustrative and not forecasts. At one end, he said, the technology lifts productivity and real GDP growth persistently above its long run average with the economy near full employment; in another, growth rises but automation and labour market frictions push joblessness above full employment levels. In the third, AI disappoints after heavy capital investment, equity valuations fall and the economy slows, and Warsh said the Fed's role is neither advocacy nor opposition, but to recognise that adoption will have important implications for monetary policy down the road.
