Boehringer Ingelheim and Sino Biopharmaceutical announced a multi-program option and licence agreement on Wednesday covering six preclinical cancer programs built on antibody-drug conjugate and T-cell engager technologies, five of them from Sino’s LaNova Medicines subsidiary in Shanghai and one from Beijing Tide Pharmaceutical. Sino will receive $358 million in upfront and near-term payments and could receive a further $7 billion in option-exercise, development, regulatory and commercial milestone payments, plus tiered royalties of up to double digits on future sales.

Sino will lead early development of the programs through global Phase I trials. Once agreed clinical milestones are reached, Boehringer may take exclusive development, manufacturing and commercialisation rights to four programs outside mainland China, Hong Kong and Macau, and to the remaining two programs worldwide.
