Koryx Copper is proposing a bigger mine at its Haib project in southern Namibia, where its first prefeasibility study raises the upfront cost by 32 percent and planned annual payable copper output by 41 percent for the first decade.

The study puts the cost of building Haib at $2.06 billion (C$2.94 billion), up from $1.56 billion in the September 2025 assessment. The open pit would produce 130,000 tonnes of payable copper a year over its first decade, up from 92,000 tonnes, and average 105,000 tonnes of copper, 12,000 ounces of gold and 1.13 million pounds of molybdenum a year over 24 years. At a copper price of $5 per pound, the project carries an after-tax net present value of $2.54 billion at an 8 percent discount rate and a 25 percent internal rate of return, up from $1.35 billion and 20 percent in the earlier study, which used $4.31 per pound. Life-of-project cash costs of $2.19 per pound came in below analyst estimates.

The November 6 estimate gives Haib its first mineral reserve: 1.12 billion tonnes of probable reserves at 0.27 percent copper, containing about 3 million tonnes of copper, 65,000 tonnes of molybdenum and 689,000 ounces of gold. The redesign drops heap leaching, cuts the waste-to-ore ratio from 1.88 to 0.98 tonnes, and sends higher-grade material to the mill first.

Koryx applied for a mining licence in September 2025 and submitted its environmental assessment in August 2026. The project needs about 20 million cubic metres of water a year, drawn from the Orange River only when flows exceed downstream and environmental needs and stored behind an off-channel dam; the study budgets $80 million for water supply works and $26 million for the dam, and analysts flagged a stressed Orange River as the main risk. The company aims to secure major permits around mid-2027, start early works late next year and begin construction in the first quarter of 2028. Koryx shares fell 7.8 percent to C$3.18 at Tuesday's close in Toronto, valuing the company at about C$390 million, against cash of about C$35 million at the end of October.
