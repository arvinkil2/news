The Financial Times reported that Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner used a meeting in Miami last week with officials from Britain, France, Germany, the EU, NATO and Ukraine to propose unfreezing part of Russia's frozen assets as a way to restart peace negotiations, with the released funds placed in a special financial vehicle under Washington's control. European officials rejected the proposal, according to the report, which a US source described as part of the work toward a comprehensive peace agreement, with the release and spending mechanisms still under discussion. The account surfaced in Russian media on Wednesday evening and has not been confirmed by European or US officials in the sources scanned for this item.

Update, October 15: The rejection is now on the record at head of government level. Chancellor Friedrich Merz told reporters at the European Council in Brussels on Thursday, in remarks carried by Reuters, that frozen Russian assets must stay in Europe, a pointed rebuff to the US proposal reported by the Financial Times. The summit's Ukraine session follows on Friday, with leaders also expected to address Ukraine's 2027 funding needs, for which the frozen assets remain the largest candidate source.
