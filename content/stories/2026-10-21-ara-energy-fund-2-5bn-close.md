Ara Partners has reached final close on Ara Energy Fund I, raising 2.5 billion dollars including 1.8 billion dollars of fund commitments and more than 700 million dollars of associated co-investment capital. The fund launched in the second quarter of 2025, held a first close that December, and reached its hard cap about ten months later, adding new institutional investors including pension funds, insurers, sovereign wealth funds, endowments and foundations across North America and Europe.

The strategy is deliberately unfashionable. Ara launched the energy business in partnership with HF Capital, which invests for the Haslam family, to acquire, optimise and, where economically attractive, decarbonise assets along the conventional energy value chain. The seed portfolio already spans three areas: thermal generation across the NYISO, ISO New England and PJM markets totalling about 3.0 gigawatts, US ethanol production of about 400 million gallons a year, and JET, a retail fuel network of about 970 stations in Germany and Austria held with Ara Infrastructure.

The senior team, led by partner Shameek Konar, worked together for years before joining Ara. Konar described the three sectors as uncorrelated and said the capital will increase grid capacity, strengthen energy security and extend the life of critical infrastructure. Ara, founded in 2017, had about 10.6 billion dollars of assets under management as of June 30, 2026. Threadmark acted as global placement agent and Davis Polk as legal counsel.

Set beside Blackstone's 8.7 billion dollar transition close the same week, Ara's raise shows the bid for conventional assets is not only defensive. Two hard cap closes, one for building new energy companies and one for improving existing thermal, fuel and distribution assets, suggest institutional investors are funding both sides of the transition at once.
