For the first time in its history, the Emmy Awards will have a dedicated home: Prime Video, worldwide, free, with no Amazon subscription required, starting with the 2027 ceremony. The deal ends the "wheel" rotation among ABC, CBS, Fox and NBC that has carried the show since 1955, and gives the Television Academy a single long-term distribution partner instead of renegotiating with a different network each year.

Amazon did not disclose the price, but the press estimates cluster around a meaningful step up from what the networks paid. Variety puts the deal at $90 million over six years, about $15 million a year against the $9.5 million the broadcasters paid on rotation; Deadline reports $20-25 million a year, worth $120 million or more, with Engadget citing up to $25 million a year. The global rights and year-round Academy programming Prime Video will develop are the difference between the two numbers.

The economics of the trade are visible in the ratings. The 78th Primetime Emmys on NBC drew 6.87 million viewers, down nearly 10 percent from the year before, though above the all-time low of 4.3 million in January 2024. The Academy has floated moving the date to avoid clashing with NFL games. The irony is sharp and unavoidable: at the most recent ceremony, 18 of the 19 primetime trophies went to streaming or cable programs, including Netflix, Apple TV and HBO.

The pattern across the awards calendar is now clear. The Oscars move to YouTube in 2029 under a four-year deal, the SAG Awards have streamed on Netflix since 2024, and the Grammys head to ABC and Disney+ this February after 53 years on CBS. Prime Video's Mike Hopkins said the six-year pact is meant to pull the Emmys toward a younger audience and give the presentation more room to innovate.
