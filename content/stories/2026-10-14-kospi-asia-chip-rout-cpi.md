Latest: Seoul rebounded sharply on Friday. The Kosdaq rose more than 5 percent intraday and triggered a buy sidecar at 12:55 p.m. Seoul time, pausing program buy orders for five minutes after the Kosdaq 150 futures rose 6.02 percent and the Kosdaq 150 index rose 5.65 percent. At that point the Kosdaq stood up 5.27 percent at 800.90, back above 800 a day after its circuit breaker close, and the Kospi was up 4.09 percent at 5,781.27. Investing.com put chip shares at the front of a wider Asian recovery, with Samsung Electronics up 2.9 percent and SK hynix up 2.22 percent in its snapshot, and the Nikkei 225 up 1.7 percent.

Earlier: Seoul closed at its lowest since April. The Kospi ended down 454.59 points, or 7.57 percent, at 5,554.22, its lowest close since April 7 and a seventh straight down session, the longest streak since September 2024. The afternoon fall reached 9.29 percent and triggered a full circuit breaker, halting trading for 20 minutes: the first since July 29, the tenth this year and the sixteenth in the index's history. Samsung Electronics closed down 9.5 percent and SK hynix down 11.1 percent, foreign investors net sold 3.6 trillion won on the main board, and the Kosdaq ended down 7.65 percent at 760.82 after its own circuit breaker. South Korea's financial authorities held an emergency meeting and pledged a proactive response on market stability. In Tokyo the Nikkei 225 closed down 4.8 percent, with SoftBank Group down 9.4 percent and Kioxia down about 11 percent, while Taiwan's Taiex fell about 3 percent ahead of TSMC's earnings.

Earlier: the Seoul selloff deepened sharply after the open. The Kospi extended its loss to 7.77 percent, trading at 5,539.51 at 10:30 a.m. local time and touching 5,534.24 at the session low, before paring to 5,605.10, down 6.75 percent, at 11:20 a.m., a level last seen in April. Sell side sidecar curbs, which pause program selling for five minutes, triggered on the Kospi at 9:07 a.m. and on the Kosdaq at 9:12 a.m., the first time both markets triggered on the same day since July 29. Samsung Electronics fell 9.05 percent to 211,000 won and SK hynix fell 10.71 percent to 1,267,000 won. Foreign investors had net sold 1.92 trillion won of main board shares by 10:30 a.m., an eighth straight session of selling, and the VKOSPI volatility gauge rose to 53.84, more than double its long term average. A close lower would make seven straight down sessions, the longest streak since September 2024, and Goldman Sachs likened the multi day pattern to the early pandemic selloff and the 2011 US credit downgrade episode.

Asian chip stocks sold off hard on Thursday morning as the previous day's hot US inflation print travelled into the region's most crowded trade. South Korea's Kospi opened down 5.59 percent at 5,673.01 before paring the loss to about 4.5 percent, putting the index at its lowest since July and on course for its longest losing streak in two years after a sixth straight down session on Wednesday. Samsung Electronics fell about 5.4 percent in early trade and SK Hynix about 6.6 percent, with CNBC putting Hynix's intraday fall at 8 percent, while Samsung Electro-Mechanics and Hanmi Semiconductor dropped more than 8 percent. In Tokyo the Nikkei 225 fell as much as 2.39 percent, with Kioxia down about 8 percent and SoftBank Group down about 7 percent.

The trigger was the same one that hit Wall Street on Wednesday. US consumer prices rose 3.7 percent from a year earlier in September and core prices 2.6 percent, both above forecasts, and traders marked up the odds of a Federal Reserve rate hike in October. US chip shares had already fallen sharply, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index down 4.3 percent, the S&P 500 down 1.11 percent and the Nasdaq down 1.88 percent, and US futures extended the losses in Asian hours while Treasuries gained.

Rates are only half the story Seoul is trading on. Analysts cited by Seoul Economic Daily said the market's test for the AI cycle is shifting from the size of spending to cash generation, pointing to a Gartner warning that trillions of dollars of AI infrastructure investment face impairment risk by 2030 and to CoreWeave's 15 percent fall in the US session. TSMC reports earnings later on Thursday, and Seoul analysts framed the results as the session's turning point: a strong print is the nearest available evidence that the buildout still pays, and a weak one would hand the rout a second leg.
