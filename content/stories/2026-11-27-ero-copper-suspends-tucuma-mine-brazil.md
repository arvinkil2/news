Ero Copper has suspended all production at its Tucumã operation in Pará state, Brazil, in compliance with a notice from SEMAS, the state environment secretariat. Heavy rain from this year's El Niño conditions overwhelmed the pumping capacity of a surface contact-water sump and caused two localized overflows; about 275 mm of rain fell in the first 20 days of November, roughly 170 percent above the normal total for the full month, the company said. SEMAS found anomalous pH readings in water samples near the operation and first ordered mining to stop while processing continued; all production activities have since halted, and operations will resume only after SEMAS reviews and approves the remediation plan Ero expects to submit shortly. Fines tied to the overflows total about US$2.9 million.

The stoppage puts a large share of Ero's output on hold. Tucumã, which began commercial production in July 2025, was expected to produce 32,500 to 37,500 tonnes of copper this year, about 48 percent of the company's 2026 target at the midpoint, and Ero shares fell 7 percent to C$29.85 in Toronto on Friday morning, The Northern Miner reported. Ero also disclosed a separate municipal fine of about US$1.9 million over two fish mortality reports near São Félix do Xingu, about 50 km from the mine; the company said the reports predated the overflows, that it has not received documentation supporting the fine, and that no link to Tucumã has been identified.
