Turkish police raided the Istanbul offices of Halk TV, the pro-opposition broadcaster, on Wednesday morning and a government trustee was appointed to run the channel. The station said hundreds of police entered the building and that entry was barred while a search was conducted; its live broadcast was cut at 6:30 a.m. local time and replaced with pre-recorded material, and editor-in-chief Suat Toktas was not permitted to keep the broadcast running, a Halk TV programmer said. Euronews reported 11 people were arrested in the operation.

There was no immediate comment from the authorities and it was not clear why the raid was ordered. Ozgur Ozel, who leads the main opposition party, condemned the move as an attempt to stop people accessing news, likening trustee appointments at broadcasters to the removal of elected mayors in the wider legal crackdown on the opposition. Rights groups and Western countries have repeatedly criticised President Tayyip Erdogan's government over press freedom, pointing to access bans, fines and restrictions imposed on opposition and independent outlets.
