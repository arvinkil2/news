The Conference Board’s consumer confidence index rose 11.4 points to 103.3 in November, up from 91.9 in October and back to levels last seen in January 2025, the board said on Tuesday. The present situation index rose 13.7 points to 132.8 and the expectations index rose 9.9 points to 83.6, above the 80 level the board has historically associated with a recession signal. The reading was well above the 92.5 consensus reported by investinglive.

The board’s senior economist Fergus McCormick said confidence rallied again in November, potentially due to lower uncertainty following the midterm elections, with the gain led by stronger perceptions of present and future labour market conditions. Consumers planned slightly higher spending on goods and vacations, while average 12-month inflation expectations eased for a second month to 5.7 percent, still elevated.
