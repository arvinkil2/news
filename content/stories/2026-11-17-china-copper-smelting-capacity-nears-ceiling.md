China's concentrate-fed copper smelting capacity has nearly hit a ceiling in 2026, Chen Xuesen, vice president and spokesperson of the state-backed China Nonferrous Metals Industry Association, told Reuters on Tuesday on the sidelines of an International Copper Association event, adding that the association will work with relevant government authorities to establish a capacity monitoring and early-warning mechanism to guide smelters to adjust production. Spot treatment and refining charges, the fees miners pay smelters to turn concentrate into metal, have collapsed to a record low of minus $247.20 on November 6, according to Argus data carried in the report, from minus $42.50 a year earlier, meaning smelters now pay miners instead. The remarks come as the industry gathers in Shanghai this week for the World Copper Conference Asia, where negotiations between Chinese smelters and Chilean miner Antofagasta on next year's term contracts are being closely watched.
