Spain's hotels charged much more for slightly fuller rooms in October. The National Statistics Institute's provisional data show the Hotel Price Index up 7.4 percent year over year, average daily rate up 7.9 percent to 130.4 euros per occupied room, and revenue per available room up 9.0 percent to 93.9 euros. Overnight stays rose only 1.6 percent, to more than 34.7 million.

Occupancy barely moved. Hotels filled 61.8 percent of available places, up 0.3 percent on a year earlier, with the Canaries highest among regions at 74.8 percent. Non resident stays rose 1.2 percent and resident stays 2.7 percent, with visitors from the United Kingdom and Germany accounting for 25.9 and 19.8 percent of non resident nights.

The price detail is broad. Five star hotels averaged 312.1 euros per occupied room, four star 131.5 euros and three star 99.6 euros, and the largest regional price increase was 20.1 percent in Ceuta against 2.5 percent in Navarra.

For euro area inflation watchers, the release is a services stickiness datapoint: the sector is raising prices at 7 to 9 percent annual rates without needing volume growth to do it. Over the first ten months of the year, overnight stays are up 1.5 percent, so October is not an outlier.
