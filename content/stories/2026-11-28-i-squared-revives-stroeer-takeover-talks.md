Infrastructure investor I Squared Capital is in advanced talks to buy Ströer, Germany's largest outdoor advertising group, according to two people familiar with the matter cited by Reuters on November 26. The discussions revive a process that stalled earlier this year, and one of the people said there remains a risk the talks collapse, as the previous effort did.

Ströer's market value has fallen more than 30 percent since January 2025 to about 2.08 billion euros, or $2.28 billion. If the talks progress, I Squared would look to arrange debt financing for the acquisition, the people said. Spokespeople for Ströer and I Squared declined to comment.

The shareholder register is the deal. Co-founder Udo Mueller holds about 24 percent of Ströer and Dirk Ströer, a member of the founding family, holds about 20 percent, so no transaction proceeds without both. I Squared and French infrastructure investor Infravia reached an agreement in principle with key shareholders in July, Manager Magazin reported at the time, but that deal did not materialise.

Ströer operates thousands of poster sites, billboards and digital screens in German railway stations, shopping centres and town squares, an asset base infrastructure funds treat as a contracted, location-scarce network rather than a media business. The company said in 2025 that it had received indicative offers from private equity investors for its out-of-home business.

The revival comes at a lower price than the summer's attempt, which cuts both ways: the equity cheque is smaller, but the sellers are being asked to accept a valuation they already declined once this year.
