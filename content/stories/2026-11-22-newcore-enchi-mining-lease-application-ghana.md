Newcore Gold has submitted a mining lease application for its Enchi gold project in southwest Ghana, covering 53.37 square kilometres of the 248 square kilometre property, which runs along about 40 kilometres of the Bibiani Shear Zone. The application is the key permitting step that moves Enchi from exploration ground toward a developable mine.

Ghana's Minister for Lands and Natural Resources, Emmanuel Armah-Kofi Buah, welcomed the submission in the company's release, calling Enchi one of the most advanced large scale gold projects in development in Ghana and saying the ministry aims to grant a mining lease in the first half of 2027, working with Newcore and the Minerals Commission. Newcore president and chief executive Luke Alexander called the filing an important step in the permitting process. The company expects the review to be completed by mid 2027.

The project behind the application is at prefeasibility stage, completed in June 2026. The study outlined initial capital of $351 million and average annual production of about 104,000 ounces of gold over an initial 9.3-year mine life, with about 130,000 ounces a year in the first three years from higher grade areas and 953,350 payable ounces in total. The resource estimate behind those figures uses drilling through October 2025, so more recent results are not in the economics.

A granted lease would not authorise construction. It would allow Newcore to proceed to a formal environmental authorisation application with Ghana's Environmental Protection Authority, and a final investment decision would still depend on that approval, further technical work and financing. The sequencing matters for the 2027 timeline: the lease decision in the first half of the year is the gate, and the environmental process is the longer pole behind it.
