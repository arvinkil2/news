India will allow Japanese troops to conduct live-fire missile training at its firing ranges for the first time, under a letter of intent announced on November 19 after a meeting of the two countries' foreign and defence ministers in Tokyo, Defense News reported. Japanese officials visited India in October to assess whether its ranges could accommodate training with anti-ship missiles and smaller attack drones, and Japan Ground Self-Defense Force chief Masayoshi Arai said making full use of the roughly 1,000 kilometre range of the Type-25 surface-to-ship missile was a major challenge at home.

The ministers also agreed to accelerate negotiations on the two countries' first defence technology cooperation initiative, under which an Indian state company would co-produce the UNICORN integrated antenna mast, developed for the Japanese navy, for Indian Navy warships. Japan began deploying long-range missiles on Kyushu this year as part of its shift toward stand-off defence against China's military activity in the region.
