**Latest (Sunday 19:46 ET):** Araqchi made the remarks in Baghdad, where he was attending an Organisation of Islamic Cooperation meeting, and urged Washington to accept the proposal, Yonhap reported, citing Reuters. The plan, first put to the United States last month around the United Nations General Assembly, has not been published, but foreign reporting summarised by Yonhap puts its conditions as the United States lifting its maritime blockade, returning frozen Iranian funds at once, waiving sanctions on Iranian oil exports and halting fighting on all fronts, in return for which the strait would reopen within six days and talks would resume on the seventh. Iran said on October 11 that a United States reply had met only some of the conditions. Reuters, as reported by Yonhap, identified Iran's nuclear programme as the remaining disagreement: Washington insists the nuclear issue be addressed from the start of any talks, while Tehran says the focus should first be reopening Hormuz and securing safe passage.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi told Iranian media outlets on Sunday that Iran's offer to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days is still on the table, Arab News reported. If Washington accepts Tehran's conditions, he said, the waterway would be open within six days and United States-Iran talks would resume on the seventh day.

The report did not state what Iran's conditions are, and it carried no United States response. The strait has been effectively closed through the eighth month of the war, cutting off about a fifth of world oil supply, and Gulf Arab states were separately reported on Sunday calling on Iran to guarantee freedom of navigation through Hormuz and asking to be included in any United States-Iran negotiations.
