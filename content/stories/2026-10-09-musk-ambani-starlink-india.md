**Latest (Friday, October 9):** Musk stepped up his attack in a Friday post on X, mockingly calling Ambani the country's "prime minister" to suggest he wields outsized control over its government, and asked: "Naturally, you would prefer to maintain your monopolistic exploitation of the great people of India, but would you nonetheless consider allowing Starlink to compete?" He argued Starlink could bring internet access to unconnected areas, giving children more learning opportunities and helping small businesses reach customers worldwide. Reliance Jio did not immediately respond.

India's communications ministry rejected the blocking allegations, saying the satellite-communications framework is "fair and non-discriminatory" and that Starlink plus two other licensed operators are at "broadly the same regulatory stage," undergoing security assessments that must finish before satellite spectrum is assigned. Telecom minister Jyotiraditya Scindia pushed back as well, saying India "does not allow a monopoly in any sector."

The background: Starlink won the earlier spectrum fight, New Delhi allocated it administratively rather than by auction over Jio's objections, but has not received the go-ahead for commercial operations despite key clearances. Meanwhile Musk's broader India push is stumbling: Tesla, which launched in the country last year, registered just 699 vehicles in its first 12 months of deliveries per data cited by Electrek, with steep import duties pricing the Model Y at nearly twice its US price even after cuts earlier this year.

Elon Musk asked whether Mukesh Ambani is "the real boss of India" in a social-media post on Thursday, a day after accusing unnamed Indian oligarchs of maintaining a "monopolistic chokehold on the Indian people" that has kept Starlink out of India despite five years of complying with "every single law and requirement."

Starlink won an Indian license in July 2025 and signed cross-selling pacts with Ambani's Jio Platforms and Airtel in March, but Bloomberg has reported New Delhi froze approvals over concerns Starlink terminals could be used without being licensed; meanwhile Jio is building its own low-earth-orbit network of about 1,600 satellites and 23 ground stations, and Jio Platforms chief Akash Ambani has said India needs its own indigenous technology stack. Opposition leader Rahul Gandhi replied to Musk's post with "Wait till you meet the other one," a veiled reference to Gautam Adani, and Musk responded "This is indeed troubling"; Reliance Industries did not respond to CNBC's request for comment.
