The tanker campaign in the Strait of Hormuz escalated through the weekend. UKMTO logged two more projectile strikes on tankers on October 2-3 — one vessel suffered a small fire and blackout before resuming its voyage, the second a crude tanker east of Oman — and a fresh strike was reported early on October 4 with the crew safe. Five Indian seafarers were rescued after a tanker hit, per Indian embassy and Omani authorities.

The US response is now a convoy operation: Centcom is escorting roughly 40 ships carrying about 18 million barrels through the strait. Iraq moved 2 million barrels through with Iranian permission and is actively shopping for tankers of its own — Baghdad is building a workaround export architecture inside a war zone. Iran's top negotiator, Mohammad-Bagher Ghalibaf, says the strait stays closed until Washington meets seven conditions rooted in the so-called Islamabad Memorandum.

Trump says the war will end "very quickly" after the November 3 midterms — "the easy way or the hard way." Weekend add-ons widened the diplomatic frame: the Houthis claimed a strike on an Aramco site in Riyadh on Saturday, Portugal's prosecutors are examining the legality of US use of the Lajes air base, and Senator Chris Murphy toured Qatar and Saudi Arabia. Supply fragility, not OPEC+ quotas, is the price driver now — and the Fed's oil-driven rate path is the transmission into markets.
