The International Court of Justice ruled on Thursday that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya minority in northern Rakhine state, in a 9 to 6 decision that marks the first time the World Court has found a state responsible for genocide.

Court President Yuji Iwasawa said Myanmar "has committed genocide against the Rohingya group in northern Rakhine state, in violation of its obligations under the Genocide Convention." The 15-judge panel rejected Myanmar's defence that its 2017 campaign was a legitimate operation against insurgents. Iwasawa said the court found that Myanmar's security forces killed thousands of Rohingya civilians, perpetrated widespread acts of sexual violence and extreme violence against children, and attacked Rohingya as they fled in large numbers. The court said the evidence showed about 10,000 Rohingya were killed, and that the only reasonable inference from the pattern of atrocities was an intent to destroy a substantial part of the group.

The Gambia brought the case in November 2019 under the 1948 Genocide Convention, backed by the 57-member Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, asking the court to find that Myanmar had breached the convention, to order an end to illegal acts against the Rohingya and to ensure those responsible are punished. The court ordered provisional measures to protect the Rohingya in January 2020, rejected Myanmar's preliminary objections in 2022, and heard the merits over three weeks in January 2026, with 11 states intervening. Myanmar has consistently denied genocide and said its operations targeted Rohingya militants.

The judgment is final, cannot be appealed, and is legally binding. The court cannot enforce it itself: its only route is to report non-compliance to the UN Security Council. More than 700,000 Rohingya fled to Bangladesh during the 2017 crackdown, and the ruling now puts the questions of reparations, accountability and safe return in the hands of governments rather than judges.
