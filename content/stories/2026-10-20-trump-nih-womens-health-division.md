President Trump signed an executive order on Monday creating a new division devoted to women's health within the National Institutes of Health and a new White House task force on the subject. First lady Melania Trump announced the initiative, saying the generation would fight to find cures so a woman can live her life with health and to the fullest. The NIH division is to be set up within 90 days.

According to an NIH spokesperson speaking to STAT, the division will work with the existing Office of Research on Women's Health, which it does not replace, to expand and coordinate research on understudied women's health conditions across all stages of life, improve diagnosis and treatment, strengthen the inclusion of women and girls in clinical trials, and increase accountability for considering sex as a biological variable in NIH funded research. The task force, whose first session was set for Monday, is led by White House chief of staff Susie Wiles and includes cabinet secretaries Robert F. Kennedy Jr. and Pete Hegseth, acting FDA Commissioner Kyle Diamantas and CDC Director Erica Schwartz. The Department of Health and Human Services will pay the task force's administrative costs; funding for the new division itself was not disclosed.

The order arrives against a contested backdrop. Women's health research has been a flashpoint since early in this administration, with scientists saying work was caught in reviews triggered by the word women in grants and by wider funding cuts. The structure is now ordered; the money and staffing behind it are not yet public, and those are the figures that will show whether the division is a research programme or a reorganisation.
