Real gross domestic product was essentially unchanged in August, the second straight month with no growth, after July was revised to unchanged as well. Nine of the 20 industrial sectors expanded. Goods producing industries edged up 0.1 percent, with manufacturing up 0.3 percent and construction up 0.2 percent partly offset by a 0.4 percent fall in utilities, while mining, quarrying and oil and gas extraction was essentially unchanged. Services producing industries edged down 0.1 percent.

Wholesale trade fell 1.4 percent, the largest drag on the month, and retail trade rose 1.2 percent, more than offsetting its July contraction. The public sector fell 0.2 percent, its first decline in six months, as federal government public administration dropped 2.1 percent with the temporary 2026 Census work continuing to wind down. Statistics Canada's advance estimate has real GDP down 0.1 percent in September, which would leave the economy up 0.3 percent in the third quarter as a whole.
