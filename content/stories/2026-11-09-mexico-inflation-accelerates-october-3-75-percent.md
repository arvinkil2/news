Mexico's national statistics institute INEGI reported on Monday that the consumer price index stood at 147.026 in October, up 0.65 percent from September, which put annual inflation at 3.75 percent. That is an acceleration from the 3.62 percent annual rate recorded in the first half of October, and core inflation, which strips out the most volatile items, was 3.80 percent on the year.

The month's increases were led by farm products including tomatoes and squash, air transport, electricity, professional services and LP gas, INEGI said, while falls in limes, serrano chiles, oranges, avocados and potatoes, along with cheaper telecommunications packages, pulled the other way. The print follows Banco de México's November 5 decision to cut its overnight rate by 25 basis points to 6.25 percent, when the bank also raised its forecast for inflation at the end of 2026 to 3.7 percent.
