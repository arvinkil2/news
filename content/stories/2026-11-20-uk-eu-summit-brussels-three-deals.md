**Latest.** The joint statement agreed at the summit was published on Friday afternoon. It records the conclusion of negotiations on a Common Sanitary and Phytosanitary Area, under which the vast majority of movements of animals, animal products, plants and plant products between Great Britain and the EU would take place without the certificates or controls now required, and sets an aim to apply the agreement from March 1, 2028. The emissions trading link is to apply from 2028, with mutual recognition of EU and UK allowances and exemptions from each side's carbon border levy. The Youth Experience Scheme will allow 100,000 young people from the EU and 100,000 from the UK to reside for up to three years for work, study, au-pairing, volunteering or travel; the two sides aim to apply it no later than 2028 and to have places available no later than January 2030. The three agreements are to be concluded under each side's legal framework and to enter into force jointly. On tuition fees, the statement commits both sides to work towards a supplementing agreement to the Trade and Cooperation Agreement and to conclude those negotiations within six months of the summit. It also commits them to conclude the negotiations on UK participation in the EU internal electricity market in 2027, to launch an EU-UK Digital Partnership early in 2027, and records the extension of the Trade and Cooperation Agreement's Energy Title to March 31, 2028 and full reciprocal fisheries access to June 30, 2038.

Britain and the European Union have agreed a reset deal and concluded negotiations at Friday's Brussels summit, Prime Minister Andy Burnham's office said. Downing Street said both sides had agreed timelines for implementing the youth mobility scheme, the sanitary and phytosanitary (food trade) agreement and the link between their emissions trading systems, with the dates to be set out in a joint statement published later on Friday. The UK government claims the food and agriculture deal will add up to £5.1 billion a year to the economy and the emissions trading link up to £3.8 billion, and Burnham told business leaders that Treasury analysis put the boost at up to 0.5 percent of GDP, calling it the single biggest growth intervention of this Parliament. The food deal would see Britain align with EU sanitary and phytosanitary rules from March 2028.

One obstacle remains open. The EU wants students from member states to pay the same tuition fees as British students, £9,970 a year against up to £38,000 now, which the UK has resisted over the cost to universities; a six month deadline has been set on the fees talks, and an EU official said the agreements would have to come into force in a balanced way, raising the prospect that implementation of other elements waits on a fees settlement. A UK government spokesman said the deal is done and negotiations have concluded, with a legal process now to follow on each side, and that talks will continue on some areas to be set out in the joint statement. Reform UK leader Nigel Farage called the deal a betrayal and said the Brexit debate had started again.

The UK and the EU hold their second post-Brexit summit in Brussels today, with Prime Minister Andy Burnham meeting European Commission president Ursula von der Leyen and European Council president António Costa, and negotiators closing in on three linked agreements: an SPS deal removing much of the red tape on animal and plant products, a link between the British and EU emissions trading systems that would exempt businesses on both sides from each other's carbon import charges, and a youth mobility scheme for 18 to 31 year olds, with the UK having conceded 100,000 young EU citizens a year. The EU has tied the three together so they enter into force as a package, having dropped its demand that EU students pay full UK home tuition rates in favour of a discount on international fees, while British inclusion in the EU's Made in Europe industrial scheme is not expected to be settled today because the underlying EU law is unfinished. Burnham said on Monday that "the hard Brexit we were given is not good enough for Britain" and that he would set out the long-term options for the relationship after the summit.
