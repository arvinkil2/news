The European Central Bank's November Financial Stability Review says the euro area economy has proved resilient this year to energy supply disruptions, trade frictions and extreme weather, helped by drawdowns of petroleum reserves and by optimism about artificial intelligence, but warns that the financial stability outlook is clouded by AI valuations, fiscal imbalances and geoeconomic tensions. The review names three linked risks: market pricing that depends on extraordinary earnings growth for AI firms, a challenging fiscal outlook in some major advanced economies that could test investor confidence and reprice sovereign risk, and the exposure of banks and non-banks alike to market, credit and funding risks that interconnection could amplify.

AI-related debt issuance has hit record highs this year, the review says, with the cumulative capital expenditure of US big tech firms projected to total 5.5 trillion dollars by 2030, a significant share of recent AI issuance raised in euro, and IPO volumes on track to surpass the dotcom bubble peak as a share of US GDP. Sovereign debt levels are high in many advanced economies including some euro area countries, funding costs have risen, and the review says fiscal consolidation is overdue; banks, by contrast, remain strongly positioned, with ample liquidity buffers, sizeable capital headroom and robust profitability.
