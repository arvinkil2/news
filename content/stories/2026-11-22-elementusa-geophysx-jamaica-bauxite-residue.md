ElementUSA, a US developer of critical mineral recovery technology, has signed a memorandum of understanding with Geophysx Jamaica Limited to recover critical minerals, iron and other valuable materials from Jamaica's bauxite residue, the red mud left by decades of alumina production and held in large volumes in storage sites across the island.

Under the agreement, ElementUSA will lead the technical work on a pilot at new and existing facilities in Jamaica, processing Jamaican residue with its recovery technology; pilot results will guide the design of a commercial scale operation. Geophysx, a Jamaican founded explorer established in 2017 with about $25 million of private investment behind it, contributes resource development experience, access through its existing licences and work with local stakeholders.

The process development draws on ElementUSA's Critical Resource Accelerator in Cedar Park, Texas, which has received support from the US Department of War and the US Department of Energy, and builds on the company's existing work turning bauxite residue into critical minerals and pig iron. The partners frame the programme as both an environmental remediation route for legacy sites and a supply chain play: a secure, diversified source of critical minerals for manufacturers in the United States and allied nations.

The MOU is an early stage instrument. No volumes, capital cost or schedule were disclosed, and a commercial plant remains contingent on pilot results, feasibility work, financing and regulatory approvals. What it establishes is the pairing: a US backed processing technology with a Jamaican licence holder positioned over the residue itself.
