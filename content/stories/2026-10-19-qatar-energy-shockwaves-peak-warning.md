Qatar's deputy prime minister, Sheikh Saoud bin Abdulrahman Al Thani, told the Global Security Forum in Doha on Monday that the energy shockwaves from the war on Iran have not yet peaked, warning of further repercussions for supplies, trade and growth in the coming days and months. He rejected transit fees for shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, saying the uncertainty would ultimately reach consumers worldwide, and said Qatar's Ras Laffan industrial complex, damaged by Iranian strikes in March, faces repairs expected to take several years. Al Thani said Qatar has never stopped communicating with Tehran and hopes mediation involving Qatar, Pakistan and Oman can produce a settlement between the United States, Iran and other parties.
