The Bank of Ghana's Monetary Policy Committee, at its 133rd meeting from November 16 to 18, aligned the monetary policy rate from 14 percent to 11 percent, narrowed the policy corridor from plus or minus 400 basis points to plus or minus 100 basis points, and replaced the 14-day open market operations instrument with a seven-day instrument. The committee said market rates had evolved below the announced policy rate, so the effective stance was already lower than 14 percent. Headline inflation was 5.5 percent in October, banks' average lending rate had eased to 15.8 percent, and gross international reserves stood at US$12.1 billion, about 4.4 months of import cover, as at November 17. Joy Business reported on November 23 that the combined changes cut the central bank's overnight lending rate to banks from 18 percent to 12 percent while the deposit rate stays at 10 percent, and that analysts are split on whether the tighter corridor lifts market rates toward 11 percent in practice.
