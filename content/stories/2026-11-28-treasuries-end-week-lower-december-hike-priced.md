Treasury yields climbed in Friday's shortened post-Thanksgiving session as equities extended their rebound and demand for haven assets faded, but the move only retraced part of the week's rally. The five-year yield rose 8.6 basis points on the day to 4.75 percent, the 10-year rose 8.8 basis points to 4.94 percent and the 30-year rose 9.0 basis points to 5.32 percent. On the week, all three finished lower: the five-year down 12.0 basis points, the 10-year down 11.6 and the 30-year down 7.6.

The two-year yield, the tenor most sensitive to the policy path, told the flatter story. It rose 4.6 basis points in Asian trading to 4.680 percent, according to Tradeweb data reported by Dow Jones, and ended the week slightly higher even as the long end rallied, a flattening pattern consistent with traders pricing near-term tightening while doubting how much growth can absorb it.

Pricing for the December 8 to 9 FOMC meeting is no longer a live question. CME FedWatch implied a 99.4 percent probability of a 25 basis point increase, which would lift the target range to 4.00 to 4.25 percent, up from about 97 percent a day earlier, and futures put the chance of a further increase in January at about 64 percent. LSEG data show about 71 basis points of hikes priced by mid-2027, a path Jefferies doubts the Fed will match; its economist Mohit Kumar said the December meeting should mark the peak of the Fed's hawkishness.

The Federal Reserve's pre-meeting blackout period begins today, Saturday, so there will be no official commentary before the decision. Strategists at Societe Generale said a restrictive Fed path is largely priced in, capping upside for five-year yields unless policymakers turn more hawkish, while SEB said it is premature to expect a sustained drop in the 10-year even if it has reached its cycle peak. That leaves the November employment report on December 4 as the last data point capable of moving pricing before the meeting.
