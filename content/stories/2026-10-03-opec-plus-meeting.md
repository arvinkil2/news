## Latest — Sunday, October 4, ~7:00 AM EDT

OPEC+ has agreed in principle to keep November production targets steady, three sources close to the talks told Reuters early Sunday, with Bloomberg independently reporting a deal outline for unchanged November quotas. The November increment on the table was about 137,000 barrels a day; that increase is now off the table — November target change is zero. No official OPEC communique had been published as of Sunday morning, so this remains REPORTED, not CONFIRMED.

The 2027 capacity and quota framework review — the meeting's substantive agenda — was not decided. Instead it was pushed to mid-November, with the war having made reliable capacity estimates hard. That means no new quota framework before 2027. The seven core members (Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Algeria, Kazakhstan, Oman) agreed unanimously in principle ahead of their 11:00 GMT session; the JMMC also met Sunday in its monitoring role. Roughly 2 million barrels a day of cuts remain in place. Reuters reported the seven pumped 25 million b/d in August — up 630,000 from July but still about 5 million below pre-war February — with Gulf exports running 20 to 40 percent below normal.

Versus expectations: a flat November was the base case, but dropping even the 137,000 b/d increment is a mild downside surprise, and the deferral of the 2027 review was not expected today. For markets, the signal is that OPEC+ will not add supply into the G7's 100-million-barrel coordinated stock release — supportive of the $102 Brent / $91 WTI price floor while physical Gulf flows stay disrupted. Watch for the official OPEC communique later Sunday for confirmation.

## Earlier — Saturday, October 3

OPEC+ meets on Sunday, October 4, and expectations are for November quotas to be held broadly steady — the increment on the table is only about 137,000 barrels a day. The substantive agenda is the 2027 capacity and quota review, not monthly fine-tuning.

Context matters more than the communique. Seven members held October output flat at the September 6 meeting, completing the 1.65 million barrel-a-day voluntary-cut rollback. The combined October quota is roughly 31.01 million barrels a day, with Saudi Arabia at 10.478, Russia at 9.949, and Iraq at 4.431. But quotas have been nominal since March: with Hormuz disruptions restricting flows, Gulf producers are physically unable to fill them.

The group's composition also keeps shifting underneath the negotiations. The UAE exited OPEC on May 1, and Sunday's meeting is expected to proceed without it — one fewer voice at a table that is really arguing about who gets to pump what in 2027.
