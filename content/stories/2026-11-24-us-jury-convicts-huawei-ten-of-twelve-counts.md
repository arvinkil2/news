Sentencing is now scheduled for September 14, 2027, CNBC reported, with the Justice Department saying the defendants face financial penalties including criminal fines, forfeiture and restitution. Huawei said in a statement on Wednesday that the charges were without legal or factual merit, welcomed its acquittal on the racketeering charge, and said it would review the verdicts and consider further legal action. The Justice Department's own release names the convicted subsidiaries as Huawei Device Co. Ltd. of China and the US subsidiaries Futurewei Technologies Inc. and Huawei Device USA Inc., convicted after a 12-week trial before Judge Ann M. Donnelly.

A US jury in Brooklyn convicted Huawei Technologies on ten of twelve criminal counts on Tuesday, finding the Chinese telecoms equipment maker guilty of conspiracy to steal trade secrets, bank fraud, money laundering and sanctions violations, and acquitting it of racketeering conspiracy and conspiracy to obstruct justice. The case, first indicted in 2018, alleged that Huawei misled HSBC, Standard Chartered and BNP Paribas about its business in Iran and conspired over two decades to steal technology from US companies, including Cisco router source code and Quintel antenna technology. The trial ran more than two months, with prosecutors calling over 60 witnesses; sentencing has not been scheduled.
