**Latest.** President Donald Trump, answering a Fox News question in Shenzhen on Wednesday during the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit, said he did not think Washington residents should worry about an Iranian dirty bomb, adding that if it does happen, the United States will wipe Iran out. He said there was a huge indication that Iran has been looking at gases and biological weapons, and that using nuclear weapons is much harder. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton told lawmakers the same day that threats from Iran have substantially increased, while Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin had said on Tuesday that the homeland is secure and that Iran's planning was aspirational, with no new threat. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi dismissed the report in a post on X as rubbish and Iraq War style propaganda, and foreign ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei called the allegations baseless. None of the claims on either side is independently corroborated.

**Earlier.** Iran's foreign ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei on Tuesday called CNN's dirty bomb report a fabricated media narrative and denied it outright, posting on X that the accusers fabricate baseless accusations to create a pretext for further aggression, and warning that Iran will impose unforgettable punishment on any act of aggression. Parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, who also heads Iran's negotiating delegation, said the same day that Iran would regard all dams, ports and power plants near US or Israeli bases as dual-use facilities, responding to a New York Times report raising suspicions that Iranian reservoirs could conceal military facilities and saying Tehran would apply Washington's own logic consistently. Neither the US intelligence claim nor Iran's characterisation of it is independently corroborated.

New US intelligence indicates Iran is developing plans that could target senior Trump administration officials in Washington with a dirty bomb or a chemical weapon, CNN reported on Monday, citing seven US officials, with two sources saying the plotting includes specific locations and targets and has prompted several contingency planning meetings over the past week. The officials described the planning as Tehran giving itself options rather than a sign of an imminent attack, and remain divided over whether Iran has the capability, the people in place, or the intent to carry it out. The White House said the United States is monitoring all potential threats but that there are currently no credible threats to the homeland, Iran's mission to the United Nations rejected the chemical weapons allegation as false and fabricated, and CNN reported that Iranian threats led the Secret Service to move Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent into military housing last week.
