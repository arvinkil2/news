The Pentagon awarded Raytheon a $20.7 billion contract for AMRAAM missiles on September 25, the largest single AMRAAM procurement in history, spanning five base years plus two option years and covering the US Air Force, the US Navy, and 16 foreign military sales partners.

The award is industrial policy by procurement. It underwrites production capacity at Tucson directly, removing the demand uncertainty that would otherwise constrain investment in plant, tooling, and workforce. In the same week, Archer's acquisition of Boeing's Wisk Aero, SkyGrid, and Insitu showed the private side of the same logic, control of autonomy IP via equity, with Boeing retaining a stake and technology access rather than full ownership.

Smaller private-equity add-ons continued in the background, avionics, bearings, and Italian aerospace components changing hands, but the week's defense capital story is the state as anchor customer at unprecedented scale.
