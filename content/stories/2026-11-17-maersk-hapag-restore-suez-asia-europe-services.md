Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd announced that their Gemini Cooperation's AE1, AE2 and AE3 services will return to the trans-Suez route and the Red Sea, replacing voyages diverted around Africa, FreightWaves reported. AE2 will transition first, with the Mary Maersk starting its rotation at Ningbo on December 4, followed by AE3 from Shanghai on December 20 and AE1 on January 14, 2027, and shipping analyst Linerlytica said the changes will make Gemini the first alliance to restore Suez routing across all of its Asia-Europe services.

Maersk said the revised rotations would improve transit times while cautioning that implementation depends on continued stability in the Red Sea. DHL Global Forwarding said the return would release capacity absorbed by the Africa diversions, but warned that congestion, with more than 10 percent of the global container fleet tied up in ports, and extensive network adjustments could keep supply chains under pressure into 2027.
