US Treasury Department officials met government authorities, regulators and national security bodies in Singapore and Malaysia this week, urging both countries, and the banks and law firms operating there, to watch for Iranian front companies in high-risk sectors such as maritime transport, aviation services and procurement networks. A Treasury official told Reuters the United States will target what it calls Iran's lifelines without negotiation when it finds them, and said the meetings also raised an Iranian airline that has recently flown to Malaysia, which Reuters identified from flight records as Iran Airtour's Tehran to Kuala Lumpur service.

Singapore said in a statement it is firmly committed to preventing bad actors from abusing its corporate vehicles; Malaysia's government did not respond to requests for comment. The push follows waves of US sanctions since February's strikes on Iran, including July designations on Singapore and Hong Kong entities linked to Iranian oil trader Mohammad Hossein Shamkhani and September sanctions on companies in Malaysia and four other countries accused of supporting Iran's aviation sector.
