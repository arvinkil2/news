Equatorial Guinea will approve a new hydrocarbon law on Monday, its hydrocarbons minister said, offering more flexible contract terms and a wider range of structures for oil companies. Output fell to about 20,000 barrels a day in September, down more than 85 percent in six years, and the government says it will also negotiate directly over available blocks.

The test will be whether the new terms produce actual drilling in mature fields and unexplored acreage, after ExxonMobil left the country in 2024 and with Chevron now the main international presence, in gas. The country has set no production target.
