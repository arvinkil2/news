The rupiah touched a record low of 18,290 per dollar in Jakarta trading on Monday before strengthening to 18,180 by mid morning, after Bank Indonesia publicly linked the currency's support to talks between MSCI and President Prabowo Subianto. Erwin Gunawan Hutapea, the central bank's head of monetary management, said remarks by MSCI chief executive Henry Fernandez during a recent direct meeting with Prabowo had raised positive expectations for improvements in Indonesia's stock market and further supported rupiah appreciation.

The background is MSCI's review of Indonesia's market status. Bloomberg News reported, citing people with knowledge of the discussion, that Fernandez conveyed a willingness to consider alternatives to a two step downgrade of Indonesia's stock market status on a November 10 conference call with Prabowo, and that the call lasted more than an hour. Reuters said it could not immediately verify that account, and MSCI and the president's office did not immediately respond to requests for comment, so the detail of the call remains single sourced to Bloomberg's reporting.

What is confirmed is the central bank's own positioning. Bank Indonesia chose to comment on an index provider's conversation with the president on the day the currency printed a record low, which tells markets the exchange rate, not the policy rate, is the binding constraint this week. A separate politics story today covers MSCI opening alternatives to the downgrade itself.

For rates, the sequence matters. Every session the rupiah spends near record lows is a session in which Bank Indonesia's easing room narrows and its intervention and liquidity operations carry more of the adjustment. The recovery to 18,180 shows the statement bought relief, not a reversal.
