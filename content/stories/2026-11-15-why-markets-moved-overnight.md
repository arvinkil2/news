Tanker freight rose 6.29 percent to 618.68 dollars, the biggest move in Friday's tables for a second day running. This morning's reporting keeps the same tightness theme underneath it: the Hormuz story was updated with US Central Command dismissing Saturday's claimed mine strikes as false, three commercial ships were struck by drones off Romania over two nights (see today's Romania Black Sea story), and Durban port delays are approaching 20 days (see today's Durban story).

Broadcom fell 4.76 percent to 347.01 dollars and the semiconductor index fell 3.67 percent, with Nvidia down 2.01 percent and the tech sector down 2.01 percent. The falls came after a very strong week, with Broadcom still up 18.16 percent on the week and semiconductors up 9.35 percent. No clear single driver for Friday's chip selling appears in this morning's reporting; today's Nvidia story is about board succession and Asian governments checking supply commitments, not about Friday's price action.

Natural gas fell 3.29 percent to 3.23 dollars per mmBtu, while keeping a 7.74 percent gain on the week. No clear single driver appears in this morning's reporting; today's commodities audit notes the comfortable US storage position, with stocks 75 Bcf above the five year average, as the background to the pullback.

Meta rose 2.9 percent to 641.69 dollars, the largest gain among the big tech names. No story in this morning's reporting explains the move; the company's latest filing in the pool was a routine disclosure that warranted no story.

Copper miners fell 2.51 percent, the weakest mining group, following copper itself down 1.49 percent on Friday. Today's mining dashboard records the whole miner tape lower on Friday, from gold miners down 0.24 percent to rare earth miners down 1.69 percent, with no single company catalyst in this morning's reporting behind the copper miners' fall.

Dry bulk freight fell 2.12 percent to 12.93 dollars, moving opposite to tanker rates. No clear single driver appears in this morning's reporting.

Across the tables the read is two-sided rather than risk-off outright. Shipping and energy logistics stayed bid on physical tightness, while chips and miners took profits after strong weeks, and the rates tables show the week's repricing holding, with the US 10 year at 5.15 percent, up 29.5 basis points on the week, and the dollar index firmer at 103.41.
