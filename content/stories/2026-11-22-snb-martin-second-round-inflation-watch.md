Swiss National Bank Vice Chairman Antoine Martin said policymakers are watching closely for second round inflation effects, in an interview published Sunday by Le Matin Dimanche, after Swiss price growth accelerated faster than the central bank expected. Martin said inflation had continued to rise since the SNB's last policy meeting and that the December rate decision would rest on the bank's reading of incoming economic and monetary data.

Swiss annual inflation climbed to 1.5 percent in October from 1.0 percent in September, topping economist forecasts. The reading remains inside the SNB's 0 to 2 percent definition of price stability, but the size of the jump caught the bank off guard: Chairman Martin Schlegel said after the data that the surge had surprised the institution. The increase was driven mainly by energy, with transport prices up 5.3 percent year over year and imported goods up 3.7 percent, while core inflation held at 0.5 percent.

The SNB has held its policy rate at zero, supported by its own forecast of average inflation of just 0.7 percent in 2026, a projection the October print already exceeds on an annual basis. Economists surveyed recently look for the first increase only in June 2027, but market pricing and several bank economists now put a December or March move in play, and the bank's next decision falls on December 10.

Martin named exchange rate risk as the SNB's main concern, pointing to fluctuations between the franc, the dollar and the euro. The franc weakened against both earlier in the year before strengthening sharply in October amid volatility linked to artificial intelligence stocks, higher energy prices and worries about French public debt, a reminder that a rate increase would tend to strengthen a currency the bank already watches for excess strength.
