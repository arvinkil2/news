Anthropic said on Sunday it will commit 5 percent of its shares to a newly created Anthropic Public Benefit Fund, pending stockholder approval. The fund is structured as a wholly owned entity within the company, described in initial reporting as a limited liability company rather than a traditional nonprofit, and it could gain a further 2 percent of shares at the board's discretion for every 50 percent increase in the company's value above a set baseline, so its resources grow with Anthropic's valuation.

The company offered broad examples of how the money might be used, including large donations to traditional nonprofits and direct distributions to specific populations. It pointed to the impact AI might have on the job market, an area where it has already committed 200 million dollars to study the effects, and said that given the pace of AI progress, bolder approaches may be needed and the fund will have the versatility to pursue them.

Anthropic said none of the equity it holds will be used for lobbying or political giving. Combined with earlier commitments, including the co-founders' pledge to donate 80 percent of their equity holdings, roughly 20 percent of Anthropic shares will be dedicated to public benefit purposes, the company said.

The announcement comes as the AI industry faces increasingly negative public sentiment, which chief executive Dario Amodei has described as fundamentally a crisis of trust. Anthropic said it will share more information about the fund's structure and governance in the coming months, which is where the commitment will be tested: who decides which public benefits are funded, and on what timetable, is not yet specified.
