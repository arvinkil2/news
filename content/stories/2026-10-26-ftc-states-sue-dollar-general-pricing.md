The Federal Trade Commission and the attorneys general of 20 states filed the complaint on Monday in the US District Court for the Eastern District of Pennsylvania. It alleges that Dollar General has for years repeatedly mislabeled prices, charged customers more at the register than the prices displayed on shelves, price tags and advertisements, and extracted millions of dollars in unlawful overcharges across thousands of its more than 21,000 stores, while failing to put safeguards in place despite extensive corporate knowledge of the problem and tens of thousands of consumer complaints. The plaintiffs allege violations of the FTC Act, the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act and state laws.

FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson said the long mispricing of household goods "has to stop." Dollar General, whose shares slipped less than 1 percent on Monday, could not immediately be reached for comment on the complaint; it has previously told CNBC that it is committed to providing customers with accurate prices. The suit follows earlier state cases over the same conduct, under which the company has paid 18 million dollars in civil penalties to 26 states since 2017.
