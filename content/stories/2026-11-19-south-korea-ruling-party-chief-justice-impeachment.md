South Korea's ruling Democratic Party decided on Thursday to pursue the impeachment of Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, setting the policy at a closed-door emergency general meeting of its lawmakers held hours after the Supreme Court overturned and remanded the 15-year prison sentence of former prime minister Han Duck-soo in the December 2024 martial law case, a ruling this site covered earlier on Thursday. Party leader Kim Min-seok called the Han ruling a historic distortion that justified the insurrection and said the party could not overlook it; the party left the impeachment content and procedure to its leadership, with the position due to be adopted as the formal party line at a general meeting on Friday.

The decision was contested inside the party, where lawyer-turned lawmakers argued the grounds were insufficient because the court had still recognised the insurrection charge in Han's case, and others warned that public support for impeachment does not reach a majority. The opposition People Power Party called the move a declaration of a parliamentary coup to trample the judiciary.
