**Latest.** The National Security Agency said it "experienced a temporary degradation of its operational capability during scheduled routine maintenance," in a statement carried by ERT News. "Although some internal systems were affected, vital functions remain fully operational and our ability to defend national security continues," the agency said, adding that its infrastructure is designed with multiple layers of redundancy so operations are maintained during scheduled maintenance or unforeseen incidents. The Wall Street Journal, citing a well-informed source, reported the agency was carrying out a software upgrade, and White House spokesperson Lauren Biss told Newsmax there was no evidence of a cyberattack and no crisis for Americans to worry about.

The highly classified network that the US National Security Agency uses for its internal communications is down, CNN reported on Tuesday, citing five people familiar with the matter. The agency was working to understand the scope of the problem, and there was no immediate evidence of a cyberattack, CNN reported.

The NSA did not immediately return a message seeking comment about the reported outage. The report gave no formal name for the network and no cause for the failure.
