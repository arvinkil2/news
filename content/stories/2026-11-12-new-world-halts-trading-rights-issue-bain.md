**Latest.** New World Development on Sunday proposed a rights issue to raise about HK$24.9 billion ($3.17 billion), offering shareholders 11 new shares for every existing share at HK$0.90 each, a discount of 81.5 percent to the last closing price of HK$4.87 and 26.9 percent below the theoretical ex-rights price of HK$1.23, Reuters reported from the company's filing. About 27.68 billion new shares would be issued, and the proposal needs approval from independent shareholders and a waiver from takeover rules. Controlling shareholder Chow Tai Fook Enterprises, which owns about 45.3 percent, has committed to take up its full entitlement and to underwrite the shares other investors leave behind, a commitment that could reach about HK$18.7 billion.

The company plans to use about 34 percent of the proceeds to prepay a tenth of its roughly HK$82 billion in bank loans and to extend the maturity of the remaining 90 percent by two years. Total debt stood at about HK$143.3 billion at the end of June; after the issue, the net debt to total equity ratio would fall to 48.3 percent from 68.3 percent, and the group aims to cut total debt by HK$40 billion to HK$50 billion by the end of 2031. Chairman Cheng Kar Shun said combining the equity fundraising with the debt maturity extension would address short term funding needs while giving the business greater flexibility to realise asset value in an orderly manner. The shares, suspended on Friday ahead of the announcement, resume trading on Monday.


New World Development Co. suspended trading in its Hong Kong shares on Friday morning pending an announcement to investors, a month after the developer reported a record annual loss of $3.6 billion, its worst ever performance, after a writedown on an aborted deal with the Hong Kong airport. The company is considering a rights issue to shore up its finances after Bain Capital decided against investing in the property company, according to people familiar with the situation.

The rights issue could seek as much as HK$25 billion ($3.2 billion), and the Cheng family, New World's controlling shareholder, intends to participate and inject fresh capital, the people said. The developer has been carrying a heavy debt pile that pushed it into eleventh-hour negotiations with bank lenders last year, and its shares have fallen 33 percent this year.
