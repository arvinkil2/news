Nigeria's Pension Industry Leadership Council and FSD Africa, the UK funded development agency, have signed a memorandum of understanding to support the development of a proposed 750 million dollar infrastructure fund, to be called the Pension Industry Infrastructure Platform. The initiative was developed by Nigerian pension fund administrators with support from the National Pension Commission, whose director general Omolola Oloworaran chairs the Leadership Council.

The platform targets an initial 350 million dollars at first close, expected in 2027, before scaling to its full target size. At full scale it aims to mobilise approximately 600 million dollars from Nigerian pension funds and at least 150 million dollars from development finance institutions. Nineteen Nigerian pension funds have expressed interest in participating. At least 60 percent of the portfolio is to be directed to Nigerian infrastructure projects, with the remainder available for selected markets in the region.

An initial assessment of Nigeria's infrastructure pipeline identified 39 projects with potential relevance to the platform, representing about 20 billion dollars of investment needs, across energy, transport and digital infrastructure.

The platform is a proposal, not a fund with committed capital, and the distinction matters: the memorandum starts the development work, and the 2027 first close is the first hard test. Its significance is structural. Nigeria's pension assets have grown to more than 31 trillion naira, and the industry is trying to convert a fraction of that domestic, long duration money into infrastructure equity at a scale that foreign investors have historically been asked to provide. Yesterday's Dangote refinery story showed the limits of Nigeria's retail capital plumbing; this is the institutional answer to the same problem.
