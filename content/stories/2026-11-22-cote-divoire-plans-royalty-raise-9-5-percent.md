Côte d'Ivoire plans to raise the royalty mining companies pay on mineral revenue to 9.5 percent from 6 percent, according to people familiar with the matter cited by Bloomberg, as part of an overhaul of the country's mining code. The community development levy, which funds infrastructure and social projects in mining areas, would rise to 3 percent from 0.5 percent on the Bloomberg account; Africa Intelligence, cited in parallel reporting, puts a parafiscal local development levy rising from 0.5 percent to 3.5 percent and reports value added tax being reintroduced in the sector. The final text will settle which figures apply.

The measures have been adopted by cabinet and submitted to parliament, which has yet to vote. Mines, Petroleum and Energy Minister Mamadou Sangafowa-Coulibaly told lawmakers last week that the government aims to adopt a new mining code by the end of 2026. The mining ministry declined to comment on the reported rates.

The timing is fiscal. The country's $4.8 billion IMF financing programme ended in June 2026, and the government is seeking talks on a new facility; stronger domestic revenue mobilisation would support its case. Endeavour Mining, Montage Gold and Allied Gold are among the gold producers operating in the country, and Perseus Mining and Fortuna also have Ivorian mines.

Jurisdiction Watch: this would be the second Ivorian royalty reset in two years. In 2025 the country replaced a 3 to 6 percent sliding scale with a flat 8 percent rate, applied retroactively from the first quarter of 2025; miners initially refused to pay, arguing their contracts shielded them, then complied after the government held its position. Endeavour disclosed in its third quarter filing that the 2025 change, with higher prices, lifted group royalties by $116.7 million year over year in the first nine months of 2026 to $340.4 million. If the new 9.5 percent replaces the 8 percent now in force, the incremental step is 1.5 points of turnover rather than the headline 3.5. The pattern matches the wider West African mining code rewrites: states are using record gold prices to raise their take, and the retroactive precedent, not the headline rate, is what investors in the region will price.
