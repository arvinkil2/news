An oil spill off the United Arab Emirates has widened, satellite images show, prompting more beach closures, gCaptain reported on October 26. The maritime trade publication based its account on satellite imagery of the slick rather than an official UAE statement.

The first reports did not establish the spill's source, its volume, or which authority was leading the cleanup. Those gaps mattered for scale: a sheen visible from orbit and a major crude release carry very different costs. The Reuters reporting appended below pins down the official response and the expert attribution of the vessel, though the volume released is still not officially stated.

What is established is the setting. The spill sits on a coastline already absorbing the costs of the Hormuz disruption, where beach tourism, desalination plants that draw seawater along the Gulf coast, and port operations all compete for the same narrow shoreline. Even a contained spill that closes beaches for days lands on an economy working around a war at its maritime doorstep.

Update, morning rerun: Reuters reporting read in full in this run adds the detail the first version lacked. The UAE said on Friday that efforts were under way to contain oil leaking from a tanker offshore, with its Ministry of Climate Change and Environment conducting an environmental assessment. Authorities have not named the tanker or said what caused the spill. Experts told Reuters the spill was coming from a tanker called the Gem No.2, which shipping sources said had been struck by an unidentified projectile last month, causing a fire that was later extinguished. The ministry said all beaches and coastal areas around the city of Umm Al Quwain were temporarily closed, while authorities in neighbouring Sharjah shut access to Al Hamriyah Beach as a precaution. October has been the worst month for attacks on commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and nearby waterways, according to data compiled by the Washington Institute for Near East Policy.
