Spartan Metals estimates it would cost $683 million (C$978 million) to build an underground tungsten and molybdenum mine at Victorio in New Mexico, with a plant to convert tungsten concentrate into ammonium paratungstate, a feedstock for cutting tools and other industrial products. The preliminary economic assessment, released Thursday, puts the after-tax net present value at $2.14 billion at an 8 percent discount rate with a 31 percent internal rate of return, over a 24-year mine life, with a further $2.03 billion of sustaining spending.

The study assumes $1,350 per 10 kilograms of tungsten trioxide and $20 per pound of molybdenum; at the lowest prices tested, $750 and $15 respectively, the after-tax net present value falls to $392 million and the return to 14 percent. Victorio would use block caving and produce about 4,100 tonnes a year of tungsten trioxide contained in ammonium paratungstate and 8.65 million pounds of molybdenum in concentrate. Measured and indicated resources stand at 76.7 million tonnes grading 0.1 percent tungsten trioxide and 0.09 percent molybdenum, with a further 92.4 million inferred tonnes.

The strategic case is import dependence. The United States last mined tungsten commercially in 2015, according to the US Geological Survey, and China accounts for about 85 percent of global capacity to refine ammonium paratungstate, according to S&P Global, with Chinese export controls keeping supply tight. Spartan plans to advance Victorio to prefeasibility; the study recommends $13.2 million of further work. Spartan shares closed unchanged at 26 Canadian cents on Thursday, valuing the company at about C$15.4 million.
