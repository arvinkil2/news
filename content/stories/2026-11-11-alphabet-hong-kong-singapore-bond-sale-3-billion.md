Alphabet is looking to raise about $3 billion equivalent through its first bond sales in Hong Kong and Singapore dollars, Reuters reported on Wednesday, citing two people with knowledge of the matter and a term sheet the wire saw earlier that day. One of the people said the sales would likely raise at least $2.2 billion, with minimum sizes under consideration of HK$8 billion (about $1 billion) for the Hong Kong dollar portion and S$1.5 billion (about $1.2 billion) for the Singapore dollar portion; final size, timing and pricing have not been set, and Alphabet did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

The Hong Kong dollar bonds could carry two-, five- and 10-year maturities and the Singapore dollar bonds two, five, 10 and 30 years, with HSBC and JPMorgan mandated as joint global coordinators. The borrowing adds to a heavy issuance year driven by data centre and AI spending: Alphabet said in an October 29 filing that it issued $45 billion of US dollar notes and $35.8 billion of foreign currency notes in the nine months to the end of September, and it raised 6 billion euros in a euro sale last week.
