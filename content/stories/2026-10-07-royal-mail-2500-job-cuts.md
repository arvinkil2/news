Royal Mail plans to cut 2,500 head-office and support-function roles by the end of 2027, roughly 2 percent of its 131,000 staff, through voluntary redundancies and natural departures, the BBC reported Wednesday. Frontline posties and drivers are excluded; chief executive Alistair Cochrane said the move removes duplication and frees investment for customer service.

The announcement comes with formal consultation underway with the CWU and Unite unions. The CWU called the cuts evidence of a company "demoralising staff," while owner EP Group's Daniel Kretinsky reiterated he will not walk away from the six-day-a-week Universal Service Obligation while running the service.
