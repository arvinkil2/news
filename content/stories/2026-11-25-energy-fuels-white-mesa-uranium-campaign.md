Energy Fuels Inc. plans to commence a large scale uranium processing campaign at its White Mesa mill in Utah, the largest conventional uranium processing facility in the United States, in December 2026 through the first quarter of 2027. The campaign is expected to produce roughly 1 million pounds of finished natural uranium concentrate (U3O8) from ore produced at the company's Pinyon Plain mine in Arizona and La Sal complex in Utah, together with other uranium bearing alternate feed materials. The concentrate will be delivered to a North American uranium conversion facility, after which the mill is expected to transition back to rare earth element production from monazite.

Energy Fuels expects to produce approximately 1.6 million to 2 million pounds of mined and produced uranium in 2026. Production at Pinyon Plain is expected to wind down in the coming years, and the company is advancing several additional US projects to replace it. Collectively, the Whirlwind project in Colorado and the Energy Queen, Bullfrog and Nichols Ranch projects in Utah and Wyoming have the potential to add approximately 1.25 million pounds of annual U3O8 production.

Whirlwind, a partially developed conventional uranium and vanadium deposit at an advanced stage of permitting, has been dewatered and its existing decline rehabilitated, with first production currently scheduled for 2028. Nichols Ranch, a fully permitted in situ recovery project, produced approximately 1.2 million pounds of uranium between 2016 and 2018 before being placed on standby amid weak market conditions, and could be restarted relatively quickly, the company said.
