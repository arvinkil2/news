Saudi Arabia's annual consumer price inflation eased to 1.7 percent in October, the General Authority for Statistics reported on November 15, down from 1.9 percent in September and back at the rate the kingdom recorded in April. The October reading, carried by Arab News from the GASTAT release and recorded at 1.7 percent in market calendar data published the same morning, keeps Saudi Arabia the only Gulf Cooperation Council state with inflation below 2 percent.

The September detail shows where the pressure sits and why the headline is falling. Housing and utilities inflation eased to 3.6 percent year on year in September from 3.9 percent in August, with actual housing rentals up 3.5 percent, while food and beverages rose 1.4 percent and transport 2.3 percent. Headline prices were unchanged on the month in September. A five-year freeze on annual rent increases in Riyadh, in force since September 2025, continues to anchor the largest single component of the basket.

The consumer picture contrasts with costs further up the chain. Saudi wholesale prices rose 4.2 percent year on year in September, led by industrial inputs, a gap that reflects fuel subsidies and rent controls absorbing shocks that other Gulf states have passed through to households. The International Monetary Fund's October outlook had already revised its 2026 Saudi inflation forecast down to 1.9 percent from 2.2 percent in July, and October's outturn now sits below that revised path.

For monetary policy, the print changes nothing mechanically and confirms the stance. The Saudi riyal is pegged to the US dollar, and the Saudi Central Bank has kept its key interest rates unchanged since December 2025, following the Federal Reserve's path at a distance while domestic inflation runs at roughly a third of the US rate. With inflation contained and the peg intact, the kingdom's rates story remains one of imported US policy rather than a domestic price problem.
