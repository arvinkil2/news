The pollsters missed by roughly four points, and Brazil's rates market now has three weeks to price the error. Flávio Bolsonaro took 47.1% of the vote, El País reported, defeating Lula and forcing an October 25 runoff. The currency soared and equities rallied on the outcome, with the Bovespa hitting a record high, according to CNN and Reuters coverage.

The two runoff scenarios point in opposite directions for duration. A Lula second term comes with a public promise of a debt ceiling — significant with public debt above 82 percent of GDP — but also continued fiscal drift and distance from Washington; a Bolsonaro presidency, aligned with President Trump, could ease the US tariff architecture on Brazil while inheriting the same debt arithmetic.

Either way, the Selic at 13.75 percent has no room to fall until the fiscal question is settled. The fiscal anchor is the whole game: debt above 82 percent of GDP needs either credible consolidation or growth, and neither candidate's first-round campaign offered a binding path. For EM rate desks, the cleanest expression is the real: it strengthened on the relief bid, and it will trade the polls until October 25. The caution is that the fiscal figures in the weekend's coverage come from single outlets; the election result itself is TSE-certified, the policy promises are not.
