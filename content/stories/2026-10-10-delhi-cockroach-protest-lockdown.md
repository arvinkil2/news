**Latest (Monday, October 12):** Rahul Gandhi personally returned to the streets of central New Delhi on Monday, leading dozens of members of parliament demanding the election chief's resignation, per an Al Jazeera feature read in full. At a Monday press briefing, Gandhi said the Modi government had put the capital city "in jail" and called for chief election commissioner Gyanesh Kumar to be tried for treason: "We will not spare you." He wrote on X that Modi "cannot silence India's voice" and called on people from all walks of life to join Monday's protest. The youth-led Cockroach movement threatened to protest on Tuesday if it is not allowed to rally at Jantar Mantar, which was the focus of the summer demonstrations. Both the CJP and Gandhi's Congress are adopting flash-mobilisation tactics: AISA president Neha Bora announces protest locations only 30 minutes in advance via social media, evading police barricades in a daily "cat-and-mouse chase," while Congress holds flash rallies at unannounced venues. Political analyst Asim Ali told Al Jazeera the mobilisation would not stop but the government was unlikely to back down, and said the democratic gains won since Indira Gandhi's 1975 emergency had vanished: "there are no constraints on this government by independent institutions." Analyst Rasheed Kidwai called the violent detention of members of parliament particularly worrying, a break from decades when police did not interfere with big opposition rallies near parliament.

**Latest (Monday, October 12):** the street protest is merging with the opposition's campaign. Opposition parties and supporters of the Cockroach Janta Party's youth movement planned to march to India's Supreme Court on Monday to protest the controversial voter-list revision, demanding the resignation of chief election commissioner Gyanesh Kumar, Reuters reported via the Guardian. Delhi police shut six metro stations and warned of traffic disruptions ahead of the march, according to the same reporting; the station names and closure timing were not visible because the article body sits behind the Guardian's sign-in gate. Al Jazeera's live blog confirms the main opposition Congress party announced the rally to the top court over what it calls "vote theft," marking the first time a major national party has formally joined the march on the Election Commission issue.

**Latest (Sunday, October 11):** A Delhi executive court ordered the release of CJP founder Abhijeet Dipke, co-convener Ashutosh Ranka and Saurav Das early Sunday after nearly 24 hours in detention, with a one-year condition of "no breach of peace" (ANI cited Delhi Police saying a 50,000-rupee bond was imposed for "maintaining peace and good behavior"). The party had alleged the three were held at an undisclosed location without access to lawyers. Influencer Dhruv Rathee, detained at his Gurugram home on Saturday, was released overnight on condition he take part in no further protests, per an anonymous Interior Ministry official cited by Reuters (single-sourced). India's Supreme Court held a rare Sunday hearing on a contempt petition over the capital's transport restrictions, alongside petitions challenging the detentions. The movement called a fresh march to Jantar Mantar on Sunday, announced a Mumbai protest at 4 p.m. local time with more cities to follow, and scheduled a 2 p.m. press conference; interim leader Ajinkya Shinde said "We're not backing down!" The CJP's X account posted "Cockroaches never die" after the release.

**Latest (Sunday, October 11):** DW reported Saturday's Delhi protest was met by a "mammoth deployment" of police and paramilitary who outnumbered demonstrators, with mobile internet suspended in central Delhi, more than 50 metro stations closed and trains to two major stations canceled (partly reversed after a Supreme Court order). Organization chief Ajinkya Shinde claimed 15,000+ supporters were detained across India; The Hindu cited police sources saying 2,500 were detained in Delhi plus 1,000 in overnight preventive pickups, all released Saturday per Delhi Police. Four former chief ministers were briefly detained. In Mumbai, about 600 protesters including Tushar Gandhi and actor Imran Khan were detained and later released. Indian police boarded a plane on Saturday to detain Abhijeet Dipke, founder of the satirical Cockroach Janta Party (CJP), as he landed at New Delhi's Indira Gandhi International Airport for the youth movement's planned protest, the party said. Dipke, co-conveners Saurav Das and Ashutosh Ranka were stopped inside the aircraft after their flight arrived from Mumbai; the party said police had detained thousands of supporters in several states on Friday.

The capital was sealed on Saturday in anticipation of the rally, the second round of the CJP's campaign against Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar. Central Delhi was patrolled by thousands of police and paramilitary personnel, 57 metro stations were shut from Friday night, some long-distance trains were canceled on Friday, and mobile internet was ordered suspended in a 4km radius around the protest area from Friday night to Saturday night, ANI reported citing a government order.

Delhi Police said on Friday that it had denied permission for the demonstration: the application came on October 7, well short of the usual ten-day notice, and the CJP had violated conditions attached to an earlier agitation, the force said. CJP leaders rejected the decision, called the rally their fundamental right, and said it would proceed at Jantar Mantar, the designated protest site in the heart of New Delhi, at 10 a.m. local time (0430 GMT). With its top three leaders detained, the party said organization chief Ajinkya Shinde would lead the rally.

At the center of the dispute is the Election Commission's Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls, an exercise the commission says is meant to remove duplicate and ineligible voters. Election Commission data shows more than 130 million names have been struck from draft voter lists across 30 states and union territories, a figure larger than the population of Japan. The campaign against Kumar gathered pace after the Indian Express reported that his two deputies, election commissioners Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi, had raised at least 14 formal objections over a ten-month period to orders they said were issued in the commission's name without their knowledge. The commission and the BJP have denied the charges of list manipulation, which opposition parties say are aimed at stripping eligible voters to benefit Prime Minister Narendra Modi's party.

The opposition has joined the pressure on the commission this week: India's INDIA bloc alliance marched on the commission's building on October 7, during which opposition leader Rahul Gandhi scuffled with a policeman, and its MPs staged a separate protest at the gates on October 6. The CJP began in May as a Gen-Z internet joke and in July forced the government's biggest climbdown in years, when education minister Dharmendra Pradhan stepped down after weeks of sit-ins and marches over an exam-paper leaks scandal. Key state elections begin in early 2027, including in the BJP-ruled most populous state of Uttar Pradesh, raising the stakes of the fight over the rolls.

The legal track moved on Tuesday. The Supreme Court adjourned the protest related pleas to October 15, citing a judge's ill health, extended the deadline for a compliance report on its October 9 directions, and issued notice on a fresh petition from the Association for Democratic Reforms challenging the statutory immunity given to election commissioners for acts in office. A separate public interest petition from opposition leaders seeks disclosure of the Election Commission's files on the voter roll revision and an independent examination of how the decisions were taken. The court has not suspended the chief election commissioner, and no finding on the underlying allegations has been made. Status: litigated, hearing now set for October 15.
