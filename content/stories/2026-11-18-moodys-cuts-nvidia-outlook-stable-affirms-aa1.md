Moody's Ratings on Wednesday revised Nvidia's credit outlook to stable from positive and affirmed the chipmaker's Aa1 senior unsecured rating and Prime-1 short-term rating, saying the abrupt departure of longtime chief executive Jensen Huang and the absence of a formal succession plan have introduced uncertainty about the company's business strategy and financial policies. The agency signalled that a further upgrade is unlikely over the next 12 to 18 months while the board searches for a permanent chief executive, and warned that government investigations into the events around the exit could distract management.

The affirmation itself rests on the balance sheet. Moody's cited Nvidia's dominant position in AI data centre computing and held $98 billion of cash and marketable securities against roughly $39 billion of debt and lease liabilities at the third quarter, with free cash flow after dividends expected at about $339 billion in fiscal 2028, enough in the agency's view to absorb the company's potential guarantee obligations. Nvidia shares closed down 2.89 percent at $150.96 on Wednesday.
