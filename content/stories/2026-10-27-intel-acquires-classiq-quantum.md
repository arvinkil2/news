Latest: Intel has now put its own price on the deal. In its third-quarter Form 10-Q filing with the US Securities and Exchange Commission, Intel disclosed that it had entered into a definitive agreement to acquire 100 percent of Classiq for approximately 1.9 billion dollars, subject to customary purchase-price adjustments, plus up to 350 million dollars in contingent consideration tied to specified performance milestones that Intel has not disclosed, CTech reported. The potential total of about 2.25 billion dollars is broadly in line with the 2.3 billion dollar valuation reported at announcement, Intel may settle the price in cash or in shares of its common stock, and it has not yet completed its accounting assessment of the transaction. Intel shares closed at 90.47 dollars on October 26, the day the deal was announced, and at 52.25 dollars on November 5, a fall of about 42 percent, so a stock settlement at current prices would take materially more shares than one struck at announcement.

Intel has agreed to acquire Classiq, the Tel Aviv company whose operating system turns high-level models into optimised quantum circuits that run across different types of quantum hardware, and said the deal will make it a full-stack quantum solutions provider, adding quantum processing units to its compute portfolio. The acquisition is expected to close in the first half of 2027, subject to regulatory approvals, the two companies will operate separately until then, and Intel said Classiq will become its global development centre for quantum computing. Intel did not disclose the price in its own statement; the 2.2 billion dollar figure comes from reporting by Bloomberg and Israeli outlets, and Intel chief executive Lip-Bu Tan was one of Classiq's earliest investors, backing its 2022 Series B round and its May 2025 Series C.
