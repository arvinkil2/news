Asian stock markets fell sharply again on Wednesday as investors braced for the US Trade Representative's findings from its probe into excess industrial capacity at 16 major trading partners, which Reuters reported are due imminently and could set up new tariffs. Taiwan's Taiex closed 4.94 percent lower, with falling stocks outnumbering advancers by 1,060 to 42, South Korea's Kospi tumbled 5.8 percent, Japan's Nikkei 225 sank 2.8 percent to 57,022.19, and MSCI's broadest index of Asia-Pacific shares outside Japan fell 2.4 percent, Reuters reported.

The selling reverses the rally that began on Monday after Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia chief executive, and it hit commodities as well, with Brent crude down 1.4 percent and Shanghai copper down 4.66 percent. In Japan, government bonds slumped after the 40-year auction drew weak demand: the Ministry of Finance result shows 786.5 billion yen of competitive bids for 299.6 billion yen accepted at a highest accepted yield of 4.090 percent, with a 3.8 percent coupon.
