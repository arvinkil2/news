Russia expects to begin regular year round eastbound shipping along the Northern Sea Route in early 2027, Vladimir Panov, head of the Arctic Directorate at state nuclear corporation Rosatom, said, with preparations for regular winter voyages in their final stages.

Panov pointed to Novatek's LNG shipments along the route and to the start of oil exports from Rosneft's Vostok Oil project, where six cargo voyages have been completed. The nuclear powered icebreaker Chukotka is scheduled to enter service in early 2027, bringing the operational nuclear icebreaker fleet to nine, and the Zvezda shipyard has delivered two second generation Arc7 ice class gas carriers designed for winter navigation.

The commercial timing is pointed. The European Union's ban on Russian LNG imports under long term contracts takes effect on January 1, 2027, which raises the value of an all winter route to Asian buyers for Novatek's Arctic plants. Russia has separately said it expects Northern Sea Route transit cargo to nearly double in 2026 to a record 7 million tonnes.

The caution is in the record. Russia previously aimed for year round eastbound navigation in early 2024 and did not achieve it, trial winter voyages in 2021 to 2023 notwithstanding, and Panov acknowledged that convoy speeds and emergency response, communications and medical infrastructure along the route still need work. The hardest late winter months, March and April, remain the operational test.
