The Bank of England published the results of DyGIST 2026, its dynamic general insurance stress test, on Monday. The scenario ran a sequence of severe natural catastrophes and cyber events alongside a financial market stress over three weeks in May 2026, and was designed to push most of the 25 participating insurers beyond their internal risk appetite limits; the Bank said the results demonstrated the sector's financial resilience in the scenario, with reinsurance reducing gross losses from the insurance events by around 72 percent.

The Bank's main warning was about the plans behind the numbers. Insurers leaned on wider group resources, capital injections, credit facilities and dividend retention, and the Bank said firms must distinguish legally enforceable support from discretionary support and test whether it would remain available, timely and sufficient when groups and counterparties face competing demands. The Prudential Regulation Authority will use the findings in its supervisory work on the governance and executability of management actions.
