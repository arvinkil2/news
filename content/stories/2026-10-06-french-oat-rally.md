## Friday: no rescue coming

The Eurogroup ended any hope of a European rescue on Thursday. Finance ministers meeting in Luxembourg agreed that no eurozone institution is prepared to intervene to bring down France's borrowing costs, per Reuters, telling Paris to resolve its own political uncertainty by passing the 2027 budget. ECB President Lagarde stressed the transmission protection instrument has strict criteria, deficit at or below 3 percent of GDP and debt at or below 60 percent, that France cannot meet at 5.1 percent and 115.6 percent.

The refusal lands against a brutal tape. The OAT-Bund spread broke 150bp on October 2, the widest since the 2010 to 2012 eurozone debt crisis, and French 10-year yields sit just short of 5 percent, the highest since July 2002, up nearly 80bp since early September. Thirty-eight percent of French corporate bonds now trade wider than the sovereign, 18 times the share at the start of the year, per Bloomberg, as investors price France like the periphery rather than the core. Assembly debate on Part I of the budget bill starts October 13, with a formal vote October 20. The spread, not the ECB's October 29 meeting, is what will decide French financial conditions.


## Thursday

The 2027 Budget Act gets its first legislative act Thursday, opening the parliamentary path for the draft presented October 1: a 54 billion euro adjustment, 43 billion of it new measures, targeting a 5.0 percent of GDP deficit in 2027 versus 5.4 percent in 2026, with debt projected at 119.3 percent of GDP in 2026 and 121.7 percent in 2027 and debt service running at 79.2 billion euros in 2026 and 91.2 billion in 2027. Debate on Part I of the budget bill runs October 13 to 19 with a formal vote on October 20, and without a majority the government faces a choice between negotiating support and invoking Article 49.3, which risks a no-confidence motion. Finance Minister Roland Lescure told the Wall Street Journal the government is prepared to use special constitutional powers to circumvent parliament if talks stall, with two red lines: a maximum 5 percent deficit and no changes that hurt growth.

Markets have partially stabilized but not forgiven. The 10Y OAT-Bund spread was last near 136bp Wednesday, per Tradeweb data reported by Dow Jones, below Friday's peak just shy of 159bp but still the eurozone's widest stress point. French 10Y yields rose 9.7bp Wednesday to 4.849 percent, near 24-year highs, with roughly 340 billion euros of gross medium and long-term issuance planned next year as sub-1 percent legacy coupons refinance near 4.9 percent. Morningstar DBRS has already cut France's outlook to negative, Scope downgraded it to A+, Fitch and S&P have it at A+ with stable outlooks, and Moody's, at Aa3 with a negative outlook, reviews France on October 23. Marine Le Pen's rival 140 billion euro shadow plan, unveiled October 6, promises a primary surplus by 2028 and a return to the 3 percent deficit ceiling by 2032 at the latest, sharpening the political pricing of the spread.

The euro held near $1.12 in the middle of all this, just above Monday's 16-month low, while the Stoxx 600 banks index fell as much as 2.2 percent Thursday, compounding Wednesday's 3.3 percent selloff into the sector's worst two-day drop since March, per Bloomberg. The budget debate is now the binding constraint on the ECB's October 29 meeting: a widening spread tightens French financial conditions faster than any rate decision.

## Tuesday's rally

French government bonds closed Tuesday with their biggest one-day rally since May. The 10-year OAT yield dropped 14bp to 4.72 percent after energy prices fell, easing the second energy-supply shock that ECB chief economist Philip Lane had flagged the prior day. Against a German 10-year Bund easing to 3.45 percent, the OAT-Bund spread sat near 129bp by the close, about 30bp inside Friday's widest since 2011.

Politics did the rest of the work. Marine Le Pen raised her pledged spending cuts to 140 billion euros in net savings by 2032 versus the 2026 baseline, up from the 125 billion she had planned, and said most of the cuts would land in the first three years, mainly from streamlining government. She said the plan would bring the deficit to the 3 percent Maastricht ceiling as soon as 2030 from 5.4 percent this year, and that she wants discussions with the ECB to help ease France's borrowing costs once the country regains control of its finances. She also proposed capping France's net EU budget contribution at 5 billion euros and will present pension reform plans in the coming weeks. The pledge is aimed squarely at bond investors after Banque de France Governor Francois Villeroy de Galhau warned France risks being "strangled by interest rates." The euro held near $1.12, just above Monday's 16-month low.

The calm is still rented, not owned. Le Pen's trajectory is actually slower than the incumbent government's: Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu targets a deficit below 3 percent by 2029, while her plan reaches Maastricht only in 2030-2032. And the supply test is concrete: France plans a record 340 billion euros of medium and long-term issuance net of buybacks in 2027, about 10 percent above 2026, as redemptions rise. No budget has passed and the first legislative act on the French budget is scheduled for Thursday, October 8, the next hard test of whether markets accept either side's arithmetic.
