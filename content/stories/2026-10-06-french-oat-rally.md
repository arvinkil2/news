French government bonds closed Tuesday with their biggest one-day rally since May. The 10-year OAT yield dropped 14bp to 4.72 percent after energy prices fell, easing the second energy-supply shock that ECB chief economist Philip Lane had flagged the prior day. Against a German 10-year Bund easing to 3.45 percent, the OAT-Bund spread sat near 129bp by the close, about 30bp inside Friday's widest since 2011.

Politics did the rest of the work. Marine Le Pen raised her pledged spending cuts to 140 billion euros in net savings by 2032 versus the 2026 baseline, up from the 125 billion she had planned, and said most of the cuts would land in the first three years, mainly from streamlining government. She said the plan would bring the deficit to the 3 percent Maastricht ceiling as soon as 2030 from 5.4 percent this year, and that she wants discussions with the ECB to help ease France's borrowing costs once the country regains control of its finances. She also proposed capping France's net EU budget contribution at 5 billion euros and will present pension reform plans in the coming weeks. The pledge is aimed squarely at bond investors after Banque de France Governor Francois Villeroy de Galhau warned France risks being "strangled by interest rates." The euro held near $1.12, just above Monday's 16-month low.

The calm is still rented, not owned. Le Pen's trajectory is actually slower than the incumbent government's: Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu targets a deficit below 3 percent by 2029, while her plan reaches Maastricht only in 2030-2032. And the supply test is concrete: France plans a record 340 billion euros of medium and long-term issuance net of buybacks in 2027, about 10 percent above 2026, as redemptions rise. No budget has passed and the first legislative act on the French budget is scheduled for Thursday, October 8, the next hard test of whether markets accept either side's arithmetic.
