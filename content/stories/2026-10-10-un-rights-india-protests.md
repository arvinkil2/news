**Latest (Monday, October 12):** the protest movement has a new face and a new venue. Authorities closed half a dozen Delhi Metro stations and warned of traffic disruptions on Monday as opposition parties prepared to march to the Supreme Court over controversial voter-list revisions, Al Jazeera reported. Thousands of police and security personnel were deployed across central New Delhi for a third straight day, with heavy metal barricades and key metro stations closed, ahead of a march called by Rahul Gandhi, the leader of the opposition in the Lok Sabha. Gandhi, a Congress party leader, urged supporters to join the march to press for Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar's resignation over revisions to voter lists that critics say benefit the BJP; the youth-led Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) movement pledged to keep protesting until Kumar steps down.

The weekend's crackdown drew international notice. Parliamentarian John Brittas of the Communist Party of India (Marxist) alleged he was "manhandled, dragged, assaulted" by police during Sunday's demonstrations, calling it an assault on democratic freedoms and the rule of law. Reporters Without Borders said at least 16 journalists were detained while covering the protests, despite showing press cards. Media reports put the number of names removed from electoral rolls at more than 133 million, with critics saying minorities were disproportionately affected; the Election Commission of India denies wrongdoing, saying the revisions targeted duplicate, deceased and fraudulent entries. Police did not respond to questions about the detentions. Al Jazeera notes the protests have become one of the most significant political challenges for Modi in his 12 years in power.

The UN human rights office (OHCHR) voiced concern Saturday over reports of mass detentions in India of demonstrators, protest leaders, civil society members, lawyers and journalists, before and during large-scale protests in Delhi. Spokesman Thameen Al-Kheetan called on authorities to ensure the right to peaceful assembly is fully respected, said restrictions on peaceful protests must meet strict tests of necessity and proportionality, and urged law enforcement to show utmost restraint.

The demonstration was called by a youth-led movement seeking electoral reforms ahead of state elections, with its chief demand being the resignation of the official heading India's election commission. Police had refused permission for the protest at its planned venue in the capital, and the movement's leaders were reportedly among those detained.
