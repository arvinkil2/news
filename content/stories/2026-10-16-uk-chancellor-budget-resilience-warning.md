Chancellor Rachel Reeves used her Washington appearance at the annual meetings to frame the coming UK budget as a resilience exercise, telling reporters that protecting the economy against international headwinds is her job and that the budget will do it. The Treasury's own release carried the message verbatim, a sign of how deliberately the pitch is being made.

The audience is not really Washington. It is the gilt market, which today is being asked to fund a 225 million pound linker tap inside a heavy issuance programme, covered in the rates edition, and which retains the power to reprice the government's entire programme in an afternoon. Every word of the chancellor's framing, headwinds external, discipline domestic, is chosen for that reader.

The substance waits for the budget itself. What the statement rules in is a defence of fiscal rules under pressure; what it cannot yet answer is where the resilience is bought, tax, spending or borrowing, and that answer is now the single largest scheduled risk in European rates this autumn.
