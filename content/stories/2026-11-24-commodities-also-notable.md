Dry bulk freight rose 4.49 percent to 12.66 dollars and tanker freight rose 1.91 percent to 760.03 dollars at Monday's close in the estate market tables, with tanker freight up 19.05 percent on the week. Freight strength alongside a falling crude price points to physical cargo demand and route disruption, including the Arctic and Mediterranean developments in today's full stories, rather than to oil market tightness.

In Libya, the Sharara pipeline is back in service, with crude moving into storage at the Akakus site in Zawiya, the chairperson of the National Oil Corporation told Reuters on Monday. The restart is one of the supply recoveries behind Monday's three-month low in Brent, and its durability is a watch item for the OPEC+ meeting on November 29.

The Democratic Republic of Congo's renewed cobalt export ban takes effect on November 30, a story carried in full on this site on November 23. No new figures were published in this window; the item is noted here because the effective date falls inside the next session's horizon for battery metals pricing.

Two of today's full stories are updates to developments already on this site rather than new events. Japan's positive spark spread and returning LNG buying, and China's extension of the gallium and germanium ban pause to January 27, were both published this morning as developing stories and are not duplicated here. The December natural gas contract expires on NYMEX on November 25, with the January premium over December still the market's price for the first full winter month.
