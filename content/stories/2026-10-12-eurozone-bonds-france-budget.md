European bonds caught a mild bid on Monday while US markets sat out Columbus Day, but the relief was thin. The 10-year German Bund yield fell 2.6 basis points to 3.463% and the French OAT declined 1.5 basis points to 4.847% in early trade, according to Tradeweb quotes carried by Dow Jones, putting the OAT-Bund spread at roughly 138 basis points. That is a touch inside the 141 basis points that followed the October 1 budget presentation and the 159 basis point intraday peak of October 2, the widest since the 2012 euro crisis, yet still wider than Italy's spread to Bund, a line France crossed this month for the first time in the modern era.

The calm is tactical. France's National Assembly opens debate Tuesday on the draft 2027 budget presented October 1 by Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu: a 54 billion euro adjustment, built on frozen public-sector wages and pensions, targeting a deficit of 5.0% of GDP in 2027 against 5.4% expected this year. Markets have treated the arithmetic with open skepticism: without the measures, the deficit would hit 6.5% of GDP, public debt is forecast to rise to 122% of GDP, and France plans a record 340 billion euros of medium and long-term issuance next year as sub-1% legacy coupons roll over near 5%. With no parliamentary majority, Continuum Economics sees weeks of horse trading toward a modified budget that may still not command a formal vote.

The political overlay keeps the pressure bid. Marine Le Pen's National Rally has published a rival fiscal path, promising a deficit of 3.7% in 2027, a primary surplus by 2028 and the 3% Maastricht ceiling by 2032, with 140 billion euros of savings versus the 2026 baseline. The near-term calendar offers no relief valve: the revenue-side Part I vote is October 20, Moody's reviews France on October 23 with the rating at Aa3 on negative outlook, and the ECB meets October 29 with markets pricing about an 80% chance of another hike by year end.

**Update (Tuesday, October 13):** France's prudential watchdog, the ACPR, moved to close off the bank-sovereign doom-loop reading of the selloff as the Assembly debate opened. Secretary General Emmanuelle Assouan said French banks hold about 190 billion euros of domestic public debt, which is 24% of their total sovereign bond holdings and only about 2% of total consolidated assets, and that the exposure remains limited and does not pose stability risks even as yields surge. The figures are reported from the ACPR's Tuesday press conference; treat them as the regulator's own accounting of the exposure, not as a market verdict on the spread, which remains the widest since the euro crisis era.

The politics behind those yields hardened before the floor debate opened. The Assembly finance committee rejected the revenue part of the bill as a whole on October 9 by 31 votes to three, so the chamber begins its debate on Tuesday, October 13 from the government's original draft rather than a committee text. The committee's general rapporteur Philippe Juvin estimated the week's committee changes would have widened the 2027 deficit by about 10 billion euros, to roughly 5.35 to 5.4 percent of GDP against the government's 5.0 percent target, after members removed a pensioner tax relief cut the government had counted on for 1.4 billion euros in savings. The solemn vote on the revenue part is set for October 20. Nothing in the committee vote enacts or kills the budget: under the constitution the floor debates the government text.
