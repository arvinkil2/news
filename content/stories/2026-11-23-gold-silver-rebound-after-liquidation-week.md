Gold rose 3.16 percent to 3,626.40 dollars an ounce in the latest session in the estate market tables, silver rose 3.67 percent, and copper rose 0.98 percent, the first broad metals rebound after the worst liquidation week of the autumn. The recovery only retraces a fraction of the damage: on the week recorded in yesterday's edition, gold fell 9.54 percent and silver 16.24 percent as rising rate expectations and equity weakness forced selling of the most liquid assets first.

The technical picture explains both the violence of the fall and the speed of the bounce. A post-market summary of Friday, November 20 put gold's 14-day relative strength index at 11.3 and silver's at 12.8, readings deep in oversold territory, with both metals trading far below their 50-day and 200-day averages. Moves that extreme tend to attract bargain buying on any softening in the dollar or yields, and base metals joined the turn, with copper erasing a multi-day decline and aluminium bouncing from its lowest close of the year in London reporting.

Nothing in the physical market changed over the week. Chinese refined copper premiums were at a record and Shanghai exchange stocks at their lowest since December 2023 in the reporting carried in this site's November 20 edition, even as futures fell. The divergence remains the central fact of the metals complex: paper markets are trading rates and liquidity, physical markets are trading scarcity, and Friday's rebound narrowed that gap only slightly.
