Semiconductors fell 8.24 percent, the largest move in the tables, with Broadcom down 5.48 percent, Nvidia down 4.22 percent and the broader tech sector down 4.89 percent. No story in today's edition identifies a single company trigger for Friday's chip selloff. Today's base metals story describes the wider selling as liquidation driven by the technology rout, and today's Infineon story shows the other side of the trade, a commitment of about 1.1 billion euros to new gallium nitride capacity at Villach in Austria.

Silver took the hardest hit in metals. Silver futures fell 5.59 percent to 49.28 dollars an ounce and silver miners fell 5.58 percent. Today's base metals selloff story records the same move across London and Shanghai, with COMEX silver down 6.2 percent in the session reported by Shanghai Metals Market, and it frames the decline as liquidation rather than a change in supply.

The rest of the miners fell in line: uranium miners down 5.57 percent, gold miners down 5.21 percent, copper miners down 5.19 percent and rare earth miners down 5.12 percent. Gold futures fell 3.29 percent to 3,898.9 dollars, below 4,000 dollars, and copper fell 1.5 percent to 6.48 dollars a pound. Today's Australian miners story sets the contrast, with local materials shares up 4.06 percent on the week because gold remains high in Australian dollar terms even as the dollar price falls.

Oil and freight moved the other way. WTI crude rose 2.08 percent to 104.12 dollars and tanker freight rose 2.05 percent to 968.92 dollars, up 8.06 percent on the week. Today's tanker freight story ties the freight rise to Hormuz risk after the Qatari LNG carrier Al Mayeda was struck in the Gulf, reported in today's Qatari LNG carrier story, and today's OPEC+ story confirms December output targets were held steady on Sunday, so no new supply offsets that risk. Brent eased 1.26 percent to 113.43 dollars; no story in today's edition explains Brent's fall on its own.

Treasury yields fell in the risk-off bid, the US 5 year down 8 basis points to 4.72 percent and the 10 year down 6 basis points to 4.93 percent. No story in today's edition gives a yield-specific driver beyond that close, and today's central banks week ahead story puts the next tests in front of markets this week, with five central bank decisions and the US Treasury quarterly refunding due on Wednesday.

The midday read is the same risk-off split that closed the week, unchanged because nothing has traded since Friday. Growth and metals exposure sits sold, government bonds sit bid, and the only firm prices are the ones tied to physical risk in the Gulf, in crude, in tanker freight and in European gas, where today's TTF story has Dutch gas at a four year high with storage at its lowest seasonal level since 2011. Monday's open, not Sunday's headlines, decides whether the liquidation is finished.
