Endeavour Silver has completed the feasibility study for Pitarrilla, its silver, lead and zinc project in Durango State, Mexico, clearing the main technical gate before a development decision. The study designs a mechanised, ramp access underground mine at 4,000 tonnes per day, producing silver-lead and silver-zinc concentrates by conventional crushing, grinding and flotation, with filtered tailings in a dry stack facility.

The economics, at a base case silver price of $50 an ounce, are an after-tax net present value of $736 million at a 5 percent discount rate and an after-tax internal rate of return of 16.37 percent. Initial capital is $585 million, including $65 million of contingency, with a further $119 million of sustaining capital over the mine life and a construction period of about 30 months. The company estimates after-tax operating cash flow of $2,443 million over the life of the mine and payback at about five years after tax.

Production would average about 5.4 million ounces of silver, 13,000 tonnes of lead and 29,000 tonnes of zinc a year, or 7.5 million silver equivalent ounces, over 18 years, rising to 7.9 million silver equivalent ounces a year in the first five full years. Silver accounts for about 73 percent of revenue. All-in sustaining costs are estimated at $15.06 per payable ounce of silver net of by-product credits, and the company puts the break-even silver price below $30 an ounce.

That break-even is the number that matters this week. The study's $50 base case was set before the latest leg down in silver, and the Northern Miner framed the plan as facing a silver slide. A sub $30 break-even gives the project a wide margin against the spot price even after the fall, but the gap between the study price and the market price is what a board will test before committing $585 million. The study covers only part of Pitarrilla's broader resource, across some 4,950 hectares on the eastern flank of the Sierra Madre Occidental, about 160 kilometres from both Durango city and Torreón.
