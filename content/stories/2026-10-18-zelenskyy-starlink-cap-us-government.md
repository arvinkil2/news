President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said the message to restrict Starlink and Starshield for Ukraine came from the United States government, telling NBC's Meet the Press on Sunday that he did not know exactly who sent it, that SpaceX carried the restriction out technically, and that it has not been lifted: the maximum speed is 161 kilometres per hour. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, on the same programme, said President Trump and Secretary of State Marco Rubio had categorically denied that anyone in the administration did it, called Zelenskyy's charge very reckless, and warned him to be very careful about making statements that are not true. Elon Musk posted last week that Starshield systems are owned and controlled by the United States government and that any limits on speed or use are decided by the United States military and ultimately the president.

Separately, Lithuanian authorities reported an attempted arson attack overnight on a company site housing Starlink satellite antennas and a control centre, where fire accelerant was found and no location was disclosed. Prime Minister Mindaugas Sinkevicius told the public broadcaster LRT that the attempted sabotage was obviously part of Russia's hybrid warfare, with investigators still to establish who gave the order.
