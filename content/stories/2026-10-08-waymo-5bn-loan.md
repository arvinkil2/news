Waymo closed a $5 billion term loan, the company's first debt financing, with PIMCO, Blackstone and Sixth Street as lead syndicated lenders. Capital Group, Loomis Sayles and T. Rowe Price were significant lenders, with Apollo, Blue Owl, Diameter Capital Partners, Franklin Templeton, Fidelity Management & Research, HPS Investment Partners and Oaktree also participating. Goldman Sachs served as the sole lead bookrunner. Waymo called the deal "an important step in our evolution into a scaling commercial enterprise," saying the debt provides financial flexibility to strengthen the balance sheet.

The company now runs robotaxi services in 15 markets, including Los Angeles, San Francisco, San Diego, Austin, Dallas, Houston, Miami, Orlando and Tampa. It launched operations in its 15th US city last month and announced an expansion to Singapore, with testing underway in London and Tokyo ahead of launches there. The debt raise follows a $16 billion February equity round that pushed Waymo's valuation to $126 billion, led by Dragoneer Investment Group, DST Global and Sequoia Capital, with Alphabet still the majority investor.

**Update (Friday, October 9):** Waymo's own announcement confirms the lender roster and frames the borrowing as complementary to the February equity raise, a signal the company believes its commercial momentum supports leverage. Until now, Waymo had relied on its parent company and outside equity investors; the shift to debt is one of the few times money has come from an outside source.
