The Bank of England, the Financial Conduct Authority and the Prudential Regulation Authority proposed joint guidance on Thursday that brings together existing cyber requirements and supervisory lessons to clarify what they expect of financial firms, without imposing new rules. October 2026 saw the second-highest monthly number of cyber incidents reported to the FCA in five years, 52 percent above the previous 18-month peak recorded in May 2025, and the Bank's latest Systemic Risk Survey cited cyber as one of the risks with the greatest potential impact on the UK financial system.

The guidance covers governance, identifying critical infrastructure, protection, detection, and response and recovery, and aligns with the National Cyber Security Centre's Cyber Assessment Framework; the consultation closes on February 26, 2027. Separately, the regulators published effective practices on AI and quantum risks, covering threat actors using AI and the risk that quantum computing could undermine the public-key cryptography underpinning financial transactions, and the release said 88 percent of respondents to a Bank and FCA survey expect frontier AI cyber capabilities to have a moderate or high impact on their risk profile over the next two years.
