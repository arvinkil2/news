General Flash, a Cambridge, Massachusetts startup, said on Tuesday that it has raised $128 million in equity capital across its seed and Series A rounds, from backers including Lux Capital, Construct Capital, Overmatch Ventures, GreatPoint Ventures, Pillar VC and Lowercarbon Capital. The company converts metal oxides directly into high purity, semiconductor grade metal using a process it calls Solid-State Flash Electrolysis, which it says is faster and lower cost than conventional refining and produces no emissions. The process can refine tungsten, tantalum, titanium, hafnium, zirconium, germanium, yttrium, niobium, molybdenum and rare earth elements.

Chief executive Ric Fulop said the funding fully finances production of the company's first large plant in the United States, following a multi-ton pilot facility near MIT. General Flash targets commercial production of 1,000 metric tons per year by summer 2027. The raise lands ahead of defence acquisition rules that, from January 1, 2027, bar Chinese supply for many of these critical materials in US defence supply chains.
