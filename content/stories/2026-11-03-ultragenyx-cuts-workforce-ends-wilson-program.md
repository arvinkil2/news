Ultragenyx used its third quarter results on Tuesday to announce a broad cost reset: headcount falls by about 20 percent to a workforce of roughly 1,000, the UX701 program for Wilson disease is terminated after not achieving its study goals, and all ongoing studies of GTX-102 for Angelman syndrome will be wound down. The company expects the actions to cut 2027 research and development expenses by at least 50 percent against 2025, to less than $350 million, with selling, general and administrative expenses below $375 million, and it reaffirmed guidance for profitability in 2027.

The restructuring follows a quarter in which the FDA approved the company's first two gene therapies, for glycogen storage disease type Ia and Sanfilippo syndrome type A, within a month of each other. Third quarter product revenue included $109 million from Crysvita and $30 million from Dojolvi, and the company said launches of the newly approved treatments are progressing with growing demand.
