The Spanish government has approved a royal decree regulating the energy sustainability, environmental and digital sovereignty requirements for data centres, at the same Council of Ministers session that updated the national electricity plan through 2030. Third Vice President Sara Aagesen argued the regulation lets Spain anticipate the rise in electricity demand from these facilities and prevent the costs being passed to consumers as a whole; without it, she estimated, the impact on electricity bills could reach 5 billion euros.

The central requirement is that data centres cover at least 80 percent of their electricity consumption with energy from newly installed renewable sources, demonstrated through an hourly correlation between consumption and the associated generation. An optional transitional regime phases the requirement in at 60 percent initially, 70 percent in 2029 and 80 percent in 2032. The decree retains the provision operators opposed most: data centres may have to accept power reductions or disconnections without financial compensation under certain circumstances.

On water, facilities must meet the highest-level European water efficiency label, adjusted by climate region, with a consumption target of around 0.1 litres per kilowatt-hour. On efficiency, the transitional regime allows a maximum power usage effectiveness of 1.25 for certain facilities through the end of 2031. The regulation takes effect on publication in the Official State Gazette.

The scale behind the rules is large. Spain has already granted 15 gigawatts of access permits for data centres, 6 gigawatts on the transmission grid and 9 gigawatts on distribution, with about 3 gigawatts granted in the last three months alone, and more than 20 gigawatts of applications pending. The updated grid plan calls for 17.142 billion euros of transmission investment, 146 percent more than the current plan, to meet 47.4 gigawatts of new demand.
