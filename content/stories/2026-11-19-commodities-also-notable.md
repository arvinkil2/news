**Platinum swings from surplus to deficit on an investment bet.** The World Platinum Investment Council's Platinum Quarterly, published on Wednesday, raised its 2026 surplus forecast to 468,000 ounces, from 265,000 ounces previously, on weaker investment demand, and issued its first 2027 forecast: a deficit of 279,000 ounces. The swing runs through investors. The council expects investment demand to turn by 911,000 ounces, more than the entire change in the balance, with net investment of 513,000 ounces in 2027 after outflows this year, bar and coin purchases up 55 percent to 448,000 ounces, exchange warehouses adding 110,000 ounces and exchange-traded funds still losing 150,000 ounces. Total demand is forecast to grow 12 percent in 2027 against 1 percent supply growth. Chief executive Trevor Raymond said above-ground stocks remain critically depleted after three consecutive years of significant deficit, and the council expects stocks to cover less than three months of demand by the end of 2027.

**Ghana's gold carries a record trade surplus.** Ghana's export earnings reached a record $30.4 billion in the first ten months of 2026, up from $23.3 billion a year earlier, according to the Bank of Ghana's latest Summary of Economic and Financial Data. Gold accounted for $20.9 billion of that, up from $15.2 billion, with cocoa at $3.1 billion and oil exports at $3.2 billion. Imports rose to $17.1 billion, including $6.3 billion of oil imports, leaving a trade surplus of $13 billion against $8.7 billion a year earlier. Gross international reserves eased to $11.9 billion in October, 4.4 months of import cover, and the central bank's gold holdings fell to 26 tonnes from 38 tonnes a year earlier.
