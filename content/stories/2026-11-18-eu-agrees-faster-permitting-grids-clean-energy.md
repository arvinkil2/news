The Council and the European Parliament reached a provisional agreement on Tuesday night to make permit-granting faster and more transparent for renewable energy projects, energy storage and recharging stations, and for new electricity and gas infrastructure, streamlining deadlines for approval and grid connection. Member states will be able to prioritise renewables and grids by presuming them to be of overriding public interest, with discretion not to apply that principle for security, defence and cultural heritage, and may treat an authority's silence on intermediary steps as tacit approval. The agreement, the permitting half of the European grids package proposed in December 2025, also provides for single digital permitting portals and benefit-sharing with communities hosting renewable projects above 7 MW.

The provisional agreement must now be endorsed by both institutions and formally adopted after legal-linguistic revision, after which member states will have 1.5 years to transpose it into national law. The Council noted that electricity transmission grids currently take around ten years to complete, with permitting accounting for more than half of that time.
