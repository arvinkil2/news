Bank of Japan board member Naoki Tamura said on Thursday that the central bank must respond swiftly to signs of an inflation overshoot, including by accelerating the pace of interest rate hikes, keeping alive prospects of a rate increase as soon as December. In a speech to business leaders in Kofu, Yamanashi Prefecture, Tamura said financial conditions remain accommodative and the Bank needs to bring its policy rate to a level deemed neutral to the economy soon, citing price pressure from higher crude oil costs linked to the Middle East conflict, a weak yen and robust demand for AI-related goods.

At a news conference after the speech, Tamura said his base case is a 25-basis-point hike once every few months until the rate reaches neutral, which he still puts at around 2 percent against the current 1.25 percent, but that a faster pace, and even a 50-basis-point hike, may become necessary if upside price risks materialise. Markets widely expect the Bank to raise the policy rate to 1.5 percent at its meeting ending December 18, Reuters reported.
