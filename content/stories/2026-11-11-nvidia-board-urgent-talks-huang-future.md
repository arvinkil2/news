**Latest.** Huang is out. Nvidia announced on Thursday, in a press release issued after the board's late Wednesday meeting, that Huang had resigned as chief executive, president and board member effective immediately, and that Colette Kress, the chief financial officer since 2013, will serve as interim chief executive while the board searches for a permanent successor among internal and external candidates, The Verge and Axios reported. The company also named Harvey C. Jones, the former Synopsys chief executive, as the first board chair in its history. The announcement carried no quote from Huang; Kress said the company's strength comes from its people and that she is committed to building on the foundation Huang created over 33 years. The shares fell about 10 percent in Thursday morning trading, CNBC's market data showed, after a premarket fall of about 5.4 percent, extending a week in which the stock had already fallen 7 percent. Six analysts downgraded the stock between Wednesday night and Thursday morning, CNBC reported, among them Melius, Evercore, William Blair, Oppenheimer, Bernstein and KeyBanc, several citing the absence of a succession plan. The resignation follows reports of two extramarital affairs, one with a woman United States intelligence believes is a Chinese spy and one with an Nvidia executive who reported to him; the underlying allegations remain reported claims, and the board has said it has no reason to believe national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised.

**Latest.** The board met late on Wednesday and had announced no decision by Thursday morning. Nvidia shares fell about 6.3 percent in early premarket trading on Thursday after dropping roughly 8 percent in after-hours trading on Wednesday, Bloomberg reported, and the stock has now lost more than $450 billion of market value since the company disclosed its investigation into Huang's conduct on November 8. No Nvidia statement, denial or confirmation of the ouster reporting had appeared by Thursday morning, and no November filing with the SEC announced any change; the company's last public word remains its November 8 board statement expressing confidence in Huang, and Huang remains chief executive. Bloomberg also reported the board is weighing whether to name a temporary chief while it searches for a permanent one. The spillover reached Asia, where Alibaba fell as much as 7.1 percent in Hong Kong, leading losses on the Hang Seng Tech Index, as investors weighed tighter United States scrutiny of Chinese cloud operators' access to advanced chips; Alibaba rents Nvidia processors through Megaspeed and its United States listed shares fell about 5.7 percent.

**Latest.** Beijing has escalated its response. Foreign ministry spokesperson Mao Ning told a regular briefing on Thursday that the United States should stop fabricating so-called spy narratives and using them as a pretext for imposing a technological blockade on China, Reuters reported. Mao said China's scientific and technological progress is driven by independent innovation and open cooperation, and that China is willing to work with the United States to implement the consensus reached by the two heads of state. Agence France-Presse reported the same briefing, in which Beijing dismissed the claims about spying on Huang as groundless speculation and an attempt to smear China. The statement is Beijing's strongest framing yet of the Huang affair, moving from denying the espionage allegation to accusing Washington of using it to justify wider technology restrictions.

**Latest.** Taiwan's government has put its own record on the file. Chiu Chui-cheng, minister of the Mainland Affairs Council, told parliament on Thursday that Alice Huang, the former Megaspeed executive at the centre of the espionage allegations, entered Taiwan eight times in 2024 and 2025, three times in 2024 and five times in 2025, on a Hong Kong passport under standard business visitor status, with no alert flags on her at the time and no entry recorded this year. Chiu said the council never received the warning the New York Times reported the United States gave Taiwan in 2024, and that Taiwan's security agencies are investigating possible links with foreign intelligence. Agence France-Presse, which reported the testimony, noted that Beijing has rejected the espionage accusations; the claim that she worked for Chinese intelligence is a United States belief, not an established finding.

**Latest.** Attention has turned to succession, and no board decision has been announced. Reuters reported late on Wednesday that analysts and investors see two internal candidates as particularly likely, at least for the short term: Ian Buck, the engineer who helped create the CUDA software platform and leads Nvidia's hyperscale AI products, and Colette Kress, the chief financial officer since 2013, whom Semafor reported some board members favour as interim chief executive. Arm chief executive Rene Haas and 32-year Nvidia veteran Jeff Fisher were also raised in the speculation. One portfolio manager, Armina Rosenberg of Minotaur Capital, suggested a co-chief structure pairing Kress on the business and finances with Buck on product and technology. Nvidia did not respond to a request for comment on the board meeting, and none of the named candidates is confirmed as chosen.

**Latest.** The congressional pressure named names on Wednesday. Senator Kevin Cramer, a Republican on the Senate Banking Committee, renewed his call for Huang to resign, arguing he is too vulnerable to blackmail to remain in the job, and Representatives Suhas Subramanyam, a Democrat, and Don Bacon, a Republican, also said Huang should step down. The chairs of the House Foreign Affairs, China select and Intelligence committees, Brian Mast, John Moolenaar and Rick Crawford, said on Wednesday afternoon that they had not yet heard from Huang on their November 4 request that he testify on November 18, that the deadline they set was that day, and that they would pursue a subpoena if he does not agree to appear. Lawmakers are also discussing tighter AI chip export controls in this year's National Defense Authorization Act: Senators Elizabeth Warren and Jim Banks urged colleagues to include the AI Overwatch Act, one of three bills named in the discussions alongside the MATCH Act and the Chip Security Act, CNBC reported.

**Latest.** The selling reached index futures in evening trading. Nasdaq 100 futures were down 1 percent at 28,205.75 at 7:25 PM ET and S&P 500 futures were down 0.4 percent at 7,608.75, with Dow futures flat, Investing.com reported, and Al Jazeera put Nvidia's after-hours fall at 8.6 percent. Neither report added a board decision, a meeting outcome or a named successor.

Nvidia's board held urgent talks on Wednesday about the future of its founder and chief executive, Jensen Huang, after the Wall Street Journal reported that the board is preparing to oust him. The report, citing people familiar with the matter, followed a New York Times report earlier Wednesday that Huang had a romantic relationship with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice president of healthcare and a direct report of his. Nvidia shares, which had closed up 2.87 percent at $173.73, fell about 8 percent in after-hours trading, trading at $160.15 by 6:42 PM ET. Reuters said it could not immediately verify the Journal's report, and Nvidia did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

The Powell relationship is the second to surface in a week. On Sunday Huang admitted to an affair with Alice Huang, an executive at an Nvidia customer, two days after the Financial Times first reported that relationship. US intelligence believes Alice Huang was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security, and President Trump said on Monday that the FBI is investigating. The board's own investigation, which on Tuesday it said would be led by a former federal prosecutor it has hired, is examining whether Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence operation. As recently as Sunday the board had backed Huang to remain as chief executive.

Washington is moving in parallel. Two House Republicans said on Wednesday that Congress is preparing to subpoena Huang over the spy allegations if he does not voluntarily accept a summons to a closed-door briefing, and senior members of both parties have called for congressional investigations. Huang has become an influential voice in the president's circle on AI policy and export controls, which is what turns a personal conduct scandal into a national security file.

The market question is succession. The stock has lost more than $300 billion in market value since the first affair reports broke, according to Reuters, and analysts noted on Wednesday that Nvidia has no announced succession plan. Gil Luria of D.A. Davidson said an immediate removal would be challenging for that reason, and that investors would expect the board to act as soon as it has proof if Huang broke the law or violated company policy. Huang co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and has led it throughout its rise to a market value of about $4 trillion.
