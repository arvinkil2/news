Consumer sentiment held steady in October, the University of Michigan's final reading showed, with the index at 48.0 against 48.1 in September and 53.6 a year earlier. The current conditions index fell 8.3 percent to 46.7, a drop the survey's director, Joanne Hsu, said was driven entirely by buying conditions for durables, which fell 18 percent on higher interest rates and affordability concerns, while the expectations index rose 5.4 percent to 48.8. Year-ahead inflation expectations rose from 4.6 percent to 4.7 percent and long-run expectations from 3.4 percent to 3.5 percent, both up for a second straight month to their highest since May, and sentiment now stands 15 percent below February, just before the Iran conflict began, Hsu said.
