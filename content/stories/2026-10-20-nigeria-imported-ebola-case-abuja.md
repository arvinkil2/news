**Latest:** The World Health Organization said on Thursday that the Ebola outbreak that emerged in the Democratic Republic of Congo is growing more complex, with cases now present in countries in East and West Africa, and that the source of Nigeria's first infection is still unknown. Mohamed Janabi, the WHO regional director for Africa, told an online media briefing that health authorities were investigating where the Nigerian patient, confirmed this week, was infected. Wessam Mankoula of the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention said both the first Nigerian case and the first Kenyan case had left Entebbe on October 3 and that a possible encounter between them was under investigation, and he and Africa CDC director general Jean Kaseya said there was no validated information that the Nigerian patient had been to the Congo. Patrick Otim, a WHO emergency response manager, said investigators were also looking at which Abuja health facilities she visited between the onset of symptoms and her hospitalisation 48 hours later. A Swiss government statement to Reuters said the patient is believed to have contracted the disease while travelling in Uganda. Ugandan government spokesman Alan Kasujja told AFP it was preposterous to link Uganda to the case simply because she had holidayed there. Kenya had traced all 135 contacts of its first case, its health ministry said, and Nigeria was tracing 44 contacts, with more since identified, Al Jazeera reported.

The outbreak in the Congo has now killed at least 4,600 of the 9,500 people infected there, the Associated Press reported, up from the more than 4,200 deaths the WHO reported as of October 18, and Ebola disease from the outbreak has spread to four other countries, farther across Africa than any previous Ebola outbreak, reaching the central, western and eastern regions. Nigeria is the latest country affected, Uganda's outbreak was declared over on August 27 after 20 confirmed cases and it reported no new cases on Tuesday, Kenya has nine cases with no evidence of transmission beyond known contacts of its first patient, and France recorded the only case outside Africa, in a doctor who returned from the Congo in June and recovered, the AP said. The Africa CDC announced late on Wednesday an extraordinary virtual meeting of national health officials on October 28, which its director general, Dr. Jean Kaseya, called a chance to strengthen the collective response, Deutsche Welle reported.

The World Health Organization said Nigeria has become the fifth country to detect Bundibugyo Ebola since the outbreak was declared in the Democratic Republic of the Congo in mid-May 2026, in a woman who recently travelled from Uganda and whose symptoms began on October 7, four days after she arrived. She sought care at a private health facility in Abuja on October 11, laboratory testing confirmed the infection on October 20, she remains in isolation, and 44 contacts are being monitored, the WHO said. The Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention has activated its incident management system, the WHO and partners are supporting case investigation, contact tracing and infection control, including protective equipment for health workers, investigations continue into the likely source of her infection and her full travel and exposure history, and the WHO has not recommended travel or trade restrictions on affected countries. The WHO put the Congo outbreak at more than 9,500 cases and more than 4,200 deaths as of October 18, with nine cases in Kenya and one in the Netherlands linked to it.

Nigeria has confirmed its first imported case of Bundibugyo Ebola in the Federal Capital Territory, after laboratory confirmation on Tuesday of an infection in a woman who had recently arrived on an international flight from Uganda, Health Minister Muhammad Pate said at a media conference in Abuja. The woman is in isolation and receiving care after spending several days at a private hospital in the capital before her diagnosis was confirmed, and contact tracing has identified 44 close contacts so far, including health workers, patients, caregivers and travel contacts. Pate called it a contained case, said surveillance has been intensified and the hospital is receiving support to strengthen infection prevention and control, and recalled that Nigeria successfully contained Ebola in 2014.
