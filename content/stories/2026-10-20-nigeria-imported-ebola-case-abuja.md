**Latest:** The World Health Organization said Nigeria has become the fifth country to detect Bundibugyo Ebola since the outbreak was declared in the Democratic Republic of the Congo on May 15, 2026, in a Nigerian woman who had recently returned from the Congo and whose symptoms began on October 7, four days after she arrived. She sought care at a private health facility in Abuja on October 11, a sample taken on October 18 confirmed the infection on October 20, and 44 contacts are under follow-up, the WHO said. The Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention has activated its incident management system, the WHO and partners are supporting case investigation, contact tracing and infection control, including protective equipment for health workers, and the WHO advised against travel or trade restrictions on affected countries. Its account of the patient's travel differs from the minister's earlier account of an arrival from Uganda, and the WHO said investigations are under way to establish the likely source of infection.

Nigeria has confirmed its first imported case of Bundibugyo Ebola in the Federal Capital Territory, after laboratory confirmation on Tuesday of an infection in a woman who had recently arrived on an international flight from Uganda, Health Minister Muhammad Pate said at a media conference in Abuja. The woman is in isolation and receiving care after spending several days at a private hospital in the capital before her diagnosis was confirmed, and contact tracing has identified 44 close contacts so far, including health workers, patients, caregivers and travel contacts. Pate called it a contained case, said surveillance has been intensified and the hospital is receiving support to strengthen infection prevention and control, and recalled that Nigeria successfully contained Ebola in 2014.
