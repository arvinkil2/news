**Latest, November 2:** The Treasury said on Monday it expects to borrow 618 billion dollars in privately held net marketable debt during the October to December 2026 quarter, assuming an end of December cash balance of 850 billion dollars. The estimate is 10 billion dollars lower than announced in August, primarily because the quarter began with a higher cash balance than had been assumed, partly offset by lower projected net cash flows. Excluding the higher opening balance, the current quarter estimate is 24 billion dollars higher than announced in August. For the January to March 2027 quarter, the Treasury expects to borrow 800 billion dollars, assuming an end of March cash balance of 1 trillion dollars. Further financing details will be released at 8:30 a.m. on Wednesday, November 4, with the quarterly refunding statement.

In the July to September quarter the Treasury borrowed 742 billion dollars and ended the quarter with a cash balance of 984 billion dollars, against an August estimate of 739 billion dollars of borrowing and an assumed end of September balance of 950 billion dollars. The department said the higher borrowing resulted primarily from the higher end of quarter cash balance, offset by higher net cash flows, and that excluding the higher closing balance, actual borrowing was 30 billion dollars lower than announced in August.

The department's economy statement for the Treasury Borrowing Advisory Committee said real GDP grew 3.4 percent at an annual rate in the third quarter on the advance estimate, after 2.2 percent in the second quarter, with private domestic final purchases up 4.9 percent and business fixed investment up 12.7 percent. Net exports subtracted 3.3 percentage points from growth as imports of capital goods rose. The statement put twelve month headline CPI inflation at 3.7 percent through September and core CPI inflation at 2.6 percent.

The decision investors are watching on Wednesday is guidance. The Treasury has for several quarters said it anticipates maintaining nominal coupon and floating rate note auction sizes for at least the next several quarters. Citigroup expects that language to give way, with reductions in 20 year and 30 year issuance beginning this month, while JPMorgan strategists expect guidance to be maintained through 2027, with auction sizes stable until August next year and increases then concentrated in two year to 10 year maturities. If sizes are unchanged, the refunding totals 125 billion dollars: 58 billion of three year notes on November 9, 42 billion of 10 year notes on November 10 and 25 billion of 30 year bonds on November 12.

Buybacks are the second decision. The August statement anticipated up to 38 billion dollars of liquidity support purchases of off the run securities over the August to October quarter, plus up to 25 billion dollars of cash management purchases at the short end. Two weeks later, after the 30 year yield touched its highest in 19 years, the Treasury raised the maximum for liquidity support operations in the 10 to 20 year and 20 to 30 year sectors from 2 billion dollars to at least 4 billion per operation, and operations since then have run with a maximum of 6 billion dollars in those sectors, according to Newsquawk. Wednesday's statement is expected to address sizes beyond November 4.

The stakes are set by the level of yields. The 10 year closed Friday at 4.93 percent and the 30 year at 5.31 percent in this site's market tables, and bills already account for about 22 percent of outstanding marketable debt, above the 20 percent long run average the Treasury Borrowing Advisory Committee has recommended. A shift toward bills would relieve long end supply pressure now at the cost of more refinancing later, which is the trade off the refunding statement will price.
