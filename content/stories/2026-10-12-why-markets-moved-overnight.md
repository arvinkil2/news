Rare earth miners fell 4.61%, the largest move in the tables, giving back part of last week's run as the mining complex turned mixed. No single catalyst in today's reporting explains the size of the drop; the only rare-earth item in today's coverage is the mining roundup's note that EU-China dialogue on Beijing's rare-earth and magnet export restrictions will continue after last week's trade deal, which eases the supply-risk premium the sector had been pricing.

Meta rose 3.7%, the largest gain among the big technology names. No clear single driver in today's reporting explains the move; today's technology coverage centered on the Senate's AI data-center report and chip-design security rather than social media.

Semiconductors fell 2.2%, extending Friday's lag in the AI complex even as the broader indexes held near flat (the S&P 500 was down 0.02% at midday and the Nasdaq 100 down 0.56%). No clear single driver in today's reporting ties directly to the drop; the sector remains the soft spot in the AI trade ahead of Wednesday's CPI print (see today's rates story on the CPI and Warsh week).

Uranium miners fell 2.15%. No clear single driver in today's reporting explains it; today's energy coverage was dominated by oil, freight and the Gulf tanker war, not nuclear fuel.

Tanker freight rose 2.11%, continuing a run that has taken it up 14.5% on the week. The driver is in today's commodities coverage: record supertanker rates, with US Gulf-to-China fixtures near $80 million a cargo, have closed the US-Asia crude arbitrage and pushed Asian refiners toward Middle Eastern and South American grades (see today's tanker arbitrage story).

Natural gas fell 2.05% with no clear single driver in today's reporting; today's LNG coverage focused on the conditional Korean interest in the Alaska LNG project and on tanker markets rather than US gas balances.

Brent crude rose 1.85% to $106.66 a barrel against Friday's close, and WTI rose 1.88% to $93.58, even though Brent dipped toward $103.50 intraday. Today's oil story explains the two-sided trade: hopes of a Russia-Ukraine energy ceasefire pulled prices down in the morning, while continued Gulf tanker attacks and Kyiv's charge that Moscow is already violating the truce kept a risk premium in the price.

The cross-market read is a narrow, commodity-led risk bid rather than a broad one: energy, freight and the Hang Seng (up 1.79%) and TSX (up 1.48%) advanced, the dollar index firmed 0.37% to 102.58, and US yields were effectively unchanged with the Treasury market closed for Columbus Day (10-year at 5.24%). Strategic minerals and the semiconductor complex sold off against that tape, leaving the inflation print on Wednesday, not today's flows, as the week's real test.
