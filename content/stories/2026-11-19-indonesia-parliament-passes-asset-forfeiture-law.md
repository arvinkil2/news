Indonesia's House of Representatives approved the asset forfeiture bill at a plenary session in Jakarta on Thursday, passing into law a measure that had spent 18 years in parliament. The session, chaired by House Speaker Puan Maharani, followed a report from Commission III chair Habiburokhman, whose committee completed its level-one deliberations with the government on Wednesday after 87 hearings since September 2025.

The law covers forfeiture of assets linked to corruption and other criminal acts, and Commission III highlighted its application to fraud and embezzlement carried out through information technology with large numbers of victims, citing bogus investment schemes and bogus stock trading. Criminal penalties for offenders remain in force alongside forfeiture, and the law sets out the duties of a body to manage seized assets, from storage and security through to sale, return or release.
