President Donald Trump concurred on Thursday with the Secretary of Commerce's section 232 finding that wind turbines and their components and subcomponents are imported in circumstances that threaten to impair the national security of the United States. The Secretary's report cited wind energy's intermittency, cybersecurity vulnerabilities in foreign-made inverters that convert turbine output for the grid, radar interference affecting air traffic control, weather forecasting and defense missions, and a decade of rising component imports while domestic capacity for products such as blades has not meaningfully increased.

The proclamation imposes no new tariffs. It directs the Secretary of Commerce and the United States Trade Representative to pursue or continue negotiations with foreign trading partners and to update the president within 180 days, and it says the president may consider alternative remedies depending on the outcome, including if no agreement is reached within that period or an agreement is not carried out. It does not modify the existing section 232 tariffs on steel, aluminum and copper, which already apply to some turbine components, and Commerce will continue monitoring imports.
