Traditional landowners in Papua New Guinea are seeking to block development of the giant Panguna copper mine, closed for almost 40 years, saying they are its true owners under a regional constitution. Beverly Bakaori, chief of the Bakoringku clan, is challenging the legality of the granting of a 25 year mining lease for the Panguna deposit to parties including India's Lloyds Metals and Energy, according to an emailed statement from Numa Numa Resources, a closely held company backing the local landowners.

Bakaori has sought an injunction to prevent Lloyds and the Autonomous Bougainville Government from progressing mining, and to hand over control of the copper and gold deposit. She represents five regional clans, as well as Panguna Newco, a venture between the traditional owners and Numa Numa that will manage the lawsuit and itself seeks to redevelop the mine.

Panguna was closed down in 1989 under the ownership of Rio Tinto after local protests over environmental damage and revenue distribution degenerated into a civil conflict that killed as many as 20,000 people. The mine's remaining reserves are estimated at 5.3 million tonnes of copper and 19.3 million ounces of gold, worth more than $130 billion at current prices. The dispute is therefore not over whether Panguna should reopen, but over who controls the reopening: the leaseholders backed by the Bougainville government, or the customary owners who shut the mine a generation ago.
