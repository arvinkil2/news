French officials on Tuesday ordered a corruption case involving former President Nicolas Sarkozy and his wife Carla Bruni to go to trial, setting an initial pre-trial hearing for April 1, 2027. The French financial prosecutor's office said Sarkozy and Bruni have been charged with receiving stolen goods, witness tampering and criminal conspiracy. Their lawyers did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

The case centres on the retraction of testimony by French-Lebanese businessman Ziad Takieddine, who had alleged that the late Libyan leader Muammar Qaddafi sent millions of euros in cash to Sarkozy's successful 2007 presidential campaign before withdrawing his account in 2020. Sarkozy was convicted last year in a related case of criminal conspiracy over attempts to procure campaign funds from Libya and received a five-year jail sentence; he was released under judicial supervision three weeks into the sentence and has appealed. He has denied the allegations.
