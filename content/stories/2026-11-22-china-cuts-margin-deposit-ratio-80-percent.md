China's Shanghai, Shenzhen and Beijing stock exchanges said on Sunday that, with approval from the China Securities Regulatory Commission, the minimum margin deposit ratio for investors buying securities on margin will fall from 100 percent to 80 percent from November 30, lifting the maximum leverage in A-share margin trading back to 1.25 times from 1 times. The exchanges said margin trading activity has fallen noticeably and overall leverage is not high, and described the cut as a countercyclical adjustment to free up margin capacity and improve market liquidity, with unsettled margin contracts also able to be recalculated under the new ratio. The move reverses the January increase to 100 percent, which was imposed to curb rapid growth in the margin balance, and Xinhua reported industry estimates that the change could in theory bring nearly 400 billion yuan in incremental funds.

**Update, November 22.** Yonhap, reporting from Shanghai in Korean, said the margin cut was announced alongside two further stabilization steps on Sunday: the Shenzhen Stock Exchange will introduce a market-maker system on the ChiNext board and has begun consulting on the detailed rules, and the Shanghai and Shenzhen exchanges announced fee-reduction measures. Yonhap, citing Bloomberg's tally, put the combined market value lost by stocks in the Hang Seng Tech, ChiNext and STAR 50 indexes since the end of June at about 1.7 trillion dollars, with the three indexes down 21.5 percent, 49.1 percent and 44.2 percent respectively from their June 30 closes.
