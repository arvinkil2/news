The European Commission said on Saturday it was postponing until January 1, 2030 adjustments to its emissions rating for certain plug-in hybrid vehicles, a move likely to be welcomed by EU automakers. The Commission had planned to update the utility factor, the official share of distance a PHEV is assumed to cover under electric power, on November 29; the adjustment was designed to reflect real-world driving, in which owners charge their vehicles less than official tests assume.

Under the proposed amendments, the current utility factors will be frozen, applying retroactively from Sunday, only for PHEVs with an electric range of at least 80 kilometres. The Commission said the temporary flexibility gives manufacturers the opportunity to invest in improving such vehicles and incentivises longer electric ranges; the utility factor will be updated regularly from 2030 on the basis of real-world PHEV performance. The proposal goes to EU member states and the European Parliament, either of which may object.
