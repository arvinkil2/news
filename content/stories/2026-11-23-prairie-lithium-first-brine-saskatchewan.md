Prairie Lithium has successfully extracted brine to surface from the production well at Pad #1 at its Prairie Lithium project in Saskatchewan, the company's first brine produced to surface from the well. A local service rig re-entered the well and completed production zones in the Duperow Formation through a perforation and swabbing programme.

The result delivers the initial brine production testing milestone set out in the company's September 2026 quarterly activities report, and completes site expansion, contouring and grading at Pad #1 ahead of the Canadian winter. The recovered brine will now be used for continued metallurgical testing.

A four-column commercial-scale direct lithium extraction unit has already been delivered to site, leaving installation and integration among the main tasks ahead. Phase 1 targets an initial 150 tonnes a year of lithium carbonate equivalent capacity as the first step of the development.

The project is supported by C$6.3 million in interest-free Canadian government financing through Prairies Economic Development Canada for Phase 1 processing equipment and balance of plant, and Prairie holds a binding offtake agreement with Hydro Lithium covering 100 percent of planned Phase 1 production. Commercial lithium production remains a separate objective beyond this testing milestone.
