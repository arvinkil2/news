European Union ambassadors agreed on Wednesday to add 145 people and entities to the bloc's Russia sanctions lists, Reuters reported, citing EU diplomats. Of the listings, 113 fall under the main regime for actions undermining Ukraine's territorial integrity and include 50 Russian banks, enablers of the shadow fleet that moves Russian oil, energy companies and additional Russian business leaders. A further 31 listings fall under the regime for hybrid activities and foreign manipulation, and one under the cyberattacks framework.

The hybrid listings respond in part to an attempted drone attack at Leipzig/Halle airport in Germany, which Berlin has blamed on Moscow, one diplomat said; Russia has repeatedly denied involvement in hybrid warfare or sabotage in NATO countries. Foreign ministers are expected to adopt the lists on Monday, after which they take effect on publication in the EU's Official Journal. Finance commissioner Maria Luís Albuquerque told the European Parliament on Thursday that the listings cover Russian oil refineries and the shadow fleet ecosystem, and that a 22nd sanctions package is being finalised.
