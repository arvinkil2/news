Goldman Sachs commodity analysts raised their Brent crude forecasts, now expecting the benchmark to average 105 dollars a barrel in December and 88 dollars a barrel in 2027, up from previous forecasts of 90 dollars and 80 dollars, OilPrice.com reported from the bank's note. The December revision is an increase of nearly 17 percent.

The bank cited heightened risks to supply security. Other banks have moved the same way: ANZ raised its Brent forecast by 10 percent last week and expects the benchmark to end 2026 at 105 dollars, while UBS sees 110 dollars at the end of the year. The upgrades come despite multiple reports that oil flows from the Persian Gulf are returning to pre war levels and at times topping them.

Goldman's Daan Struyven summed up the condition for a durable fall: for the risk premium to come off quickly, markets would probably need a diplomatic solution between Iran and the United States that participants see as sustainable. Eight months of adapting to disrupted Middle Eastern supply have materially weakened the buffers that absorbed the initial shock, ANZ's analysts wrote.

Monday's spot trade moved in the opposite direction to the forecast path. Brent fell below 110 dollars as Iran and Qatar talks entered a third day, and the site market tables print Brent at 108.18 dollars. The gap between a 105 dollar December average forecast and a spot price falling through it in a single session is the market's live argument about whether diplomacy or depleted buffers set the price into year end.
