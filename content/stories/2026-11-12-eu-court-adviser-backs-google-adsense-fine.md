Google suffered a setback on Thursday when Advocate General Kokott faulted the General Court's 2024 decision to annul a 1.49 billion euro ($1.7 billion) European Commission fine over restrictive clauses in Google's AdSense publisher contracts, saying the lower tribunal imposed excessive requirements on the scope of its examination and did not sufficiently tie its conclusion to the criteria for dominance. The Commission found in 2019 that the clauses, in place from 2006 to 2016, prevented rivals from placing search advertisements on publishers' websites; Google removed them in 2016.

The Court of Justice, which usually follows the majority of such recommendations, will rule next year. The AdSense penalty was one of six EU antitrust fines that have cost Google 10.38 billion euros over nearly two decades.
