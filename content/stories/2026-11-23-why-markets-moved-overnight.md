Nvidia fell 14.01 percent to $125.53, the largest single move in the tables, and Broadcom fell 12.19 percent to $304.41. No clear single driver for either move appears in this morning's reporting; the technology beat records the selloff's scale without attributing it to a specific announcement, and the one large technology deal of the weekend, CrowdStrike's $43.9 billion acquisition of Zscaler, was announced this morning, after Friday's close (see today's CrowdStrike story).

The semiconductor index fell 10.55 percent and the broader tech sector fell 7.27 percent, with Tesla down 7.64 percent and Meta down 5.93 percent. The Nasdaq 100 fell 5.61 percent. This morning's reporting offers no single explanation for the sector-wide move, and none is claimed here.

Uranium miners fell 6.56 percent, silver miners 5.59 percent and gold miners 5.0 percent, even as gold futures rose 3.16 percent and silver futures rose 3.67 percent on Friday (see today's gold and silver story). The miners fell with the equity market while the metals they produce rebounded, a divergence the mining beat's Friday tables make plain.

The rates market moved the other way. The US 10-year yield fell 14.0 basis points to 4.91 percent and the 30-year fell 9.8 basis points to 5.30 percent, a bid the rates beat attributes to growth fears rather than policy relief (see today's US long yields story). Brent crude fell 4.05 percent to $88.82 a barrel on surplus expectations ahead of the November 29 OPEC+ meeting (see today's Brent story).

The cross-market read is risk-off with a growth scare underneath it: equities and the miners sold off hard, haven duration was bid, and the dollar index was nearly flat, up 0.03 percent. Nothing in this morning's reporting identifies a single catalyst for the equity fall, so the honest summary is a broad de-risking whose cause is still unconfirmed, set against a rates market already trading on growth fears.
