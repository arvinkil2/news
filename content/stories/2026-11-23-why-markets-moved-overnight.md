Nvidia rose 31.8 percent to 165.45 dollars, the largest move in the tables by a wide margin, reversing most of Friday's 14.01 percent fall. Today's Nvidia story explains the move: the board reinstated Jen-Hsun Huang as president and chief executive on November 22, disclosed in an 8-K filed this morning, and the shares opened about 33 percent higher at 167.08 dollars (see today's Nvidia story).

The rest of the technology complex followed. Semiconductors rose 13.2 percent to 139.68 dollars, the tech sector rose 8.98 percent and the Nasdaq 100 rose 6.99 percent to 26,778, with the same Nvidia story reporting that the Nasdaq opened up 7.30 percent on Huang's return after last week's sharp fall in AI-related stocks. No other single driver for the sector-wide reversal appears in today's reporting.

Tesla rose 12.4 percent to 388.70 dollars and Alphabet rose 6.94 percent to 283.71 dollars. No story published today explains either move, and none is claimed here; both are reversing part of last week's falls alongside the sector.

The miners caught up with their metals. Copper miners rose 7.26 percent and rare earth miners rose 7.16 percent, while gold miners rose 6.61 percent and silver miners 6.5 percent, reversing Friday's divergence in which the miners fell with equities even as gold and silver futures rose (see today's gold and silver story). Copper itself rose 3.09 percent, gold futures rose 4.17 percent to 3,661.90 dollars and silver futures rose 5.68 percent. No story published today explains the rare earth miners' move specifically; today's Thailand story concerns a ban on exports of rare earth mining chemicals, not miner pricing, and today's BHP story is a South Australian copper expansion plan, not a stated cause of the sector's rise.

The cross-market read is a sharp risk-on reversal in equities that the rest of the board does not fully share. The S&P 500 rose 4.88 percent, but Brent crude fell another 5.67 percent to 87.32 dollars a barrel on the surplus case ahead of the November 29 OPEC+ meeting (see today's Brent story), the 10-year Treasury yield rose 3.4 basis points to 4.95 percent, and the dollar index rose 0.66 percent to 104.50. Equity risk is being repriced on one company's news; oil and rates are still trading the growth and supply picture that drove Friday.
