Puig said it will acquire 75 percent of Artessence from a holding whose owners include the fund Advent and founder Julien Sprecher, who stays on as the brands' creative director, with an agreed option to buy the remaining 25 percent within four years at a price tied to the company's performance. Puig forecasts Artessence will post net sales of 380 million to 400 million euros and EBITDA of 160 million to 170 million euros in the fiscal year ending March 31, 2027, and expects the deal, financed with debt and its own resources, to close in the second quarter of 2027 subject to regulatory approvals and a shareholder vote.

The deal follows the same majority-stake structure Puig used for Byredo and Charlotte Tilbury, and lands months after its merger talks with Estée Lauder broke down in May. Counting the previously announced 1.2 billion euro purchase of the Esteve family's 50 percent of ISDIN, Puig's announced acquisitions total 2.4 billion euros, against a market value of about 3.7 billion euros at Friday's close; the company says it still expects to stay within its leverage threshold of two times at the end of fiscal 2027.
