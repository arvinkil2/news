The Government Accountability Office's latest F-35 report, published on November 9, finds that the Department of Defense continues to face basic challenges in accounting for the parts of its most expensive weapons programme. The F-35 inventory comprises approximately 26 million parts, from engines to tires, valued at approximately 31 billion dollars and held at over 700 contractor and government managed facilities around the world. The parts are DOD owned, but DOD does not maintain complete records of them.

GAO found that records in the programme's accountable property system of record remain unreliable because they do not reflect real time changes and are inaccurate and incomplete, and that the Joint Program Office has not fully developed procedures to validate the data it enters. Physical inventory procedures did not adequately verify the existence of parts or validate their completeness. As of March 2026, the programme office did not know the status of lost F-35 parts valued at over 85 million dollars that GAO had reported on in 2023, and it had not established procedures for reviewing losses reported at government managed facilities.

The report attributes much of this to the absence of a mature property accountability programme: no programme specific procedures and training for the accountable property officers who carry the responsibility. GAO made four recommendations, covering procedures for monitoring loss submissions and trend analysis, programme specific procedures and training for property officers, and a clarified structure for the officer programme. The Department of Defense concurred with all four and cited actions it is taking.

The finding matters beyond bookkeeping. Without accurate inventory data, GAO argues, managers lack the information needed to support warfighter readiness and reliable financial reporting, and the department cannot detect the full extent of loss, fraud, waste or theft. The F-35's overall life cycle cost is estimated at more than 1.9 trillion dollars. The report was produced in connection with GAO's statutory mandate to audit the US government's consolidated financial statements, the audit the Pentagon has never passed, and the F-35 parts problem is one of the standing reasons why.
