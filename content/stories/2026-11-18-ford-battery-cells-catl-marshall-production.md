Ford has started production and shipments of battery cells at its $3 billion plant in Marshall, Michigan, using lithium iron phosphate technology licensed from Chinese battery maker CATL, CNBC reported. Cell production began last month and the cells are moving to an adjoining pack plant for use in prototypes of Ford's Fathom small electric pickup; the plant is expected to make about 40 cells a minute, or 20 gigawatt-hours a year, down from the 40 gigawatt-hours originally planned, with a ramp-up due next year ahead of the Fathom's launch in late 2027.

The plant has drawn criticism from some lawmakers and from Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy, who wrote to chief executive Jim Farley in September expressing "profound concern" about Ford's ties to Chinese companies; the White House commended Ford's domestic manufacturing a day later. Ford executive Alan Clarke said licensing from CATL, the world's largest battery maker, was the fastest route to producing affordable EVs in the United States.
