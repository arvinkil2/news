The European Union is supporting the first phase of the project that will link Greece's Dodecanese islands to the mainland electricity grid. With an estimated budget of €2.54 billion, phase 1 is supported under the Public Sector Loan Facility through a €228.9 million EU grant, the largest contribution to a single project under that facility to date, combined with a €1.53 billion loan from the European Investment Bank and a €534 million grant from the Greek Decarbonisation Fund.

Implemented by the Independent Power Transmission Operator (IPTO), phase 1 will build a high voltage direct current submarine link between Corinth on the mainland and the island of Kos, together with two AC/DC converter stations and associated transmission infrastructure. The link forms the backbone of the Dodecanese interconnection and integrates Kos into the Hellenic Electricity Transmission System. A second phase, outside the EU supported project, will add AC interconnections bringing Rhodes and Karpathos into the system.

CINEA said phase 1 alone is expected to cut carbon dioxide emissions by around 7.7 million tonnes over 25 years, roughly 309,000 tonnes a year, by phasing out polluting oil fired power plants on the islands, and will create 40 permanent jobs. Emma Toledano of the Commission's Directorate-General for Regional and Urban Policy called the project an example of Greece, the Commission and the EIB turning clean energy ambitions into reality.
