A pilot study published by the International Maritime Organization tracked 10.5 billion dollars in direct maritime climate finance flows, representing 0.14 percent of global climate finance. The report, Maritime Climate Finance Landscape, was developed under the IMO's GHG-SMART Programme with the Climate Policy Initiative and covers 2018 to 2023.

Annual financing reached 4.7 billion dollars in 2023, a compound annual growth rate of 49 percent over the study period, but the report estimates decarbonising international shipping and its shore-based infrastructure will require between 1.2 trillion and 1.6 trillion dollars of capital investment by 2050.

Public and private finance contributed comparable shares, concentrated in ports and alternative fuels. Port infrastructure received 39 percent of tracked funding and alternative fuel production and distribution 24 percent, while maritime operations and technologies received 11 percent and shipbuilding and recycling just 0.02 percent. Small island developing states and least developed countries received 68 percent of tracked maritime adaptation finance, largely for ports.

The IMO cautions that the figure is not a complete accounting: domestic public spending and private investment in ship retrofits, energy efficiency and shipbuilding are not consistently captured in the data.
