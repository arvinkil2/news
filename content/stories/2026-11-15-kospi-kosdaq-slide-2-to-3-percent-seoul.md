**Latest.** Seoul closed at the lows. The Kospi ended down 190.03 points, or 3.59 percent, at 5,100.50, after an intraday low of 5,069.83, and the Kosdaq closed down 34.01 points, or 4.86 percent, at 665.65, a third straight daily decline for both. Foreigners net sold 1.3837 trillion won of Kospi shares, a second straight day of selling, and institutions sold 459.8 billion won, while individuals net bought 1.3484 trillion won; Samsung Electronics fell 4.47 percent to 196,800 won and SK Hynix fell 7.89 percent to 1,120,000 won, and the won strengthened slightly to 1,335.7 per US dollar. Yonhap tied the close to AI investment worries spreading after the Jensen Huang affair and his dismissal as Nvidia chief executive, caution ahead of Nvidia earnings and Federal Reserve speeches this week, and a United States court decision letting a DRAM price-fixing class action against Samsung Electronics, SK Hynix and Micron proceed to trial.

South Korean stocks slid on Monday morning, with the Kospi and the Kosdaq both down between 2 and 3 percent by 10:25 a.m. in Seoul and foreign investors selling, Yonhap reported, after both indices had opened higher. At 9:25 a.m. the Kospi stood at 5,286.90, down 0.07 percent after opening up 0.28 percent at 5,305.40, and the Kosdaq was down 0.63 percent at 695.28, with foreigners net sellers of 155.8 billion won of Kospi shares at that point.

Yonhap tied the weak sentiment to the fallout from Jensen Huang's departure from Nvidia and to Friday's selloff in United States chip stocks, when the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 3.43 percent, Nvidia fell 2.01 percent and SK Hynix's United States listing fell 4.30 percent. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix were both lower in early Seoul trading.
