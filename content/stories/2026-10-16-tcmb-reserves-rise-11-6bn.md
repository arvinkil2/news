Turkey's gross international reserves rose by 11.6 billion dollars in the week to October 9, the central bank's weekly statistics showed, one of the largest single week increases this year. The build extends a run of accumulation that has defined the central bank's year.

The quality question follows every Turkish reserves headline. Gross reserves include gold, swaps with domestic banks and other borrowed elements, and analysts separate the owned portion before declaring the external position transformed. Even on the cautious reading, a weekly rise of this size reflects strong portfolio and deposit inflows rather than valuation alone.

For the lira, reserves are the credibility mechanism: the bigger the owned buffer, the less the exchange rate has to do the adjusting when the next external shock arrives, which is the difference between this cycle and Turkey's previous experiments with unorthodoxy.
