AustralianSuper said on November 24 that it intends to vote in favour of Brookfield Capital Partners' proposed $2.53 billion acquisition of Reliance Worldwide Corporation at the scheme meeting next week, in the absence of a superior proposal. The pension fund held about 18.49 percent of Reliance Worldwide's shares at the close of business on November 20, making it the company's largest shareholder, and it reserved the right to deal in the shares in the meantime.

Reliance Worldwide, an Australian plumbing supplies and water control products maker, agreed in September to a scheme implementation deed under which Brookfield and its affiliates, bidding through Aurora Bidco, would acquire all of its shares for $3.38 each in cash. Brookfield had made three bids through April and May, at $4.15, $4.25 and $4.50 per share in Australian dollar terms before the agreed scheme price was set. The bidder has written confirmation from the Foreign Investment Review Board and says it has all required regulatory approvals.

No competing proposal emerged during the go-shop period, and the board has reaffirmed its unanimous recommendation, subject to the independent expert continuing to conclude the scheme is in shareholders' best interests. Reliance Worldwide shares jumped nearly 12 percent to A$5.07, their highest level since mid-March 2025.

The vote arithmetic is the story: a scheme of arrangement needs 75 percent of votes cast, and an 18.49 percent holder declaring in advance, with no rival bidder in the field, makes failure unlikely. At the scheme price, AustralianSuper's stake would realise roughly $758 million.
