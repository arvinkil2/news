The FDA has approved Ekitna (bezuclastinib), from Cogent Biosciences, in combination with sunitinib for adults with advanced gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST) who have already received imatinib. The approval came ahead of the drug's November 30 PDUFA goal date, under priority review, and the drug is now available by prescription in the United States. It is Cogent's first approved product.

The decision rests on PEAK, a global randomised Phase 3 trial in 413 patients whose disease had progressed on, or who could not tolerate, imatinib. The Ekitna combination produced median progression-free survival of 16.5 months against 9.2 months for sunitinib alone, a hazard ratio of 0.50, and an objective response rate of 46 percent against 26 percent. Cogent said Ekitna is the first treatment to show a statistically significant progression-free survival benefit against an active comparator in this setting, and the first approval in the setting in more than 20 years. The recommended dose is bezuclastinib 600 mg once daily with sunitinib 37.5 mg once daily, and the label carries a boxed warning for liver toxicity.

Bezuclastinib is a selective tyrosine kinase inhibitor designed to inhibit the KIT D816V mutation and other KIT exon 17 mutations that drive resistance in GIST. The same drug is Cogent's candidate in systemic mastocytosis, so the GIST launch builds the commercial infrastructure, including a patient support programme, Cogent Connect, ahead of any second indication.

Cogent gave no pricing, sales outlook or revenue guidance in its launch release. The commercial question is how quickly a small, newly commercial company can convert a clear trial win in a rare cancer into uptake against entrenched later-line options.
