Natural gas rose 2.68% to $3.25 per mmBtu, the largest gain in the tables. Today's reporting explains the tight backdrop: LNG flows from the Persian Gulf remain more than 75 percent below pre-war levels, and European gas is trading at multi-year highs near 83 euros per megawatt hour with EU storage well below its seasonal average (see today's coal and LNG story and today's European gas story).

Silver miners fell 2.67%, gold miners fell 1.85% and copper miners fell 1.69%, extending Monday's broad miner weakness into a second session. Silver itself fell 1.12% to $59.47 an ounce, but gold was little changed, down 0.24% to $4,180.50, and copper rose 0.43% to $6.73 a pound, so the miner falls were again wider than the moves in the metals they produce. No story in today's file identifies a single driver for the miner selloff.

BHP fell 2.46% and Rio Tinto fell 1.26%, moving with the wider miner complex. Today's mining coverage is strategic rather than price-setting, covering Anglo American's R11.2 billion Sishen iron ore investment and Glencore's Sydney secondary listing debut (see today's Anglo story and today's Glencore story), and neither item explains the falls in BHP or Rio Tinto.

Meta rose 2.21% to $759.39, extending its gain from Monday. Meta's Superintelligence Lab released Muse Wonder, its first frontier model, on Monday, priced at $4 per million input tokens through the Meta Model API (see today's Meta story), and the shares remained the clear large-cap technology gainer at midday even as semiconductors eased a further 0.66% after the Asian chip rout.

Dry bulk freight fell 1.83%. No story in today's file explains the dry bulk move. Tanker freight also eased 0.87%, even as OPEC and non-OPEC ministers warned that shipping and insurance costs have surged to more than one-third of the value of energy cargoes (see today's OPEC shipping story).

Oil was mixed at midday, with Brent down 0.96% to $105.63 a barrel and WTI up 0.17% to $93.90. Crude remains caught between the prospect of additional Russian diesel supplies and the supply risk from attacks on Saudi Arabia and tanker strikes near Hormuz (see today's oil story).

The midday pattern is sector-specific rather than macro. The S&P 500 was down 0.06% and the Nasdaq 100 down 0.29%, the US 10-year yield eased 1.7 basis points to 5.22%, and the dollar index fell 0.33% to 102.24. What is selling is the crowded miner and chip complex, for a second day, while gas tightens on the LNG shortage. That reads as position trimming in specific trades, not a broad risk-off move, and it leaves Wednesday's CPI print as the next catalyst that could change the picture.
