Israel's election campaign has turned uglier in its final days, Arab News reported on Saturday, three days before Israelis vote on Tuesday, October 27. The deterioration sits on top of the two tracks covered in full yesterday, the West Bank closure order after the Hebron checkpoint shooting and the fight over small right wing lists near the Knesset threshold, and the vote itself is the next verifiable step.

Egypt's parliament has assured the public that Egypt is not at war with the Houthis, Egypt Independent reported on Saturday. The assurance matters because Cairo sits between the Red Sea shipping crisis, the European Parliament's call this week for a Houthi terrorist listing and a stronger Aspides mission, and its own mediation posture, and a parliament level denial is a positioning statement rather than a policy change.

In India, protesters marched to the Election Commission demanding the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar, Deutsche Welle's live coverage reported on Friday. The march puts the commission's credibility, a recurring opposition theme in Indian politics, back in the street ahead of the next state election cycle, though no institutional step followed in the reporting read.

In Australia, embattled Greens senator Jordon Steele-John has confirmed he will take leave from parliament after allegations against him were made public, The Guardian reported on Saturday. The leave removes a Greens vote from the Senate while the allegations are addressed, in a chamber where the government's margins are routinely tight.

In the United States, President Trump called for Representative Ilhan Omar's immediate resignation, saying she should resign immediately over what he called a fake name, The Hill reported, and Arab News carried the same call. The demand is rhetoric from the president against a sitting member of Congress, reported as such, with no resignation and no formal process attached to it in the reporting read.

Finally, the BBC reports on the rise of AI generated election advertising in the US midterm campaign. Read alongside today's full story on the People's Party deepfake in Spain, the pattern is now transatlantic: synthetic media is a standard campaign tool eleven days before the US vote, and the regulatory response in both countries is still catching up with the practice.
