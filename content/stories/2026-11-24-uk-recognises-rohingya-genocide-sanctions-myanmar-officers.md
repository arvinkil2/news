Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband told the House of Commons on November 23 that the United Kingdom formally acknowledges the genocide committed against the Rohingya, following the International Court of Justice judgment of November 19 that found Myanmar responsible for genocide in northern Rakhine State during the military's 2017 clearance operations. Miliband said Britain's approach is that genocide determinations follow the judgment of a competent court, and that test has now been met.

Miliband announced sanctions against four major generals and a brigadier general who oversaw troops that carried out civilian killings and repression, the expulsion of Myanmar's defence attaché ordered the previous week, and legislation in the coming months to stop the export and supply of aviation fuel to Myanmar, which he said the military uses in lethal air attacks on its own people. He also announced 5 million pounds of additional humanitarian aid and 3 million pounds for accountability and justice work, and said Britain, as Security Council penholder on Myanmar, would press for a Council session on compliance with the ruling.
