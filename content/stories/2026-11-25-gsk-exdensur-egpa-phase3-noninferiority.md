GSK announced positive headline results from OCEAN, a Phase 3 non-inferiority trial comparing twice-yearly depemokimab, sold as Exdensur, with monthly mepolizumab, sold as Nucala, in people with eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (EGPA) receiving standard of care. The trial met its primary endpoint: depemokimab was non-inferior to mepolizumab in achieving disease remission, the central treatment goal in EGPA.

OCEAN randomised 162 participants across 19 countries. Depemokimab was given as a 200 mg injection every 26 weeks, against mepolizumab 300 mg every four weeks, over a 52-week treatment period, on top of oral corticosteroids with or without immunosuppressants. GSK said the overall incidence and severity of adverse events were similar in both arms and consistent with depemokimab's existing safety profile. The company disclosed no remission rates or other effect sizes in the headline release.

EGPA is a rare, chronic vasculitis of small and medium blood vessels that can damage organs and become life-threatening. Patients are commonly held on high-dose oral corticosteroids and immunosuppressants, which often fail to maintain control and carry their own long-term risks. Nucala was the first biologic approved for EGPA, so the comparison is against GSK's own incumbent: the commercial question is whether twice-yearly dosing can shift patients from a monthly injection to two injections a year.

Depemokimab is not approved for EGPA anywhere. It is approved in the United States for severe asthma, and in the EU, Japan, China and the UK for severe asthma and chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps. GSK said full OCEAN results will be presented at an upcoming scientific congress, published in a peer-reviewed journal, and used to support regulatory filings planned for the first half of 2027.
