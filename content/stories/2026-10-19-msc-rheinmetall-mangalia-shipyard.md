Mediterranean Shipping Company has been confirmed as joint buyer, alongside Germany's Rheinmetall, of the Mangalia shipyard on Romania's Black Sea coast, in a 138 million euro purchase expected to complete within weeks. Rheinmetall won the yard at auction earlier this month; Monday's confirmation adds the Aponte family's shipping group as co-funder of the acquisition, with neither side disclosing its stake.

The partners plan to run Mangalia as a dual-use yard, dividing the site into separate production and security zones for commercial and naval work. For Rheinmetall Naval Systems the immediate workload is a Romanian naval programme worth about 920 million euros covering four vessels, two 96 metre offshore patrol vessels and two 43 metre diver support vessels. MSC's interest, reported since June 2025, runs to cruise ships, ropax vessels and tugboats, and the group is separately pursuing a potential investment in Germany's Meyer Werft.

The price tells the distress story. The yard sold for 75 percent of its 184 million euro starting valuation after three auction rounds attracted no bidders, and a court-appointed liquidator had set a liquidation value near 84 million euros. Mangalia, one of Europe's largest yards with three drydocks, was run from 2018 as a joint venture between the Romanian state and Dutch shipbuilder Damen, which held 49 percent. Damen withdrew in 2023 after a dispute over management rights, and the yard entered insolvency in June 2024.

What is new today is the ownership structure, not the sale: Europe's rearmament demand is now pulling commercial shipping capital into naval capacity, with a state naval order book underwriting the revival of a yard the market had left unsold three times.
