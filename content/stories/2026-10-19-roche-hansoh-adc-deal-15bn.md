China's Hansoh Pharmaceutical said on Monday that its units have entered a partnership and licensing deal with Roche's F. Hoffmann-La Roche covering a portfolio of antibody drug conjugate programs, potentially worth more than 15 billion dollars. Hansoh will receive an upfront payment of 400 million dollars, a first anniversary payment of 110 million dollars, and further milestone payments.

Hansoh will lead early development of the programs until global Phase Ia clinical trials are complete. Roche will then develop them worldwide, except in mainland China, Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan, where Hansoh keeps the rights to develop, register and sell them. Hansoh also has the option to take part in developing up to two programs outside those markets.

The two companies have partnered before, on colorectal cancer treatments, including Roche's earlier licence of Hansoh's CDH17 ADC. The structure is the now familiar one for China to West licensing: the Chinese originator is paid for discovery and early clinical work, the Western major funds and runs global late stage development, and the headline total depends on milestones that most programs never reach. The committed cash at signing is the 400 million dollar upfront payment.

Genetic Engineering News's fuller account, published October 20, fills in the structure and the market's verdict. The collaboration, Roche and Hansoh's second, consists of two preclinical Hansoh ADC programs plus multiple ADC programs built on Roche-identified targets using Hansoh's ADC platform, across multiple undisclosed cancer types. Counting the upfront, the anniversary payment and more than 15 billion dollars in development, regulatory and commercialisation milestones, the total exceeds 15.5 billion dollars, plus tiered royalties. Hansoh shares on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange rose nearly 10 percent over two days, from HKD 31.38 to HKD 34.38 at Tuesday's close, while Roche's two SIX-listed share classes each climbed roughly 3 percent. "Expanding our strategic collaboration with Hansoh marks an important step in advancing our ADC vision," Roche's head of corporate business development, Boris L. Zaitra, said in a statement.

The patent cliff is the strategic driver. Roche's best-selling multiple sclerosis drug Ocrevus, which generated CHF 7.01 billion in 2025, faces loss of exclusivity in 2027, and Roche has been rebuilding around ADCs licensed largely from China: it now markets Kadcyla and Polivy, which analysts expect to deliver a combined CHF 3.5 billion this year, and has added collaborations since August with DualityBio (45 million dollars upfront, more than 1 billion in milestones), Rona Therapeutics on an RNAi obesity candidate (up to 1.16 billion dollars), and others. The Hansoh package is among the largest Western-China partnerships on record, though AstraZeneca's up to 18.5 billion dollar collaboration with CSPC Pharmaceutical remains larger.
