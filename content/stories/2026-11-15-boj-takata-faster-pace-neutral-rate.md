In a speech in Tokushima on November 16, Takata said Japan's economy had shown no marked slowdown despite United States tariffs and higher energy prices, with real wages recently turning positive and producer prices up 7.1 percent on the year in October. He said the Bank should shift from encouraging a rise in underlying inflation to anchoring it, place the policy rate near the middle of the neutral rate range at an early stage, and not be bound by particular intervals or ranges anticipated in the markets.

Takata also warned that rate rises abroad could push Japan's neutral rate above the level markets expect, and that the cumulative effect of the Bank's rate hikes since 2024 is still some way off, because firms' capital ratios are high and financial conditions at home and abroad remain accommodative.
