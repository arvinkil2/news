Asian shares followed Wall Street lower on Tuesday as the United States stepped up efforts to curb China's access to advanced AI chips, with South Korea's tech-heavy KOSPI sliding 2 percent shortly after the open, Taiwan stocks falling 1.4 percent, Japan's Nikkei easing 1 percent and MSCI's broadest index of Asia-Pacific shares outside Japan last trading down 1.2 percent. In China and Hong Kong, the CSI300 fell 0.5 percent and the Hang Seng Tech Index shed roughly 2 percent, while the dollar peaked at 103.69 against a basket of currencies, its highest since April 2025, and Brent crude slid 0.9 percent to $91.62 a barrel. The session's test comes after the US close on Tuesday, when Nvidia reports earnings and investors judge whether its AI spending outlook survives the export curbs and Jensen Huang's departure.
