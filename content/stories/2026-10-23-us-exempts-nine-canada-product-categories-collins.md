Senator Susan Collins announced on Friday that nine categories of products crucial to Maine's agricultural industries will be exempted from United States tariffs on Canada, after her continued conversations with administration officials including Trade Representative Jamieson Greer and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick. The categories are corrugated paper and paperboard cartons, plastic boxes, cases and crates, plastic packing bags and sacks, refrigeration and freezing equipment, agricultural cleaning, sorting and grading machinery, filling, sealing, closing and labelling machinery, packing and wrapping machinery, non-adhesive plates, sheets, film, foil and strip, and seaweed and algae products for human consumption, inputs that Maine's blueberry, potato and apple growers rely on for harvesting and bringing produce to market.

The announcement expands exemptions Collins had already secured for pulp, road salt and cement. The Wall Street Journal had reported earlier on Friday that the administration was expected to remove the 50 percent tariffs on certain agricultural and forestry products, possibly balanced by higher levies on other Canadian goods, and the White House had called the reports speculation; the list Collins announced is narrower than that reporting suggested, centred on farm inputs and packaging rather than the produce itself.
