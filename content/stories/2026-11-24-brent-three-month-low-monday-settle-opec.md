Brent crude settled at 89.37 dollars a barrel on Monday, down 3.20 dollars or 3.46 percent, its lowest closing level since August 31, while WTI settled at 80.17 dollars, down 3.24 dollars or 3.88 percent, its lowest close since August 10. Early Tuesday trade held Brent near that low, at 88.97 dollars around 0147 GMT, down a further 0.45 percent.

The fall is a supply story, not a demand shock. Libyan supply is recovering, with the Sharara pipeline back in service and crude moving into storage at Zawiya, the National Oil Corporation chair told Reuters on Monday. The International Energy Agency said in its November report that the global market would return to surplus in the fourth quarter, with output running 1.7 million barrels a day ahead of demand and world supply at 105.4 million barrels a day in October. Deutsche Bank put the October build in observed liquids stocks at 52 million barrels.

The United States Energy Information Administration expects inventories to grow in the fourth quarter and then climb by an average of 3.5 million barrels a day across the first half of 2027, and JPMorgan sees futures in the high 70s in that period. ANZ senior commodity strategist Daniel Hynes cautioned that despite improving inventories and product availability, the market remains under severe strain, a reminder that prompt physical tightness and forward surplus are coexisting.

Everything now runs through Sunday, November 29, when OPEC+ ministers meet to set the 2027 framework, including new production baselines and the fate of a layer of cuts of roughly 2 million barrels a day that expires at the end of 2026. The seven countries making additional voluntary adjustments left December targets unchanged on November 1, a third straight monthly pause. A sub-90 dollar Brent raises the cost of letting that restraint lapse.
