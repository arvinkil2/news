Oracle has scuttled a $7 billion chip leasing contract with Tencent after United States officials approached the company to raise concerns about the deal, the Financial Times reported on Wednesday. The five-year lease, signed earlier this year and first reported in September, would have given Tencent access to as many as 100,000 advanced artificial intelligence chips in data centres across Southeast Asia, with an upfront payment of about 30 percent of the total value. It was Tencent's largest overseas chip leasing agreement.

The arrangement was not ended by a new rule. Overseas leasing of AI chips remains permitted under existing rules, according to the FT's reporting; Washington ended this particular deal by pressure rather than by law. Tencent was also pressed from the other side: officials in Guangdong province, where the company is based, visited Tencent and asked it to support domestic Chinese chips, the FT reported.

Reuters, which carried the FT's account, said it could not immediately verify the report, and that Oracle, Tencent and the White House did not respond to requests for comment. The financial impact on Oracle is unclear, since the company never disclosed the expected revenue or profit from the agreement.

The cancellation sits inside a broader tightening around Chinese access to advanced compute. On November 3 the FT reported that Nvidia had instructed cloud providers to ensure at least 51 percent of their capacity is sold to non-Chinese companies, and Bloomberg reports today that Chinese buyers of Nvidia's export-legal RTX Pro 5500 expect to receive only about 1 percent of their orders in the next four months. ByteDance and Alibaba still have chip leasing deals in place, and Oracle remains a major supplier to ByteDance in Asia, with several data centres in Malaysia.
