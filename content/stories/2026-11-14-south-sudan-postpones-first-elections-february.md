South Sudan's National Elections Commission, chaired by Gabriel Bol Deng, announced on Saturday that the country's first-ever presidential and parliamentary elections will be held on February 11, 2027 instead of December 22, 2026, and gave no reason for the postponement. Under the revised timetable, voter registration runs from November 14 to November 28 at 3,161 centres staffed by more than 22,000 people, followed by a 45-day campaign from December 27 to February 9, in a country where about 90 percent of 15 million inhabitants have no state-recognised identification, according to the World Bank.

The delay is at least the third since the vote was first expected in 2022, and it follows President Salva Kiir's dissolution of the transitional unity government in September, which analysts and watchdogs said dismantled a key power-sharing arrangement and raised the risk of renewed widespread conflict.
