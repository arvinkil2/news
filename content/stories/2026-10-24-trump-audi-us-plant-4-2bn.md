President Trump announced at a campaign rally in Perry, Georgia on Saturday that Audi will build a $4 billion assembly plant near Macon, producing 200,000 SUVs a year in what would be the German brand's first factory in the United States, Reuters reported. Audi chief executive Gernot Doellner spoke at the same event and confirmed that Audi plans to bring its biggest and most premium SUVs to production in Macon, calling it a long-term investment in the American market. Audi put the employment figure at about 4,500 jobs, with construction scheduled to begin next year and production starting in the early 2030s. Mr Trump put the investment at $4.2 billion and credited his tariffs for the decision.

The plant is expected to assemble the Q7, Q8 and Q9 SUVs, and Audi also plans to build 25,000 vehicles a year at the Scout Motors plant under construction near Columbia, South Carolina, a US official told Reuters. Audi imports all of its US market vehicles from Europe or Mexico, and Reuters reported that tariffs cost the company about 1.2 billion euros last year, while its US sales are down 16 percent this year.
