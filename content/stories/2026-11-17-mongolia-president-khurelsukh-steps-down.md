**Latest.** Mongolia's Standing Committee on State Structure voted on Wednesday to endorse President Khurelsukh Ukhnaa's request to leave office early, with 14 of the 18 members present in favour and 4 against, and backed a proposal for Speaker Sandagiin Byambatsogt to exercise presidential powers until a successor is elected if the full parliament approves. The 126-member State Great Khural is expected to decide at its plenary sitting on Thursday, November 19, and Khurelsukh remains president until it does. Prime Minister Nyam-Osoryn Uchral said on Wednesday that everyone connected to the Tavantolgoi to Gashuunsukhait railway and its offtake agreements should face scrutiny regardless of faction, and that the government would seek an independent audit of the project. No Mongolian head of state has left office voluntarily before, and an election would follow within four months of a parliamentary approval.

Mongolia's President Khurelsukh Ukhnaa announced in a livestreamed statement on Tuesday that he will resign, saying he agreed with the opposition's demand that he step aside so allegations against senior officials could be investigated, and that he was prepared to undergo investigation himself. The announcement followed a November 16 demand by the Democratic Party's parliamentary caucus, which accused senior members of the ruling Mongolian People's Party of corruption and cronyism and called for public disclosure on coal offtake agreements involving Erdenes Tavan Tolgoi, the country's largest coal mining company. Khurelsukh called the accusations against him extremely serious and unfounded, while Democratic Party chairman Tsogtgerel Odon alleged roughly $110 million was linked to corruption, including $22 million he claimed may have gone to the ruling party's secretary general.
