Mongolia's President Khurelsukh Ukhnaa announced in a livestreamed statement on Tuesday that he will resign, saying he agreed with the opposition's demand that he step aside so allegations against senior officials could be investigated, and that he was prepared to undergo investigation himself. The announcement followed a November 16 demand by the Democratic Party's parliamentary caucus, which accused senior members of the ruling Mongolian People's Party of corruption and cronyism and called for public disclosure on coal offtake agreements involving Erdenes Tavan Tolgoi, the country's largest coal mining company. Khurelsukh called the accusations against him extremely serious and unfounded, while Democratic Party chairman Tsogtgerel Odon alleged roughly $110 million was linked to corruption, including $22 million he claimed may have gone to the ruling party's secretary general.
