**Update, Monday in Asia.** Gold rebounded from its biggest weekly decline in eight months as the technology stock rout pushed traders toward haven assets, driving down US Treasury yields and the dollar, Bloomberg reported. Bullion rose as much as 2.6 percent to around 3,630 dollars an ounce, after falling about 9 percent last week to a 14-month low.

Gold completed its worst week in months on Friday. Futures settled at 3,515.40 dollars an ounce in the estate market tables, down 4.62 percent on the day and 9.54 percent on the week. Spot gold touched 3,513.54 dollars intraday, its lowest level since September 2025, and silver fell harder, down 7.33 percent on the day to 41.07 dollars and 16.24 percent on the week.

The trigger was rates, not geology. Bets on a December increase in US interest rates lifted Treasury yields and the dollar, raising the cost of holding a metal that pays no income. As technology stocks fell, bullion also became a source of cash for investors covering losses elsewhere, Saxo Bank's Ole Hansen wrote on Friday.

The fall lands on an industry whose costs are at a record. All-in sustaining costs averaged 1,930 dollars an ounce in the second quarter, up 22 percent year on year, the steepest rise since 2013, according to World Gold Council data reported by Mining.com. Sustaining capital spending reached a record 441 dollars an ounce, up 34 percent from the first quarter, and accounted for about 80 percent of the quarterly cost increase. The margin above those costs fell 16 percent from the first quarter to 2,582 dollars an ounce.

Producers are not yet cutting plans. Agnico Eagle's finance chief Jamie Porter said this week that nothing changes at 3,500 dollar gold, and nothing changes at 3,000 dollars, and the company expects capital spending above 3.5 billion dollars next year. Barrick realised 174 dollars less per ounce in the third quarter while its sustaining costs rose 45 dollars to 1,911 dollars an ounce. The VanEck Gold Miners ETF closed the week about 12 percent lower, and gold miners in the market tables fell 16.22 percent on the week.
