Colombia's government has asked X to suspend the account of former president Gustavo Petro. The Ministry of Information Technologies and Communications (MinTIC) said in a statement that it sent the request on Sunday to X's public affairs, security and rules compliance team, citing possible incitement to violence.

The request stems from a post Petro published on Saturday, November 21, in which he wrote, "I ask the people of Colombia to demand Abelardo's resignation already," accompanied by video of a November 19 speech in Cali during a national work stoppage. The ministry also pointed to the size of Petro's following and to his presence on the US Office of Foreign Assets Control list, and recalled that Colombia's Council of State ordered Petro on October 30 to remove earlier messages in which he alleged electoral fraud or attributed crimes to an official of the National Civil Registry.

Petro has rejected the request as unconstitutional censorship. The ministry's letter is a request, not an order: the account remains active and any suspension decision rests with X under its own rules.

The episode sharpens the confrontation between President Abelardo de la Espriella's government and the former president who now leads street opposition to it, and it puts a US platform in the position of arbitrating a dispute between a sitting government and its predecessor. Status: announced (request sent); no platform action reported.
