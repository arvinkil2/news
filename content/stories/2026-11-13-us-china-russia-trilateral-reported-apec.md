**Latest.** A rift over Ukraine's war strategy opened in public on Tuesday, a day before the trilateral. At the Ukraine Defence Contact Group meeting at NATO headquarters in Brussels, US Undersecretary of Defense Elbridge Colby told Ukraine's backers that deep strikes inside Russia have not been effective in driving an end to the war or blunting Russian advances, that strikes on what he called civilian infrastructure, citing the online retailer Wildberries, had probably hardened Russian resolve, and that support should focus on front-line equipment and mid-range strikes, which he called very effective. Germany and Britain rejected the criticism at the same meeting: German Defence Minister Boris Pistorius called criticising the strikes quite a stupid thing and pointed to Russia's nightly strikes on Ukrainian civilian infrastructure, and British Defence Secretary Wes Streeting said it is not for the allies to tell Ukraine how to fight its war and that deep strike capability has degraded Putin's war machine. Kyiv says the warehouses its drones targeted supplied Russian forces with military components, which Wildberries and the Kremlin deny, and Colby declined to comment on allied complaints that restrictions on the Starshield satellite system are hindering Ukraine's drone interceptions.

**Latest.** President Trump arrived in Shenzhen on Wednesday and was greeted at Shenzhen Baoan International Airport by President Xi, a welcome Chinese presidents rarely extend to a visiting leader on landing, CNBC and Al Jazeera reported. Earlier on Wednesday, Presidents Xi and Putin held talks on the APEC sidelines, their fifth meeting this year; Russian officials said the two were to discuss energy and finance, then foreign policy including Ukraine and North Korea in a smaller format, AFP reported. Xi opened the APEC summit on Wednesday with a call for free and open trade and greater cooperation on artificial intelligence, and Putin told the summit that sanctions on Russia had backfired. The trilateral meeting is set for Thursday, alongside a Trump-Xi bilateral; CNBC and Al Jazeera put the Iran war, Russia's war in Ukraine and strategic stability at the centre of the expected discussion, with expectations for concrete outcomes low and an extension of the US-China trade truce, which runs to January 10, 2027, seen as unlikely at the summit.

**Latest.** President Trump opened the door on Monday to a meeting with North Korea's Kim Jong Un during the China trip. "We may meet with Kim Jong Un also, so that'll be good," he told reporters as he left the White House, AFP reported, adding that President Xi might invite Kim when the leaders gather in Shenzhen for the APEC summit. "He may come, I don't know. That's up to president Xi," he said. The remark reverses the White House's position on Friday, when a US official said Trump was not expected to meet Kim during the trip; Trump had said in August that he expected to meet Kim later this year. The Kremlin said on Monday that separate Putin-Trump talks in China are "being worked out", which Trump did not confirm. He also said he "may" talk about the Nvidia issue with President Xi, where officials briefing on Friday had said he did not plan to raise it.

**Latest.** Beijing set out its own framing of the meeting on Monday and named no topics. Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning told the daily briefing in Beijing that the three leaders would hold in-depth exchanges on safeguarding world peace and promoting strategic stability in major-power relations, calling the meeting one of "groundbreaking significance", Reuters reported. She said it would be the first face-to-face meeting among the heads of state of China, Russia and the United States, that the three countries bear an important responsibility to cooperate in addressing global challenges, and that stronger communication among them, with differences managed, serves the interests of all three. She gave no details of the agenda, and Beijing's account names neither nuclear arms control nor Iran, the two items the White House says President Trump will raise. Speaking in Manila the same day, UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres said the United Nations is prepared to support any denuclearisation effort that may arise from the summit.

**Latest.** Moscow and Washington now describe the agenda differently. Russian presidential aide Yuri Ushakov said last week that the Shenzhen talks would continue diplomatic efforts aimed at reaching a negotiated settlement to the Ukraine war, CNBC reported, citing Russia Today; the White House has said President Trump does not plan to discuss Ukraine at the meeting and will raise nuclear weapons reduction and the US war with Iran. China specified no agenda in its confirmation. President Trump has departed Washington for a four-day trip to Asia that also includes a stop in the Philippines, ahead of the November 19 meeting.

**Latest.** Beijing confirmed the meeting on Monday. A Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson said President Xi will hold a summit in Shenzhen on November 19 with Presidents Putin and Trump, Xinhua reported, in China's first official confirmation of the trilateral meeting after the White House and Kremlin announcements. The announcement set out no agenda; Yonhap noted the Ukraine war and nuclear disarmament are mentioned as possible topics, which leaves the stated agenda resting on the White House account, under which Ukraine is not planned for discussion.

**Latest.** Moscow confirmed the meeting on Saturday. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov told the TASS news agency that it had been agreed a trilateral meeting between Presidents Putin, Xi and Trump would be held on November 19, in Shenzhen as part of the APEC summit, Anadolu Agency reported; Reuters, citing RIA, carried the same confirmation. Peskov's reported remarks set out no agenda, so the meeting's stated business still rests on Friday's White House account: nuclear weapons reduction and the US war with Iran, with Ukraine not planned for discussion.


**Latest.** Senior US officials told a press call on Friday that President Trump does not plan to raise the Nvidia matter, including the allegations against Alice Huang, with President Xi, saying the situation should not have any bearing on the trip and that the United States does not negotiate its export controls with China. On the economic side, the officials said, the Xi meeting will focus on implementation of the trade agreement. Elon Musk will accompany the president to Shenzhen, an official said, pointing to his business interests in the US-China relationship and his role as an adviser.

**Latest.** The White House confirmed the meeting on Friday and set out its agenda. The three leaders meet on Thursday, November 19, on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen, in what the Associated Press called an unprecedented gathering of the heads of the world's biggest nuclear powers. White House spokeswoman Olivia Wales told reporters the president will discuss the reduction of nuclear weapons, calling proliferation very dangerous, and a senior US official who briefed reporters said the meeting is expected to start three-way nuclear talks, with no agreements expected to be announced. Trump does not plan to discuss Ukraine at the meeting, the White House said; the US war with Iran is on the agenda. President Zelensky has expressed concern the meeting could become a platform for discussing a Ukraine deal behind Kyiv's back. The last US-Russia nuclear pact, New START, expired in February, removing caps on the two largest arsenals for the first time in more than half a century, and Trump rejected a one-year extension in favour of a new deal that includes China, whose arsenal the Pentagon estimates at more than 600 warheads against more than 5,000 each for the United States and Russia.

The New York Post reported on Friday that the leaders of the United States, China and Russia will meet in a trilateral summit on November 19, on the occasion of the APEC leaders meeting in Shenzhen on November 18 and 19, citing a White House official, according to a Yonhap News Agency dispatch from Washington. The agenda is expected to centre on nuclear disarmament, and the official was reported as saying the Iran war and North Korea could also be discussed. Yonhap noted the meeting has not been officially announced, that President Trump has repeatedly said he wants to discuss nuclear disarmament in a three-way summit, an idea President Putin first raised alongside an end to the Ukraine war, and that China does not welcome the format, so the meeting could prove formal rather than substantive if it takes place.
