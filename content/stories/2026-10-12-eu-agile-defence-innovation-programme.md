The Council of the EU formally adopted the AGILE regulation, completing the legislative path that began with the Commission's proposal in March 2026 and the provisional political agreement reached with Parliament on July 15, 2026, under the Irish Presidency. The 115 million euro programme backs 20 to 30 projects through two tracks: mission-driven development of disruptive defence products, and market delivery to move solutions into actual markets.

The programme's core pitch is speed. Grants are to be awarded within four months of a call closing, up to 100 percent of eligible costs can be covered, and firms can claim costs retroactively up to three months before a call closes. The July political deal locked in the four-month time-to-grant and added fast-track access to testing and experimentation facilities, matchmaking with established defence primes, and simplified procurement of AGILE-supported products. Eligibility is aimed at SMEs, start-ups and scale-ups, including Ukrainian and EFTA-EEA firms, with a stated goal of reducing strategic dependencies on non-associated third countries.

Executive Vice-President Henna Virkkunen said AGILE shows Europe is eager to lead in defence innovation, while Defence Commissioner Andrius Kubilius said modern warfare is won by those who innovate fast. Lead MEP Tonino Picula framed the programme as accelerating the path from innovation to deployment while safeguarding against strategic dependencies. The regulation enters into force 20 days after publication in the Official Journal, with the programme on track to become operational from early 2027.

AGILE is designed as a nimble complement to the EU's larger, slower defence industrial instruments, explicitly targeting emerging and disruptive technologies such as AI, quantum, robotics, cyber and space where small firms move faster than primes. Parliament's industry committee chair called it a potential blueprint for the European Competitiveness Fund and the next generation of defence programmes.
