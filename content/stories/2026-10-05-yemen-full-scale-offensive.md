## Latest

Yemen's Saudi-backed government forces said Monday they had secured the Bab el-Mandeb Strait from the Houthis, one day after President Rashad al-Alimi declared the start of the "Dawn of Yemen" offensive in a televised address. Maj. Gen. Majid al-Nujaili, a spokesman for the government forces, said the Giants Brigades and National Shield Forces had "succeeded in securing Bab el-Mandeb," cutting the road between Dhubab and Mocha and advancing toward the port city, according to Spain's EFE news agency. Reuters, citing military and government sources, separately reported government forces had seized much of the Dhubab area and were pushing to establish full control.

The Saudi-led coalition has formally joined the operation. Coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki al-Malki said 100 fighter jets were taking part, with 324 key targets struck; al-Nujaili put the government count at 1,122 precision strikes on Houthi artillery batteries, rocket launchers, defensive positions and command centers.

The claims are unverified and the Houthis have conceded nothing. The coalition dismissed Houthi claims of a retaliatory strike on Riyadh as "misleading," a reminder both sides are waging the information war alongside the ground one.

## Earlier

Yemen's government announced a full-scale offensive against the Houthis on Sunday, according to Al Jazeera and CNN, with backing from the Saudi-led coalition. Presidential Council chief Rashad al-Alimi framed it as a counter-offensive to retake Houthi-held territory, launched as Houthi forces cut a key supply route to the besieged city of Taiz. Loud explosions presumed to be airstrikes were heard over Sanaa, the Houthi-held capital, across the weekend.

The Houthis answered with their sharpest claim yet: a ballistic-missile attack on the Aramco oil refinery in Riyadh, carried out in retaliation for Saudi attacks on Sanaa and other provinces. Saudi Arabia has not confirmed any damage, and the claim remains unverified — but it extends a pattern that escalated last week, when the Saudi-led coalition accused the Houthis of a drone attack on a Medina power station that knocked one transformer out of service.

The geography widened on Monday: a tanker reported multiple explosions about 60 nautical miles south of Mokha, Yemen, near the Bab el-Mandeb approach, with the crew safe and authorities investigating. That is the chokepoint Saudi Arabia has leaned on as its Red Sea workaround for Hormuz. With Yemen going to a full-scale offensive and the Houthis pressing around Taiz, the workaround itself is inside the threat envelope. Fixed assets can't reroute — which is a fundamentally different risk register than the Hormuz convoy problem.
