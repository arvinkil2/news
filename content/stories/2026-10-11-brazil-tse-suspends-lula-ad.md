Brazil's Superior Electoral Court (TSE) ordered Lula's campaign to pull electoral ads that linked senator Flavio Bolsonaro to alleged dealings with Banco Master and tied his running-mate Alfredo Gaspar to a criminal investigation. TSE president Kassio Nunes Marques granted a preliminary injunction on October 10, finding the spots omitted relevant information and made criminal imputations without a minimal factual basis, in violation of the electoral law's ban on calumny and defamation.

The ads cited leaked messages pointing to a US$24 million negotiation between Flavio Bolsonaro and former Banco Master owner Daniel Vorcaro, and a statutory-rape investigation against Gaspar that the Supreme Court archived after the prosecutor's office cited lack of evidence. The court ordered the material removed from the free electoral airtime until the merits are judged. Lula faces Flavio Bolsonaro in the presidential runoff.

**Latest (Sunday, October 11):** the Meta fight escalated from a court order to a campaign accusation. Lula's campaign accused Meta of election interference on Saturday, claiming the company's platforms restricted the distribution of the president's content: Meta "prevented President Lula's profile from promoting Instagram feed posts," a post from his official account "experienced an interruption in view counts, which affected its reach," and profiles of Lula and other leftist leaders "suddenly disappeared from Instagram's search function" before last Sunday's first round, per an AFP dispatch. "The series of technical issues... raises strong suspicions that Meta's actions constitute an attempt to interfere in the Brazilian electoral process to the benefit of Flavio Bolsonaro's candidacy," the campaign said. The TSE on Saturday gave Meta 48 hours to clarify whether it reduced the reach of Lula's campaign posts and ordered it to report "profile by profile" all measures applied to Lula's official accounts and explain the metric anomalies. Meta's Brazil office denied the charges, calling them "simply false" and "not supported by the facts," telling AFP many of the reported issues did not affect only Brazil or specific accounts. The interference claim is the campaign's accusation, denied by Meta; the 48-hour court order and the denial are both reported by AFP.
