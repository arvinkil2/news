Ethiopia's Ministry of Finance formally launched an exchange offer and consent solicitation on November 25, 2026, seeking approval from creditors holding at least 75 percent of the outstanding principal of its 1 billion dollar Eurobond. Investors representing about 45 percent of the debt have already endorsed the terms, leaving the government to secure backing from holders of roughly another 300 million dollars.

Under the exchange, the existing bond is replaced by a new 880 million dollar bond, a 12 percent reduction in principal, carrying a 6.15 percent coupon, down from 6.625 percent, and maturing on July 15, 2029. The government will also settle 99.38 million dollars of unpaid interest from three missed semi-annual coupons in December 2023, June 2024 and December 2024, subject to deductions for restructuring expenses. Bondholders have until December 11 to submit instructions, and the government expects settlement on December 18; if settlement has not occurred by December 31, the offer terminates automatically.

Ethiopia has been in default since missing a coupon in December 2023, and the principal itself fell due in December 2024, leaving about 1.1 billion dollars outstanding including unpaid interest. Fitch kept the sovereign at Restricted Default in October while indicating the exchange could complete before year-end.

The terms implement the agreement in principle reached on June 29, 2026 with an ad hoc bondholder committee, which the Official Creditor Committee, co-chaired by China and France, assessed in August as compliant with the G20 Common Framework's comparability of treatment principle. Once the 75 percent threshold passes, the restructuring binds all holders, including those who decline to participate.
