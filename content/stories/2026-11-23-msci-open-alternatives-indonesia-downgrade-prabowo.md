MSCI chief executive Henry Fernandez conveyed a willingness to consider alternatives to a proposed two-step downgrade of Indonesia's stock market in a conference call of more than an hour with President Prabowo Subianto on November 10, Bloomberg News reported, citing people familiar with the matter. The call was brokered by Bridgewater Associates founder Ray Dalio, an informal adviser to Prabowo's government, and Fernandez indicated MSCI did not necessarily intend to downgrade Indonesia and would keep advising its authorities on the reforms needed to meet MSCI standards.

The reforms under discussion include removing politically affiliated individuals from the board of the Financial Services Authority, improving communication with investors, and clearer rules on concentrated shareholdings and on changes to the LQ45 index, and Prabowo pledged stronger enforcement. MSCI had threatened in October to move Indonesia directly from emerging market to standalone status over ownership transparency, suspected coordinated trading and weak enforcement; it is accepting feedback until April 30 and plans a final decision by the end of June 2027. The Jakarta Composite rose 1.59 percent to 5,228.24 on Monday and the rupiah strengthened after the report.
