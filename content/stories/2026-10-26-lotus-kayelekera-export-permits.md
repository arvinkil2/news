Lotus Resources has cleared the last major regulatory hurdle for its first uranium export from the Kayelekera mine in Malawi, securing transport and export authorisation from Namibia's National Radiation Protection Authority. That completes the principal permit package across the three countries on the route: Malawi's Atomic Energy Regulatory Agency approved the export at origin, logistics partner Alistair Group holds the Zambian transit licence, and Namibia now authorises both transit and export.

About 145,000 pounds of uranium oxide concentrate, packed in four containers, is expected to leave the mine site within a week, with road haulage mobilised and a November shipping booking secured from Walvis Bay. The cargo is destined for Orano's conversion facility in France. Routine per shipment notifications, inspections and approvals remain, but the company says the principal authorisations are in place. The containers had been packed and waiting since at least mid October, held by the single outstanding Namibian approval.

The export machinery sits alongside financing arranged earlier this month. Lotus has executed full documentation with Mercuria for a marketing platform covering up to 3.0 million pounds of U3O8 and an inventory backed prepayment facility of up to US$30 million, which lets the company draw cash against product in inventory and transit before final sale.

Operations remain the constraint behind the logistics. The Kayelekera acid plant is producing high concentration sulphuric acid at about 50 to 60 percent of design capacity, and uranium production is broadly tracking acid availability; output in August and September was about 100,000 pounds. Uranium miners were flat in the latest market tables, up 0.06 percent on the day and down 3.73 percent on the week, so the permit is a company specific catalyst rather than a sector move.
