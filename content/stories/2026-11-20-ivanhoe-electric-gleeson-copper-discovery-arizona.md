Ivanhoe Electric, backed by Robert Friedland, has discovered high-grade copper, gold and silver mineralization at its Gleeson project in southeastern Arizona and made it the company's top exploration priority. Drilling identified two mineralized zones, including 123.6 metres grading 0.91 percent copper, 0.44 grams of gold per tonne and 5.27 grams of silver per tonne from a depth of 601.7 metres, with a higher-grade core of 71.03 metres grading 1.38 percent copper, 0.52 grams of gold and 6.73 grams of silver.

A second hole returned 381.66 metres grading 0.34 percent copper, 0.29 grams of gold and 1.07 grams of silver per tonne from 587.85 metres. The upper breccia zone spans roughly 500 by 500 metres from about 600 metres below surface, and deeper drilling below 1,000 metres found intrusive-hosted, skarn and carbonate-replacement mineralization across about 800 metres north to south and 600 metres east to west. The project sits 28 miles north of the historic Bisbee mining district, which produced more than 200 million tonnes of ore over 133 years.

Ivanhoe controls about 20,000 acres of mineral rights and more than 2,000 acres of private surface rights at Gleeson. Since November 2025 it has completed 11 drill holes, with full assays for seven; two rigs are operating and 16,000 metres of drilling are targeted by year-end. Gleeson remains an early-stage discovery with no defined mineral resource or demonstrated economic viability.

The results landed in a falling copper market. Copper prices plunged about 6 percent this week to $12,471 per tonne on the London Metal Exchange, their lowest since April, as investors unwound bets on AI-driven demand and potential US tariffs on refined copper. Physical markets read tighter: the Yangshan copper import premium reached a record $360 per tonne, up from less than $100 in September, and Shanghai Futures Exchange inventories fell 18 percent over the week to 27,600 tonnes, their lowest since December 2023. Gleeson would sit alongside Ivanhoe's Santa Cruz copper project near Casa Grande, where the company plans to produce refined copper cathode domestically.
