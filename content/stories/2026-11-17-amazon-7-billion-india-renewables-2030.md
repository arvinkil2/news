Amazon has set out plans to invest $7 billion in renewable energy projects in India by 2030, building on $4 billion of renewable capacity the company says it has already contracted in the country. As part of the commitment, Amazon signed power purchase agreements with four new utility-scale wind and solar projects with a combined capacity of 934 megawatts: a 234 megawatt wind farm in Karnataka, a 200 megawatt combined solar and wind project also in Karnataka, a 300 megawatt solar project in Madhya Pradesh and a 200 megawatt solar project in Rajasthan.

The Karnataka wind farm is the largest wind project Amazon has agreed to take power from in Asia to date, the company said, without disclosing the developers or the length of the agreements. Amazon's chief sustainability officer, Kara Hurst, said the investment underlines the company's long-term commitment to bringing additional wind and solar capacity online in India. The commitment sits inside a new global goal to cut the carbon intensity of the electricity powering Amazon's operations by 65 percent by 2030 from a 2019 baseline.
