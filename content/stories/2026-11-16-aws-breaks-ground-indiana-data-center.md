Amazon Web Services has broken ground on its 7 billion dollar data center project in northwest Indiana, Data Center Dynamics reported, citing The Times of Northwest Indiana. Construction has begun on Project Rise at Wheatfield in Jasper County, where AWS plans up to nine buildings on a plot of about 304 acres sold to the company by the Northern Indiana Public Service Company, with the utility's Schahfer Generating Station about half a mile away. Ryan Companies is handling construction, which is expected to take several years.

AWS will pay 98 million dollars in taxes upfront, with 700 million dollars contributed over the next 30 years, Data Center Dynamics reported. The campus will primarily use air cooling and use water for cooling only around 2 percent of the year, during the hottest days. The Northwest Indiana Forum, which announced the project in June, said the Jasper County campus makes up 7 billion dollars of about 26 billion dollars Amazon committed to Indiana data center infrastructure across 2024 and 2025, and is expected to create about 250 permanent positions.

The Wheatfield campus sits alongside AWS facilities already operating at New Carlisle and another under development at Hobart. Data Center Dynamics put Amazon's combined investment across the New Carlisle, Hobart and Wheatfield sites at 33 billion dollars. The groundbreaking moves the Indiana programme from approvals into build at a moment when grid capacity, rather than capital, is the constraint most often cited for hyperscale projects in the US Midwest.
