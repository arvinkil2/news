## Latest

CrowdStrike said on Monday it has agreed to acquire Zscaler in an all-stock transaction that values Zscaler at about $43.9 billion. Zscaler shareholders will receive 1.135 CrowdStrike shares for each Zscaler share, an implied value of about $260.41 per share, or a premium of about 34 percent to Zscaler's last close. Existing CrowdStrike shareholders will own about 84.1 percent of the combined company, and both boards have approved the agreement unanimously.

The combined company is expected to start with more than $10 billion in combined annual recurring revenue. CrowdStrike said it expects the deal to be accretive to free cash flow per share before synergies, targets about $500 million in annualized cost synergies within 18 to 24 months of closing, and expects revenue synergies to build to about $1 billion in annualized run rate by the end of 30 months after close. It put the expansion of its addressable market at more than $185 billion in calendar 2029, and cited a path to sell its platform to about 8,000 Zscaler customers.

Zscaler confirmed the definitive agreement in its own release on Monday, published with first quarter fiscal 2027 results: revenue rose 20 percent to $949.3 million in the quarter ended October 31, 2026, and annual recurring revenue rose 21 percent to $3,882 million. Because of the transaction, Zscaler will not hold its previously scheduled results call and is withdrawing its previously issued financial guidance.

## Earlier

CrowdStrike was reported on November 20 to be in talks to acquire Zscaler, according to Reuters, citing two people familiar with the matter, with Zscaler's market value then put at about $33.5 billion and no terms disclosed. Zscaler shares rose about 17 percent in after-hours trading that day and CrowdStrike shares fell about 8 percent. Neither company confirmed the talks at the time, and the discussions were reported as capable of ending without an agreement.
