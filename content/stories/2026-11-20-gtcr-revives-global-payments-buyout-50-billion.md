## Latest

Blackstone, Warburg Pincus and Apollo are in talks to help GTCR finance its roughly $50 billion take-private acquisition of Global Payments, Reuters reported on November 24, citing three people familiar with the matter. The firms are discussing equity or preferred equity investments that would reduce GTCR's debt burden for the deal, the report said, and no individual amount for any firm was stated.

Reuters said GTCR disclosed its intention to pursue a deal in a regulatory filing on Friday, and that Global Payments had said days earlier that it had received an unsolicited cash offer of $88.00 a share from an unnamed strategic acquirer; the report does not say that acquirer is GTCR. The people cited cautioned that assembling the financing remains complex and that the discussions could still fall apart. Blackstone, Apollo and Warburg Pincus declined to comment, and GTCR and Global Payments did not immediately respond, Reuters said. GTCR holds a 16.35 percent stake and is the largest shareholder.

GTCR is exploring a takeover of Global Payments eight days after an earlier buyout pursuit of the payments processor was abandoned, Bloomberg reported on Friday, citing people familiar with the matter. The private equity firm is working with a group of other buyout firms to finance a potential transaction worth about $50 billion including debt and a premium, which would rank among the largest leveraged buyouts ever attempted. Global Payments shares traded more than 10 percent higher on Friday after the report.

No final decision to pursue an acquisition has been made, the report said. The Atlanta based company has a market value of about $22.5 billion and an enterprise value of about $41.5 billion including debt, and its shares had gained 9.8 percent this year before Friday's jump, according to the Bloomberg account carried by Investing.com. Reuters, carrying the same Bloomberg report, said GTCR and Global Payments did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

The revival reverses the outcome this site reported on November 12, when Clayton, Dubilier and Rice and GTCR dropped a joint effort after early stage talks stalled on the complexity of financing a take private of this size. This time GTCR is assembling a different financing group. GTCR is already Global Payments' largest shareholder, owning about 16 percent of its publicly traded shares, a position it acquired through its sale of 55 percent of Worldpay to Global Payments last year.
