Venezuela's National Assembly will repeal the Simon Bolivar Law, the 2024 statute that barred anyone who requested or supported foreign sanctions against the country from holding public office, Assembly President Jorge Rodriguez announced on Thursday. Rodriguez said the repeal process would begin on Tuesday and that he expected the reforms to be approved next week.

The law was passed in November 2024, months after the disputed July 2024 election in which the electoral authority declared Nicolas Maduro the winner without publishing detailed voting tallies. It imposed permanent bans from political office and asset confiscation on people who backed sanctions, and separate reporting of the statute records prison terms of 25 to 30 years and disqualifications of up to 60 years for promoting coercive measures against Venezuela.

Rodriguez said the law had responded to a very specific moment in the country's situation and that no politician had been subjected to its provisions. The decision followed the fourth round of transition talks on Monday between the interim government of Delcy Rodriguez and an opposition delegation, where the two sides had agreed to review several laws as part of what the opposition called a route map towards democracy.

The repeal does not extend to the separate Law Against Hate, which Rodriguez said would remain in force. The interim government has promised free and fair elections but has not set a date, and opposition leader Maria Corina Machado, who was barred from running in 2024 and whose ally Edmundo Gonzalez stood in her place, is widely expected to seek office if a vote is held.
