Core Scientific has expanded its hosting agreement with CoreWeave, adding further high density colocation capacity for the AI cloud company's GPU fleet and taking the total contracted value of the relationship above 1.1 billion dollars across its term. Core Scientific, which emerged from bankruptcy as a bitcoin miner and rebuilt itself as a data centre landlord, will deliver the additional capacity at its existing sites.

The timing is the story. This morning's watcher reported CoreWeave's June bonds yielding 15 percent after a sharp selloff, with analysts treating the company as an early warning for AI credit. The operating business, meanwhile, is still absorbing capacity as fast as landlords can build it, and Core Scientific's shares price the landlord's side of that trade: contracted, credit enhanced revenue against a tenant whose own credit is being repriced.

Both things can be true, and for the data centre landlords they usually are, until a tenant's financing costs change the expansion maths. The agreement's delivery schedule, and whether CoreWeave exercises its further options, is where the two stories meet.
