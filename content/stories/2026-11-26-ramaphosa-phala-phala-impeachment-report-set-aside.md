President Cyril Ramaphosa has won his legal challenge to the independent panel report that found he may have committed a serious violation of law in how he handled the theft of foreign currency at his Phala Phala farm. The Western Cape High Court on Wednesday declared the report unlawful and set it aside in its entirety, and set aside as well the steps the National Assembly had taken pursuant to it, which removes the basis for the parliamentary impeachment process that rested on the report. The rand strengthened on the ruling.

The judgment was delivered by a full bench of Judge President Nolwazi Mabindla-Boqwana and Judges Mark Sher and Ncumisa Mayosi. "The report of the panel cannot stand; the panel fundamentally misconstrued its functions, the nature of the inquiry it was to conduct, and the recommendation it was required to make to the speaker. It went beyond its lawful remit, asked itself the wrong questions and made several material errors of law," Mabindla-Boqwana said. The court did not decide whether the president was guilty of the allegations; it decided whether the panel's procedure was lawful, and found it was not.

The panel, chaired by former chief justice Sandile Ngcobo, reported in 2022 that Ramaphosa should face an impeachment inquiry over his handling of the theft of hundreds of thousands of dollars hidden in a couch at his Phala Phala game farm in Limpopo province. Parliament initially rejected those findings, but the Constitutional Court ruled on May 8 that lawmakers had erred and ordered them to establish a committee to investigate the president's conduct. Ramaphosa took the panel's report on review, and in August the High Court ordered the parliamentary committee, chaired by Makashule Gana, to pause its hearings pending the lawsuit. Gana said after Wednesday's judgment that the committee was seeking legal advice from parliament's legal service.

Ramaphosa has repeatedly denied wrongdoing, saying he sold 20 buffalo to a Sudanese businessman for 580,000 dollars and that the money was stolen from his farm while he was abroad. In 2023 the tax agency found the president and the entities that run his cattle business tax compliant, the graft ombudsman cleared him, and the central bank said it was unable to conclude whether he broke foreign exchange rules. Wednesday's ruling can be appealed, and the National Assembly could in principle open a fresh impeachment inquiry regardless of the panel report, but as the judgment stands the existing process has fallen away. Status: judgment delivered November 25, 2026; appeal possible.
