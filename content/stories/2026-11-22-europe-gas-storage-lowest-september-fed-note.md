Europe is entering the 2026-27 winter with unusually low natural gas inventories. Storage in the EU was about 72 percent full at the end of September, the lowest September fill rate since EU-wide data became available in 2011 and about 11 percentage points below last September, according to a Federal Reserve FEDS Note by Johannes Fleck published on Friday. Germany was especially low, at 58 percent full, with stocks of about 80 billion cubic metres across the EU.

The note projects inventories through June 2027 under three scenarios. In the baseline, with supply matching last year and consumption at its average of the past four winters, storage falls to about 30 percent of capacity in March. In a harsh winter, using the highest monthly consumption of those winters, stocks fall to about 16 billion cubic metres at the end of March, or 15 percent of capacity, and stay below 20 percent from February to April.

The binding risk is imports, not weather. In the import scarcity scenario, a notable decline in LNG imports leaves storage at about 6 percent of capacity in April, far below any level seen since 2011, and below 20 percent through June, so refilling for the next winter would start from almost zero. In all three scenarios, refilling for 2027-28 starts from a lower level than this year.

The structural backdrop is a market rebuilt since 2021. Russian pipeline gas has largely disappeared, replaced by Norway and LNG, and EU consumption fell from 413 billion cubic metres in 2021 to 332 billion in 2024, about 20 percent. In the first seven months of 2026 consumption was about 1 percent higher than a year earlier but still 19 percent below the same months of 2021.
