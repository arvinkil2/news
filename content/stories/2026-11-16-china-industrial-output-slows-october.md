China's industrial output grew 4.8 percent year on year in October, the National Bureau of Statistics said on Monday, slowing from 5.5 percent in September and missing the 5.3 percent median forecast in a Reuters poll of economists. Manufacturing output rose 5.4 percent, with equipment manufacturing up 10.6 percent and high technology manufacturing up 17.6 percent, while mining output rose just 0.7 percent. Output rose 0.21 percent from September, and industrial production for the first ten months was up 5.3 percent.

Consumption weakened further. Retail sales of consumer goods rose 0.4 percent year on year in October to 4.6463 trillion yuan, down from 1.1 percent growth in September, and rose 1.0 percent over the first ten months. Services retail sales grew 4.9 percent in the ten months, against 0.9 percent for goods, a split the bureau's figures show widening between services and merchandise spending.

Investment contracted more deeply. Fixed asset investment excluding rural households fell 8.1 percent year on year in the first ten months to 36.4824 trillion yuan, with property development investment down 21.6 percent, infrastructure investment down 5.1 percent and manufacturing investment down 2.9 percent. New home sales by floor area fell 13.7 percent in the ten months and by value 14.3 percent, while second hand home transactions rose 11.4 percent by area.

Trade was the offset. Goods trade rose 31.2 percent year on year in October, with exports up 28.7 percent and imports up 34.7 percent, and rose 20.0 percent over the ten months. Consumer prices rose 0.7 percent year on year in October, producer prices rose 5.7 percent with the increase widening by 0.7 percentage points from September, and the surveyed urban unemployment rate eased to 5.1 percent. The bureau said the economy remained generally stable but that strong supply and weak domestic demand remained prominent and the foundation for recovery still needed consolidating.
