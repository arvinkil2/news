The Department of Agriculture announced its intent to purchase up to 472.5 million dollars in American grown and raised agricultural commodities, to be distributed to the charitable food network and other nutrition assistance programs. The purchases will be made by the Agricultural Marketing Service under Section 32 authority, the department's standing tool for supporting producers when market conditions are tight.

The composition is the signal. Dairy is the largest line at 165.5 million dollars, about 35 percent of the total, followed by pork at 41 million dollars, almonds at 30 million dollars, lentils at 29 million dollars, rice at 21 million dollars, chickpeas at 20 million dollars and chicken at 20 million dollars, with split peas, dried fruit, raisins, citrus, hazelnuts, Pacific whiting and catfish also on the list. Secretary Brooke Rollins framed the purchase as moving American milk, cheese, lentils, almonds, rice and catfish from farms to people in need rather than to waste.

Two cautions belong with the headline. The announcement is an intent to purchase, not a completed transaction, and the department gave no timeline for when the food will be bought or delivered. The market effect therefore arrives through tenders as AMS issues them, commodity by commodity, rather than as a single 472.5 million dollar order.

The purchase lands in a harvest season when the department is also running emergency diesel relief for producers, with 31 states having taken action alongside the federal dyed diesel measure, an item carried in today's roundup. Together the two programs show the administration working both sides of the farm margin at once, output demand and fuel cost, while grain prices sit soft: corn eased 0.33 percent to 449.8 cents a bushel and soybeans rose 1.21 percent to 1,234.5 cents in the site market tables.
