**Latest (Saturday, October 10):** The unions are escalating. The socialist FGTB now has a mandate to call a general strike during the week of November 23 if the federal government exceeds the unions' red lines, and a separate public-sector walkout is scheduled for Monday, per reporting on the aftermath. The demands remain the same: maintain automatic wage indexation, avoid a VAT increase and reject annualised working time, with an alternative 21-billion-euro package funded by higher taxes on capital income and large fortunes. The Friday action also had economic fallout: about 140 flights were canceled at Brussels Airport as the strikes disrupted public transport, air travel, schools and childcare, and police reported 21 detentions, including 13 minors. Union leaders condemned the violence near Brussels Central and said those responsible were not affiliated with their movement. The pressure is timed: the five-party governing coalition must finalize its budget plan and present it to the European Commission before October 15.

Belgium's two biggest unions, the socialist ABVV and the Christian ACV, brought thousands to Brussels on Friday morning to protest the federal government's austerity plans, marching from Brussels Central station to Brussels South under the slogan "stay out of our pockets." Police said about 30,000 people attended; ABVV had estimated 50,000. On the sidelines of the main rally, groups clashed with police near Brussels Central, throwing wooden beams, glass bottles and rubbish bins, per The Belgian Times via Al Jazeera; riot police responded with tear gas, water cannon and baton charges. Police said the main union rally was peaceful and the troublemakers were not linked to it.

The anger targets a government budget that unions say threatens purchasing power, the wage indexation system and value-added tax, alongside plans for annualised working time. Some young protesters said they were inspired by France's student demonstrations and came to talk about education, telling Al Jazeera's correspondent they feel they are not being listened to. The clashes follow a tense week in Liege, where authorities banned gatherings after students clashed with police and vandalised shops, with more than 200 arrests and dozens of police injured. The ACV separately organised a protest at the governing coalition parties' headquarters in the capital. Single-sourced to Al Jazeera's reporting.
