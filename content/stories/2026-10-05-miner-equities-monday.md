Copper miners led Monday's rebound with a 3.01 percent gain, followed by lithium and battery mineral names at plus 1.55 percent. The diversified majors participated: BHP rose 1.68 percent and Rio Tinto 1.44 percent. Gold miners added 1.20 percent and silver miners 1.02 percent, uranium miners 0.51 percent, and rare earth miners 0.41 percent.

The afternoon tape sharpened the picture. Copper closed at $14,340 per tonne, its highest since May, on weak US jobs data that eased pressure on the Federal Reserve. Copper miners led the equity tape into the close: Freeport-McMoRan rose about 4 percent in pre-market trading, Teck added 5.1 percent and Vale 2.3 percent. The moves were metal-driven rather than company-specific, with the possible exception of Freeport, where the Q3 production update provided a modest fundamental anchor.

Gold steadied at $4,157 per ounce after falling 3.4 percent last week, its biggest weekly decline since June — but gold miners underperformed the bounce, with Newmont down 4.8 percent on the week. The metal-versus-miner divergence is the session's most interesting signal: if bullion holds $4,100-plus into quarterly results, the recent underperformance of gold producers looks like positioning rather than fundamentals.

The move looks like broad risk-beta rather than company-specific news — no major producer results, guidance changes or operational disruptions drove the session. Copper's underlying metal gave the copper-miner move fundamental cover, while lithium names bounced on M&A sentiment from the Lynas and Titan deals rather than on spodumene pricing. Silver held at $61.5, iron ore at $91.2, uranium futures at $89.8 per pound, and Chinese lithium carbonate at $19,161 per tonne with spodumene at $1,735 per tonne CIF.

On a weekly basis the bounce only partially repairs recent damage: gold miners remain down 5.48 percent on the week and rare earth miners down 5.18 percent, reflecting the selloff into last week's rate spike. Demand-side data offered a constructive undertone for battery metals: EU electric-vehicle sales hit a record 1.64 million units in January to August, up 45 percent year on year.
