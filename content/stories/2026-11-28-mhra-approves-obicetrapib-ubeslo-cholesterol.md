The MHRA has approved obicetrapib, brand name Ubeslo, to lower LDL cholesterol in adults with primary hypercholesterolaemia or mixed dyslipidaemia, the agency announced. The marketing authorisation was granted on November 26 to Menarini. The drug is a once daily film coated tablet, available on prescription, and can be taken on its own or alongside existing cholesterol lowering therapies.

Obicetrapib blocks the transfer of cholesteryl esters from HDL to other lipoproteins, a CETP inhibition mechanism, which helps remove LDL cholesterol from the blood. A fixed dose combination of obicetrapib with ezetimibe, brand name Evlarco, was authorised in the same round of decisions for adults who have not reached their LDL targets on ezetimibe and a maximum tolerated statin dose.

The benefit was shown in two 52 week randomised, double blind, placebo controlled studies. Obicetrapib reduced LDL cholesterol by 36 percent and 33 percent respectively against placebo, with the maximum effect seen by week four and maintained over the year of treatment.

The most common side effects, affecting up to one in ten people, include dizziness, headache, high blood pressure, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, and raised blood glucose and creatine phosphokinase levels, the MHRA said.

The authorisation gives UK prescribers a new oral option for patients whose cholesterol remains above target on statins and ezetimibe. The product information will be published on the MHRA Products website within seven days of approval. Obicetrapib was developed by NewAmsterdam Pharma and is licensed to Menarini for Europe.
