The African Export-Import Bank and the African Petroleum Producers' Organisation have formally established the Africa Energy Bank, announcing the new institution at its inaugural general meeting in Abuja, Nigeria, on October 31. The bank carries authorised capital of $5 billion and will finance projects across the energy value chain, including exploration and production, transportation, processing, refining and distribution.

The founding meeting appointed Helmy Ghazi, formerly deputy chief executive of HSBC Bank Egypt, as the bank's first president and seated an inaugural board drawn from the public and private sectors and policy institutions across the continent. The bank will be headquartered in Abuja and operate as an independent supranational institution, raising funding from governments, national oil companies, financial institutions and private investors.

The founders put Africa's energy investment needs at an estimated $200 billion. Against that figure, $5 billion of authorised capital is a catalytic balance sheet rather than a solution: the bank's role is to anchor and crowd in other lenders for projects that international banks increasingly decline on climate policy grounds, even as African governments argue hydrocarbons remain central to their energy systems.

The African Energy Chamber, which has championed the bank since 2024, said an opening phase of ten flagship projects is planned, with Nigeria, Angola and Libya among the target markets, and that financing is expected to begin in early 2027. None of that timetable appears in the founders' statement, and no first transaction has been named.

What is established is the institution itself: charter, headquarters, president and board. The test that matters, paid-in capital and a first loan book, is still ahead.
