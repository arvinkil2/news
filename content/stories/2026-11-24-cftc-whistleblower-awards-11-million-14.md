The Commodity Futures Trading Commission announced on November 23 that it is awarding more than 11 million dollars to 14 whistleblowers whose original information led to the successful enforcement action against an extensive fraudulent scheme. The agency does not disclose the specific enforcement action or the exact amount of any individual award, because the Commodity Exchange Act protects whistleblower confidentiality.

Raagnee Beri, director of the Whistleblower Office, said the awardees supported an unusually challenging investigation and lawsuit, and that with their high-quality information and assistance they distinguished themselves from dozens of other witnesses to the defendants' wrongdoing. Enforcement Division director David I. Miller said each whistleblower provided critical information that helped the CFTC stop the fraud and recover investor losses.

General counsel Tyler Badgley said the awards continue a record year for rewarding meritorious whistleblowers. Since its first award in 2014, the programme, established by Section 748 of the Dodd-Frank Act, has paid whistleblowers more than 590 million dollars in connection with enforcement actions that produced more than 5.1 billion dollars in monetary sanctions.

Eligible whistleblowers receive between 10 and 30 percent of the monetary sanctions collected. Awards are paid from the CFTC's Customer Protection Fund, which is funded entirely by sanctions paid by violators, not by money withheld from harmed customers. The release follows the commission's final rule on event contract reporting published last week and keeps the enforcement, rather than rulemaking, side of the regulator in view heading into year-end.
