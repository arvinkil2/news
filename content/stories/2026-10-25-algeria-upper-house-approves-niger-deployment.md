**Latest, October 30:** The first Algerian military convoy has reached Arlit, a uranium mining town in northwestern Niger, Reuters reported, in Algeria's first foreign troop deployment since it sent forces to support Egypt in the 1973 war. Algeria has not disclosed the size of the force or its timetable. The stated purpose remains the protection of the country's energy and economic interests, including state oil and gas firm Sonatrach's investments in Niger and the planned Trans-Saharan Gas Pipeline, which would carry Nigerian gas through Niger and Algeria to Europe, in a country whose military government is fighting insurgents linked to Al-Qaeda and Islamic State.

**Latest, October 26:** President Abdelmadjid Tebboune has ordered troops to Niger, Algeria's presidency announced on Monday, after both chambers of parliament approved the deployment on Sunday. The presidency said Niger's authorities had requested military assistance and that the decision was taken under the constitutional provisions allowing the military to operate abroad with parliamentary approval. The troops are to protect energy and economic interests shared by the two countries, and Senator Belkacem Boulghiti said Algeria was not sending its soldiers to wage war in Niger. No troop numbers or start date have been announced.

Earlier on October 26: The lower house, the People's National Assembly, ratified the deployment on Sunday evening, completing the two thirds approval in both chambers that Article 91 of the constitution requires before President Abdelmadjid Tebboune can send units abroad, Reuters reported. Senator Belkacem Boulghiti described the mission as protection for Algeria's economic and strategic interests rather than a combat mission, pointing to Sonatrach's oil interests in Niger's Kafra region and the planned Trans-Saharan Gas Pipeline carrying Nigerian gas through Niger and Algeria to Europe. Reuters noted that no deployment had officially taken place at the time of its report, and authorities have published no troop numbers or timetable. The official agency APS account placed the deployment at Madama, an old French base in northern Niger near the Libyan border, securing hydrocarbon facilities including Kafra.

Algeria's upper house, the Council of the Nation, unanimously approved the deployment of troops to neighbouring Niger in an emergency session on Sunday, and the lower house, the People's National Assembly, was expected to vote later in the evening, according to the newspaper El Khabar as reported by Al Jazeera. The Algerian constitution allows the president to send military units abroad only if both chambers approve by a two thirds majority, and if the lower house follows, it would be Algeria's first mission outside its borders since the 1973 war. The units are expected to be tasked with securing oil facilities in Niger, a step that follows a military cooperation protocol signed during Nigerien defence minister General Salifou Modi's visit to Algiers last week and Algeria's deployment of fighter jets to Niamey in late August, at the Nigerien authorities' request, to help thwart an attempted coup.
