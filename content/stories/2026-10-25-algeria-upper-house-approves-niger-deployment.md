**Latest, October 26:** The lower house, the People's National Assembly, ratified the deployment on Sunday evening, completing the two thirds approval in both chambers that Article 91 of the constitution requires before President Abdelmadjid Tebboune can send units abroad, Reuters reported. Senator Belkacem Boulghiti described the mission as protection for Algeria's economic and strategic interests rather than a combat mission, pointing to Sonatrach's oil interests in Niger's Kafra region and the planned Trans-Saharan Gas Pipeline carrying Nigerian gas through Niger and Algeria to Europe. Reuters noted that no deployment had officially taken place at the time of its report, and authorities have published no troop numbers or timetable. The official agency APS account placed the deployment at Madama, an old French base in northern Niger near the Libyan border, securing hydrocarbon facilities including Kafra.

Algeria's upper house, the Council of the Nation, unanimously approved the deployment of troops to neighbouring Niger in an emergency session on Sunday, and the lower house, the People's National Assembly, was expected to vote later in the evening, according to the newspaper El Khabar as reported by Al Jazeera. The Algerian constitution allows the president to send military units abroad only if both chambers approve by a two thirds majority, and if the lower house follows, it would be Algeria's first mission outside its borders since the 1973 war. The units are expected to be tasked with securing oil facilities in Niger, a step that follows a military cooperation protocol signed during Nigerien defence minister General Salifou Modi's visit to Algiers last week and Algeria's deployment of fighter jets to Niamey in late August, at the Nigerien authorities' request, to help thwart an attempted coup.
