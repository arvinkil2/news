Serbia's student led movement may not form a government until the new year, officials said this week, a month after student backed candidates won the October 25 parliamentary election in a landslide and ousted longtime populist leader Aleksandar Vucic. The movement has not nominated a prime minister or a cabinet, and its stance on key domestic and foreign policies remains unclear.

Jovo Bakic, who heads the Student List parliamentary faction, said a government would not be formed until the second half of December at the earliest, and that temporary financing was almost certain even if a government took office in December. Oliver Toskovic, a Student List deputy, said the movement had the backbone of a government and planned to cut the number of ministries from 25, adding that there was a chance of a government by the end of the year. Under the constitution, the Student List has until February 15 to form a government or a new parliamentary election must be called.

Parliament convened on November 17 and appointed Student List lawmaker Aleksandar Cupic as speaker; he will also serve as interim president until the presidential election on December 27. If parliament misses the December 20 cutoff for the 2027 budget, Serbia will face three months of temporary financing from January 1, which blocks new capital spending.

The delay also lands on energy. Serbia's gas supply contract with Russia's Gazprom ends on December 31, and Serbia relies on Russia for around 80 percent of its natural gas, bought at a discounted price through the TurkStream pipeline. Talks between state utility Srbijagas and Gazprom are ongoing, with no volumes or duration announced. The country's only refiner, NIS, majority owned by Gazprom Neft and Gazprom, operates under a US Treasury licence that expires on December 11, and Hungarian oil company MOL is negotiating to buy the majority stake, with nothing finalised.
