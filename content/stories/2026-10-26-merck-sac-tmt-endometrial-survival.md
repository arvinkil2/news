Sacituzumab tirumotecan, or sac-TMT, the TROP2 antibody-drug conjugate Merck is developing with China's Kelun-Biotech, reduced the risk of death by 32 percent in patients with advanced or recurrent endometrial cancer in the Phase 3 TroFuse-005 trial, according to results presented at the ESMO Congress in Madrid on Monday and published simultaneously in The Lancet. At 14 months of follow-up, median overall survival was 19 months on sac-TMT against 12.1 months on chemotherapy, the risk of progression or death was reduced by 41 percent, and the response rate was 35.1 percent against 17.3 percent, FirstWord Pharma reported from the presentation.

The trial enrolled 776 patients whose cancer had progressed after platinum based chemotherapy and anti PD-1 or anti PD-L1 immunotherapy, and compared sac-TMT with a physician's choice of doxorubicin or paclitaxel. STAT reported that Merck is running 18 global Phase 3 studies of sac-TMT, including 10 in women's cancers, to support approvals outside China, where Kelun has already won approval, and that the drug is crucial to replacing revenue lost when Keytruda loses patent protection in 2028.
