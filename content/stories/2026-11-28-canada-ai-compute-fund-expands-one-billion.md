The Government of Canada announced on November 26 that it is adding 700 million dollars to the AI Compute Access Fund through its national AI strategy, AI for All, bringing the fund's total to 1 billion dollars from the initial 300 million dollars committed under the Sovereign AI Compute Strategy. AI minister Evan Solomon announced the expansion in Toronto and opened a new call for proposals.

The new phase tilts the subsidy toward domestic capacity. The fund will cover up to 80 percent of eligible costs when a company buys compute from a provider that is owned, operated and governed in Canada, and up to 50 percent for other eligible providers. The first targeted call covers health and life sciences projects, with statements of interest due December 17, 2026 and full applications, for those invited, by January 4, 2027.

Solomon also announced more than 52 million dollars in support for 45 companies under the fund's first phase, bringing total commitments to more than 118 million dollars across 89 Canadian companies. The program is open to for-profit companies, non-profits and charities with fewer than 500 employees, for projects ending by March 31, 2030.

The expansion is one leg of a Budget 2026 commitment to bring 500 megawatts of sovereign AI compute online. For Canadian GPU cloud operators, including the domestic providers now financing clusters against government-adjacent contracts, a subsidized demand pool of this size is a material support for utilization in the fund's early years.
