**Latest.** Governor Michael Barr recapped the report's AI financing findings in a speech on Monday. On balance sheet debt at the seven largest AI firms has grown significantly in 2026 and is expected to exceed $800 billion before the year ends, he said, while the potential maximum value of their off balance sheet obligations, including data centre and compute leases, energy supply contracts and residual value guarantees, now exceeds $3 trillion. Hyperscalers and large GPU producers also hold more than $100 billion in cumulative equity investments in large language model labs and cloud start ups, a circular financing pattern in which the recipients spend the funds back on the investors' chips and compute, and Barr warned that opacity about where risk resides can exacerbate selloffs when sentiment shifts. He said he is less concerned about direct losses, given the still limited amount of debt backing the boom and high capital at the largest banks, but that high hedge fund leverage and broader risk appetite could turn a sharp reversal into rapid deleveraging and self reinforcing fire sales.

Artificial intelligence topped the Federal Reserve's survey of salient risks in its November Financial Stability Report, cited by 87 percent of the 23 market contacts surveyed in September and October, up from 50 percent in the spring survey. Respondents named three AI concerns: elevated equity valuations that could fall if earnings disappoint, cybersecurity risk from state actors or from frontier AI models themselves, and risks in complex forms of AI related financing. The next most cited risks were a correction in risk assets at 52 percent, higher long term rates at 48 percent, and cyberattacks and fiscal debt sustainability at 43 percent each, with the report also noting that hedge fund leverage reached its highest level since 2013 and stayed concentrated in the largest funds.
