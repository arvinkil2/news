Kazakhstan has joined Iraq in asking OPEC+ to raise its estimate of the country's crude production capacity, according to an OilPrice.com report citing Reuters, four days before the group meets on November 29 to set output policy for 2027. The review, OPEC's first of its kind, assesses how much oil each member can produce sustainably, with US consultancy DeGolyer and MacNaughton assessing every member except Russia, Iran and Venezuela, and its findings will help set production targets for next year.

Kazakhstan's OPEC+ quota for November and December is 1.628 million barrels per day, while the International Energy Agency estimated its October crude output at 1.76 million barrels per day, about 8 percent above target. Iraq disputes a preliminary finding putting its capacity at 4.9 million barrels per day against a quota of 4.431 million, wants recognition of around 6 million barrels per day, and has warned it may leave OPEC if its quota is not raised, the report said.
