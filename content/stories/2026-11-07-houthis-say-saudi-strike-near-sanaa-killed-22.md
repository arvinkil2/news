**Latest.** The Saudi-led coalition said on Monday that it had intensively struck and destroyed Houthi ballistic missile production facilities, Yonhap reported, citing a statement by coalition spokesman Turki al-Malki posted on X. The targets the coalition named were a factory assembling and producing ballistic missile parts, a chemical plant mixing and producing missile fuel, including solid fuel, and an electronics workshop that assembled electronic systems for ballistic missiles. Yonhap gave no location for the facilities and no casualties, and no independent source has confirmed the claim. The announcement followed a Houthi claim the previous day to have attacked Riyadh International Airport, an Aramco crude oil storage tank and a financial centre, an Aramco facility in Abqaiq and Saudi military gathering points in Al Jawf and Hajjah provinces with ballistic and cruise missiles; that claim is likewise the Houthis' own and uncorroborated.

Saudi Arabia's Energy Ministry confirmed on Sunday that an administrative building belonging to Saudi Aramco in Riyadh was subjected to an attempted attack, and that falling shrapnel caused limited damage to the building, with no injuries or fatalities, El Pais and Reuters reported, citing the ministry's statement via the Saudi Press Agency. A Reuters witness saw blown-out windows across several floors of the Aramco Tower in the King Abdullah Financial District. The district has been closed for almost a month and its employees have been working remotely, El Pais reported. The confirmation settles the mechanism the earlier witness reports left open: the building was damaged by debris from the interceptions, not by a direct hit. Reuters also reported that Saudi authorities said they were investigating the airstrikes near Sanaa that the Houthi health ministry says killed at least 33 people.

Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District was targeted on Sunday and Saudi Aramco's tower there was damaged, witnesses told AFP, in reporting carried by ERT News and Clarin; one witness said it was certain the Aramco tower in the district was targeted, and two others confirmed they saw damage to the building. Yonhap, citing Reuters and AFP witnesses and sources, likewise reported a strike on a building in the district and damage to the Aramco tower, and said it has not been confirmed whether the building was directly hit by a missile or damaged by debris falling from a projectile intercepted in the air. Saudi authorities had not responded to questions on whether there were casualties or on the exact scale of the damage, Yonhap reported, and none of the reports carried casualties in Riyadh.

The coalition later said it had intercepted and destroyed two cruise missiles launched toward Riyadh and the southern region, Al Jazeera and ERT News reported. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree claimed the Houthis had carried out six operations targeting Aramco oil facilities, Riyadh airport and Saudi military positions on both sides of the border, Al Jazeera reported; that is the Houthis' own claim and no independent source has corroborated it.

Earlier on Sunday, Saudi air defences intercepted and destroyed two ballistic missiles fired toward Riyadh, coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki Al-Maliki announced, Arab News reported. Saudi Civil Defence issued warning alerts for Riyadh City and Najran Province on Sunday morning and lifted them shortly afterwards, and the report gave no damage or casualties from the interceptions. The coalition put its tally of ballistic and cruise missiles and drones intercepted or destroyed across the kingdom this month at at least 15. The attribution of the missiles to the Houthis is the coalition's; the report carried no Houthi claim and no independent source has verified the launches.

The Houthi health ministry raised the death toll from the Saudi-led coalition bombing of al-Shariya village near Sanaa to 33, with 39 people wounded, Deutsche Welle reported, citing AFP and Reuters. Health ministry spokesman Anees Alasbahi, posting on X, gave an almost definitive breakdown of 12 men, eight women and 13 children among the dead; the Houthi-run Saba agency had earlier reported 27 dead and said aircraft bombed emergency teams twice while they were recovering victims from the rubble. Saudi Arabia gave no statement on al-Shariya in the report.

Separately, the Saudi-led coalition said its air defences intercepted and destroyed a Houthi ballistic missile launched toward Saudi Arabia's Eastern Province, ERT News reported, citing Reuters. Saudi Civil Defence had issued warning alerts for the city of Dammam and for the al-Ahsa and Abqaiq areas. The Eastern Province holds the kingdom's main oil infrastructure; this interception is separate from the Riyadh-bound missile the coalition reported on Saturday, which is covered below.

The Houthi health ministry said on Saturday that a Saudi airstrike on al-Shariya village near Sanaa killed 22 people and wounded 30, with spokesman Anis al-Asbahi saying most of the victims were women and children, according to the Houthi-run Saba news agency as reported by Yonhap. No independent source has verified the strike and Saudi Arabia gave no immediate response. The Saudi-led coalition said separately on Saturday that it had intercepted and destroyed a Houthi missile fired toward Riyadh, and that an alert it had issued for the southern Najran region had been lifted.
