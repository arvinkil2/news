**Latest.** President Donald Trump confirmed the line himself at a White House press conference on Friday, held after his Asia tour. Asked whether he would ease semiconductor export controls following his summit with Chinese President Xi Jinping, Trump said the United States is discussing a very comprehensive agreement with China, that it wants to export only older chips, and that China will make its own chips if the United States does not sell them. Yonhap reported the remarks in its updated comprehensive account of the press conference; the agreement was described as under discussion, not concluded.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said US policy allowing Nvidia H200 sales to China remains unchanged. The H200 is the threshold level of chips that can be sold to China and that continues as planned, he said, adding that he would not anticipate any change in the policy. The remarks, in a CNBC Squawk Box interview on Friday, follow Nvidia's statement that it had stopped selling H200s to China.

Bessent also said former Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang was not mentioned in the administration's latest talks with China, answering "No" when asked whether Huang had come up. Asked about enforcement against chip diversion to China, he said the proper chip restrictions are in place and remote access has been curtailed.
