European Union wheat exports are surging ahead of their usual pace as war disruption to Russian and Ukrainian shipments reshapes global trade. Kpler estimates cumulative EU soft wheat exports since the start of the 2026/27 season in July at around 16.5 million metric tons, 45 percent above the year-ago level and the highest in at least five years.

Official EU data tell a smaller story, showing exports of 10.5 million tons, just 1 percent ahead of last season, but those figures lack data for several countries, including France and Bulgaria. Attacks in the Black Sea have forced Moscow and Kyiv onto alternative export routes with much less capacity, pushing demand first to Romania and Bulgaria and increasingly to France. France has covered demand from buyers more often supplied by Russia, such as Sudan and Saudi Arabia, while Argentina and Australia have filled more demand from Asia. "The wheat trade map is changing," Rabobank analyst Andrick Payen said.

FranceAgriMer this week raised its forecast for French soft wheat exports outside the EU this season by 11 percent to 7.2 million tons. Chicago wheat in the estate market tables closed Friday up 2.38 percent at 592 cents a bushel, with corn up 2.22 percent and soybeans up 1.1 percent.

Traders expect the pace to fade. The EU may have shipped around 60 percent of its potential full-year exports, including durum, by the end of November and 70 percent by the end of December, CM Navigator analyst Donatas Jankauskas said, and newly harvested Argentine and Australian wheat will compete for the same buyers. A shipping truce in the Black Sea would cut the other way, releasing cheaper Russian and Ukrainian volumes.
