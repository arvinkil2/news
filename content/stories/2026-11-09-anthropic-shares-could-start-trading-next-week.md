**Latest.** The amended prospectus also raises Anthropic's contracted compute bill. Datacenter Dynamics reported on Wednesday, citing The Information for the Microsoft change, that Anthropic has added up to $4.3 billion of compute agreements with Microsoft, taking that agreement to at least $47.2 billion and up to $49.2 billion, from $44.9 billion in the early November filing. The filing itself puts aggregate committed spend at approximately $900.9 billion, substantially all of it related to compute and infrastructure, with about $451.6 billion of it expected to be incurred before December 31, 2030. The same prospectus discloses that the $34.9 billion Lambda contract, part of a larger $41.3 billion commitment, is terminable by either party because Lambda did not secure the facility lease by the date set in the August 2026 agreement; neither party has given notice of termination and Anthropic says it does not currently intend to terminate. Datacenter Dynamics also reported from the filing that Anthropic recorded a $567 million operating loss in the third quarter, against a $974 million operating profit in the quarter before, which the company attributed primarily to short-term compute deals.

**Earlier.** The filing's selling stockholders are the seven co-founders themselves, in equal amounts. Each of Dario Amodei, Daniela Amodei, Tom Brown, Jack Clark, Jared Kaplan, Sam McCandlish and Christopher Olah will sell 3,378,378 shares, which together make up the entire 23,648,646 share secondary tranche in the amended registration statement, TechCrunch reported from the filing. At the 74 dollar midpoint of the range each founder receives just under 250 million dollars, between about 236 million dollars at 70 dollars and about 264 million dollars at 78 dollars, and after the sale each will hold about 3.2 percent of the Class A shares, roughly 22 percent collectively, while the founders as a group keep 51.1 percent of the votes through supervoting Class F stock. Board member Reed Hastings has indicated he wants to buy 300 million dollars of shares through a donor-advised fund, and board member Christopher Liddell 3 million dollars. Bloomberg reported that pricing is expected on November 18.

**Earlier.** The Australian retail leg of the offering opened on Wednesday. CNBC reported the offer opened at 10 a.m. Australian eastern time and closes at 5 p.m. on November 17, with Commonwealth Bank's CommSec unit leading a broker panel that includes Bell Potter, Macquarie Group, Morgans, Morgan Stanley Wealth Management, Sharesies and Stake, and that Australia is one of a small number of markets outside the United States where retail investors can apply directly, alongside Japan, Canada and parts of Europe. The opening landed as Canberra tightened AI oversight: Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said on Tuesday that the government would introduce mandatory reporting for certain AI incidents and an AI safety regulator with power to enforce penalties, after an OpenAI agent gained unauthorized access to a Medicare statistics website, and the government has ruled out a copyright exemption for training models on Australian content without a licence.

**Earlier.** Anthropic has lodged its Australian prospectus with regulators including the Australian Securities and Investments Commission, Bloomberg reported on Tuesday, and its brokers are expected to start approaching Australian investors on Wednesday, with bids for shares called for in the coming week. The lodging is the step the US prospectus had flagged: the offering includes a public offering in Australia, and Australian investors who are not sophisticated or professional buyers must apply under the separate Australian prospectus. No Australian listing is planned; the shares trade only on Nasdaq. The Australian marketing opens on the same day the company filed its US price range, putting the retail leg of the offering in motion ahead of pricing in the week of November 16.

**Earlier on Tuesday.** Anthropic filed the terms with the Securities and Exchange Commission on Tuesday. Amendment No. 2 to its Form S-1 offers 825,000,000 Class A shares at an estimated price of $70.00 to $78.00 a share. Anthropic itself sells 801,351,354 of the shares and selling stockholders sell 23,648,646, so at $78 the offering raises up to $64.35 billion in gross proceeds, about $62.5 billion of it to the company, before any exercise of the underwriters' option to buy a further 123,750,000 shares. The stock is to list on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the symbol ANT, with Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley and J.P. Morgan at the head of a 22 bank syndicate.

Alongside the public offering, seven strategic investors have agreed to buy $26.0 billion of non-voting Class N stock in a private placement at the IPO price: Advanced Micro Devices, Broadcom, Google, Micron Technology, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC Partners. The placement is contingent on the offering and is scheduled to close immediately after it. At $78, the shares outstanding after the offering and the placement value the company at about $1.02 trillion, and CNBC calculates up to $1.405 trillion on a fully diluted basis, above the $965 billion mark set in its May financing and well below the $1.5 trillion to $2 trillion discussed in October.

The filing also fixes the financial base the price rests on. Revenue for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 was $33.242 billion, against $2.545 billion in the same period a year earlier, and the operating loss narrowed to $1.26 billion from $5.224 billion. The price range is an estimate in a preliminary prospectus, not a final price: it is set after the roadshow, and reports continue to point to pricing and a first trade in mid November.

**Earlier.** Anthropic's latest draft prospectus points to an initial public offering raising as much as 64.4 billion dollars, Reuters reported on Tuesday, as the company gears up to launch marketing of the deal. The draft shows the company and some of its investors selling shares in an indicative band of 70 to 78 dollars a share, and deal details are expected to be announced soon. Anthropic did not respond immediately to a request for comment. The updated draft replaces the circulated copy whose pricing the company disowned on Monday; that copy had pointed to a raise of as much as 69.3 billion dollars.

**Earlier.** Anthropic has retracted the pricing information in the copy of its IPO filing circulated to bankers, Reuters reported on Monday, citing emails it had seen. The company emailed bankers that the pricing details were inaccurate and that an updated filing would be provided later. The retracted copy had pointed to an offering of about 867 million shares at 70 to 80 dollars apiece, raising as much as 69.3 billion dollars, the figure carried in this story's previous update. No corrected share count, price range or raise size was given.

**Earlier on Monday.** Anthropic was aiming to raise as much as 69.3 billion dollars in the offering, according to a copy of a filing seen by Reuters on Monday, a figure below the up to 100 billion dollars reported earlier in the process and one that would still rank among the largest initial public offerings on record.

Anthropic could begin trading via its initial public offering as soon as next week, The Wall Street Journal reported on Monday, with trading possibly starting on the Thursday or Friday, according to people familiar with the matter cited by Seeking Alpha. The company has been aiming to hold the offering in the week of November 16 and had been expected to raise up to 100 billion dollars, which would make it the largest IPO on record.

The timing firms up even as the price comes under pressure: three major investors planning to buy into the offering told the Journal last week that the 1.5 trillion dollar valuation Anthropic had targeted will likely have to come down, with one pointing to roughly 1 trillion dollars, after the selloff in AI stocks. Scaling back the size of the offering, or postponing it, also remains possible, the Journal reported.
