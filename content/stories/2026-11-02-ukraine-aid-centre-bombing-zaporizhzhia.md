**Latest, November 2:** Ukraine's emergency services agency put the death toll from Monday's bombing of Komyshuvakha at 26, up from the 24 reported earlier in the day, with about 20 people still considered missing in the rubble of collapsed buildings as responders searched by flashlight after dark, local officials said. President Volodymyr Zelensky said 10 guided bombs targeted an area that included the school building used to distribute humanitarian aid and a nearby shelter. Rescue work was again hindered by Russian drones attacking the bombed area, according to Mykhailo Semikin, a military official in Zaporizhzhia, and Ukrainian authorities reported separate drone attacks elsewhere that injured at least 17 people.

The strikes landed on the day a limited ceasefire expired in the neighbouring Kherson region. The pause, which began on October 27 to allow humanitarian aid and evacuation missions into the Russian occupied city of Oleshky, was described by the Associated Press as the most significant truce between Kyiv and Moscow since the full scale invasion began. As it ended, Ukraine's general staff said Russia's military had used the pause to bolster its drone defences and replenish ammunition supplies. The Russian military had not publicly commented on the Komyshuvakha strike.

Russian bombs hit an aid distribution centre in the village of Komyshuvakha in Ukraine's southern Zaporizhzhia region on Monday, killing at least 24 people, according to Ukrainian officials. President Volodymyr Zelensky said the first strike hit the local school, which was used for humanitarian needs, and a second hit the place where people were sheltering, while regional head Mykhailo Semikin said at least nine 3,000-kilogram aerial bombs hit the village, five people were injured and rescue work was repeatedly halted by Russian drone attacks. Ukraine's state emergencies service said the dead included two rescuers and four police officers, and the Russian military had not publicly commented.
