**Latest, November 3:** The death toll at Komyshuvakha rose to at least 33 on Tuesday, and President Volodymyr Zelensky said more than 50 people could still be trapped under the rubble of the collapsed shelter, calling the strike one of the largest mass killings Russia has committed in the war. Russia's defence ministry commented for the first time, saying the attack had targeted Ukrainian military units and killed 25 servicemen; the BBC's Russian defence correspondent reported no credible evidence that the site was being used by the Ukrainian military, and the UN human rights monitoring mission said the aid distribution at the school had been highly publicised and the attack appeared to violate the most fundamental principles of international humanitarian law. The BBC put the civilian death toll from Russian strikes across Ukraine over the past 24 hours at at least 38, including two people killed in a separate strike on Dnipro, where a missile destroyed a hospice and nearly 50 terminally ill patients were transferred elsewhere, and five killed in the Donetsk region; a hospital strike in Kharkiv wounded 73 people, most of them elderly patients, according to Zelensky, and strikes on energy infrastructure in nine regions cut off Kyiv's water supply entirely and suspended the capital's metro network on Tuesday.

**Earlier, November 3:** The death toll rose to 29 on Tuesday as rescue teams kept clearing collapsed buildings in Komyshuvakha, regional head Mykhailo Semikin said, with 48 people still unaccounted for and at least five injured. Matthias Schmale, the United Nations humanitarian coordinator for Ukraine, condemned the strikes in a statement issued through the UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs, saying the first attack hit residents who had come to the local school to receive aid and that subsequent strikes at the same location killed two rescuers and four police staff who had responded. Zaporizhzhia region declared November 3 a day of mourning. The Russian military had still not publicly commented.

**Latest, November 2:** Ukraine's emergency services agency put the death toll from Monday's bombing of Komyshuvakha at 26, up from the 24 reported earlier in the day, with about 20 people still considered missing in the rubble of collapsed buildings as responders searched by flashlight after dark, local officials said. President Volodymyr Zelensky said 10 guided bombs targeted an area that included the school building used to distribute humanitarian aid and a nearby shelter. Rescue work was again hindered by Russian drones attacking the bombed area, according to Mykhailo Semikin, a military official in Zaporizhzhia, and Ukrainian authorities reported separate drone attacks elsewhere that injured at least 17 people.

The strikes landed on the day a limited ceasefire expired in the neighbouring Kherson region. The pause, which began on October 27 to allow humanitarian aid and evacuation missions into the Russian occupied city of Oleshky, was described by the Associated Press as the most significant truce between Kyiv and Moscow since the full scale invasion began. As it ended, Ukraine's general staff said Russia's military had used the pause to bolster its drone defences and replenish ammunition supplies. The Russian military had not publicly commented on the Komyshuvakha strike.

Russian bombs hit an aid distribution centre in the village of Komyshuvakha in Ukraine's southern Zaporizhzhia region on Monday, killing at least 24 people, according to Ukrainian officials. President Volodymyr Zelensky said the first strike hit the local school, which was used for humanitarian needs, and a second hit the place where people were sheltering, while regional head Mykhailo Semikin said at least nine 3,000-kilogram aerial bombs hit the village, five people were injured and rescue work was repeatedly halted by Russian drone attacks. Ukraine's state emergencies service said the dead included two rescuers and four police officers, and the Russian military had not publicly commented.
