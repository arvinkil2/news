Wheat rose 3.96 percent to 610.2 cents a bushel, the largest gain in the tables. No story in today's edition explains the wheat rise.

Microsoft fell 3.08 percent to 502.01 dollars. No story in today's edition directly explains Microsoft's fall; the closest reporting is today's FCC transceiver story, which names Microsoft among the firms lobbying against a proposed ban on new Chinese optical transceiver imports, and today's technology roundup, which reports that the United States has extended chip sale restrictions to more Asian countries.

Dry bulk freight rose 2.70 percent to 12.94 dollars, though it remains down 4.64 percent on the week. No story in today's edition explains the dry bulk move; today's commodities roundup covers Southern California port imports passing one million TEU in October, not freight prices.

The Nikkei 225 fell 2.42 percent to 59,662 points. No story in today's edition explains the Nikkei fall.

The DAX fell 2.08 percent to 26,045.4 points. Today's Deutsche Bank story reports the bank's shares fell 5.2 percent in early Frankfurt trade after reports based on leaked internal documents, and today's European Commission autumn forecast story reports euro area inflation lifted to 3.2 percent this year and 3.1 percent next, with the return to the ECB's 2 percent target delayed to 2028.

The tech sector fell 1.78 percent. Today's reporting on the sector is dominated by export controls: the technology roundup's chip restriction extension, the FCC transceiver ban story, the Nvidia board's new national security and export control committee, and the US departments' statement on the Megaspeed criminal probe.

The FTSE 100 fell 1.76 percent to 10,689.9 points. No story in today's edition directly explains the FTSE fall; today's UK story reports unemployment fell to 4.6 percent and regular pay growth slowed to 3.5 percent, figures that fill in the UK rate picture but do not account for the index move on their own.

The STOXX 600 fell 1.75 percent to 650.7 points. The move sits alongside the same two European stories cited for the DAX: Deutsche Bank's 5.2 percent fall in Frankfurt and the Commission forecast of higher inflation for longer.

The cross-market read at midday is risk-off in equities, narrow elsewhere. The S&P 500 fell 1.05 percent, the Nasdaq 100 fell 1.17 percent and the Russell 2000 fell 1.47 percent, while Treasury yields eased only a little, the five-year down 3.8 basis points to 4.90 percent and the ten-year down 2.4 basis points to 5.10 percent, and the dollar index was nearly flat, down 0.12 percent to 103.43. Brent crude rose 1.06 percent to 93.42 dollars a barrel, a move that fits today's Bahrain story, in which Bahrain intercepted Iranian drones aimed at a US radar system in the third Iranian strike in five days, while gold futures rose 0.37 percent and silver futures rose 1.45 percent, with gold miners up 1.65 percent and silver miners up 1.55 percent against the broader equity fall.
