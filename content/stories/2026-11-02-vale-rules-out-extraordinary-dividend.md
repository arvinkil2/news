Vale has poured cold water on hopes of an extraordinary dividend this year. Speaking on the company's third quarter results call, chief financial officer Marcelo Bacci said a special payout is unlikely, not because cash flow is weak, but because geopolitical uncertainty in next year's outlook argues for retaining more cash and running with lower debt. He added that the shares look inexpensive and that buybacks are very attractive, with any capital generated in coming months likely to be balanced more toward repurchases.

The stance follows a softer quarter. Pro forma EBITDA fell 20 percent year on year to 3.5 billion dollars, hit by lower realised iron ore prices, higher fuel costs and a stronger Brazilian real. Free cash flow was 2.2 billion dollars, helped by working capital release and hedging gains, and capital expenditure of 1.25 billion dollars is tracking toward the lower end of the 5.4 billion to 5.7 billion dollar full year guidance range.

The balance sheet is not the constraint. Expanded net debt ended the quarter at 16.4 billion dollars, down about 200 million dollars and comfortably inside the 10 billion to 20 billion dollar target range, and it fell even after 1.7 billion dollars of dividends and interest on capital were paid in September. Currency and oil hedges have generated more than 700 million dollars of cash benefits so far in 2026, according to management.

Chief executive Gustavo Pimenta gave the market context for the caution. He said about 10 percent of global seaborne iron ore supply, roughly 160 million tonnes, is effectively losing money at current prices, which is the level at which marginal output starts to go offline and a soft floor forms under prices if demand holds. Vale itself is positioned to meet the low end of its annual iron ore production guidance and the upper half of guidance for copper and nickel.

For shareholders, the message is a change of instrument, not a change of generosity. Special dividends were the signal of peak cycle cash in the iron ore sector; buybacks at a price management calls cheap, funded while debt still falls, is the mid cycle version, and it leaves Vale with the cash buffer it wants if 2027 opens as uncertain as this quarter closed.
