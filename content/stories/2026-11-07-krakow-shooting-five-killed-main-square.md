Latest: The suspected gunman died in hospital early on Monday, Malopolska Policja announced on X, two days after officers shot him to stop the attack. The Press Association reported that a British man was among the six people shot dead in the square and another Briton was among the injured, that the wounded foreign nationals came from the UK, Portugal, Russia, Germany and Austria, and that police have launched a blood appeal for senior constable Jakub Kulik, the officer fighting for his life after confronting the attacker. Reuters described the gunman on Monday as a 24 year old from Ostrowiec Swietokrzyski; authorities had described him as 25 over the weekend.

Poland's National Prosecutor's Office said on Sunday it had launched an investigation into the Krakow attack as a terrorist offence, covering the killing of six people using a firearm and the attempted murder of many others, including police officers. Its spokesperson Daniel Prokopowicz said it was highly likely that the killings in Krakow and in Ostrowiec Swietokrzyski, about 150 kilometres northeast of Krakow, where the three bodies were found at the suspect's home, were carried out by the same person. The traditional hourly trumpet call from St Mary's church on the square was suspended for 12 hours to commemorate the dead, the first time that has happened since 1939, the Polish Press Agency reported.

Poland began two days of national mourning on Sunday for the victims of the Krakow main square shooting, Reuters reported, with the square and surrounding streets still closed, flags lowered at the town hall and residents placing candles at several locations. Authorities now put the injured at 17, including the two police officers and the gunman, who remains in critical condition in hospital, and police issued an urgent call for blood donations for one officer who is fighting for his life. Police and a local official said the three bodies found at the shooter's home were his parents and his sister. Warsaw police said security had been strengthened in crowded tourist areas, and Prime Minister Donald Tusk wrote on X that the two officers who stopped the gunman are true heroes who likely saved many lives.

Earlier: Three dead people were found at the perpetrator's home, in addition to the at least six people killed in the main square, NRK reported, citing Polish police for the square toll and Prime Minister Donald Tusk for the statement that the dead in the residence are probably family members of the shooter. That brings the total number of dead to at least nine. This story earlier carried the bodies at the home without a count, because the Press Association report did not state one; the count of three, first carried by Russian wires citing Polsat, is now corroborated by NRK's report.

Earlier: Prime Minister Donald Tusk said at a news conference that drone footage appears to show dead bodies inside the home of the suspect in the Krakow shooting, and that the victims appear to be close members of the suspected gunman's family, the Press Association reported. Authorities have not identified the alleged attacker, but said he is a 25 year old Polish man who holds ultranationalistic views; Interior Minister Marcin Kierwinski said the suspect was fascinated with far right ideologies and held very radical views. Defence Minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz called the shooting an act of terror on the people of Krakow. Of the 16 people injured, 13 were taken to hospital with serious injuries, and one of them was a foreign citizen, the Press Association said.

Earlier: Interior Minister Marcin Kierwinski said 16 people were hospitalised following the shooting, among them five non-Polish nationals, and that one foreign national was among the six killed, The Guardian's live blog reported. Police had earlier put the injured at 15; the minister gave no breakdown reconciling the two figures.

Earlier: Two police officers were shot while detaining the attacker, and one of them, shot three times, is in a very critical condition and undergoing surgery, the BBC reported, citing a Polish government minister. The six people killed were four women and two men. Prime Minister Donald Tusk was travelling to Krakow and was expected to address the country later on Saturday, and the US ambassador to Poland, Tom Rose, condemned the shooting.

Earlier: The death toll rose to at least six, with 15 people injured, in updated Polish police figures reported by Deutsche Welle with AP and Reuters. The gunman, reportedly a Polish citizen who acted alone, aimed at random people as he drove by the main square, police said, and was shot by officers and taken to hospital.

Earlier: The gunman acted alone, Krakow police spokesperson Katarzyna Cislo told TVN24, in remarks carried by the Press Association. She said the perpetrator was wearing military-style fatigues and used a military-type long gun, and that police found many other weapons, as well as ammunition, in his car. Police officers are among the wounded, and a tram driver reported his vehicle was shot at by a man in a moving car near the square, shattering the window of his cabin, the Krakow municipal transport company said. President Karol Nawrocki said his thoughts were with the victims, the wounded and their families.

Polish police said in their first account that five people were killed and more than 10 injured on Saturday when shots were fired at random people from a car that had entered Krakow's main market square, with the first reports reaching police at about 3 p.m. local time. Police said officers shot the perpetrator, who was being taken to hospital, while France 24 reported the shooter was caught while trying to escape. Prime Minister Donald Tusk wrote on X that police are investigating the shooting and that he is being briefed by the interior ministry, and no motive has been stated.
