Chinese lithium carbonate futures slid about 10 percent on Monday, Bloomberg reported, after the European Union moved to cap hybrid-vehicle imports at 350,000 a year under a four-year safeguard measure. The most-active contract on the Guangzhou Futures Exchange fell 11.8 percent to 104,740 yuan per metric ton, its steepest one-day drop since March and a near 10-month low, according to Finimize's Monday write-up. The selling spread from futures into major producers' shares, with Tianqi Lithium and Ganfeng Lithium named among those caught in the downdraft.

The trigger was Friday's EU-China political deal, reached during Trade Commissioner Maros Sefcovic's Beijing mission, to limit hybrid imports, a number Brussels then wrote into a safeguard measure effective as early as December 1, rising 6 percent a year over four years. EU officials estimate Chinese hybrid exports to the bloc could reach 800,000 units in 2026, so the cap would cut them by more than half. Traders' logic, as Finimize puts it, is about the model mix: hybrids carry far smaller batteries than full electric vehicles, so weaker growth in EU-bound electrified exports translates quickly into less lithium per vehicle.

The move arrived with sentiment already shaky. Prices have roughly halved from their prior peak as investors question demand strength a few years out, and intermittent supply chatter, including talk of a possible restart at CATL's Jianxiawo mine, added selling pressure. The irony for miners is sharp: the same EU deal that steadies Brussels' auto-trade politics (and that continues dialogue on rare-earth and magnet export restrictions) is an immediate demand-side shock for lithium. For producers like Tianqi and Ganfeng, the episode is a reminder that lithium valuations now hinge on demand-model revisions as much as on any supply headline.

Update, October 15: The selloff extended this week. Bloomberg reporting carried by OilPrice put the most active Chinese lithium contract down about 14 percent since the start of the week, with the contract briefly dipping below 100,000 yuan a tonne. Goldman Sachs analysts cited in the reporting now see the Chinese and global markets moving from deficit to near balance in the fourth quarter, then into surplus over the following two years. The lithium and battery miner basket fell 1.88 percent in the October 15 market table.
