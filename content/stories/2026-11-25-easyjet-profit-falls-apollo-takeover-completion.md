easyJet reported its final full-year results as a listed company on November 25, and they show why the board recommended a sale. Headline pre-tax profit fell 89 percent to 71 million pounds in the year to September 30, from 665 million pounds a year earlier, missing the analyst consensus of 87.7 million pounds. Reported pre-tax profit fell 96 percent to 24 million pounds. Group revenue rose 6.2 percent to 10.74 billion pounds.

The damage came from the Middle East conflict. Fuel costs rose 11 percent, an increase of 244 million pounds, to almost 2.5 billion pounds, and demand weakened for a prolonged period after the conflict began. The airline business recorded a pre-tax loss of 195 million pounds, a 610 million pound deterioration on the prior year, partly offset by easyJet holidays, where pre-tax profit rose to 266 million pounds on revenue up 19 percent to 1.7 billion pounds. The group carried 96.4 million passengers, up 3 percent, and is not recommending a dividend.

The recommended cash acquisition by Eagle Bidco, a company indirectly owned by funds managed by Apollo Global Management affiliates, values easyJet at 5.7 billion pounds at 715 pence per share, with an unlisted share alternative. Shareholders approved the deal on November 11, and the company said it remains on track to complete in the first quarter of 2027, subject to remaining regulatory clearances. easyJet gave no financial guidance for the coming year because of the pending acquisition.

For Apollo, the results frame the underwriting question plainly: the holidays arm is compounding profitably while the airline absorbs fuel and demand shocks it cannot hedge away, with only about 69 percent of first-half 2027 fuel needs hedged. The takeover price was agreed in August, before these numbers; nothing in them reopens the terms.
