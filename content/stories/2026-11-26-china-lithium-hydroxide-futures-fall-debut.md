Lithium hydroxide futures fell on their debut on the Guangzhou Futures Exchange on Thursday, with the most-active June 2027 contract dropping 6.56 percent soon after launch to 70,080 yuan a ton, from a listing benchmark of 75,000 yuan a ton set for all six contracts, which cover delivery from June to November 2027.

The new contract gives miners, refiners and battery makers a hedge for hydroxide, the input used in many high-nickel batteries, alongside the exchange's existing lithium carbonate futures. Everbright Futures said hydroxide's supply and demand balance looks weaker than carbonate's, with carbonate averaging about 10,000 yuan a ton more than hydroxide this year, and softer battery demand and the European Union's temporary limits on hybrid vehicle imports from China weighed on sentiment.
