A Cairo court has convicted Lina Attalah, the editor-in-chief of the independent news outlet Mada Masr, of managing an unlicensed website, and ordered the site shut down. Attalah was briefly detained in the courtroom and released after the outlet paid a fine of 3 million Egyptian pounds, about 53,500 dollars, her lawyer Hassan al-Azhari confirmed to AFP, and the hearing also surfaced a 2024 in absentia sentence of a year in prison and a separate 20,000 pound fine, which her defence has appealed, with a retrial hearing set for November 11.

Mada Masr says it applied for a licence three years ago and received no reply. Hossam Bahgat of the Egyptian Initiative for Personal Rights, who attended the hearing, said the authorities were moving from harassing independent media to wiping it out completely, and PEN International said this was the sixth case brought against Mada Masr in six years and the first referred to trial.
