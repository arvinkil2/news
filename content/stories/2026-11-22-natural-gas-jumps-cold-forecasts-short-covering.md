December natural gas closed up 14.3 cents, or 4.38 percent, at 3.411 dollars per million British thermal units on Friday, reversing Thursday's 3.08 percent fall and reaching a nine-month high for the prompt-month contract. The January contract, next in line for the prompt slot, rose 6.7 cents to 3.748 dollars, the level shown in the estate market tables, where the front contract series gained 14.69 percent on the day and 16.04 percent on the week.

Forecasts for colder US weather next week sparked short covering. The National Weather Service expects a cooldown across the Midwest and Northeast through November 28, which would lift heating demand just as the December contract heads into its November 25 expiry, a day before the Thanksgiving holiday.

The move was amplified by positioning. Funds held a near-record net short of about 235,983 contracts in the week ended November 10, the largest in almost seven years, so a cold forecast forced rapid buying back of those bets.

Fundamentals alone did not justify alarm. The EIA's weekly report showed storage rose 20 billion cubic feet in the week ended November 13, above the five-year average build of 15 billion, leaving inventories 1.6 percent below a year earlier but 2.1 percent above the five-year seasonal average. Lower-48 dry gas production was 112.7 billion cubic feet a day on Friday, up 2.6 percent year on year, and the US gas rig count rose by one to 128, Baker Hughes reported.
