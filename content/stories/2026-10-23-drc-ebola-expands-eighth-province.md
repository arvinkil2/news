**Latest, October 26:** A nurse responding to an Ebola alert was killed by protesters on Saturday in Bafwasende, a rural town in Tshopo province, and a second health worker was severely injured and remains in hospital, UN News reported. Julien Harneis, the UN's senior Ebola response coordinator, condemned the attack and called for a thorough investigation, and WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said health workers must be able to work in dignity and safety. UN News, citing recent WHO figures, put the outbreak at more than 9,760 cases and some 4,727 deaths in the DRC since it was declared on May 15, the deadliest in the country's history, across eight provinces. The same report said the Nigerian patient and her husband, both Swiss citizens, were medically evacuated to Switzerland on Monday morning, leaving Nigeria with no Ebola cases, with 52 contacts still monitored there.

**Latest, October 25:** Uganda ordered citizens returning after travel to DR Congo to quarantine for 21 days and to pay for their own quarantine, Health Minister Chris Baryomunsi announced in Kampala on Sunday, with stronger checks at Entebbe airport and land crossings and contact forms for all incoming travellers, Al Jazeera reported from AFP and Reuters. Uganda says it has no active infections, its own outbreak having ended in July, and Baryomunsi disputed the Swiss authorities' conclusion that a woman whose infection Nigeria confirmed on October 20 had caught Ebola during a September visit to Uganda, saying relatives who had visited DR Congo had met her near Kampala. WHO said the information it had received did not show the woman herself had travelled to DR Congo, and Nigerian and Ugandan officials are investigating her movements.

Latest: The first clinical trial of an Ebola vaccine in this outbreak began on Friday in Bunia, the capital of Ituri province, WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said. The first participants were vaccinated with Ervebo, the vaccine licensed against the Zaire species of Ebola, under the Tumaini trial, a single Phase 2/3 framework that will test the safety, immune response and efficacy of several vaccines against the Bundibugyo species, for which no vaccine is approved. The trial uses ring vaccination, immunising high-risk contacts of confirmed cases, and will include pregnant women and adolescents aged 12 and older, groups often excluded from clinical trials. Two Bundibugyo-specific candidates, one developed by the University of Oxford with the Serum Institute of India and one by Moderna using mRNA technology, are expected to join it. CEPI, which is funding and supporting the DRC-led study, said it began as the country faced its largest recorded Bundibugyo outbreak, with 9,552 confirmed cases and 4,602 deaths as of October 21; AFP reports the outbreak has since passed 9,700 cases. No efficacy results have been announced, and Ervebo's effectiveness against Bundibugyo in humans has not been established.

Kenya has recorded a second Ebola death, the health ministry said late on Friday. The patient was a 65-year-old woman who developed symptoms during quarantine. She was a close relative and a direct contact of the Kenyan man who travelled from Congo to Nairobi and died at a Nairobi hospital on October 5, the country's first identified case. The ministry said the death brings the number of deaths to two among the nine confirmed cases in Kenya, that the remaining seven patients continue to receive care in two isolation facilities, and that there was no evidence of community spread in the country. Kenyan authorities have traced 135 contacts, all of whom were placed in quarantine. Reuters described the outbreak, which started in the Democratic Republic of Congo and has now been reported in four African countries, as the widest geographic spread of any Ebola outbreak on the continent to date, and noted that the United States State Department advised Americans on Monday to reconsider travel to Kenya because of health risks.

The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo reached an eighth province this week, as Nord Ubangi, which borders the Central African Republic, confirmed infections amid armed violence that continues to uproot thousands of people. The outbreak, declared in mid-May and caused by Bundibugyo virus, had led to more than 4,600 deaths and a total of 9,552 confirmed infections as of October 21, and around six in ten deaths have occurred outside health centres, a sign that community transmission is still outrunning the response.

The outbreak is now regional. Nigeria is the fifth country this year, after the DRC, France, Uganda and Kenya, to confirm Bundibugyo virus disease. Abuja's first case arrived from Uganda, developed symptoms on October 7 and was hospitalised 48 hours later, and investigators still do not know where she was exposed. More than 40 close contacts have been identified and genetic sequencing is under way to confirm the strain matches the one circulating in the DRC. Kenya has reported nine cases including one death, of a Kenyan national who had worked in the DRC and flown back to Nairobi via Entebbe, and its health ministry says all 135 traced contacts of that patient have been accounted for. Uganda previously recorded two deaths and 20 confirmed cases before declaring the threat over at the end of August.

Inside the DRC, the hardest-hit areas remain the least accessible. WHO reported 15 deaths per day last week in the rural commune of Mangina in Beni territory, North Kivu, where an emergency team negotiated access two weeks ago after initial community resistance. Until Friday, vaccination research rested on the Ervebo vaccine, designed for the Zaire species, with no data on whether it cross-protects against Bundibugyo; the Tumaini trial is designed to produce that evidence.

Ebola does not wait, WHO's Africa regional director Mohamed Janabi said, adding that the virus is no longer confined to one place and is a regional concern. The response test for the coming weeks is whether contact tracing in Kenya and Nigeria holds the imported cases to single chains, and whether access in North Kivu improves enough to cut the share of deaths occurring outside health centres.
