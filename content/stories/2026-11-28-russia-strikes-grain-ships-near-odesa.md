Russia attacked two foreign cargo ships carrying more than 100,000 metric tons of grain overnight near Ukraine's Black Sea port of Odesa, Ukrainian Prime Minister Sergiy Koretskyi said on Thursday, in the latest strike on the corridor that carries Ukrainian farm exports. Koretskyi, posting on Telegram, did not say where the grain originated or where the vessels had loaded.

Shipping press reports identified the vessels as the bulk carriers Gladiator and Virtuous Striker, sister Supramax ships managed by the Greek firm Enterprises Shipping and Trading. About 50,000 tonnes of corn were aboard the Gladiator, which was damaged in its superstructure and hull above the waterline, while the Virtuous Striker, carrying wheat, took a hit on deck without major damage, Marine Insight reported. There were no human casualties.

Russia's Ministry of Defence gave a different account, claiming it had targeted a dry cargo ship southeast of Odesa that was allegedly carrying military equipment and dual use goods. The two accounts cannot both describe the same cargoes, and neither has been independently verified at the vessel level; what is not in dispute is that strikes on merchant shipping in the corridor are continuing.

The commercial effect runs through freight and insurance rather than through the two cargoes themselves. Ukraine has exported 7.6 million tons of grain so far in the 2026/27 July to June season, down 35 percent from 11.5 million tons in the same period a year earlier, as shipowners avoid Odesa area ports and Kyiv reroutes exports through Danube river ports and rail. Every further hit validates the war risk premiums that are doing that rerouting.
