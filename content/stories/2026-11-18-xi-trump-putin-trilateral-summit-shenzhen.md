**Latest.** The three leaders issued a joint declaration after the meeting, a four point statement Yonhap, citing China's Xinhua agency, called a US-China-Russia leaders' peace declaration. The declaration says the three will work together for world peace and the common welfare of humanity, will hold dialogue on reducing nuclear risk and preventing nuclear proliferation and will urge all nuclear weapon states to uphold the arms control and non-proliferation system, will cooperate on climate change, food and energy security, public health and artificial intelligence security, and will work to expand trade, investment and economic cooperation. The leaders also agreed to meet regularly, including in China next year. In the same declaration, Reuters reported via Xinhua, the three committed to working together to expand the supply of refined oil and reaffirmed the importance of stable and reliable energy flows for the global economy, and reiterated that the misuse of the International Criminal Court's jurisdiction must be avoided and that the use or threat of force must be avoided in international relations. No volumes, dates or other figures accompanied the oil commitment, and no US or Russian official text of the declaration had been published in the coverage read in this run.

The formal trilateral meeting of Xi, Trump and Putin began in Shenzhen on Thursday, the first meeting of its kind between the leaders of China, the United States and Russia. Opening the session at Qilin Villa, Xi called it groundbreaking in significance and said the world is at peace when great powers live in harmony, and that the three countries should work together to address global challenges. Putin said the three have shared interests in Ukraine, referring to his meetings over the past 24 hours with Xi and Trump, and, as RIA Novosti summarised his remarks, thanked Xi for organising the talks and proposed discussing global and regional ties and energy security. Trump said the three countries are uniquely positioned to discuss nuclear weapons, that past leaders too often avoided talking to leaders they disagreed with, and that the three have great responsibilities to solve problems that affect the entire planet. No statement on Taiwan or Iran from the trilateral session itself, and no specific nuclear proposal, had been reported as the session opened, and no agreement or joint statement had been announced. Shortly before the session, US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff were reported leaving Shenzhen for Berlin to meet President Zelenskyy and European leaders.

Earlier on Thursday, Trump and Xi held their bilateral meeting at Qilin Villa, on the sidelines of the APEC summit. Trump said the two share a strong consensus that oil transport through the Strait of Hormuz should take place, a remark Yonhap read as emphasising that Washington and Beijing agree on the need to normalise shipping through the strait while the Iran war disrupts it, and he asked Xi to do more for American farmers, against the background of US pressure for larger Chinese purchases of soybeans and other farm goods. Trump also called Xi his friend and said the two leaders have a very special relationship. The Hormuz and farm remarks come from Reuters reporting carried by Yonhap; no Chinese or US official reaction was quoted. Earlier, Trump and Putin met one on one in Shenzhen, settling the question the two capitals had disputed a day earlier. Trump told Putin that a lot of wonderful things could come out of the meeting and, in a separate White House video, pointed at him and called him his friend. Ahead of the meetings, Trump told Fox News he would welcome an agreement with China and Russia to cut nuclear arsenals, put the US stockpile at about 4,000 warheads against 3,800 for Russia and 400 to 500 for China, and said he would accept inspections in all three countries. Opening the APEC leaders' meeting, Xi called for lower trade and investment barriers and announced 20 million dollars to support practical cooperation in the region, the same pledge carried in the wire queue.

Presidents Xi Jinping, Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin exchanged greetings in Shenzhen on Wednesday and gathered with other Asia-Pacific leaders for a welcome dinner and group photo, ahead of a formal trilateral summit on Thursday that will be the first meeting of its kind between the leaders of China, the United States and Russia. Xi personally welcomed Trump on his arrival in the city, an honour AFP described as the first time in decades a Chinese leader has greeted a foreign leader at the airport, and Xi and Putin also held their own talks, their fifth meeting this year.

The summit's agenda was still unclear on Wednesday, but its scope widened during the day. Washington had signalled that the Ukraine war was not expected to be addressed in the three-way meeting, before Trump said that it would. According to Washington, the talks are also expected to cover the Iran war and nuclear disarmament, following the expiry in February of the last US-Russia treaty limiting atomic weapons. Beijing has long held that Washington and Moscow should cut their far larger arsenals before it joins talks on its own smaller stockpile.

Whether the summit includes a separate one-on-one meeting between Trump and Putin is disputed. Axios, citing a US official, reported that Trump will hold a one-on-one meeting with Putin on Thursday as part of a renewed push for a breakthrough on ending the war in Ukraine. Moscow, according to Russian state media cited by AFP, said full-scale talks solely between the two are unlikely during the visit. Putin struck a defiant tone at the APEC gathering on Wednesday, saying the Western sanctions imposed over his 2022 invasion of Ukraine had "failed" and calling for "unity rather than division, and dialogue rather than ultimatums."

The prospect of the three leaders deciding Ukraine's future without Kyiv or Europe at the table drew pointed reactions. Ukraine's foreign minister, Andriy Sybiga, said in Kyiv that he hoped Xi and Trump would put "pressure on Russia to end the war." Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney, also in Shenzhen for the APEC summit, said sanctions on Russia would "continue to increase as long as this war goes on."
