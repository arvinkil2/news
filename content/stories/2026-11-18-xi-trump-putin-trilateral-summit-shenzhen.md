**Latest.** Trump and Putin met one on one in Shenzhen on Thursday, on the sidelines of the APEC summit, settling the question the two capitals had disputed a day earlier. Trump told Putin that a lot of wonderful things could come out of the meeting and, in a separate White House video, pointed at him and called him his friend. The formal trilateral session with Xi was still scheduled for later on Thursday, and no agreement or joint statement had been reported by midday Shenzhen time. Ahead of the meetings, Trump told Fox News he would welcome an agreement with China and Russia to cut nuclear arsenals, put the US stockpile at about 4,000 warheads against 3,800 for Russia and 400 to 500 for China, and said he would accept inspections in all three countries. Opening the APEC leaders' meeting, Xi called for lower trade and investment barriers and announced 20 million dollars to support practical cooperation in the region, the same pledge carried in the wire queue.

Presidents Xi Jinping, Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin exchanged greetings in Shenzhen on Wednesday and gathered with other Asia-Pacific leaders for a welcome dinner and group photo, ahead of a formal trilateral summit on Thursday that will be the first meeting of its kind between the leaders of China, the United States and Russia. Xi personally welcomed Trump on his arrival in the city, an honour AFP described as the first time in decades a Chinese leader has greeted a foreign leader at the airport, and Xi and Putin also held their own talks, their fifth meeting this year.

The summit's agenda was still unclear on Wednesday, but its scope widened during the day. Washington had signalled that the Ukraine war was not expected to be addressed in the three-way meeting, before Trump said that it would. According to Washington, the talks are also expected to cover the Iran war and nuclear disarmament, following the expiry in February of the last US-Russia treaty limiting atomic weapons. Beijing has long held that Washington and Moscow should cut their far larger arsenals before it joins talks on its own smaller stockpile.

Whether the summit includes a separate one-on-one meeting between Trump and Putin is disputed. Axios, citing a US official, reported that Trump will hold a one-on-one meeting with Putin on Thursday as part of a renewed push for a breakthrough on ending the war in Ukraine. Moscow, according to Russian state media cited by AFP, said full-scale talks solely between the two are unlikely during the visit. Putin struck a defiant tone at the APEC gathering on Wednesday, saying the Western sanctions imposed over his 2022 invasion of Ukraine had "failed" and calling for "unity rather than division, and dialogue rather than ultimatums."

The prospect of the three leaders deciding Ukraine's future without Kyiv or Europe at the table drew pointed reactions. Ukraine's foreign minister, Andriy Sybiga, said in Kyiv that he hoped Xi and Trump would put "pressure on Russia to end the war." Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney, also in Shenzhen for the APEC summit, said sanctions on Russia would "continue to increase as long as this war goes on."
