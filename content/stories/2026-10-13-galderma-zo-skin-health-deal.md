Galderma, the Swiss skincare group behind Cetaphil and Dysport, in which L'Oreal holds a 20 percent stake, has agreed to acquire ZO Skin Health for around $1.5 billion, Reuters reported, citing two people familiar with the matter who said an announcement could come in the coming days. ZO, founded by dermatologist Zein Obagi in 2007 and sold through physicians and skincare professionals, has been owned by Blackstone's tactical opportunities fund since 2020, and Reuters reported last month that Blackstone was exploring a sale with Citigroup and Raymond James advising.

Neither Galderma nor ZO responded to requests for comment outside regular business hours, and Blackstone declined to comment, so the terms remain unconfirmed until an announcement is made.

Update, October 16: Galderma announced on October 14 that it has signed an agreement to acquire 100 percent of ZO Skin Health for an enterprise value of 1.5 billion dollars in an all-cash transaction, from shareholders including funds managed by Blackstone Tactical Opportunities and the Obagi family. The announcement confirms the agreement first reported here on October 13 from Reuters sourcing, and this file is updated in place rather than duplicated.

ZO recorded net sales of approximately 330 million dollars in the last twelve months as of June 2026, which puts the enterprise value at roughly 4.5 times sales, a calculated figure. Its more than 40 products are sold through over 16,500 physician, medical spa and online accounts in more than 40 countries. Galderma said the deal is expected to be core EPS neutral in the first full year after closing, slightly dilutive to its EBITDA margin in 2027, and to close in the fourth quarter of 2026 or early 2027 subject to regulatory approvals. Galderma will increase commercial investment in ZO in its first year of ownership.
