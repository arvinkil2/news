On October 2–3, Russia struck Kyiv's Northern and Southern bridges over the Dnipro — the Southern Bridge remains fully closed, disrupting some 500,000 daily left-bank commuters — and hit Kharkiv and Dnipro, killing 2 and injuring around 40. Russia's Foreign Ministry warned diplomats and foreigners to leave Kyiv. The New York Times assesses Moscow is planning a three-stage campaign against Ukrainian nuclear substations, hydro, and water infrastructure ahead of winter.

Ukraine's answer reached deep into Russia. Overnight October 1–2, Ukrainian forces struck the Transneft tank farm at Samara — Europe's largest — and a Volgograd refinery and chemical complex, and confirmed the first combat use of its domestically built FP-7 ballistic missile.

At Valdai on October 2, Putin said that if Kaliningrad were attacked, "the issue of using all weapons in our country's arsenal will inevitably and unavoidably emerge" — including nuclear. Russian embassies in London, Dublin, and Brussels simultaneously claimed NATO is preparing an air and naval blockade of the exclave; that claim rests on Russian-embassy assertion alone. Rhetoric on this file has preceded physical escalation before, which is why the warning matters more than the blockade claim.
