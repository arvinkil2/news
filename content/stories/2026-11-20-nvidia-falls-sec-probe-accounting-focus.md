**Latest.** President Donald Trump on Friday asked Nvidia to reinstate Jensen Huang as chief executive, telling a press conference he would love to see Huang back and that Nvidia should give him a second chance. Trump called Huang one of the brightest minds in the world and a good man who made some bad decisions, and said he would like Huang to offer a sincere apology and return. Huang resigned last week after revelations about his relationship with an alleged Chinese spy said to have facilitated the transfer of sensitive chips to Beijing. Trump also said he had discussed Huang with Chinese President Xi Jinping at their summit earlier this week and that Xi praised Huang as brilliant, which contradicts Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, who said hours earlier that Trump had not raised Huang with Xi at the Shenzhen meeting. Neither Nvidia nor Huang is reported to have responded.

Nvidia shares fell 11.5 percent on Friday after Reuters reported that enforcement staff at the US Securities and Exchange Commission are examining potential accounting issues at the chipmaker, citing two people briefed on the matter. The stock had already fallen about 7 percent before the report appeared, amid a broad technology selloff, and its loss widened by roughly a further 4.5 percentage points afterward, although the extent to which the report drove the additional selling was unclear.

The accounting angle is a new development in a federal inquiry whose existence was first reported by the Wall Street Journal earlier this week, which had not determined its focus. Reuters did not identify the accounting practices under scrutiny or establish when the inquiry began. The SEC declined to comment and Nvidia did not immediately respond to requests for comment, the agency report said. An SEC investigation does not establish wrongdoing, and inquiries can close without enforcement action; the reporting provides no basis to judge whether this one will lead to charges or to changes in Nvidia's financial statements.

The scrutiny lands on a company already under pressure on several fronts. Former chief executive Jensen Huang agreed on Friday to testify at a public Senate hearing, according to a senator's aide, California's attorney general has subpoenaed the company in a separate inquiry into possible chip diversions to China, and the shares have been falling with the wider artificial intelligence trade all week.
