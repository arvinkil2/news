March #11 sugar on ICE settled at 20.78 cents per pound on Monday, up 0.85 cents or 4.3 percent, after reaching 20.81 cents intraday, a new 52-week high. The move extends Friday's 5.2 percent surge to 19.93c, which was the highest close since April 2025. Volume was heavy, with roughly 127,000 contracts in the March contract on the day.

The fundamental drivers carried over from Friday's reporting. Excess rain in Brazil may leave a record 35 million metric tons of cane standing, per Datagro, and StoneX sees Center-South output below 38mmt versus the 40mmt previously expected, per specialist-ag reporting citing Reuters. India recorded its weakest monsoon in a decade (-12.6 percent below normal) and opened a 1mmt duty-free import quota, a bullish structural signal for the world's second-largest producer.

The deficit outlook is hardening across forecasters: the International Sugar Organization projected a 2026/27 global deficit of 200,000 metric tons on September 1, Covrig Analytics sees a 300,000-ton deficit, and Czarnikow predicts a 2.9 million-ton deficit for 2027/28 as Brazilian mills divert more cane to ethanol. Fund-long accumulation into the deficit narrative continues, which raises crowded-long risk but has not yet contradicted price action. The March premium over later contracts bears watching for signs of near-term physical tightness.
