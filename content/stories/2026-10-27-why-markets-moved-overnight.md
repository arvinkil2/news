United States natural gas was again the largest move in the site market tables, up 15.49 percent to 3.01 dollars per million British thermal units in the midday tables, updated at 12:29 ET, after Monday's 16.64 percent jump to 3.04 dollars. No story in today's edition explains the move. Today's gas coverage is European, the EU gas storage story, which puts storage at 75 percent of capacity heading into winter, and it prices a different market on a different continent. The United States move is carried here as a large unexplained move, the second in two days.

Brent crude fell 5.30 percent to 109.67 dollars a barrel, extending Monday's 4.21 percent fall, while WTI fell 1.59 percent to 100.14 dollars. Today's Brent story supplies the context the market is trading on: Saudi air defences intercepted two drones near the Ras Tanura oil hub, the fire was contained outside the facility perimeter, and there was no damage to the plant. As on Monday, the benchmark carrying the Gulf risk premium fell several times faster than the United States benchmark, which is the pattern of premium coming out after a contained attack, and Brent is still up 0.81 percent on the week.

Tanker freight reversed. It fell 4.38 percent to 892.90 dollars at midday after rising 4.15 percent on Monday, giving back the gain that had made oil shipping the mirror image of crude. It remains up 11.45 percent on the week. Nothing in today's edition explains the fall: today's Hormuz stories, Iran's Strait authority moving against Sinokor vessels from November 1 and a tanker mine fire in the strait, point toward tighter shipping conditions, not cheaper ones, and no cause is invented here.

Uranium miners rose 4.48 percent to 35.90 dollars. Today's mining also notable file carries uranium news, Bannerman's Etango project in Namibia moving into full construction after its final investment decision, with first production targeted for 2028. That is sector news in today's reporting, but a construction decision on one Namibian project does not explain a four and a half percent move across the miners basket, and none is claimed.

BHP rose 4.18 percent to 82.43 dollars, with copper miners up 3.50 percent and copper itself up 2.19 percent to 6.65 dollars a pound. The copper side of today's file supports the direction: today's zinc story has base metals pricing tight deliverable stocks, and today's Codelco story has BHP signing the Anillo copper exploration agreement in Chile. The Anillo commitment is capped at 40 million dollars, far too small to move BHP on its own, and today's iron ore story, prices at a two year low on weak China demand, cuts the other way for both BHP and Rio Tinto, which rose 2.15 percent. No BHP specific driver in today's reporting explains the size of the move.

Rare earth miners rose 4.17 percent to 53.85 dollars, and here today's file is full. Today's Energy Fuels story has Export Finance Australia and the National Reconstruction Fund conditionally approving up to 180 million Australian dollars toward the Donald rare earth project's senior debt. Today's Australia story has Canberra refreshing its critical minerals strategy toward domestic refining, with the European Union's ambassador forecasting a wave of EU investment to diversify away from China, and today's Brazil story puts rare earths at the centre of Lula's win. Government money and government strategy are today's rare earth news on three continents.

Broadcom rose 4.16 percent to 342.85 dollars on company specific news published at midday. Today's Northstar story reports that Northstar Compute has raised 63 billion dollars in debt and equity arranged by Bank of America, Blackstone, Citi and Morgan Stanley to buy Broadcom AI accelerators and networking equipment for lease to Anthropic, with Broadcom supporting the residual value of the hardware up to the size of the first lien. It is one of the largest single AI compute financings yet disclosed, and it makes Broadcom both the seller and part of the credit support behind the sale.

Semiconductors rose 3.65 percent to 509.78 dollars, with Tesla up 3.59 percent to 405.67 dollars just behind the top eight. The Broadcom financing is the sector's named driver today, and today's AWS story, Amazon's cloud capacity largely sold out through 2027, gives the bid a demand side. Today's Tesla story, FSD flagged rentals through Turo from October 28 with about 1.7 million paid FSD customers, is the only Tesla specific news in the edition, and nothing in it is sized to a three and a half percent move.

The synthesis is risk on with the risk premium taken out of energy. The S&P 500 rose 0.63 percent, the Nasdaq 100 rose 1.38 percent and the Russell 2000 rose 1.08 percent, and today's consumer confidence story, a 7.5 point rebound to 91.7 against an expected 81, supports the bid with a household reading rather than only positioning. Bonds declined to join in either direction: the 10 year Treasury yield was unchanged at 4.99 percent, up 0.4 basis points on the day, with the Federal Open Market Committee's two day meeting now open and the statement due Wednesday at 14:00 ET, covered in today's Fed story. The dollar index rose 0.33 percent to 102.29. A tape where crude and tanker freight fall, miners and chips rise, and yields do not move is not pricing escalation or recession. It is pricing a contained Gulf, an audited AI buildout, and a central bank whose language on December is still the week's deciding event.
