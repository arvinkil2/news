**Latest, October 29:** The Senate has taken up the Binnari attack and put a much higher toll on the record. In a motion of urgent national importance on Wednesday, Senator Isa Shuaibu Lau cited about 80 people killed in the October 23 attack on Binnari, in Karim Lamido Local Government Area, and the chamber resolved to urge security agencies to deploy more personnel and establish additional security camps along the borders of Taraba, Plateau and Bauchi states, and urged the National Emergency Management Agency and the North East Development Commission to deliver relief to affected communities, before observing a minute's silence. The Senate's figure is not the police figure: the Taraba police confirmed 48 dead on October 25 (below), community accounts have put the toll near 50, and the Senate's roughly 80 has not been separately confirmed by police. What is settled is the institutional response, a Senate demand for a border security build up in a corridor where attackers are suspected of crossing from Plateau state.

**Latest, October 25:** The Taraba State Police Command put the toll at 48, its spokesperson Victor Mshelizah told BusinessDay: 10 people killed on Friday, and 38 who died or were found dead on Saturday morning, made up of 15 residents who drowned in a boat mishap as they fled, where the boat may have been over capacity and life jackets were inadequate, and 23 whose bodies were recovered from the bush. The attackers arrived on about 50 motorcycles, two armed men on each, opened fire, set houses, shops and food stores ablaze and looted cash and provisions, the report said. Police said extra personnel were sent to the area on Sunday morning and that calm had returned; residents were also reported injured and several remained missing.

Gunmen attacked Binnari village in the Karim Lamido area of Taraba state in northeastern Nigeria on Friday afternoon, killing at least 40 people, Amnesty International said. Police said a large group of attackers entered the village with heavy gunfire and looted food items from shops. Police had earlier confirmed 10 people killed, and Amnesty said more bodies were recovered on Saturday, bringing the reported toll to at least 40. Amnesty condemned the attack, saying it showed utter disdain for the sanctity of life. No group was identified as responsible and none was reported to have claimed the attack.
