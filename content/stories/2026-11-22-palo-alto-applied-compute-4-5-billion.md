Palo Alto Networks has agreed to acquire Applied Compute, a platform for post-training and inference of open-weight AI models, in an all-stock transaction valued at $4.5 billion, subject to adjustments. The deal was announced on November 18 alongside the company's fiscal first-quarter results and is expected to close in Palo Alto's fiscal third quarter of 2027, subject to regulatory clearance and customary conditions.

Applied Compute was co-founded in early 2025 by three former OpenAI researchers and grew from zero revenue to more than $100 million in annualized revenue in under 18 months, according to EE Times. Its platform lets companies adapt open-weight models to their own data and deploy them in public and sovereign cloud environments. On completion, co-founder and chief executive Yash Patil will become co-chief AI officer at Palo Alto Networks.

The acquisition landed with a strong quarter. Revenue for the three months ended October 31 was $3.44 billion, up 39 percent from $2.47 billion a year earlier and ahead of the $3.31 billion consensus reported by LSEG, with adjusted earnings per share of $1.05 against 97 cents expected. The company also raised its full-year forecasts, and its shares rose sharply on the results.

The strategic logic is a move beyond securing other companies' AI into supplying the models themselves. Chairman and chief executive Nikesh Arora said open-weight model implementations will let Palo Alto deliver secure open-weight models to customers and enable adoption of secure AI in the enterprise. TD Cowen analyst Shaul Eyal said the deal extends Palo Alto's push beyond traditional cybersecurity into enterprise AI, while Rosenblatt's Catharine Trebnick told Reuters that AI tools are surfacing vulnerabilities faster than companies can patch them, pushing buyers to consolidate onto fewer platforms.

The price is the risk. At $4.5 billion for a company with about $100 million of annualized revenue and a platform that only recently reached customers, Palo Alto is paying for position in a market that does not yet have settled economics, and it will integrate Applied Compute in the same period as its much larger CyberArk transaction, also expected to close in fiscal third quarter 2027.
