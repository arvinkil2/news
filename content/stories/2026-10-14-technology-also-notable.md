**Pluto Security raises $42 million for AI-use security.** Pluto Security came out of stealth with $42 million in total funding, an $8.5 million seed led by TLV Partners and a $34 million Series A led by Jump Capital. The company sells visibility and control over the AI tools employees use and build, and says it has passed $2 million in annual recurring revenue with more than 30 enterprise customers since selling began in April. Single-sourced: TechCrunch.

**DayOne lines up $3.67 billion for Malaysia.** Seven lenders including CIMB, DBS, Maybank, OCBC and Standard Chartered provided a combined $3.67 billion in multicurrency financing for DayOne's Malaysian data center buildout, split between a $2.4 billion offshore term loan and a 5.5 billion ringgit Murabahah facility, as reported by The Business Times via Datacenter Dynamics. DayOne reports 962 MW in service and 1.3 GW under construction ahead of a planned Nasdaq IPO. The infra-deals beat owns the full story.

**NTT reportedly building $6.3 billion Anthropic site in Osaka.** Datacenter Dynamics, citing reports, said NTT plans a data center of about $6.3 billion for Anthropic in Japan. It is a report, not an announcement, and neither company has confirmed it; treat accordingly. Single-sourced: Datacenter Dynamics.

**D-Wave signs SkyWater to make its quantum chips.** D-Wave agreed a quantum chip manufacturing deal with SkyWater Technology, the Minnesota-based US foundry, putting fabrication of its superconducting quantum processors onshore. Terms were not disclosed in the report. Single-sourced: Datacenter Dynamics.

**Denmark breach touches 8.8 million people.** A data breach in Denmark affecting about 8.8 million people has been linked in reporting to a trivially weak password, "123456", a reminder that credential hygiene still fails at national scale. Single-sourced: The Register.

**Google Play opens to private vibe-coded apps.** Google will let anyone build private, AI-generated apps that run on Android and are hosted through Play, extending the Gemini-era app creation push to non-developers. The guardrails for quality and abuse are the open question. Reported by TechCrunch and Ars Technica.

**FBI arrests OpenAI employee over recording device.** The FBI arrested an OpenAI employee accused of planting a hidden recording device in a company conference room, according to TechCrunch. The case lands while a judge has separately ordered OpenAI to turn over hiring records in Apple's trade secrets suit against it. Single-sourced: TechCrunch.

**Scaleway plans GPU clusters in Sweden and Copenhagen.** French cloud provider Scaleway plans GPU cluster deployments in Sweden and in Copenhagen, extending European-owned AI compute capacity in the Nordics. Capacity and timing were not detailed in the report. Single-sourced: Datacenter Dynamics.
