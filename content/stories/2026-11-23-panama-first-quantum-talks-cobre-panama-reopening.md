Panama and First Quantum Minerals opened formal negotiations on Monday over the future of the Cobre Panama copper mine, which has been shut since November 2023, when nationwide protests and a Supreme Court ruling that invalidated its operating contract forced its closure. Trade and Industry Minister Julio Moltó was to lead the government's first official meeting with the Canadian miner's local subsidiary, Minera Panamá, after President José Raúl Mulino said last week that his administration was ready to seek a resolution, and the president's office called the talks the start of a transparent process with no decision on restarting imminent.

The government has set conditions for any agreement, including Panamanian ownership of the mineral resources, maximum economic benefit for the state, environmental and constitutional compliance, and an end to First Quantum's international arbitration against Panama. First Quantum shares rose 6.2 percent in Toronto on Monday morning to C$27.77, giving the company a market capitalisation of C$23.12 billion, and the mine's stockpile processing, restarted in July under government authorisation, is expected to produce 30,000 to 40,000 tonnes of copper this year without new extraction.
