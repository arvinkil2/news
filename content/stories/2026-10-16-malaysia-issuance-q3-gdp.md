Malaysia has issued RM155 billion of government bonds and sukuk in 2026 to date, Bank Negara Malaysia's market platform data show, including RM10 billion in October, taking the outstanding stock to RM1.359 trillion. The programme has run at a steady RM15 billion to RM20 billion a month with no failed tenders.

Growth is cooperating. Third quarter GDP rose 5.3 percent year on year, slowing from the second quarter but keeping Malaysia at the top of the ASEAN growth table, and Bank Negara's governor struck an upbeat tone on the economy in interviews this week.

The combination, deep domestic demand for duration from the pension and insurance base, a managed issuance calendar, and 5 percent growth, is why Malaysian yields have stayed range bound while other emerging markets have repriced on the US inflation shock. The next test is the federal budget's borrowing requirement for 2027.
