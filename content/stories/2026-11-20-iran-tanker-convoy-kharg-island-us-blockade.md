Iran has positioned more than 14 oil tankers at Kharg Island, crewed and flying Iranian flags, in a convoy poised to sail toward the Sea of Oman exit in the coming days in an open attempt to break the US naval blockade of its oil exports, according to satellite imagery and ship-tracking data cited by Euronews. Ship-tracking firm TankerTrackers said available data confirmed Iran was resuming oil exports. Kharg Island, in the northern Persian Gulf, accounts for about 85 to 90 percent of Iran's crude oil exports, and loading there has been halted since the blockade was imposed.

The preparations follow the first observed ship-to-ship transfer of Iranian crude since the US reinstated its blockade of Iranian ports in mid-July. The US-sanctioned Suezmax Olym transferred about 200,000 barrels to the medium-range tanker Hao Fan on Tuesday, south of Kharg Island, with both vessels' transponders off, according to United Against Nuclear Iran and Kpler data reported by Bloomberg. The US military issued a notice on Wednesday widening the blockade to target ships receiving cargoes at sea, warning its forces could target vessels carrying out such transfers. An IRGC-linked commentator, resharing a piece by the state-affiliated Fars news agency, warned that disrupting an Iranian tanker's route would lead to strikes on oil facilities in the United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman and Bahrain, describing that as his personal analysis.
