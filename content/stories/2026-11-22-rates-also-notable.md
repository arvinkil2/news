**Indian states lengthen maturities.** Five state governments, Madhya Pradesh, Punjab, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu and Uttar Pradesh, will convert or switch an aggregate 10,800 crore rupees face value of securities maturing in 2027 to 2029 into destination stock maturing between 2034 and 2044, the Reserve Bank of India announced. The multiple price auction runs on November 26 on the e-Kuber platform, with settlement on November 27. Switches of this kind push state redemption humps outward without new net borrowing.

**India Treasury bill auction.** The RBI will auction 23,000 crore rupees of Treasury bills on November 25: 8,000 crore of 91 day bills, 8,000 crore of 182 day bills and 7,000 crore of 364 day bills, with settlement on November 26. Results will be announced on the day of the auction.

**Waller stays off policy.** Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller's opening remarks at the College Fed Challenge finals on November 20 contained no comment on monetary policy or the economic outlook, according to reporting of the event. The remarks matter mainly for what they did not do: Waller had earlier in the week raised the possibility of a half point December increase, and his silence on Friday left that signal standing unamended into the December 8 to 9 meeting, for which 141 schools from 34 states entered this year's competition.

**The shrinking Treasury convenience yield.** A Brookings commentary published November 20 set out how the convenience yield investors accept for holding Treasuries has eroded: research by Wenxin Du, Ritt Keerati and Jesse Schreger puts the 5 year Treasury convenience yield against G10 sovereigns at an average of minus 28 basis points for 2021 to 2025, down 17 basis points from minus 11 basis points on average over 2012 to 2020. The authors link the decline in part to Treasury supply growing relative to intermediaries' capacity, a structural reason US borrowing costs no longer sit as far below peers as they once did.
