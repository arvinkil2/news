Cheyenne Light, Fuel and Power has entered into agreements to build distribution and transmission infrastructure to serve three Microsoft data centers planned for Wyoming, its parent said in a filing at the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission. The redacted agreements require Microsoft to pay for the infrastructure through contribution in aid of construction payments.

Cheyenne Light is a subsidiary of Bright Horizon Energy Corp., the utility holding company created by the November 13 merger of Black Hills Corp. and NorthWestern Energy Group. The utility said it does not believe FERC has jurisdiction over the retail agreements but filed them for review out of an abundance of caution, given the agency's recent statements on cost recovery for transmission costs caused by large-load customers.

The scale is disproportionate to the host system. Cheyenne Light has about 46,000 retail customers around Cheyenne and set a peak load record of 439 MW in July. Against that base it is executing a 3 GW data center pipeline, including 600 MW of Microsoft and Meta projects, and is in initial talks on a further 2.5 GW of early-stage opportunities, Black Hills officials said on a November 5 earnings call.

In September the utility agreed to supply a Google data center in Cheyenne with 590 MW and manage 2.1 GW of third-party supply in a microgrid. To serve it, Cheyenne Light plans two transmission projects totalling 686 million dollars, recoverable under a proposed large-customer transmission cost adjustment mechanism on which the Wyoming Public Service Commission is expected to decide by mid-2027, and a 1.8 billion dollar, utility-owned 564 MW gas plant to be built between 2027 and 2029 through an unregulated affiliate. Black Hills expects the Google deal to add 150 million dollars a year to net income from 2030, while warning in its SEC filing that the agreements increase its exposure to a single large customer and that large-load forecasts face significant uncertainty.
