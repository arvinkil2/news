Mining equities fell for a second consecutive session in the latest market tables, and this time copper led the move on both sides of the metal and miner link. Copper miners fell 2.51 percent, the largest decline of any miner group, while copper fell 1.67 percent to 6.08 dollars a pound.

The rest of the complex fell more moderately. Rare earth miners fell 1.69 percent, uranium miners fell 1.61 percent, silver miners fell 1.17 percent and lithium and battery stocks fell 1.13 percent. Silver futures fell 1.88 percent to 49.03 dollars an ounce, a larger fall than the silver miners themselves recorded. Gold miners were the relative outperformers, down only 0.24 percent, with gold futures essentially flat, up 0.03 percent to 3,886.30 dollars an ounce. Among the diversified majors, BHP fell 1.27 percent to 79.49 dollars and Rio Tinto fell 0.40 percent to 82.95 dollars.

The pattern differs from the previous session, when every miner group fell between 2.7 and 4.2 percent while gold rose, an equity led selloff. This session was metal led at the top of the table: the group that fell most was the group whose metal fell most. That is the more conventional relationship, and it arrived a day after Goldman Sachs flagged downside risks to its 15,400 dollar a tonne copper forecast for 2027 on positioning and tariff grounds.

Two down sessions together still leave the week's story intact: miner valuations are being marked down faster than the metals they produce, with gold miners now down on a week when gold itself is roughly unchanged, and copper miners absorbing both a softer copper price and a more cautious bank debate about 2027.
