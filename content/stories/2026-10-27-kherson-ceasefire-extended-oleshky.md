Update, Wednesday morning: the truce has now held for its first 30 hours with no violations reported by either side. Ukraine's human rights ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets and Russia's ombudsperson Yana Lantratova both said on Wednesday that there had been no ceasefire violations in Oleshky for 30 hours, the Associated Press reported, with Lubinets crediting regular contact between the two ombudsman offices and thanking Melania Trump for her part in organising the humanitarian mission. The six day truce runs through Sunday while the operation moves food and medicine to the roughly 2,000 residents who remain and evacuates those who want to leave. Fighting continued elsewhere in Ukraine, and the AP reported that the pause coincides with United States led plans to bring Russian and Ukrainian officials back to the table next month for technical talks, with neither side showing signs of backing down from its key demands.

The ceasefire, in place since 03:00 on Tuesday to let food and medicine reach Oleshky, has expanded beyond the city across the Kherson region to provide safe passage for humanitarian convoys, Melania Trump said on X, and Ukrainian officials corroborated the wider scope, with deputy regional head Oleksandr Tolokonnikov saying Russian shelling had stopped in Kherson city and across the region. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said everything was proceeding according to plan, no breach by either side had been reported, and David Arakhamia, an official in President Volodymyr Zelensky's parliamentary party, said the truce would continue until 22:59 Ukrainian time on Sunday, November 1, calling it the largest de-escalation since 2022. An estimated 1,700 to 2,000 people remain in Oleshky, about 500 of them had expressed interest in evacuating by Tuesday afternoon, and Zelensky warned that the routes Russia is demining lead toward Russia, while Russian installed regional head Vladimir Saldo's claim that most residents want to leave for Russia has not been corroborated on the ground.
