Alberta has introduced the strictest data centre siting regime proposed in North America. Bill 42, the Energy, Environment and Utilities Statutes Amendment Act, 2026, introduced on November 18, would prevent data centres of 10MW or larger from being built within 600 metres of the nearest home, and would bar them from the province's highest-quality farmland, protecting about 14 million acres of Class 1 and 2 land, Class 3 land in municipalities without Class 1 or 2 land, and currently irrigated land. Municipalities could ask the Alberta Utilities Commission for an alternative setback only for stand-alone sites of 10MW to under 75MW with no on-site generation; larger sites and any site with its own generation keep the full setback. Projects with an existing commission decision, a signed transmission service agreement or a municipal permit are grandfathered.

The bill also sets a provincewide standard intended to bring cooling water use close to zero, requires excess heat to be recoverable for greenhouses, industry or homes, and raises the maximum corporate fine for serious environmental contamination to $6 million. On power, a new Data Centre Designation would give qualifying projects more options to meet their own electricity needs while making proponents pay for their own power infrastructure, with those costs kept off Alberta ratepayer bills. The Alberta Utilities Commission would become the primary provincial regulator for data centres of 10MW and larger.

The province is explicit about the bargain: clear, demanding rules in exchange for investor certainty, in a province actively courting AI facilities. Legal analysis notes the detail will sit in forthcoming regulations, and that a data centre system with on-site generation and at least 75MW of transmission demand could seek designation to self-supply and export power. The rules are expected to take effect in 2027 if the bill passes.
