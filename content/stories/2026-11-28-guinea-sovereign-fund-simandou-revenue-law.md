Guinea's lawmakers have approved a sovereign wealth fund that will take 30 percent of revenue tied to the Simandou iron ore project, aiming to build a $1 billion pot within five years. The National Assembly passed the legislation on November 25 by 121 votes to none, with two abstentions, establishing the fund under President Mamadi Doumbouya's Simandou 2040 national development programme.

Under the law, the fund will receive 30 percent of the state's tax and non tax revenues from Simandou, plus 5 percent of tax and other state revenue from any new mining projects that start production after the legislation takes effect. Presenting the bill, Finance Minister Mariama Cire Sylla emphasised the need to convert Guinea's finite mineral resources into assets capable of generating lasting returns. The finance ministry said the fund would finance economic development, protect public finances against external shocks and accumulate savings for future generations, with minimum allocations of 40 percent for development, 20 percent for macroeconomic stabilisation and 20 percent for future generations.

Governance is designed to limit political meddling, though the president retains a central role. A nine member board, seven of them independent and possibly foreign, will draw up the investment strategy; a committee of ministers and civil society representatives chaired by Doumbouya will then approve it. The finance ministry and central bank each appoint one board representative. Sylla told lawmakers the institution would be neither a parallel budget nor a borrowing instrument, withdrawals would require parliamentary authorisation, and governance would follow the Santiago Principles.

The law lands as Simandou, developed by a consortium led by China's Baowu and a joint venture led by Rio Tinto, begins generating revenue; the project's first iron ore cargo to Europe is covered in yesterday's edition. The government expects Simandou to eventually double its tax revenue. The International Monetary Fund approved a 38 month, $417 million programme for Guinea this week and has pushed for clearer fiscal rules and transparency, while the World Bank has warned that mining led growth alone will not create enough jobs.
