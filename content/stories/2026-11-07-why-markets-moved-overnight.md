Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 5.96 percent to 57,435 on Friday, its worst session of a week that took 11.17 percent off the index. No clear single driver appears in this morning's reporting; the fall extends the Asian chip-led selloff this site tracked through the week, and it came on the same day US chip stocks turned higher.

US semiconductors rose 3.51 percent to 136.53 dollars and Nvidia rose 2.78 percent to 186.64 dollars, the first firm bounce after the correction that began earlier in the week. The weekly damage is still the story: semiconductors are down 13.13 percent on the week and Nvidia 8.8 percent. See today's semiconductors story for the Friday tape.

Tesla rose 4.23 percent to 358.22 dollars alongside the chip bounce, trimming a weekly fall of 10.4 percent. No new company driver appears in this morning's reporting; the stock moved with the broader recovery in the names hit hardest earlier in the week.

Uranium miners rose 4.39 percent, the strongest mining group on the day, against a weekly fall of 4.12 percent. No clear single driver appears in this morning's reporting, though the mining roundup notes continued corporate interest in North American uranium projects.

Precious-metal miners kept rising with the week's metal gains. Silver miners rose 2.63 percent and gold miners 2.16 percent on Friday, extending weekly gains of 11.38 and 13.63 percent. Gold futures eased 0.94 percent on the day but held a 4.51 percent weekly gain, and silver futures held a 5.51 percent weekly gain, as today's commodities roundup records.

Germany's DAX fell 1.92 percent to 25,820.4, moving the other way from the US bounce and closing the week almost flat. No clear single driver appears in this morning's reporting.

Read together, Friday split along the Pacific. Asia and Europe sold off while US risk assets bounced from oversold levels, and the week's winners, precious metals and their miners, kept climbing. The counterweight sits in rates: Treasury yields backed up modestly on Friday, the 10 year to 4.86 percent, as Federal Reserve speakers leaned toward further tightening after October payrolls rose 165,000 (see today's Fed speakers story). A bounce priced against a central bank still talking about hikes is a fragile one.
