The Africa Credit Rating Agency, endorsed by African leaders in 2018, will rate sovereign borrowers, financial institutions and private companies, operate independently, and be funded through shareholder capital and its operations, though the AU gave no shareholder details. Afreximbank executive vice president Denys Denya said that "when lenders don't see clearly, they charge for the fog," while former Nigerian vice president Yemi Osinbajo said AfCRA may provide a counterweight but "can't just be a chauvinistic or nationalistic agency." African economies are rated B to B-minus on average versus BB for other emerging regions, the continent's annual external debt service surged to $163 billion in 2024 from $61 billion in 2010, and 23 economies currently lack a big-three rating.
