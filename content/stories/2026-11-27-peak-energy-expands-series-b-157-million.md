Peak Energy, a sodium-ion battery manufacturer, has expanded its Series B financing to $157 million. The total comprises $132 million of equity and a $25 million debt facility from HSBC. The company first announced the oversubscribed Series B in October, led by General Motors Ventures with participation from Maverick Silicon, Infinite Capital, Eclipse, Xora Innovation and LG Technology Ventures, and expanded it after further investor interest.

The additional capital will support expansion of manufacturing capacity, commercial deployments scheduled to begin in 2027, and the supply chain as commercial production increases. Peak's energy storage system uses passive cooling, requires no active cooling systems and, according to the company, needs no planned maintenance. The company is targeting grid-scale applications and data centers, and plans a pilot project to demonstrate the long-term economics of its sodium-ion storage technology for data center customers.

Peak's vice president of finance, Zack Jacobson, said the HSBC facility gives the company flexibility to fund project deliveries without additional dilution and to reserve equity for scaling the business. Mercom's sector data, cited in its report, put venture funding for energy storage companies at $5.4 billion across 79 deals in the first nine months of 2026, up 93 percent on the same period in 2025.
