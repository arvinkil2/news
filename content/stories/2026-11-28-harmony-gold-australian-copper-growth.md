Harmony Gold Mining, the largest gold producer in South Africa, is turning to Australian copper for growth as it runs short of gold mines worth buying at home, chief executive Beyers Nel said. The company's CSA mine in New South Wales and Eva Copper project in Queensland could together produce 100,000 tonnes of copper a year by 2029, Nel told the Northern Miner Group in an interview.

"If you think about South Africa, we are a bit out of runway on new gold mining assets," Nel said on Wednesday. "There are maybe one or two assets left in South Africa that are good quality, but they are not necessarily for sale." South Africa supplied 84 percent of Harmony's gold equivalent output in fiscal 2026. Australia and Papua New Guinea together accounted for 16 percent, a share Harmony expects to reach about 30 percent by fiscal 2036, excluding the Wafi-Golpu copper gold project and a proposed extension at Hidden Valley, both in Papua New Guinea.

Harmony's South African gold output fell 5.8 percent to 1.24 million ounces in the year to June 30, and group production fell 3.4 percent to 1.43 million ounces. Higher gold prices helped lift production profit from gold by 59 percent even as all in sustaining costs rose 13 percent, both in rand terms. Harmony expects group gold output of 1.3 million to 1.4 million ounces in fiscal 2027 as its Moab Khotsong mine works through a planned gap in ore production. At Mponeng, the world's deepest gold mine, recovered grades rose 10 percent from a year earlier to 11.55 grams of gold per tonne in the quarter to September 30, though Nel cautioned the richer ore will not last indefinitely.

Harmony acquired CSA, already an underground mine, when it bought MAC Copper for $1.01 billion in October 2025. It expects CSA, its only producing copper mine, to deliver 28,000 to 30,000 tonnes in fiscal 2027 and is upgrading ventilation as it works towards 40,000 tonnes a year by fiscal 2029. The company plans to spend $1.55 billion to $1.75 billion developing Eva in Queensland, expecting first copper towards the end of 2028, subject to federal environmental approval for the second stage; Eva could average about 60,000 tonnes a year over its 15 year life. "I don't think we get any value for the copper assets in the share price," Nel said. Harmony's American depositary shares closed 2.7 percent stronger at $13.80 in New York on Wednesday, leaving them 31 percent weaker this year.

Harmony is not actively seeking acquisitions, but Nel said its deep mining experience could give it an edge at an Australian mine switching from open pit to underground production. At Wafi-Golpu, held half and half with Newmont, the project could produce about 180,000 tonnes of copper and 250,000 ounces of gold a year, and could require $4 billion to $5 billion of investment; the partners are still negotiating a development contract with Papua New Guinea that could run for up to 40 years.
