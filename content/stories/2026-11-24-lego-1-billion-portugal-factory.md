Lego will invest around €1 billion, about $1.1 billion, in a new factory in Estarreja, south of Porto in Portugal, the Danish toymaker said on November 23. The plant will be its fourth in Europe and eighth worldwide, joining factories in Denmark, Hungary and Czechia in Europe. Construction is due to start in the first half of 2027 and production in 2030.

The 115,000-square-metre plant, on a 60-hectare site, will handle moulding, processing and packing and will employ more than 1,000 people at full capacity. Chief Operating Officer Carsten Rasmussen said output will mainly serve established European markets, with the plant also feeding developing markets in Africa, Southeast Europe and the Middle East. Portuguese Prime Minister Luís Montenegro attended the launch and said the government would work to make land available and accelerate construction.

Lego said demand growth is being driven by adult collectors, films and branded tie-ups, sustaining double-digit growth. The investment is manufacturing capital allocated on a decade horizon: a highly automated plant, sited inside the EU, sized for markets the company expects to grow into rather than current volumes alone.
