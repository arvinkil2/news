The US Treasury has set the composite rate on Series I savings bonds issued from November 1, 2026 to April 30, 2027 at 5.51 percent, up from the 4.26 percent composite on bonds issued in the previous six months. The new rate combines a fixed rate of 1.40 percent, raised from 0.90 percent at the May setting, with a semiannual inflation rate of 2.04 percent, up from 1.67 percent. TreasuryDirect sets the fixed and inflation rates every May 1 and November 1, and the fixed rate applies for the life of every bond issued in the six months that follow.

The Fiscal Service's formal release, published November 2, added the Series EE terms for the same window: EE bonds issued from November 2026 through April 2027 earn a fixed rate of 2.90 percent, which applies to the bond's 20 year original maturity, with the bonds guaranteed to be worth at least twice their purchase price at 20 years. EE bonds issued from May 1997 through April 2005, which earn market based rates, get 4.02 percent for semiannual periods beginning in this window. Both series have an interest bearing life of 30 years, and bonds held less than five years are subject to a three month interest penalty.
