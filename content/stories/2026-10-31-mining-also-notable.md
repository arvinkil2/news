**Aurelia Metals cash flow peaks.** Aurelia Metals generated 65.3 million Australian dollars in operating cash flow from its Cobar Region operations after sustaining capital in the September quarter, up from 53.1 million in the June quarter and its highest quarterly result since 2018. Production rose to 16,262 ounces of gold, 1,301 tonnes of copper and 8,272 tonnes of zinc, with 5.2 thousand tonnes of lead, and 226,000 tonnes of ore processed. Cash rose to 160.7 million Australian dollars, taking total available liquidity to 200.7 million, the company received final regulatory approval to expand plant processing capacity from 800,000 tonnes a year to 1.1 to 1.2 million tonnes a year, and full year production, cost and capital guidance was maintained.

**Tshipi manganese holds plan.** Jupiter Mines reported that the Tshipi manganese mine in South Africa, in which it holds a 49.9 percent interest, produced 823,800 tonnes and sold 848,566 tonnes in the first quarter of its 2027 financial year, with production, logistics and sales volumes ahead of plan despite a challenging manganese market.

**EY names complexity the top risk.** EY’s Top 10 Business Risks and Opportunities in Mining and Metals report ranks operational complexity as the sector’s leading risk for 2027, followed by capital, supply chains, rising costs and geopolitics. Regulatory compliance delays were the leading contributor to complexity, grade variability was cited by 29 percent of respondents, 53 percent were investing in technology to improve supply chain visibility, and 28 percent anticipated direct government investment over the next 12 months. Cybersecurity entered the top 10 for the first time in this ranking.

**Japan shows deep sea ambitions.** Japan is putting its deep sea mining ambitions on display on a remote island, The Diplomat reported, as international support for a moratorium on deep sea mining grows. The item is a policy signal rather than a production development, and it follows this site’s October 29 coverage of the United States deep sea minerals listing and lease sale track.

**Africa refinery boom tested.** BusinessDay examined Africa’s gold refinery boom and whether the new capacity will actually capture more mineral wealth for producing countries, the same value capture question behind Liberia’s domestic refining mandate and Nigeria’s new local processing condition for mineral titles, both covered on this site this week.

**Glencore Australian coal momentum.** Australian Mining reported that Glencore’s national coal production in Australia is gathering momentum, a volume note that sits against the group’s third quarter production report covered here on October 29, in which cobalt output halved under Congolese quotas.
