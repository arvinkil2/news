Canada hosts the Canada-European Union Summit in Montreal on Thursday and Friday, the 21st summit between the two sides. Prime Minister Mark Carney will work with European Council President Antonio Costa and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, with a leaders' meeting and a ministerial session with European commissioners scheduled for Friday.

The Prime Minister's Office frames the bargain in complementary terms: the EU brings market power, research depth and manufacturing strength, while Canada brings energy, critical minerals, capabilities in space, artificial intelligence and quantum, and a large banking system. The stated aims are more choice and lower costs for consumers, new export markets for businesses, and wider opportunities for young people to live, work and study on either side of the Atlantic.

The vocabulary is contested. Von der Leyen has described the proposed partnership as associate membership of the bloc, a term some European leaders have rejected; European officials this week used the phrase Alliance for the Future, language Carney has previously endorsed. Membership itself is off the table, officials on both sides say, and the work is in business, technology, clean energy and critical minerals.

The summit builds on ground already covered. Canada has become the first non-European country to join the EU's SAFE defence initiative, Carney was the first non-European leader to take part in a European Political Community summit, and he addressed the European Parliament in September. The existing trade agreement, CETA, is the foundation the new foreign and security partnership is meant to extend.

There is domestic friction alongside the diplomacy. Quebec's premier has pressed the federal government to let him deliver a speech at the event, and Carney faced backlash in Quebec after declining the request, Bloomberg reported on Wednesday. Thursday's agenda opens with a welcome dinner for elected and business leaders; the test of the summit is Friday's joint declaration and whether its commitments carry dates, money or only language.

**Latest, October 30:** The naming dispute has been settled in the draft, against the label. Reuters reported that the joint declaration officials plan to publish on Friday drops associate membership entirely and promises an ever closer association instead, after Germany led a group of member states arguing the term has no legal status or precedent and had not been fleshed out; six member states in all opposed it, the National Post reported. Von der Leyen nonetheless kept her own language, describing the plan at the C.D. Howe Institute in Montreal on Thursday as an association without accession, while the summit launches the Alliance for the Future framework across trade, critical minerals, technology and research. The draft's substance survived the vocabulary fight: a joint initiative on new subsea cable infrastructure linking Europe, Canada and Asia, a joint feasibility study on Canadian LNG exports to Europe, cross Atlantic youth work mobility, and the conclusion of substantive talks on a digital trade agreement. Work to define the relationship continues to the 2027 summit. The final declaration text, due today, is the confirming document; this file is updated in place and will be corrected if the published text differs from the draft reporting.

**Latest, October 30 (closing):** The summit has now closed and the joint statement is published, under the title of the European Union and Canada building together an Alliance for the future. As the draft reporting indicated, the statement makes no mention of associate membership; the leaders agreed instead to define an ever closer association by their next summit in 2027, and the statement records an unwavering commitment to effective multilateralism, democracy and the rules based international order, work to resolve the conflicts in Ukraine and the Middle East, and deeper security ties including in the Arctic. The two sides also recorded that talks have been concluded on a digital trade agreement to complement CETA. Carney kept his own vocabulary at the close, saying the two sides are working towards the deepest possible partnership short of accession, with Canada becoming the European Union's first associate member, that the scale and scope would be defined over the coming months, and that he expects to conclude negotiations in June, when he will attend a meeting of European leaders. European Council President Antonio Costa described the alliance as the closest relationship the EU has established with a country outside the bloc.

The declaration carries detailed annexes with more than 50 initiatives, the Commission says, covering cooperation on Ukraine, including possible Canadian participation in the EU's SAFE defence financing instrument to support Ukraine, critical raw materials value chains, research through a new Joint Research Committee, and health security: the two sides signed an arrangement on medical countermeasures on the day. Von der Leyen, in her closing statement, called the outcome a Strategic Partnership of the Future and said the alliance framework could in time extend to other partners, naming Australia, New Zealand, Japan and South Korea, with Canada and the EU at its core. The digital trade agreement, whose negotiations the statement records as concluded, is expected to be signed in the coming weeks.
