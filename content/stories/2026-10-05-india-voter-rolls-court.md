Hearing a petition seeking prosecution of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar, the chief justice said: "Any modified Form 6 has not been approved by us. Let's be clear." The remark matters because the Election Commission said in a September 26 press note that the new Form 6 declaration had been upheld by the Supreme Court in its May 27, 2026 judgment on the Bihar revision. The court had no occasion to examine the new declaration in that judgment, since it was introduced afterward.

The new declaration requires new voters to state whether they or their parents were mapped in the previous revision, a statement the earlier form did not seek. Two election commissioners had objected to the change, saying it required the central government to amend the Registration of Electors Rules, 1960. On Monday the Commission separately rejected opposition leader Rahul Gandhi's allegation that Kumar changed Form 6 illegally, saying its June 24, 2025 order for the Bihar revision was unanimous and upheld by the court.

The court issued formal notice on the petition, which also seeks a judge-led investigation into alleged voter deletions and cites 14 objections allegedly recorded by Election Commissioners themselves, and tagged it with a similar pending matter. The court's decision to issue notice, rather than dismiss the pleas at the threshold, keeps the pressure on the commission; no ruling on the merits has been made. The proceedings follow street protests in Delhi on Oct 4 directed at the CEC.

The political backdrop is charged. Opposition parties have spent months alleging irregularities in voter-roll management, and the Delhi protests show the issue has street-level salience. For markets and institutions, the near-term question is whether the court imposes interim constraints on the commission's revision exercises or lets the process run under judicial observation. Either path keeps electoral-integrity risk priced into Indian political risk through the next election cycle.

October 6 reporting filled in the street-level detail behind the October 2-4 protests. The demonstrations were called by a youth-led movement — the Cockroach Janata Party, led by Abhijeet Dipke — after an Indian Express expose reported around 130 million names struck from draft rolls during the nationwide Special Intensive Revision, and drew student groups, civil society organizations and opposition politicians in multiple cities; in Mumbai, Shiv Sena (UBT) and Maharashtra Navnirman Sena organized a parallel march demanding Kumar's resignation. In Delhi, authorities imposed gathering restrictions around Jantar Mantar, deployed large numbers of police and paramilitary personnel, closed metro stations, and detained more than 700 people including Aam Aadmi Party leaders and student activists, according to Indian media (status: reported; NPR, October 3; Indian media, October 4). The 130-million figure comes from Reuters reporting citing Amnesty International and other sources on draft rolls; the Election Commission says the exercise targets deceased, duplicate and ineligible voters, that many were reinstated after appeals, and that no final tally has been published.
