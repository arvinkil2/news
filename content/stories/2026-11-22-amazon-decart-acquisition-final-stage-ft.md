**Latest.** Amazon announced on November 23 that it has signed an agreement to purchase the proprietary technology behind the Decart Optimization Stack (DOS), the software that helps AI models run efficiently on Nvidia, Amazon and Google hardware, and to bring Decart co-founders Dean Leitersdorf, Moshe Shalev and Orian Leitersdorf, along with their team of AI researchers and engineers, to Amazon. Bloomberg valued the deal at $7.5 billion, according to TechCrunch; Amazon's own announcement gives no price. Amazon said Decart's team has worked closely with it since 2025 to deliver performance results on Trainium, its custom AI chip, and that the technology will help customers train and run AI models faster and at lower cost. Peter DeSantis, Amazon's senior vice president for foundational AI models, custom silicon and quantum computing, said getting more out of every chip means better training, faster inference and better models at lower cost. Completion is subject to customary closing conditions, including regulatory clearance. Amazon has so far used Trainium in its own data centres and is preparing to sell the chips to customers as a competitor to Nvidia, TechCrunch reported.

Amazon is in the final stage of negotiations to acquire Decart, an AI startup founded in San Francisco in 2023 by three Israeli engineers, for about $7.5 billion, the Financial Times reported, citing sources who said a deal could be completed as early as early next week but that the talks could still collapse. Decart builds software that creates and simulates 3D environments and makes AI models run more efficiently, and it was an early adopter of Amazon's Trainium 3 chip. The reported price is nearly double the $4 billion valuation Decart took in a $300 million funding round in May, and it follows a $6 billion approach by Anthropic that was abandoned after due diligence in September.
