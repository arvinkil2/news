Rapport Therapeutics announced positive topline results from its Phase 2 trial of RAP-219 in 253 adults with acute bipolar mania, with the drug achieving a 3.1 point placebo adjusted improvement in Young Mania Rating Scale total score at week three (a mean reduction of 15.4 points against 12.3 on placebo, p=0.045) and a statistically significant 0.4 point improvement on the CGI-BP severity of mania scale (p=0.024). The company said all treatment emergent adverse events were mild or moderate, with no severe or serious adverse events over the three week treatment period, and chief executive Abraham Ceesay called the results clinical proof of concept for the drug's TARP gamma 8 selective mechanism. Rapport plans to meet the FDA in the first quarter of 2027 and expects Phase 3 trials in bipolar mania to begin in the second half of 2027, alongside its ongoing Phase 3 epilepsy programs.

BioPharma Dive's fuller write-up adds the market and the caveats. The result was unexpected: expectations for success were low, according to Stifel's Paul Matteis, who called the surprise win a clear positive that helps validate the drug broadly, while acknowledging the efficacy data were close to not hitting statistical significance and sat at the lower end of the 2 to 6 point improvement range offered by already approved bipolar medications. Jefferies analyst Andrew Tsai wrote that RAP-219 appears to have clean safety, that continued success could add more than a billion dollars to the drug's peak sales estimates, and that it should embolden management to consider other central nervous system indications, such as bipolar depression. Raymond James analysts argued that in bipolar disorder the best treatments are the ones patients stay on, and that a novel, better-tolerated option should become commercially relevant. Rapport said drug-treated patients improved across all 11 components of the mania scale, including mood, speech, irritability and aggression, against a strong placebo arm.

The mechanism explains the surprise. RAP-219 dampens the activity of a protein found mostly in brain regions where seizures often originate, regulating receptors that respond to glutamate, the signalling molecule that prompts neurons to fire. Too much glutamate signalling is a hallmark of both epilepsy, where the drug is in Phase 3, and bipolar mania. Nearly 3 percent of US adults, more than 7 million people, have bipolar disorder. The discipline is the margin: a primary endpoint at p=0.045 in a three-week, 253-patient trial is a proof of concept, not a registration case, and the two Phase 3 trials planned for the second half of 2027 are where the claim gets tested.
