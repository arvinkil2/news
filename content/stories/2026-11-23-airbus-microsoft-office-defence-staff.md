Airbus's long migration away from Microsoft Office will take another two to three years, its executive vice president of digital, Catherine Jestin, told The Register, and it will never reach the whole company. The defence division accounts for about 22 percent of the roughly 165,000 employees, more than 36,000 people, and will keep classic Microsoft Office. Defence staff cannot use Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace because their sensitive data must remain on-premise, and, in Jestin's words, the only on-premise solution available is Microsoft, so Office will remain for colleagues working in defence and space programmes.

More than two-thirds of Airbus's employees have fully transitioned to Google Workspace since the migration began, but pockets of parallel use remain, including finance teams whose spreadsheets, some running to 20 million cells, exceed what Google Sheets can handle. Jestin said Google is still working to resolve software interface conflicts between departments and to improve features under the two companies' roadmap.

The Airbus case is the practical counterweight to Europe's digital sovereignty push: even a motivated, flagship defector keeps the incumbent's product exactly where the work is most sensitive, because no on-premise alternative exists at that scale.
