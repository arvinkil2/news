Mexican trucking companies launched an indefinite nationwide strike on Monday, organised by the National Chamber of Freight Transportation, CANACAR, and other trucking groups, protesting cargo theft, extortion, violence against drivers and rising operating costs. Demonstrations were planned in freight hubs including Nuevo Laredo, Reynosa, Monterrey, Tijuana and Mexico City, using convoys and vehicles stationed along highways and near logistics sites.

The groups are also protesting proposed changes in Mexico's 2027 fiscal package that would cut eligibility for a tax credit on some highway toll costs. Mexico's Interior Ministry urged CANACAR to return to negotiations, but the chamber kept its call for demonstrations, and the strike's duration and effect on border crossings were unclear on Monday.
