**Latest, October 26:** Serbia woke on Monday to the result Vucic conceded the night before, with celebrations reported in Belgrade and the electoral commission's official count still to be completed. No official final tally had been published by Monday morning in the reporting checked in this run, so the figures of record remain the Ipsos and CeSID projection carried below (Student List 52.0 percent and 138 seats, Vucic list 39.3 percent and 104 seats) and Vucic's own estimate of 135 to 140 seats for the Student List. France24 reported on Monday that the student movement had ousted the longtime leader in the parliament poll, and the BBC's concession report remained the international account of record. The next step is the commission's certified count and the start of government formation talks for a list that has no single leader.

**Latest, October 25:** Aleksandar Vucic conceded defeat at his party’s election headquarters on Sunday night. “We still do not have final results, on the basis of a sample … it is clear that we have lost these elections,” he said, in Reuters’ account. Vucic estimated that the Student List would take between 135 and 140 seats in the 250 seat National Assembly, against about 105 for his list. An Ipsos and CeSID projection put the Student List on 52.0 percent of the vote and 138 seats, above the 126 needed for a majority, and Vucic’s list on 39.3 percent and 104 seats. Celebrations were reported in central Belgrade after the concession. The projection is an estimate from a sample of polling stations, not the official count, and an early electoral commission snapshot, taken at 21:15 Belgrade time with 2.42 percent of stations processed, had also shown the ruling list ahead. Vucic also said no other major party would enter parliament, with national minority parties likely to take about five seats, The Guardian’s live blog reported. Russian state media reported that Vucic also said the Student List would impose sanctions on Russia in the spring; that is his claim as reported by those outlets, not a Student List statement, and the Guardian’s election night coverage carried no such remark.

**Latest, October 25:** First projections based on counted votes put the SNS led coalition on 47.6 percent and the Student List on 44.6 percent, The Guardian's live blog reported, citing the first CeSID and Ipsos results from a sample of polling stations that had opened their counts. The Socialist Party, the ruling coalition's former partner, stood at 2.3 percent, below the 3 percent threshold for seats, and the Movement of Free Citizens at 1.8 percent. CeSID and Ipsos put final turnout at 66.57 percent. The blog said the figures were an early subset that would certainly change as counting continued, and neither camp had claimed victory; Aleksandar Vucic was expected at his party's headquarters shortly.

**Latest, October 25:** Polls closed across Serbia at 8 p.m. local time, The Guardian's live blog reported, with voters still in line at the close allowed to vote. CeSID and Ipsos projected turnout of 67.6 percent at 7 p.m., the blog reported, and first projections, based on samples of polling stations that both camps say are representative, were expected around 9:30 p.m. local time. RIA Novosti's correspondent in Belgrade reported that police had closed central streets around the Republican Electoral Commission during the day and in the evening posted a cordon of officers with helmets and shields on Kralja Milana street, allowing through only people with commission accreditation; inside the commission the atmosphere was calm and officials were giving no comment outside regular briefings, the agency said.

**Latest, October 25:** Turnout reached 64.9 percent by 6 p.m. local time, the electoral commission said, already the highest turnout in a Serbian election since the breakup of Yugoslavia, with only the 1990 and 1992 elections higher, The Guardian's live blog reported. Polls remained open for another two hours, and first projections were expected around 9:30 p.m. local time. Earlier in the afternoon the EU ambassador to Serbia, Andreas von Beckerath, said the EU was deeply concerned about reported violent incidents across a number of polling stations, and called on all actors to remain calm and on the institutions responsible to ensure safety.

**Latest, October 25:** Projected turnout reached 56.4 percent by 4 p.m. local time, just over 14 percentage points higher than at the same point in 2023 and already marginally below the 2023 election's full turnout, with another four hours of voting to go, The Guardian's live blog reported. The blog did not name the estimator behind the projection. Florian Bieber, a professor at the University of Graz speaking from Belgrade, told the blog that higher turnout was strongly indicative of a good performance for the student list, while warning that Vucic could also mobilise voters in different ways. In its latest update cited by the blog, the CRTA observer mission reported pressure on voters in roughly a third of all polling stations and voters photographing their ballot papers in as many as 40 percent of stations, and urged all actors to reduce tensions. The blog said reports of physical intimidation and altercations near polling stations were circulating on social media through the afternoon, and that it would treat those unverified reports with caution until confirmed.

**Latest, October 25:** Turnout reached 42.7 percent by 2 p.m. on the CRTA observer mission's estimate, Yle reported via STT, while the state election commission put it at nearly 40 percent and an Ipsos and CeSID estimate, reported by Serbian media, put it at 46.4 percent, against 32.2 percent at the same hour in 2023. The three figures come from different counts and samples and are carried as reported. CRTA recorded irregularities at 57 polling stations, and both the student list and the ruling Serbian Progressive Party alleged major irregularities during the day. Serbian outlets Masina and N1 reported that masked men attacked student list election monitors in Pancevo with pyrotechnic devices and metal bars, with several people struck in the head and police at the scene; the authorities had not confirmed the account. Separately, the student list reported that a ruling party supporter had shot an election observer in Mladenovac, a claim carried here as that list's report only. Yle also reported organised bus transport of Kosovo Serbs to polling stations inside Serbia.

**Latest, October 25:** Local election monitors recorded cases of photographing ballots that suggested pressure on voters, attempted vote buying and carousel voting, the Associated Press reported, as the state election commission put turnout at about 35 percent by noon, against 21 percent at the same hour in 2023. Vucic said his camp would announce reliable projections about three hours after polls close at 8 p.m. local time, warned that any protests claiming fraud if his party won would be immediately quashed, and the commission is not expected to release the official tally before Monday.

**Latest, October 25:** Turnout reached 35.1 percent by noon local time, according to Ipsos research data published by the Telegraf portal and reported by TASS, against 21.33 percent at the same hour in the 2023 election. The Republican Electoral Commission registered 6,451,292 voters for 250 seats, with more than 16,000 observers monitoring the vote, including 622 from abroad, TASS reported.

**Latest, October 25:** The ruling Serbian Progressive Party said a group of young people it described as protest supporters pushed Vucic's mother, Angelina Vucic, shouted at her and tore grocery bags she was carrying from a market, and said she had reported the incident to police. TASS, which reported the party statement, gave no location for the incident and no police confirmation or comment from any other party, and the claim is carried here as the ruling party's statement only.

**Latest, October 25:** Turnout reached 12 percent by 9 a.m., pollster CeSID estimated, more than double the same hour in the previous election in 2023, Reuters reported, and even Vucic waited in line for nearly two hours to vote. After voting, Vucic said Serbia would choose its direction on Sunday, adding: "If we lose we will acknowledge the will of the electorate, if we win no one will take it (from us)." In Novi Sad, Ilija Srdanovic, who leads the student list, said he hoped the day would pass in a democratic atmosphere and that the scenes suggested a record turnout in the country's recent history.

**Latest, October 25:** Unusually long lines formed at polling stations in Belgrade and Novi Sad before voting opened at 7 a.m., Reuters reported from Belgrade, with voters telling its reporters they had come out early because the students called them. Reuters put the student list's support in the context of nearly two years of demonstrations, and noted that many voters credit Vucic with a period of modernisation, including highways, railways, schools and hospitals. Polls close at 8 p.m. local time, with first indications expected about two hours later.

**Latest:** Polling stations opened at 7 a.m. local time on Sunday and close at 8 p.m., with first indications of the result expected about two hours after the close, Al Jazeera reported. A final Ipsos poll published on Thursday put the Serbian Progressive Party at 47 percent and the student list at 40 percent, although other polls have put the students ahead. Neither Al Jazeera nor Deutsche Welle reported a turnout figure, an election day incident or an observer comment by midday in Belgrade. Analyst Jelena Vasiljevic of the University of Belgrade told Al Jazeera that turnout would decide the contest, because the governing party holds a disciplined core vote while the student list needs opposition voters, habitual nonvoters and the undecided to turn out together. Both reports said Vucic resigned as president in September, after calling the snap election, to run again for prime minister.

Serbia's student movement took its two-year protest campaign into a final mass rally on Thursday, filling Belgrade's Slavija Square and surrounding streets three days before a snap parliamentary election that pits the Student List against President Aleksandar Vucic's Serbian Progressive Party. The rally, called Victory Calling, capped multi-day marches in which students walked to the capital from across the country, and candidates promised a judiciary and prosecution free of political pressure and education and healthcare equally available to all.

The movement began after a concrete canopy collapsed at Novi Sad railway station in November 2024, killing 16 people. Protesters blamed corruption and failures in construction oversight, and the campaign widened from demands for a transparent investigation into a nationwide anti-corruption movement calling for early elections and institutional reform. The list operates without a single leader and has avoided detailed foreign policy commitments in order to hold together left, centre and right-leaning voters.

Vucic held his own closing rally at a sports hall in New Belgrade the same evening. Analysts cited in the coverage say a student-led government would face immediate domestic priorities before foreign policy, though its candidates have taken a hard line on Kosovo, proposed reviewing agreements reached under EU auspices and floated a referendum on EU membership within six months of taking office. Moscow has portrayed the protests as a Western-backed colour revolution, while the United States Congress has voiced concern about democratic standards and Serbia's ties with Russia.

What is at stake economically is the direction of a candidate country whose EU accession has stalled over rule of law concerns and limited alignment with EU sanctions, including those against Russia. A change of government would not automatically transform that position, but democratic reform, judicial independence and anti-corruption enforcement are the conditions Brussels has set for reviving the process. Polls close on Sunday, October 25.
