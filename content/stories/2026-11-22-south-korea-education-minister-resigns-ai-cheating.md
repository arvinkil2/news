Education Minister Choi Kyo-jin stepped down on Sunday after last minute measures against AI assisted cheating drew criticism ahead of South Korea's national college entrance exam, the College Scholastic Ability Test, which took place last week. President Lee Jae Myung accepted the resignation the same day, the presidential office said.

"Now that the CSAT is over, I keenly feel responsible and am offering my resignation," Choi said in a statement released through the Ministry of Education. Chief presidential spokesperson Kang Yu-jung said the president had been briefed and accepted, and that selection of a new minister would proceed according to procedure. Seoul Economic Daily reported Choi offered his resignation at around 11:30am and the acceptance was announced about an hour later.

Concern over AI assisted cheating grew after the top scorer on the certified tax accountant exam admitted on November 15 to cheating with a miniature camera and AI, four days before the entrance exam. The ministry, which had initially focused on smart glasses, rushed out additional measures in the final days, drawing criticism that the response came too late and kept changing.

The Ministry of Education said it caught 111 suspected cases of cheating with electronic devices during Thursday's exam. Lee wrote on social media that concerns over cheating that exploits AI are spreading beyond the entrance exam to essay based admissions and school tests, adding that the fairness of exams is a principle that can never be compromised.
