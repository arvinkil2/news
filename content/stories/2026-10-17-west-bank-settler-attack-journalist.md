**Latest (Sunday 04:43 ET):** Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the United States is very disturbed by the images of the attack on Heidi Levine and has asked Israel to crack down on settler violence. "We've asked the Israelis and continue to ask them to crack down on that stuff," Rubio told reporters, adding that President Trump has been very clear and repeated that he does not want to see friction or a crisis in the West Bank, or a new spark there. Rubio said he was going off media reports, that Washington had inquired with its embassy and expected a cable back later today or tomorrow, and that lifting sanctions on settlers earlier in the presidency did not mean the president supports what is happening now. He said the administration would learn all the facts first and then communicate to the Israelis how it expects them to prevent a recurrence. In Israel, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's office said Levine had been "allegedly assaulted", that the circumstances were unclear and under investigation, and that those responsible would be held accountable, giving a version in which some 200 Arabs and far-left activists confronted approximately 40 Israeli youths, with both sides throwing objects. Netanyahu's chief rival, Gadi Eisenkot, called Friday's attack unacceptable and blamed a handful of rioters whose actions stain an entire nation. A United States State Department official condemned criminal violence by any party in the West Bank, called for legal accountability and said the department had reached out to the Washington Post to offer consular assistance. The Foreign Press Association said the settlers also stole Levine's passport, phone, wallet and equipment. Levine was transferred to Israel's Beilinson Hospital in Petah Tikva, where doctors found four broken vertebrae, and she will need surgery, AFP reported.

Latest (Saturday 12:42 ET): Masked settlers on Saturday attacked a convoy of European diplomats and officials trying to reach the largely Christian village of Al-Taybeh near Ramallah, hurling stones at the vehicles and smashing a window of a Belgian consulate car, WAFA reported; the delegation included the French consul in Jerusalem and representatives from Belgium and Poland, and video showed a French town mayor in the delegation on the ground after the confrontation. The Israeli military condemned the attack, said police had opened an investigation, and noted the tour had not been coordinated with Israeli forces. The UN said on Friday that more than 200 settler attacks on olive harvesters have been recorded this season, surpassing the total for the entire 2025 harvest, killing one Palestinian, injuring more than 120 and damaging over 2,500 olive trees. Levine, the Washington Post photojournalist beaten on Friday, underwent surgery on Saturday after suffering a finger cut to the bone, a broken rib and multiple fractured vertebrae, her daughter told the Associated Press, and the Post condemned the assault and called for an investigation. Further attacks were reported on Saturday at Deir Dibwan and near Aqraba.

Dozens of masked Israeli settlers attacked Palestinian villagers during the annual olive harvest at Abu Falah in the occupied West Bank on Friday, burning olive trees, throwing rocks and injuring at least two people, including Washington Post photojournalist Heidi Levine, according to Reuters witnesses at the scene. About 80 settlers, some carrying gas canisters, came down from a hill above the terraced grove where villagers had gathered to pick the harvest, and some used slings to launch rocks at the villagers.

Palestinian medics said two people were taken to hospitals near Ramallah. Levine, who is in stable condition, said from her hospital bed that she was pushed to the ground and repeatedly kicked, and that her flak jacket and helmet likely saved her life. Video of the attack showed her lying on a dirt path with her hands over her face as at least three settlers surrounded her.

Israeli military jeeps passed through the area during the attack, and witnesses said soldiers fired tear gas toward the Palestinians; a Reuters photographer saw at least one armed settler fire a gun. The Israeli military did not immediately respond to requests for comment. An earlier attack the same morning near Turmus Ayya village saw settlers burn three cars after attacking farmers.

The Israeli monitoring group Yesh Din counts at least 170 settler attacks since September 27, around the start of the olive harvest. The West Bank is home to some 500,000 settlers and at least 3 million Palestinians; UN bodies and most countries deem the settlements illegal under international law, a position Israel rejects. The violence is now a diplomatic file as well as a security one, with longtime Israeli allies including Britain weighing sanctions over settler attacks.
