Soybeans led the grain complex higher in Friday's shortened post Thanksgiving session. January soybeans rose 1.8 percent to 12.66 and a quarter dollars a bushel, up 44 cents on the week and the highest close since October 23, with support from strong soybean meal exports and a recovering Brazilian real, Dow Jones reported. March corn rose 1.4 percent to 4.63 dollars, its highest close of November, and March Chicago wheat added 0.6 percent to 5.91 dollars, with Kansas City hard red winter wheat up 5 and a half cents at 6.36 dollars.

Export sales data for the week ended November 19 cut both ways. Corn sales for the 2026/27 marketing year totalled 1.93 million metric tons, up 21 percent from the week before and a multi week high, and wheat sales of 564,683 tons topped the range of analyst estimates. Soybean sales disappointed at 755,816 tons for 2026/27, a six week low, partly offset by a flash sale to the Netherlands reported during the session.

The rest of the farm complex moved with the grains. March cotton rose 127 points to 84.24 cents a pound, its highest close since October 22 and a weekly gain of 495 points, and March sugar settled 2.34 percent higher. In livestock, December live cattle closed at 224.35 dollars per hundredweight, up 1.02 dollars on technical buying ahead of cash trade, while lean hogs remained the weak leg of the complex near six year lows.

Traders' attention now turns to South American weather and the Southern Hemisphere wheat harvest. The soybean rally has filled a late October chart gap, and holding above it into full liquidity on Monday is the first test of whether Friday's move was pent up demand or holiday positioning.
