Saudi Arabia is preparing to postpone its Future Minerals Forum as regional attacks threaten the January gathering, the Financial Times reported on Thursday. Organizers have warned speakers and participants that the Riyadh event will likely need to be rescheduled, according to the newspaper. The forum told the newspaper it had not been cancelled and that updates would come through official channels; as of Friday, the event's website was still advertising the January 12 to 14 dates.

The uncertainty follows a Houthi attack on the headquarters of Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund in Riyadh on Tuesday that injured 11 people, the Financial Times reported. Other major Saudi events have already shifted: Cityscape Global's organizers moved the real estate exhibition from November to February 1 to 4, 2027, and the Global Health Exhibition has been deferred until October 2027.

Saudi Arabia has hosted five editions of the forum, which brings together governments, mining companies and investors to discuss mineral supply and development. The January 2026 gathering drew 21,500 participants and produced 132 agreements and memorandums of understanding valued at 100 billion Saudi riyals, about $26.7 billion, organizers said, and its ministerial roundtable included representatives from 91 countries. Past speakers included executives from BHP, Rio Tinto, Vale and Codelco.

Under Vision 2030, Saudi Arabia is seeking to reduce its reliance on oil by expanding other industries, with mining intended to become a third pillar of industrial development alongside oil and petrochemicals. The forum is part of that effort to attract investment and build mining partnerships across Africa, Western Asia and Central Asia, and the published 2027 programme carries the theme "global collaboration for resilient mineral supply".
