Norges Bank Investment Management, which manages Norway's sovereign wealth fund, has committed 1.2 billion euros to Copenhagen Infrastructure Partners' sixth flagship renewable energy fund, CI VI. The agreement was signed on October 1 and surfaced in this scan's reporting window on October 14.

CI VI will invest in renewable generation and storage projects at the development stage across OECD markets in North America, Western Europe and Asia Pacific. It follows NBIM's 900 million euro commitment to the predecessor fund, CI V, in 2024, its first indirect investment in unlisted renewable infrastructure. CI V closed at 12 billion euros; CI VI is reported to be targeting 16 billion euros.

The commitment is large in infrastructure terms and small in sovereign terms. Unlisted renewable infrastructure was 0.5 percent of the Norwegian fund's portfolio at mid year, against 72.1 percent in equities, and its mandate allows up to 2 percent. NBIM has been building the sleeve deliberately, including a separate commitment to Brookfield's Global Transition Fund, and describes the CIP relationship, running since 2024, as one that has worked well.

The read across is about who funds development stage risk. Greenfield generation and storage carry construction and power price risk that listed markets price harshly; sovereign capital with no redemption pressure is increasingly the anchor that lets flagship funds take it.
