Canada Nickel and Swiss Steel Group have signed an agreement outlining a long term strategic partnership under which Swiss Steel would get exclusive access to about one million tonnes a year of semi-finished steel products from Canada, starting in 2030. The agreement, signed on October 23, sets the framework for the intended collaboration rather than a final supply contract, and the companies will now work on commercial and technical terms.

The feedstock is not nickel sulphate but steel. Through its subsidiary NetZero Metals, Canada Nickel wants to transform the nickel chromium magnetite concentrate produced by its Crawford nickel project near Timmins, Ontario, into stainless and specialty steel billets and blooms for Canadian and European markets. An initial supply concept has been developed and technically validated for Swiss Steel facilities in the United States, Canada, Germany, Switzerland and France.

Swiss Steel chief executive Frank Koch said the partnership supports its strategy of focusing its own industrial footprint while securing flexible access to competitive, low carbon pre-material. Canada Nickel chief executive Mark Selby framed the timing around Canada hosting the European Union later this week, arguing that Canadian mineral resources and low carbon energy combined with Swiss Steel's manufacturing expertise and European market access can build secure, low carbon supply chains. Ontario minister George Pirie called it a perfect example of Northern Ontario products benefiting the world.

Shares in Canada Nickel rose 7.61 percent to 99 cents on Monday morning in Toronto. The reaction prices an option, not a contract: Crawford is still advancing toward development, the partnership volume is a 2030 target, and no price, capital commitment or final investment decision was announced. What the framework does change is Crawford's financing narrative, because a named industrial customer for a million tonnes a year of downstream product is the kind of evidence lenders and strategic investors ask for before a construction decision.
