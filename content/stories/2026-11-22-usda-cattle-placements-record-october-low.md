Cattle and calves on feed in US feedlots with capacity of 1,000 or more head totaled 11.590 million head on November 1, down 1.0 percent from a year earlier, the USDA's National Agricultural Statistics Service reported on Friday.

Placements during October totaled 1.891 million head, down 7.3 percent from October 2025 and the lowest for the month since the series began in 1996. Net placements were 1.84 million head. The decline was steeper than traders expected: a Dow Jones survey put the average estimate at 96.5 percent of a year ago, against the actual 93 percent. Marketings of fed cattle totaled 1.620 million head, down 4.6 percent and also the lowest for October in the series.

The placement fall was concentrated in the big feeding states. Nebraska placements fell 11.6 percent to 495,000 head, Kansas fell 9.9 percent to 410,000, Oklahoma fell 19.4 percent to 50,000 and Texas fell 4.9 percent to 390,000. By weight, placements of cattle under 600 pounds were 490,000 head, and the 700 to 899 pound groups together accounted for most of the national decline.

Other disappearance totaled 53,000 head, down 1.9 percent. With both entries into feedlots and exits to slaughter at record October lows, the report describes a pipeline that is shrinking from both ends.
