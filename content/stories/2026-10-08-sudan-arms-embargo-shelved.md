The United Nations Security Council extended the arms embargo on Sudan's Darfur region for another two months on October 8, unanimously adopting Resolution 2830, but failed to agree on the US proposal to widen it to the whole country after Russia and China opposed the move. The sanctions regime, first imposed in 2005 under Resolution 1591, now runs to December 11, with the mandate of the five-member panel of experts that monitors the measures extended to January 11, 2027.

The two-month technical rollover follows a pattern of shortening the horizon: the Council had already agreed on September 11 to renew the regime for just one month, to October 9, rather than the usual annual cycle, with Washington warning it would not accept a longer extension without changes reflecting the conflict on the ground. The Darfur-only regime covers an arms embargo on armed actors in the region, a travel ban and asset freeze on designated individuals, alongside the experts panel.

The expansion bid's failure was a straight great-power block. US Ambassador Jeff Bartos told the Council last month that "the current sanctions regime is over 20 years outdated, and is insufficient to address the realities of the war today," urging members to "expand the arms embargo across the entire territory of Sudan." Russia's deputy UN ambassador Anna Evstigneeva answered that Moscow would "not allow even a hint of broadening the restrictions by the Council on Sudan," with China arguing for the existing regime. Sudan's own ambassador, Al-Harith Mohamed, argued a total embargo would run against the UN Charter's guarantee of the right to self-defence.

The war between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces, which began in April 2023, has killed at least 59,000 people, though aid groups warn the true toll could be far higher. About 13 million people have been displaced and parts of the country face famine. The army holds central and eastern Sudan while the RSF controls Darfur and parts of the south, and Darfur has seen displacement camps attacked and repeated ethnic massacres.

The next concrete document is the December 11 rollover decision. Washington has now twice refused a long extension without reform, so the same fight returns in two months, with the added complication that the Council will be renegotiating under an even more compressed timetable.
