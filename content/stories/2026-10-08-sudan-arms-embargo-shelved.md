The United States has shelved its proposal to broaden the UN Security Council's Sudan arms embargo from the Darfur region to the entire country, according to The Hill's October 7 reporting, after failing to secure enough support among Council members. The US-backed plan would have covered all of Sudan's territory and warring factions, eliminating the geographic loopholes traffickers use and adding restrictions on drone supplies.

The backdrop is a Council that agreed on September 11 to extend the existing Darfur-only sanctions regime for just one month, to October 9, rather than the usual annual rollover, with Washington warning it would not accept a longer extension without changes reflecting the conflict on the ground. The US argues a nationwide ban is necessary to curb the war between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the Rapid Support Forces; Russia has firmly opposed expanding the embargo beyond Darfur, arguing it would infringe Sudanese sovereignty, and several African Council members leaned against the proposal.

The war has killed tens of thousands of people, displaced millions and created one of the world's largest humanitarian crises, with an estimated 20 million people suffering from hunger. Sudan's Red Sea coastline keeps the conflict relevant to international maritime trade and energy logistics, and the continued movement of weaponry feeds geopolitical risk across the Horn of Africa.

With the proposal shelved, the immediate question is what the Council does tomorrow when the current regime expires: a straight technical rollover of the Darfur-only measures is the likely fallback, but even that requires nine votes and no veto. The rollover vote is the concrete document to watch.
