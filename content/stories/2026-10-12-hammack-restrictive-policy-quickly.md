In prepared remarks to a National Association for Business Economics conference in Cleveland, Hammack said firms in her district plan to raise prices faster than their input costs, and that demand from the AI buildout is lifting prices for computer and electronic components well beyond data centres. She put underlying inflation in the mid to high 2 percent range and said it will take time to return to 2 percent even with appropriate policy.

Investors still expect the Fed to hold its policy rate at 3.75 to 4.00 percent at the October 27 to 28 meeting, per Reuters, and Hammack did not explicitly back a hike at that meeting. Hammack also pushed back gently on Chair Kevin Warsh's preference for a quieter Fed, arguing policymakers owe the public an explanation of how they will react to new data.
