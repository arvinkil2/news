The United States has formally extended its suspension of the Section 301 port fees on China-linked shipping for another two months. The USTR notice published in the Federal Register on Tuesday took effect on November 9 and runs through 11:59 p.m. Eastern on January 9, 2027, so no liability accrues for the maritime service fees on Chinese-owned or operated vessels, Chinese-built vessels and foreign-built vehicle carriers, and the added duties on Chinese ship-to-shore cranes and other cargo-handling equipment stay suspended as well.

The notice finalises the extension USTR proposed in October, after the one-year suspension agreed by Presidents Trump and Xi in November 2025 expired on November 9. The fees at stake run to millions of dollars for a single call, about $2.5 million for a 50,000 net ton vessel. China has extended its own suspension of reciprocal port fees on US-linked vessels until January 10, 2027.
