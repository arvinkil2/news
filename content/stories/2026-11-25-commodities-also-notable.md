Tanker freight rose 7.68 percent to 818.41 dollars at Tuesday's close in the estate market tables, extending its weekly gain to 26.33 percent, while dry bulk freight was nearly flat, down 0.11 percent to 12.65 dollars. The tanker move sits alongside today's Hormuz mine claims and the World Food Programme's Black Sea briefing: crude prices are falling on surplus expectations, but the cost of moving oil and grain by sea keeps rising. The full freight context is in today's Hormuz story, updated with this close.

Metals steadied after the liquidation of the past two weeks. Gold futures were nearly flat, down 0.10 percent to 3,558.30 dollars an ounce, silver fell 0.59 percent to 40.52 dollars, and copper rose 0.64 percent to 5.40 dollars a pound. The weekly picture remains weak, with gold down 4.91 percent, silver down 9.95 percent and copper down 4.64 percent, and the mining shares fell again on Tuesday, copper miners down 4.27 percent and gold miners down 1.96 percent in the estate mining table.

The December natural gas contract expires on NYMEX today, November 25, the event flagged in yesterday's roundup. The January premium over December is the market's price for the first full winter month, and today's gas story carries the fourth-session rally in full.

Australia's consumer price index rose 4.9 percent in the year to October 2026, per the Australian Bureau of Statistics release in today's pool. The print belongs to the rates edition, where it is covered; it is noted here only because it was flagged in this beat's gap check.
