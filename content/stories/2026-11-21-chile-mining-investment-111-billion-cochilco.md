The Chilean Copper Commission, Cochilco, forecasts $111.1 billion in mining investment between 2026 and 2035, the highest level in 13 years. The portfolio grew 6.3 percent from $104.5 billion in the previous survey and now covers 72 projects, up from 59. Copper still dominates, accounting for 84.5 percent of planned spending, or $93.9 billion, including BHP's $1.3 billion Escondida leaching extension, designed to sustain 210,000 tonnes a year of copper cathode production, and Capstone Copper's planned $500 million of improvements at Mantos Blancos, extending operations to 2041.

Lithium drove the increase. Chile's lithium investment pipeline has more than doubled to $10.8 billion, and lithium contributed $6.1 billion of the $6.6 billion rise in the total portfolio. Two projects under environmental review account for that $6.1 billion, more than half of planned lithium investment: Albemarle's $3.1 billion transition to direct lithium extraction, which the company says could recover nearly twice as much lithium as its existing operation, and the $3 billion Salar Futuro project of NovaAndino Litio, the Codelco and SQM partnership, which aims to sustain Salar de Atacama operations from 2031 to 2060 using the same technology. Both are before the Environmental Assessment Service, and their permitting progress will determine when the investments can advance.

Antofagasta leads the regions with $51.5 billion of planned investment, about 46 percent of the national total. Cochilco's acting executive vice president Claudia Rodríguez, presenting the outlook in Shanghai, said the pipeline contains more initiatives with advanced technical and environmental definitions. GEM Mining Consulting has warned that Argentina could overtake Chile as South America's largest lithium producer by 2035.
