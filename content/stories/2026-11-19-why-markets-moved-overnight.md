Wheat rose 4.12 percent to 593.5 cents a bushel and corn 3.79 percent to 452 cents, the two largest moves in the midday tables. No story published today explains the grain move, and no cause is offered here.

The mining complex fell again. Silver miners lost 2.49 percent and gold miners 2.23 percent, taking their weekly losses to 12.90 percent and 10.62 percent, as gold futures fell 1.42 percent to 3,688.8 dollars an ounce and silver futures 1.14 percent to 44.49 dollars. BHP fell 1.52 percent and copper miners 1.34 percent, with copper itself down only 0.34 percent to 5.64 dollars a pound. No story today gives a miner-specific catalyst. The fall extends the week's metals rout, and today's European Central Bank Financial Stability Review sets out the wider frame: market pricing rests on extraordinary earnings expectations for AI firms, and a shift in that narrative could reprice bond and equity markets together (see “ECB warns AI valuations and sovereign debt cloud stability outlook”).

Brent crude fell 2.02 percent to 90.09 dollars a barrel and is down 5.98 percent on the week, while WTI crude was flat at 83.35 dollars. Today's EIA figures show oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz rebounded to 9.1 million barrels a day in the third quarter from 5.1 million in the second, a partial recovery in Gulf supply, though still less than half the 21.4 million a day of a year earlier (see “Hormuz oil flows rebound to 9.1 million barrels a day”). Tanker freight rose 1.69 percent to 733.36 dollars and is up 25.99 percent on the week: the same EIA release shows Hormuz LNG flows recovered only to 1.4 billion cubic feet a day against 10.9 billion a year earlier, so shipping still prices disrupted routes even as crude volumes return.

Natural gas fell 2.19 percent to 3.30 dollars per million BTU. No story published today explains the move.

The cross-market read is risk-off in paper markets and a supply bid in physical ones. The Dow Jones fell 1.45 percent, the S&P 500 0.97 percent and the Nasdaq 100 0.88 percent, with semiconductors down 1.14 percent, in line with today's Asia coverage of Taiwan's 4.4 percent fall on AI doubts (see “Asia stocks slide as Taiwan falls 4.6 percent on AI doubts”). The 10-year Treasury yield held at 5.07 percent, up 0.2 basis points on the day, and the dollar index was flat at 103.84, so the pressure sits in valuations and in the metals complex, not in rates or the currency.
