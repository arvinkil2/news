Tanker freight was the market's biggest mover, up 11.32 percent to 721.2 dollars and up 32.74 percent on the week, as Hormuz shipping risk stayed priced in after the attack on an ADNOC vessel in the strait (see the November 18 story on the ADNOC vessel attack). Crude itself moved the other way, with Brent down 2.86 percent to 89.32 dollars a barrel, so freight, not oil, is carrying the risk premium.

The mining complex took the session's losses. Copper miners fell 6.31 percent, gold miners 5.97 percent and silver miners 5.89 percent, with BHP down 4.83 percent and Rio Tinto down 4.49 percent, extending the rout that began with copper's plunge to its lowest since April (see the November 18 copper story). The miner baskets are now down between 12 and 17 percent on the week, falling much faster than copper itself, which rose 0.46 percent on the day, or gold, down 0.19 percent with rate-hike pressure keeping it near a 14-month low after the Fed minutes and the Waller and Barr speeches pointed to further hikes.

Amazon rose 3.82 percent, the technology table's biggest gainer, a day after Anthropic priced its IPO above its range; this morning's technology roundup sets out the filing detail, which puts Amazon's stake in Anthropic at 14.7 percent after the listing. Wheat rose 3.55 percent to 590.2 cents a bushel and corn 2.99 percent, with no clear single driver in this morning's reporting.

The cross-market read is a split tape: physical shipping and grains bid on supply risk, paper metals and the miners that dig them sold on higher-for-longer rate expectations, and the largest AI winners separating from the chip complex rather than moving with it. Taiwan's 4.4 percent fall and its new short-selling curbs, in today's rates coverage, show the same AI markdown running through Asia.
