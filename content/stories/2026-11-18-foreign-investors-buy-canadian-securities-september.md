Non-resident investors acquired 19.5 billion Canadian dollars of Canadian securities in September 2026, mainly short-term debt instruments, while Canadian investors increased their holdings of foreign securities by 13.5 billion dollars, generating a net inflow of 6.0 billion dollars into the Canadian economy, Statistics Canada said. Foreign buying of Canadian money market instruments reached 14.9 billion dollars, the largest investment since November 2024, including 8.8 billion dollars of corporate paper and 5.1 billion dollars of federal government paper, the highest in a year; foreigners also added 7.7 billion dollars of private corporate bonds, mainly foreign currency instruments issued by chartered banks.

The debt inflow came alongside continued equity selling: non-residents reduced holdings of Canadian equities by 2.6 billion dollars in September, after a 9.2 billion dollar divestment in August, which Statistics Canada attributed to share retirements from cross-border merger and acquisition activity. Canadian investors bought 12.5 billion dollars of foreign bonds, the most since July 2025, led by US corporate and non-US bonds with pension funds the main buyers, while reducing foreign equity holdings by 2.5 billion dollars.
