Poundland called in administrators on Wednesday, putting 12,000 jobs at risk in the run-up to Christmas. AlixPartners was appointed by the chain's directors after a sales process "did not identify a viable solvent solution for the business as a whole," and owner Gordon Brothers rejected at least two last-ditch bids for the whole company, judging it could raise more from a break-up. The board had filed notice of intention to appoint administrators in October, and moved before a November 26 creditor-protection deadline as shelves emptied and funds to restock ran short.

The chain's 600 stores in the UK and Ireland and its distribution centres will keep trading while the administrators pursue sales of the business and its assets, in whole or in part. A break-up is expected, with discount rivals such as Poundstretcher, B&M and The Range seen taking parcels of stores. Gordon Brothers, the US finance firm that bought Poundland from Pepco for a nominal £1 in 2025 and backed a restructuring that July, said it had "explored every avenue to secure a sale of the whole company" but that the chain remained vulnerable to inflation and any downturn in sales.
