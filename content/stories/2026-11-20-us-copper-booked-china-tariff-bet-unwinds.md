Traders have booked an estimated 30,000 to 100,000 tonnes of refined copper, held outside exchange warehouses on the US West Coast, to ship to China, BMO Capital Markets said on Friday, a flow its analysts had assigned a very low probability. China's Yangshan warrant premium, the benchmark for imported copper in bonded warehouses, reached a record $500 a tonne above London prices on Thursday, while three-month LME copper traded at $12,310.50 a tonne on Friday morning, down about 8.2 percent on the week, as investors retreated from AI-linked trades despite tight physical supply in China. BMO said traders it spoke to in China generally expect the Trump administration to abandon the proposed refined-copper duty, on which a decision is pending for rates of 15 percent from January 1, 2027 and 30 percent from 2028, while Western market participants still lean toward a tariff being imposed.
