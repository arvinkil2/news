Japan's finance minister Katayama Satsuki told the International Monetary and Financial Committee that the government will place the debt to GDP ratio on a firm declining path, finance a two year cut in the consumption tax on food and beverages and new income linked benefits without relying on deficit financing bonds, and reduce its reliance on supplementary budgets by funding regular programmes in the initial budget. The statement framed the package as the way to maintain market confidence as Japan completes its transition to a world with inflation and positive interest rates.

The fiscal language is the rates content of the document. Japan carries the largest government debt stock in the developed world and funds it in the JGB market at yields that have risen through this year's Bank of Japan tightening, including the September hike that took the policy rate to about 1.25 percent. A written commitment, delivered to the IMF's steering committee, to shrink the debt ratio while cutting a consumption tax is the strongest version of the consolidation pledge Tokyo has made at these meetings, and it sets the benchmark against which the autumn budget arithmetic will be judged.

On the global agenda, Japan put its support for Ukraine at approximately 20 billion dollars to date, called for arbitrary export restrictions on critical minerals to be withdrawn, and argued that both surplus and deficit countries must act on global imbalances, with surplus economies refraining from non market policies and deficit economies consolidating. It also backed a larger role for financial interconnectedness, not just GDP and trade, in the IMF quota formula, and pledged 3.5 million dollars over five years to the fund's new anti money laundering and anti corruption facility.

The statement sits alongside the committee's own closing text, published today as a separate developing story on this site, which warned that buffers have eroded and named rising sovereign yields in systemic economies as a vulnerability. Japan's national statement is the debtor's answer to that warning from the largest debtor of all.
