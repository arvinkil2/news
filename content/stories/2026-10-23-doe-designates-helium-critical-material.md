Secretary of Energy Chris Wright designated helium as a critical material under the Energy Act of 2020 on Friday, the Department of Energy said, after the department determined that helium meets the statutory definition because of its essential role in American manufacturing and its growing exposure to supply chain risks. The department said helium's properties make it indispensable to semiconductor production, with no viable substitutes in many critical applications, and that global demand is growing faster than production.

US helium is produced as a byproduct of natural gas processing in Kansas, Texas, Wyoming, Colorado, Arizona, New Mexico and Oklahoma, with Kansas leading production and Texas holding large production and storage infrastructure, Mining.com reported. The department said the United States has significant deposits of helium-rich natural gas, but limited refining capacity and lengthy permitting timelines continue to constrain production.

The designation lands while Europe is having difficulty securing helium, Euronews reported. Qatari helium exports have been severely disrupted by the closure of the Strait of Hormuz and Iranian strikes on Qatari infrastructure, Russia introduced temporary export restrictions in April, and China, a transit hub for Russian supplies reaching Europe, imposed a temporary export ban in July, Euronews said, citing a July Financial Times report for the transit role.
