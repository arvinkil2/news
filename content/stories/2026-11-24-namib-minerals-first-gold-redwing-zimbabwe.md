Namib Minerals has poured first gold at its century old Redwing mine in eastern Zimbabwe, marking a return to commercial production after a seven year hiatus. The company said the milestone comes after mine infrastructure and processing facilities were rehabilitated and recommissioned. Redwing historically produced about 650,000 ounces of gold between 1981 and 2023.

Chief executive Tulani Sikwila said first gold is the beginning of the next phase, not the end of the job, and that the focus now is to establish consistent, reliable production, demonstrate the operating performance of the mine and execute the planned ramp-up. The company plans a staged ramp-up, first establishing a stable mining and processing rhythm, then increasing mining and processing through the installation and commissioning of a higher capacity winder, targeting higher ore processing and gold production in 2027 depending on operating conditions.

Namib aims to build combined annual production of 300,000 ounces in the long term across Redwing, How Mine and Mazowe Mine, still subject to technical studies, permitting and financing, and expects to spend $300 million on mine restarts, Reuters reported. A definitive feasibility study is fully funded with a conclusion expected in the first quarter of 2027, and resource definition drilling will follow it.

Namib already owns and operates the How Mine in Bulawayo, which produced 25,000 ounces of gold in 2025, so Redwing's restart makes it a multi-asset producer. Shares in Namib Minerals were slightly down 2.29 percent to $0.60 by midday Monday in New York, valuing the company at $33.7 million, a company specific move on a day when gold miners rose more than 6 percent.
