Thailand's cabinet approved the standards on Tuesday, Deputy Prime Minister and Finance Minister Ekniti Nitithanprapas said, setting noise at no more than 70 A-weighted decibels at a facility's boundary, a boundary temperature rise of no more than 1 to 2 degrees Celsius, and recirculated cooling water that is inspected before discharge. Data centres drawing 20 megawatts or more must sit in industrial complexes under stricter supervision, while projects approved before August 14 keep operating with one year to comply.

Licensing moves to a one-stop service that weighs the standards alongside Thai jobs, technology education and local supply chain participation, and projects that fail the environmental and security requirements will not be allowed to proceed. The Board of Investment put investment applications in January to September above $50 billion, with AI related projects about 70 percent of the total, as resident opposition over power and water use grows across Southeast Asia, including protests on Indonesia's Batam island and a cancelled project in Malaysia.
