**Latest:** Eleven people were injured in Tuesday's strike on a tower in Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District, Saudi Civil Defence said, and three witnesses cited by AFP identified the building as the 385 metre Public Investment Fund headquarters, the sovereign wealth fund's tower. The injured were two Saudi citizens, two Indians, three Nepalese and four Egyptians, the Civil Defence directorate said, and India's embassy in Riyadh confirmed two Indian nationals were hurt. The same tower was damaged in a missile strike on November 19 that caused no injuries, an attack the Houthis did claim.

Saudi led coalition spokesman Major General Turki al-Maliki called the strike a "cowardly terrorist attack" and said the deliberate targeting of civilians and civilian objects by the Houthis would be met with a firm response. The Houthis had not claimed responsibility for Tuesday's strike by late afternoon, so the attribution rests on Saudi officials.

A tower in Saudi Arabia's financial district was targeted in a terrorist attack, Maj. Gen. Turki Al-Malki, spokesperson for the Coalition to Restore Legitimacy in Yemen, said on Tuesday, according to Arab News. Al-Malki said competent authorities are responding at the site.
