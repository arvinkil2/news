Democrats have won the Senate majority, The Hill reported at 11:19pm Eastern, recapturing the chamber they lost in 2024. The Hill names no single clinching race and gives no seat total; Democrats needed a net gain of four seats, and the flips it lists run from North Carolina, the first of the night, through South Carolina and Texas to Kansas. The win returns Senator Chuck Schumer to the majority leader's office in 2027 and gives Democrats control of the Senate schedule, expanded oversight and hearing power, and a decisive say over administration and judicial nominations, which The Hill says puts President Trump's legislative agenda in limbo even in the lame-duck session.

Decision Desk HQ projects former Senator Sherrod Brown to win the Ohio Senate race, defeating appointed Republican Senator Jon Husted and completing a comeback two years after losing his seat, The Hill reported at 11:20pm Eastern. Brown led by just over 2 points with 83 percent of ballots counted in one of the night's tighter races, in a special election for the remainder of Vice President Vance's term; Brown would serve until 2028, when he must run again for a full term. Ohio is a fifth Democratic Senate pickup, beyond the four needed for the majority.

In the governors' races, Democrat Gina Hinojosa defeated Republican Governor Greg Abbott in Texas, with Abbott conceding, The Hill, Fox News and Bloomberg reported; Hinojosa would be the first Latina governor of Texas. In Ohio, Democrat Amy Acton defeated Republican Vivek Ramaswamy in the governor's race, Al Jazeera reported.

Decision Desk HQ projects Democrat Adam Hamilton to win the Senate race in Kansas, unseating Republican Senator Roger Marshall by almost 9 points with 87 percent of votes cast, The Hill reported at 10:51pm Eastern. Hamilton, 62, a Methodist pastor, would be the first Democratic senator from Kansas since 1939, in a seat Decision Desk HQ had rated a toss up going into election day; The Hill reports the projection all but ensures a Senate majority for Democrats, as a fourth pickup after North Carolina, South Carolina and Texas.

In South Korea, the Kospi index slumped as much as 3.7 percent in Wednesday trading, erasing an early gain, Bloomberg reported, with Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix each down at least 4 percent and the biggest drags on the index. Bloomberg attributed the chip selloff to concern that a strong Democratic showing in the midterms would bring greater scrutiny of the artificial intelligence industry.

Democrat Annie Andrews is projected to win the Senate race in South Carolina, defeating Republican Senator Darline Graham in the night's biggest upset so far. Decision Desk HQ made the projection, The Hill reported, with Andrews leading by 4 points with 84 percent of the expected vote in; the Associated Press also called the race, and the Guardian records Andrews as the first Democrat to win a Senate seat in South Carolina since 1998. Graham, the sister of the late Senator Lindsey Graham, was appointed by Governor Henry McMaster to complete her brother's term after President Trump personally picked her to succeed him. Andrews, a pediatrician and first-time candidate from Charleston, campaigned on health care costs. The flip is Democrats' second Senate pickup of the night after North Carolina.

Decision Desk HQ also projects Democrat James Talarico to win the Senate race in Texas, defeating Republican Ken Paxton for the seat John Cornyn held since 2002, The Hill reported at 10:16pm Eastern. Talarico, a 37-year-old state lawmaker, outraised Paxton heavily through the campaign, while a super PAC backed by Elon Musk spent $158 million supporting Paxton, according to The Hill. The Associated Press had not called Texas in the results pages checked at about 10:47pm Eastern, so this projection rests on Decision Desk HQ alone for now. With Texas, Democrats have three pickups against one Republican hold, in Florida, and the Guardian's live tally put the Senate at 46 Democrats to 37 Republicans with 18 of 35 races called; control of the chamber has not been called, and the races in Ohio, Michigan, Alaska and Iowa remain open.

Markets moved on the results in Asian trading. Bloomberg reported the Swiss franc up almost 1 percent and the yen up 0.7 percent, the biggest gains among major currencies, with its dollar spot index down as much as 0.4 percent, the most since October 2. Solar shares jumped: Hanwha Solutions rose as much as 10 percent in South Korea, and Enphase Energy, SolarEdge and First Solar rose between 8.7 and 12 percent in US overnight trading, Bloomberg reported.

Decision Desk HQ projects Democrats to win control of the House of Representatives, The Hill and Bloomberg reported on Tuesday night, flipping the chamber after four years in the minority and creating divided government for the rest of President Trump's second term. Neither report gives a seat count or names a clinching race, and Bloomberg notes that other news organizations had not yet made a call on the House majority. The Hill reports that House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries is expected to become Speaker when the new Congress votes on January 3, and that Democrats have vowed investigations beginning on their first day, alongside early bills on health care costs; none of that displaces the Senate, whose control has not been called.

The Associated Press projects Democrat Roy Cooper, the former governor, to win the Senate race in North Carolina, defeating the Trump-backed Republican Michael Whatley and flipping a Republican-held seat. At the snapshot read at about 9:12pm Eastern, Cooper had 60.2 percent of the vote to 37.8 percent for Whatley, with an estimated 78.3 percent of the vote counted. The Guardian describes the contest as the most expensive Senate race of the midterms, and the flip gives Democrats their first Senate pickup of the night.

In Florida, the Associated Press projects Republican Ashley Moody to hold the Senate seat she was appointed to in January 2025 after Marco Rubio became secretary of state, defeating Democrat Angie Nixon. Moody had 50.9 percent to 48.0 percent for Nixon at the same snapshot, with an estimated 97.9 percent of the vote counted, and she will serve the remaining two years of Rubio's term. The hold keeps a Republican seat off the Democratic pickup path, which now runs through the uncalled races in Texas, Ohio, Michigan, Alaska and Iowa.

Decision Desk HQ projects Democrat Chris Pappas to win the open Senate seat in New Hampshire, defeating former Senator John Sununu and holding the seat of retiring Democratic Senator Jeanne Shaheen. The Hill reported that shortly after 8pm Eastern Pappas had nearly 61 percent of the vote with 22 percent of ballots counted. The race had moved from the Democratic column to a toss-up and back during the campaign, and the hold denies Republicans a second pickup target on the night.

Decision Desk HQ projects Democratic Senator Jon Ossoff to win re-election in Georgia, defeating Republican Representative Mike Collins. The Hill reported the projection at 7:22pm Eastern, with Ossoff leading with about 80 percent of the vote to nearly 20 percent for Collins, with 11 percent of precincts reporting. Georgia was one of the few legitimate Republican pickup opportunities in the Senate, in a state President Trump carried in 2024, and Ossoff had led in polling and outraised Collins through the campaign.

The Guardian assesses that Democrats are poised to take control of the House, where Republicans hold 218 seats to 214 for Democrats and a majority requires 218. That is an assessment, not a call: the Guardian cites the Cook Political Report forecast of a Democratic pickup of 15 to 25 seats, which would give Democrats 230 to 240 seats, and notes that uncertainty remains over the size of the gain. That assessment preceded the Decision Desk HQ call reported above; the Senate contests in Texas, Ohio, Michigan, Alaska and Iowa remain the deciding races for that chamber.

Earlier tonight, polls began closing at 6pm Eastern in Indiana and Kentucky, and the first projection of the night went to Republicans: Decision Desk HQ projects Ed Gallrein, a retired Navy SEAL and farmer endorsed by President Trump, to win Kentucky's 4th Congressional District, the seat held by Republican Thomas Massie. Gallrein led Democrat Melissa Strange by 59 percent to 30 percent on a small share of the vote reported, after defeating Massie for the Republican nomination in May by 54.9 percent to 45.1 percent. Massie had repeatedly clashed with the president, backing war powers resolutions on Iran and legislation to force release of the Epstein files.

Voting is under way across the United States on Tuesday in midterm elections that will decide control of Congress for the final two years of President Donald Trump's second term. All 435 seats in the House of Representatives and 35 of the Senate's 100 seats are contested, and voters in 36 states are also choosing governors.

Republicans go into the day holding both chambers, with a 53 to 47 majority in the Senate and a House majority at the 218 seat threshold. Democrats need a net gain of three seats to take the House and four to take the Senate, where a 50 to 50 tie would be broken by the Republican vice president. More than 51 million people had already voted early or by mail before election day, passing the early vote total recorded in the 2022 midterms.

The outcome will turn on a small number of Senate contests. The final New York Times and Siena polls, covered in Monday's edition, put Democrats ahead in Texas, Ohio, Michigan and Alaska and effectively tied in Iowa, and the Cook Political Report moved Michigan to lean Democrat in its final rating. Polls close in waves from 6pm Eastern in Indiana and Kentucky to about 1am Eastern in Alaska, and close races, late mail ballots and ranked choice counts in Alaska and Maine could leave control undecided for days.

The vote is the first nationwide test of the Trump administration since it returned to office in January 2025, and markets are watching for a Democratic sweep that would divide government for the rest of the term. Official certification follows weeks after election night in most states, so early projections are not final results.
