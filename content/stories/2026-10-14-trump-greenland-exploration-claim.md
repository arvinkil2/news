President Trump wrote on Truth Social that massive oil, gas and mineral exploration is getting ready to begin in Greenland, saying experts believe the island's easily available oil could exceed that of almost any other land in the world, and casting Greenland as a defensive shield for the United States and Europe against future attack. He gave no details on who would explore, where, or when, and there was no immediate comment from the leaders of Greenland or Denmark in the reports read.

Greenland is a self governing Danish territory whose mineral licensing sits with its own government in Nuuk. The island hosts rare earth, graphite, uranium and base metal prospects that have drawn Western strategic interest for years, but very few are in production, and oil and gas exploration has been restricted under Greenlandic policy in recent years. A social media announcement does not change any licence, permit or fiscal term by itself.

The post follows the September 22 security agreement among the United States, Denmark and Greenland, which expanded the American military presence on the island and mentioned resource extraction and minerals cooperation without specifics.

For mining investors, the practical test is what follows: any change to Greenland's hydrocarbons policy, new exploration licensing rounds, or named US backing for specific critical mineral projects. None of that was in Wednesday's post.

Update, October 15: Denmark's Foreign Ministry has reaffirmed that the September agreement between Denmark, Greenland and the United States does not alter the rules for resource extraction and grants US investors no preferential treatment over other NATO and EU investors, Al Jazeera reported. The agreement allocated no mining or drilling rights. Greenland banned uranium mining and all future oil exploration in 2021, and its government is reviewing whether Greenland Energy can use permits that predate the ban for drilling planned in 2027.

Industry reaction split along expected lines. Greenland Mines, which is advancing the Sarfartoq rare earth and Skaergaard palladium, platinum and gold projects, publicly welcomed the presidential focus on Greenlandic minerals, while Danish analysts quoted by Al Jazeera cautioned that detail from the US side should be treated carefully. No licence, company contract or Greenlandic policy change has followed the original post.
