Chen Jining, a member of the Political Bureau of the Communist Party of China Central Committee and secretary of the party's Shanghai Municipal Committee, is under disciplinary and supervisory investigation, Xinhua News Agency said on Tuesday, citing an official statement. He is being investigated by the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection and the National Commission of Supervision for suspected severe violations of discipline and laws, the statement said. It gave no further detail.

The investigation makes Chen the fourth member of the Politburo to be probed since 2025, Reuters reported. Before Tuesday's announcement, three members of the 24-member body formed in 2022, He Weidong, Zhang Youxia and Ma Xingrui, had already been purged, and the widening anti-corruption campaign under President Xi Jinping has thinned the party's senior leadership ranks and depleted the military's top command. For officials of Chen's rank, the public announcement of an investigation by the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection generally signals their eventual political downfall, Reuters noted.

Chen, 62, had an unusual rise through China's official ranks. An environmental scientist by training, he earned a doctorate from Imperial College London, rose through Tsinghua University to become its president, and entered government as China's environmental protection minister before becoming mayor of Beijing. He joined the Politburo in 2022 and was named party boss of Shanghai, China's financial hub.

The Shanghai party secretary post is one of China's most prestigious provincial-level positions and has often served as a springboard to top leadership roles. Xi briefly served as Shanghai's party boss in 2007 before joining the Politburo Standing Committee, and Premier Li Qiang and Vice President Han Zheng also held the post before moving into central leadership roles.
