Torex Resources is considering two mill upgrades at its Morelos complex in Mexico as part of a plan to nearly double output within five years, according to Red Cloud Securities. The larger expansion would cost about $100 million (C$142.2 million) and lift processing capacity to 14,500 tonnes a day, Red Cloud mining analyst Ron Stewart said on Thursday in a note. A construction decision is targeted for the first quarter of 2028.

An earlier $5 million to $10 million upgrade would remove processing bottlenecks and raise capacity by about 15 percent by the end of 2027, Torex's latest earnings presentation shows. The next milestone is Media Luna North, where Torex expects first ore before year end; the company forecasts an average mining rate of about 1,000 tonnes a day in 2027, rising toward design capacity of about 2,300 tonnes a day.

Red Cloud's Stewart sees a path to an annualised production rate of about 800,000 gold equivalent ounces by the end of 2031, up from roughly 450,000 now. The Morelos mine in Guerrero state could supply about 650,000 ounces of that total, with the remainder coming from the Los Reyes gold silver project in Sinaloa state.

Los Reyes would require an initial $515 million, which Torex expects to fund from Morelos cash flow. The July preliminary economic assessment forecasts annual output averaging 161,000 gold equivalent ounces during the first 11 years, comprising 111,000 ounces of gold and 3.6 million ounces of silver, with a prefeasibility study planned for 2027 and a feasibility study in 2028; construction would commence in 2029 on the preliminary timeline, with first production in 2031. Torex shares eased 0.2 percent to C$64.35 on Thursday morning in Toronto, valuing the company at about C$5.8 billion.
