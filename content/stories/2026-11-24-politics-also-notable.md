**White House anomalies request.** The White House is asking Congress to include billions of dollars in additional funding in the stopgap spending bill lawmakers are expected to take up next month, in a 12 page "anomalies" request sent on Monday and reported by Politico. The requests include a 5 billion dollar annualised funding rate for the Golden Dome missile defence programme, roughly 21.3 billion dollars in annualised increases for the Navy's submarine programmes with authority to begin purchasing materials for the first Trump class battleship, billions for NASA's Artemis programme and wildfire suppression, and a 70 million dollar annualised increase for infectious disease surveillance at the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Lawmakers face a December 11 deadline to avert a government shutdown, and the request also asks Congress to revise the federal treatment of hemp derived products before a delayed ban takes effect the same day.

**UK better regulation chief.** William Sargent has been appointed Chair of the Better Regulation Executive, restored by Chancellor John Healey at the Budget, the government announced on Tuesday. The Executive carries the target of cutting regulatory administrative burdens by 25 per cent, equivalent to 5.6 billion pounds or 280 million working hours a year, by the end of the Parliament; the government says it has so far made 2 billion pounds of net progress against the target. Sargent, who led the previous Better Regulation Executive's simplification programme between 2005 and 2010, will attend Cabinet and challenge departments and regulators to remove outdated and duplicative rules.

**GAO on Medicare prevention costs.** The Government Accountability Office reports that out of pocket costs can affect Medicare beneficiaries' use of services for cardiovascular disease prevention, noting that cardiac rehabilitation typically takes place over 36 sessions so even modest cost sharing accumulates, and that research has found beneficiaries with higher out of pocket costs were more likely to discontinue rehabilitation sessions or prescribed medication. About 90 per cent of beneficiaries with traditional Medicare in GAO's analysis, 16.2 million out of 18.1 million, received at least one lipid test from 2021 through 2025, but beneficiaries received just under 1.3 million lipid tests as Medicare preventive services against 91.6 million as medically necessary services, and relatively few had claims in 2025 for cardiovascular behavioural therapy.

**Steinmeier in Accra.** German Federal President Frank-Walter Steinmeier holds talks with President John Dramani Mahama at Jubilee House in Accra on Tuesday and then opens the sixth German African Business Summit, Germany's most important business conference on the African continent, according to his published programme. The summit runs from November 23 to 25 and is expected to draw about 600 senior representatives from the public and private sectors. The Ghana visit is the second leg of a five day tour from November 22 to 26 that also takes in Côte d'Ivoire and Kenya, and Steinmeier is accompanied by a German business delegation.

**CLMV summit in Hanoi.** The Secretary-General of ASEAN will participate in the 12th Cambodia, Lao PDR, Myanmar and Viet Nam (CLMV) Summit in Ha Noi, Viet Nam, the ASEAN Secretariat announced on Tuesday. The CLMV grouping is ASEAN's sub regional framework for its four Mekong member states; the Secretariat's notice records the participation without publishing the summit's agenda or dates beyond the announcement itself.
