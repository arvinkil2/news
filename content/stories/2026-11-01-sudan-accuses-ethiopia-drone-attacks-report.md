**Latest, November 2:** Drone strikes were reported on Monday to have hit three electricity substations, at New Halfa and Aroma in eastern Kassala state and al-Ghazali in Northern State, causing widespread outages and bringing reported strikes on Sudan's electricity network to 18 in the past week, Al Jazeera reported, citing local media. Al Jazeera said it could not immediately verify the reports or social media video said to show fires at the sites. The Sudanese army also said on Monday that a group of Rapid Support Forces members had surrendered to its forces in Damazin, in Blue Nile state, the latest in what it called increasing defections.

Sudan's foreign ministry said late on Sunday that Khartoum had lodged complaints against Ethiopia with the UN Security Council and the African Union's Peace and Security Council, accusing the Rapid Support Forces of using Ethiopian territory to launch what it called brazen aggression against civilian sites and power stations, and saying the incidents place Ethiopia before international law. A spokesman for UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres said on Saturday that Guterres was appalled by the impact of drone strikes in Sudan and strongly condemned the escalating attacks by the parties to the conflict.

Sudan's army-led Transitional Sovereignty Council said on Sunday that drones have taken off from Ethiopian soil to strike Sudanese cities since February, citing evidence from downed aircraft, flight data and military records. Its report documented 70 attacks between February 15 and October 27 involving 187 one-way attack drones, which it said killed or wounded 484 civilians, killed 176 soldiers and wounded 200 more, with October alone accounting for 28 attacks.

The report named launch sites at Asosa airport in western Ethiopia, the Kurmuk-Gizen border area and Bahir Dar air base in the Amhara region, and cited an October 9 attack on displaced families in Wad el-Mahi, in Blue Nile State, that it said killed 30 civilians and wounded 100. Sudan said it would file complaints with the UN Security Council and the African Union Peace and Security Council and reserved its right to self-defence. Ethiopia denies aiding the Rapid Support Forces, the paramilitary force the Sudanese army has fought since April 2023, and accuses Khartoum of backing rebels linked to the Tigray People's Liberation Front.
