Japanese household spending fell 6.7 percent in September from a year earlier in real terms, the Statistics Bureau's Family Income and Expenditure Survey showed, far deeper than the 3.1 percent decline markets had forecast and the 3.1 percent fall recorded in August. It is the tenth consecutive monthly decline in real spending, a run that began in December 2025, and it came in a month when forecasters had expected spending to rise 0.3 percent from August on the seasonally adjusted measure.

The miss lands while the Bank of Japan is tightening and its Summary of Opinions from the October 29 and 30 meeting is due the same Tokyo morning. Persistent real spending declines, with inflation still eroding household purchasing power, are the central counterweight to the wage growth the bank has cited in support of further rate rises.
