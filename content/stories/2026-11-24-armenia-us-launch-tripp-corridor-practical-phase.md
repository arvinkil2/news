Armenia and the United States launched the practical phase of the Trump Route for International Peace and Prosperity (TRIPP) on Monday, with preparatory construction work beginning in the southern Syunik region. Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan called it the largest economic project in Armenia since independence and a historic event, and President Donald Trump, in a recorded message, announced a new joint entity, the TRIPP Development Company, to create commercial routes for goods, energy and travel across the region, and invited Pashinyan to the White House next year.

The route, a product of the August 8, 2025 Washington summit at which Pashinyan and Azerbaijani President Ilham Aliyev signed a joint declaration in Trump's presence, aims at unimpeded multimodal transit across the South Caucasus, connecting Azerbaijan with its Nakhchivan exclave through Armenian territory on terms Pashinyan said rest on full respect for sovereignty, territorial integrity and jurisdiction. The launch comes earlier than signalled: as recently as October, Armenia's ambassador to the United States had put a groundbreaking ceremony in early 2027. The peace treaty between Armenia and Azerbaijan has not yet been signed, and opposition lawmakers in Armenia argue the Washington agreements gave up control over roads in Syunik, which the government rejects.
