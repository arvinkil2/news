The European Investment Bank Group has launched a 3 billion euro financing initiative dedicated to defence technology companies, the first facility of its kind from the EU's lending arm, spanning 11 priority areas of defence technology. The programme will lend and invest through intermediaries and funds rather than directly, the EIB's standard model for reaching smaller companies.

The significance is institutional. The EIB spent decades keeping defence at arm's length under its exclusion policies; the post 2022 revisions opened dual use and then security infrastructure, and a named 3 billion euro envelope for defence tech completes the migration. For Europe's defence startups, which have raised record venture rounds while complaining that growth capital and bank debt remain scarce, the facility is designed to fill exactly the scale up gap between venture cheques and public procurement.

The announcement lands in the same week as the EU summit in Brussels, where defence financing and the next long term budget are on the table, and a day after the Commission disbursed 2.86 billion euros to Ukraine for drones and missiles, covered in the politics edition. Europe is now funding both ends of the same problem: weapons for Kyiv this month, and the industrial base to make them at home.
