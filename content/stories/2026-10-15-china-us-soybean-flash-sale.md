China has returned to the US soybean market in size, buying an estimated 8 to 10 cargoes and triggering a confirmed USDA flash sale of 470,000 tonnes, its first in about three weeks, Grain Central's daily market wire reported on Thursday. The wire said the market expects China to take another 10 million tonnes, a figure it noted is already in the balance sheet.

The purchases matter beyond the tonnage. Chinese buying of US beans is the most legible signal in the trade war's agricultural theatre: when it stops, the US carryout balloons and basis collapses in the Midwest; when it resumes at cargo scale, it signals that the political permission, not just the price, has moved. Beans opened firm on the news before sliding with the broader grain complex.

Thursday also brings the September NOPA crush report, expected at 203.7 million bushels in a Reuters survey, with US harvest progress at 46 percent complete, four points behind average. Brazil's Conab survey, due today, will set the competing supply number, with the market expecting a 181 million tonne Brazilian soybean crop.
