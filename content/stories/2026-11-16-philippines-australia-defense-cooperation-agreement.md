The Philippines and Australia signed a Defense Cooperation Agreement on November 15 in a ceremony aboard an Australian naval vessel docked in Manila, attended by Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese and Philippine Defense Secretary Gilberto Teodoro. The agreement builds on the 2007 Status of Visiting Forces Agreement between the two countries and is meant to streamline joint military activities, support military infrastructure development, and expand maritime awareness and information sharing.

Teodoro said the agreement cuts the red tape involved in implementing the visiting forces accord, which governs temporary deployments of each country's personnel to the other. It is the latest in a series of Philippine defense agreements since the 1998 Visiting Forces Agreement with the United States, following similar accords with Japan in July 2024, New Zealand in April 2025, Canada in November 2025 and France in March 2026.
