The US Senate passed the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act, known as BAAJA, by 62 votes to 33 on November 19, the first major overhaul of federal permitting to clear the chamber in decades. Support eroded across the week's votes, from 73 to 25 on the first procedural vote on November 9 to the final margin, with 14 Democrats and independent Angus King joining most Republicans in favour.

The bill, introduced on September 30 by Senators Shelley Moore Capito, Martin Heinrich, Mike Lee and Sheldon Whitehouse, sets deadlines for steps in federal review, narrows the scope and length of reviews under the National Environmental Policy Act and the Clean Water Act, limits how long lawsuits over permits can run, and bars the federal government from rescinding permits for previously authorized projects. It expands the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission's authority to permit certain interstate transmission lines, directs FERC to set ratepayer protections for large computational loads such as data centers, and speeds approvals for renewable, geothermal, hydropower and offshore projects as well as pipelines.

Oil and gas groups urged the House to finish the job before the end of the year, and the White House has backed the bill. Its prospects there are less certain: the House is in Thanksgiving recess, roughly a month remains before the new Congress is sworn in on January 3, and some House Republicans have signalled concern over the transmission provisions. If the bill is not signed before the new year it dies, and permitting reform starts over in the next session.

Environmental advocates and supporters of Native American rights opposed the measure, and Democratic efforts to strip its National Historic Preservation Act provisions were blocked. The Senate also declined to vote on a last minute amendment that would have banned data centers on public land.
