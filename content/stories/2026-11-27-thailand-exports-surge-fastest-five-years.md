Thailand's exports rose 27.5 percent year on year in October to 36.9 billion dollars, the fastest growth in five years, while imports rose 53.2 percent to 49.2 billion dollars, leaving a trade deficit of 12.3 billion dollars for the month, the Trade Policy and Strategy Office said. Industrial exports expanded 28 percent, led by computers, peripherals and parts, telephones and parts, and video recording products, and October was the first month in 15 months in which exports grew across all three categories: agricultural, agro-industrial and industrial products.

For the first ten months of 2026, exports rose 20.4 percent to 341 billion dollars and imports 36.6 percent to 397 billion dollars, a deficit of 56 billion dollars that the office's director-general, Nantapong Chiralerspong, said was not a concern because the imports were raw materials, capital goods and semi-finished goods. The office projects export growth of more than 19 percent this year to 405 billion dollars, an all-time high. Exports to the United States totalled 84.5 billion dollars in the ten months, up 43.5 percent, and the Thai National Shippers' Council warned that the proposed US Section 301 tariff on excess capacity could, over time, shift orders to countries with better tariff treatment.
