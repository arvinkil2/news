The Black Sea has become the deadliest maritime route of 2026, the World Food Programme's executive director, Luke J. Lindberg, told the UN Security Council on November 23. More than 300 attacks have been recorded on shipping in the sea this year and at least 40 people have been killed, and as of mid-October 63 percent fewer wheat and maize vessels were moving through the Bosphorus than at the same point in 2025.

Lindberg described the Black Sea, the Strait of Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb as a triple chokepoint cutting the flow of food and fertilizer while pushing up fuel and freight bills. Around 10 million tonnes of Russian and Ukrainian wheat may not reach global markets this year, a volume the agency said could feed some 44 million people. About a third of global grain exports moved through the Black Sea before Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, and roughly 30 percent of global fertilizer supplies move through Hormuz.

The UN's political affairs chief, Rosemary DiCarlo, told the same briefing that 164 attacks on vessels were reported to the UN human rights office between June and October alone, some inside Bulgaria's exclusive economic zone and others off Romania. The figures give a UN-sourced measure to a disruption this site has been tracking through the trade data: EU soft wheat exports are running at a record pace this season as Black Sea attacks stifle Russian and Ukrainian shipments, a development covered in the November 22 commodities edition.

For grain markets, the briefing lands in a holiday-shortened week with funds still heavily short Chicago wheat and corn on the last reported positioning. A route described by the UN's food agency as effectively closing is a supply fact, not a sentiment one, and it is part of the background to the European premium over Chicago wheat, which widened to its largest in fifteen months on Tuesday according to the Northern Ag Network's market commentary.
