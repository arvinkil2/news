The Maryland Transportation Authority board has approved a $38.6 million demolition and miscellaneous marine work contract to the Joseph B. Fay Company, the lowest priced qualified bidder, as the first of four planned procurements to complete the rebuild of the Francis Scott Key Bridge in Baltimore.

The contract covers removal and salvage of the remaining three span steel girder sections over the Patapsco River, demolition of eight marine piers and two land piers, removal of nine marine foundations to the river mud line, and cleaning out 96 inch steel pipe piles so concrete can be poured for the new bridge. Work is expected to begin later this fall. Trade reporting of the award put the authority's own estimate for the contract at nearly $92 million, more than double the winning bid.

The remaining three contracts cover the south land approach, to be advertised in the coming months with construction from spring 2027, the north land approach, and the main span and marine approaches, which was advertised on July 21. The authority estimates the four jobs together at between $4 billion and $4.8 billion and continues to target opening the new bridge to traffic in 2030.

The procurement reset follows Maryland's decision earlier this year to drop Kiewit, its original progressive design builder, after finding its price for the main construction phase unacceptable. Kiewit continues to finish early works, including foundation and pier protection piles for the main span towers.
