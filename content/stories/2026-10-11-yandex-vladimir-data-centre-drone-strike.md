Yandex said on October 11 that operations at its data centre in Vladimir, east of Moscow, had been completely suspended after a drone strike, with no injuries reported. The company warned in a Telegram service alert that its platform was operating in emergency mode and advised users to activate disaster-recovery plans, adding that the remaining resource configuration was considered unstable. Services disrupted include Yandex Go, Eats and Lavka along with taxi and banking functions, across Russia and in Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan, Belarus and Armenia, with Yandex Cloud moved into emergency mode.

The Vladimir strike is the third on Yandex facilities in four days. On October 8, a drone strike and fire hit the company's Sasovo hub in Ryazan, which houses two of the three supercomputers used to develop Yandex's AI models, shutting it down completely; on October 9, Ukrainian drones partly disabled its Kaluga data centre. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has said Kyiv responds in mirror-like fashion to Russian strikes on Ukrainian data centres, and Ukraine is described in reporting as expanding strikes on Russian tech infrastructure in direct response. The Sasovo, Kaluga and Vladimir facilities sit at the heart of Yandex's AI training and cloud capacity, making the four-day arc the most significant known campaign against a single Russian technology company in the war.
