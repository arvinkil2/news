Temu has committed to bring several commercial practices in line with EU consumer protection law after the Consumer Protection Cooperation Network, coordinated by the European Commission and led by the authorities of Belgium, Germany and Ireland, accepted its commitments on Thursday. The practices at issue included fake discounts, fake reviews, pressure selling, forced gamification and missing or misleading information.

The commitments end "almost sold out" pressure messages and forced games such as spin the wheel, require a fake review detector and clearer rules on how star ratings are calculated, and require Temu to distinguish genuine discounts from comparisons with a general reference price and to show taxes and fees before check-out. National authorities will monitor compliance across the EU and EEA, and can fine Temu 4 percent or more of its annual turnover in their member state if it does not comply.
