The European Parliament and EU member states agreed on Thursday to delay the bloc's methane regulation by two years, after importers warned they might not secure enough compliant supplies while energy markets are strained by the war in the Middle East. Under the agreement, suppliers and buyers will have to start reporting the methane intensity of the fossil fuels they place on the EU market in 2029. The rules, adopted two years ago, had been opposed by the United States and Qatar, whose energy ministers wrote jointly in June that there was no viable path to compliance and that exporters and importers were unwilling to sign contracts that would knowingly violate EU law.
