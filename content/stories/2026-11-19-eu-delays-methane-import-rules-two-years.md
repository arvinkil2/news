**Latest.** The European Commission confirmed on Thursday that the European Parliament and the Council reached a provisional agreement on the amendment to the EU Methane Regulation, postponing by two years the application of certain requirements for natural gas, crude oil and coal importers and EU operators. The Commission said the agreement comes amid continued strain in global energy markets linked to the conflict in the Middle East, that it preserves the overall ambition, integrity and scope of the regulation, and that the postponement will allow operators to prepare for full compliance at the end of the period. The agreement, on an amendment the Commission presented in October 2026, now requires formal adoption by both the Parliament and the Council before it is published in the Official Journal and enters into force.

The European Parliament and EU member states agreed on Thursday to delay the bloc's methane regulation by two years, after importers warned they might not secure enough compliant supplies while energy markets are strained by the war in the Middle East. Under the agreement, suppliers and buyers will have to start reporting the methane intensity of the fossil fuels they place on the EU market in 2029. The rules, adopted two years ago, had been opposed by the United States and Qatar, whose energy ministers wrote jointly in June that there was no viable path to compliance and that exporters and importers were unwilling to sign contracts that would knowingly violate EU law.
