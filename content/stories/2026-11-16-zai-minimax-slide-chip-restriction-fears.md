ZAI and MiniMax led losses across Chinese artificial intelligence stocks in Hong Kong on Monday, sliding 13.9 percent and 11.2 percent respectively, as fears grew that Washington will tighten restrictions on Chinese access to advanced AI chips, including the offshore server rentals in Southeast Asia that developers have used as a workaround since 2022. Both stocks are now down between 18 and 25 percent in November; MiniMax has wiped out all gains since its January IPO and ZAI is close to falling below its IPO price, while larger developers Tencent, Alibaba and Baidu rose, and Kuaishou fell 1.2 percent.

The pressure follows reports last week that the US is considering legislation to block Chinese access to offshore capacity running the latest Nvidia hardware, with Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick called to testify before Congress. Investing.com also reported that DeepSeek has begun introducing daily usage limits on some plans, reflecting limited computing capacity, while Chinese chipmaking stocks advanced on bets that local developers will switch to domestic AI chips.
