Tanker freight rose 11.76 percent to 724.03 dollars and is up 33.26 percent on the week. Today's ADNOC story reports that one of the company's vessels was attacked while transiting the Strait of Hormuz on the evening of November 17, leaving one crew member unaccounted for and 11 injured, the second attack on an ADNOC vessel in a week, and today's IEA story warns that extreme weather is adding to the risks facing oil and gas shipping through chokepoints with few alternative routes.

Gold miners fell 5.87 percent to 74.90 dollars and are down 12.10 percent on the week. The fall tracks the metal itself, with gold futures down 2.99 percent on the day; no story in today's edition explains the gold decline.

Copper miners fell 5.72 percent to 61.58 dollars and are down 16.13 percent on the week, the deepest weekly fall in the tables. The closest reporting in today's edition is the Freeport Indonesia story, which covers the Grasberg operator's 750 million dollar notes offering and does not address copper prices.

Brent crude fell 5.51 percent to 88.01 dollars a barrel. No story in today's edition explains the fall, which came on a day when today's ADNOC story reported another attack on a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz, the waterway that carried a fifth of the world's oil and gas supplies before the conflict.

Silver miners fell 5.41 percent to 67.00 dollars and are down 13.78 percent on the week. No story in today's edition explains the silver miners' fall; silver futures fell 4.89 percent on the day.

Copper fell 5.40 percent to 5.65 dollars a pound and is down 9.91 percent on the week. No story in today's edition explains the copper decline; today's Freeport Indonesia story is about the miner's debt refinancing, not the metal's price.

WTI crude fell 5.36 percent to 81.40 dollars a barrel and is down 5.93 percent on the week. No story in today's edition explains the WTI fall; today's EIA story covers natural gas, reporting that US gas storage ended the injection season at 3,810 billion cubic feet, almost 2 percent above the five-year average.

Silver futures fell 4.89 percent to 44.97 dollars an ounce and are down 13.31 percent on the week. No story in today's edition explains the silver decline.

The cross-market read at midday is a commodity and mining selloff, not a broad equity rout. United States equities were slightly higher, the S&P 500 up 0.18 percent and the Nasdaq 100 up 0.77 percent, though both remain down more than 3 percent on the week, while European and Asian indexes fell about 1 to 1.5 percent and the TSX Composite fell 1.46 percent. Treasury yields eased a little, the ten-year down 4.3 basis points to 5.06 percent, on the day today's Waller story reported the Federal Reserve governor renewing his call for a 50 basis point December hike, and the dollar index rose 0.30 percent to 103.80. Natural gas rose 3.87 percent to 3.33 dollars, against the direction of the rest of the commodity complex.
