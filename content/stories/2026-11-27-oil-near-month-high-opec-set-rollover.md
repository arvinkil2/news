Brent crude settled at 96.57 dollars a barrel on Thursday, up 2.1 percent on the day and its highest close since November 6, as attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz and on Saudi energy infrastructure kept the focus on lost Gulf exports. Futures eased 38 cents to 96.19 dollars in early Friday trade and later rose to 97.30 dollars, putting both Brent and West Texas Intermediate on course for their first weekly gain in four weeks. WTI stood at 85.41 dollars, up 2.07 percent from its Wednesday close; there was no US settlement on Thursday because of the Thanksgiving holiday.

The immediate supply news stayed adverse. Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery temporarily halted some operations after a projectile struck the facility on Thursday, killing a Nepalese worker, a day after the Houthis claimed attacks on Ras Tanura and the Red Sea ports of Jeddah and Jazan. Ukraine's drone campaign against Russian refineries also continued, with a fire at the Slavyansk ECO refinery in the Krasnodar region tightening diesel supply, and fires hit refineries in Thailand and Venezuela on Thursday.

Against that backdrop, OPEC+ is likely to maintain its oil output targets for at least the first quarter of 2027 at its meeting on Sunday, two people with knowledge of the talks told Reuters, and later reporting put an agreement in principle among three sources. Emirates NBD likewise reported that delegates were considering unchanged January quotas despite continued disagreement over members' capacity assessments, the dispute that Iraq and Kazakhstan have pressed in recent weeks.

ANZ analysts Brian Martin and Daniel Hynes wrote that Brent could still push above 100 dollars a barrel without another major escalation in the Middle East, but that it increasingly requires evidence of constrained deliverable supply. That is the test for Sunday: a confirmed rollover with steady actual exports would argue for a pullback toward the low 90s, while any further strike on a terminal or tanker would sharpen the rally.
