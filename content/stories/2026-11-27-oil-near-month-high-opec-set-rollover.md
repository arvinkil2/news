Brent crude settled at 95.44 dollars a barrel on Friday, down 1.13 dollars or 1.2 percent, snapping a three week losing streak to post its first weekly gain in November as attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz and on Saudi energy infrastructure kept supply risk in the price. US West Texas Intermediate settled at 83.08 dollars, down 60 cents or 0.7 percent from Wednesday's close; there was no US settlement on Thursday because of the Thanksgiving holiday. Brent had settled at 96.57 dollars on Thursday, its highest close since November 6, and traded as high as 98.06 dollars on Friday before easing into the close.

OPEC+, which includes Saudi Arabia and Russia, is set to keep first quarter 2027 output targets unchanged when ministers meet online on Sunday, three people with knowledge of the talks told Reuters. The meeting also takes up the contested review of how much each member can sustainably produce, the basis on which quotas from 2027 may be set. Iraq's oil minister Basim Mohammed Khudair said on Friday that Iraq is in negotiations with OPEC to raise its production ceiling as the country develops its energy sector, a claim that puts the capacity dispute in the open a day before the meeting.

The supply backdrop stayed adverse through the week. Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery temporarily halted some operations after a projectile struck the facility on Thursday, killing a Nepalese worker, a day after the Houthis claimed attacks on Ras Tanura and the Red Sea ports of Jeddah and Jazan, and tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz continued to disrupt transits. Ukraine's drone campaign against Russian refining also continued, tightening diesel supply.

ANZ analysts Brian Martin and Daniel Hynes wrote earlier in the week that Brent could still push above 100 dollars a barrel without another major escalation in the Middle East, but that it increasingly requires evidence of constrained deliverable supply. Sunday's decision is the next test: a confirmed rollover with steady actual exports would argue for a pullback, while any further strike on a terminal or tanker would sharpen the rally. January Brent expires on Monday, November 30, the day after the meeting.
