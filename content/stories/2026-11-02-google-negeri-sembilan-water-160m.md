Google has committed $160 million to shared water infrastructure in Negeri Sembilan, Malaysia, funding an off-river storage reservoir and a water treatment plant on the Linggi River that will serve surrounding communities as well as its planned data centre campus at Springhill Industrial Park in Port Dickson. The treatment plant is designed to supply 65 million litres of potable water a day for domestic, commercial and data centre use.

Google has appointed Gamuda Berhad to build the facilities, working with the Negeri Sembilan state government and the state water utility, Syarikat Air Negeri Sembilan (SAINS). The reservoir will capture excess river flows and rainfall during periods of high water availability, which Google says will also reduce flood risk in nearby areas, and store water for treatment during drier periods without drawing down the river's baseline flow.

Ownership sits with the state, not the company. On completion, both facilities will be owned by Menteri Besar Negeri Sembilan (Incorporated), the state statutory body that manages government assets. Google is funding infrastructure it will use but will not own, in exchange for a secured water supply for a campus whose cooling needs would otherwise compete directly with public supply.

The announcement also commits Google to pay 100 percent of the electricity the campus uses and the direct costs of connecting it to the grid. The company framed the package against its global target of replenishing 120 percent of the freshwater it consumes. Gamuda already has a data centre development relationship with Google at Elmina Business Park in Selangor, making this its second major Google-linked build in Malaysia.

The structure is the story. Water has become the binding local constraint on data centre approvals across Southeast Asia, and host governments are increasingly asking hyperscalers to fund shared utility capacity as the price of entry. Negeri Sembilan now has a priced template: $160 million of privately funded, publicly owned water capacity attached to a single campus.
