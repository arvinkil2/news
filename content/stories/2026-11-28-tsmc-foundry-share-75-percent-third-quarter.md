TSMC captured 75 percent of global pure-play foundry revenue in the third quarter of 2026, Counterpoint Research said in its quarterly tracker, up from 73 percent in each of the two preceding quarters and 72 percent a year earlier. The sector as a whole grew 36 percent year on year and 13 percent on the second quarter, as demand for advanced AI nodes and advanced packaging continued to outrun supply and higher wafer prices lifted revenue.

TSMC's own revenue rose 16 percent on the quarter, faster than the market, helped by the ramp of 2-nanometer production, full utilization of its 3-nanometer lines and expanding CoWoS advanced packaging capacity. Samsung Foundry stayed second but slipped to 6.4 percent share from 6.9 percent, because its revenue grew only 6 percent on the quarter. SMIC held third place on record quarterly revenue, also up 6 percent, on strong domestic Chinese demand.

Counterpoint expects pure-play foundry utilization to stay high from the fourth quarter of 2026 through 2027 as wafer prices rise and packaging capacity expands. The gap between TSMC and the rest is now the widest in the tracker's history: the second-ranked foundry holds less than a tenth of TSMC's share.

For chip designers, the numbers mean the queue for leading-edge capacity, and the pricing that comes with it, is set in Hsinchu. For Samsung, the quarter is further evidence that its foundry turnaround is not yet converting into share, even in a market growing by more than a third.
