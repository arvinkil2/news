HUTCHMED has initiated RANOVA, a registrational Phase 3 trial of ranosidenib, also known as HMPL-306, in China. The company announced the start on November 23 in a Form 6-K filing. The first patient received the first dose on November 20, 2026.

RANOVA is a randomized, double blind, placebo controlled, multicenter study in patients with postoperative residual or recurrent Grade 2 astrocytoma or oligodendroglioma carrying IDH1 or IDH2 mutations, who have previously received only surgical treatment. It plans to enroll approximately 210 patients in China. The primary outcome is progression free survival assessed by an independent review committee under RANO 2.0 criteria. Patients in the placebo group may cross over to ranosidenib after independently confirmed progression.

Ranosidenib inhibits both mutant IDH1 and mutant IDH2 enzymes. HUTCHMED's rationale is that mutant IDH enzymes can switch form when only one is targeted, producing acquired resistance, so dual inhibition may hold the pathway longer. The company retains all rights to the drug worldwide.

The filing puts the epidemiology at approximately 260,000 new glioma cases a year worldwide, including nearly 70,000 in China, with IDH mutations found in approximately 70 percent to 80 percent of Grade 2 or 3 gliomas. Those are company cited background figures, not trial results. RANOVA has produced no efficacy data yet, and its value depends entirely on a progression free survival readout that is years away.
