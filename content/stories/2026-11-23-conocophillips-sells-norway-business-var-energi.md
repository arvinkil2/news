ConocoPhillips has entered into a definitive agreement to sell its Norway business and its 40.25 percent interest in, and operatorship of, the UK Teesside oil terminal to Vår Energi for total consideration of $4.425 billion, less agreed adjustments of approximately $0.5 billion as of the January 1, 2026 effective date. Total consideration is expected to be about $3.9 billion, including the remaining 2026 tax liability of approximately $1.4 billion payable in the first half of 2027, leaving net cash proceeds of approximately $2.5 billion.

The acquired portfolio comprises interests in 13 producing fields on the Norwegian Continental Shelf, including the Greater Ekofisk Area, Troll, Heidrun, Alvheim, Oseberg, Visund, Aasta Hansteen, Grane and Breidablikk, together with related infrastructure. ConocoPhillips disclosed in October that it was considering a sale after receiving an unsolicited offer; its Norwegian business produced approximately 120,000 barrels of oil equivalent a day in 2025.

Vår Energi said the transaction lifts its long term production target above 500,000 barrels of oil equivalent a day once combined with the previously announced BlueNord combination, with a production mix of around 65 percent oil and 35 percent gas, operating costs of approximately $13 per barrel of oil equivalent and available liquidity of $7.5 billion at September 30, 2026. It is also establishing a commodity hedging programme covering the transaction volumes for 2027 through the first quarter of 2028.

Closing is expected around the end of the first quarter of 2027, subject to approval by the Norwegian Ministry of Energy and other regulatory conditions. The deal is the largest single transfer of Norwegian shelf ownership this year and continues the pattern of US majors exiting mature North Sea positions to focused regional independents.
