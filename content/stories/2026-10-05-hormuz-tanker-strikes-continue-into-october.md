The tanker campaign in the Strait of Hormuz escalated over the weekend even as physical flows recovered. UK Maritime Trade Operations issued Warning 134-26 after a vessel was struck by an unknown projectile at 11:00 p.m. UTC Saturday north of the Musandam peninsula; a fire broke out and local authorities evacuated the crew. Iranian media, citing the governor of Qeshm, said an Iranian commercial container vessel was hit near Hengam Island with one crew member killed and three injured, blaming an unidentified "terrorist enemy." Maritime security firm Vanguard Tech separately identified the vessel as a Panama-flagged tanker that sent a distress call, with the Omani navy responding. It is unclear whether the two reports describe the same vessel.

Separately, the Sunday engine-room strike is now a named vessel: the LR2 tanker LIPSI, linked to a Greek operator and Chinese ownership, was hit by an unknown projectile with no crew casualties, according to maritime specialist DeepDraft. And the threat corridor widened: a tanker reported multiple explosions about 60 nautical miles south of Mokha, Yemen, near the Bab el-Mandeb approach — the Red Sea route Saudi Arabia has used as its Hormuz workaround is now inside the threat envelope too.

Against the violence, the flow data moved the other way. Kpler's provisional figures, reported via Reuters, put crude flows through Hormuz at 19.5 to 22.5 million barrels a day over September 27–29 — above pre-war levels and roughly double a month earlier — with LNG flows also reported back at pre-war levels. The LNG claim deserves skepticism: QatarEnergy remains in force majeure on exports and has reportedly been buying spot cargoes to service term contracts. The Joint Maritime Information Center still rates the strait SEVERE.

Diplomatically the weekend hardened the deadlock. Iran's parliament speaker Ghalibaf said Tehran will not fully reopen the strait until Washington meets seven conditions; the Foreign Ministry said U.S. proposals delivered via a mediator "largely reflected previous positions." Iran–Oman talks on a temporary shipping lane, due Monday, were postponed. Brent traded at $101.90 early Monday, with the tanker-freight index carrying a 33 percent weekly war-risk gain.
