Congo is closing the gap on Chile as the world's biggest copper supplier. Congolese copper exports rose 8 percent year on year to 2.7 million tonnes in the first nine months of 2026, and quarterly shipments topped 1 million tonnes for the first time in the July to September period, government data show. Chile exported 1.28 million tonnes in the third quarter.

Chile's output is expected to fall below 5 million tonnes this year, its lowest since 2003, though that would still be about 40 percent higher than Congo's 2025 production. Two CMOC mines accounted for almost 550,000 tonnes of Congo's exports through September, while Kamoa-Kakula, owned by Ivanhoe Mines and Zijin Mining, shipped 187,000 tonnes.

The growth is drawing infrastructure pitches to match. Kinshasa is now marketing $15 billion of rail and other infrastructure projects to investors. A new deep water Atlantic port opens next year, and construction is starting on the rail link from the copperbelt to Angola's port of Lobito, Transport Minister Jean-Pierre Bemba said on Wednesday.

The export surge landed in a market already squeezing higher: copper posted its biggest one day gain in London since January on Friday as short sellers covered positions, and copper miners rose with the metal, Ivanhoe Mines gaining 3.7 percent in Toronto. The price move is covered in today's commodities edition; the supply story underneath it is Congo's.
