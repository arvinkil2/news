US Treasury yields fell across the curve at Friday's close. The 5 year yield dropped 16.5 basis points to 4.70 percent, the 10 year fell 14.0 basis points to 4.91 percent and the 30 year fell 9.8 basis points to 5.30 percent. On the week the moves were larger: the 5 year is down 28.0 basis points, the 10 year down 24.2 and the 30 year down 17.8.

The shape of the move matters. The front and belly of the curve fell faster than the long end, so the rally was led by the maturities most sensitive to the policy path and to growth expectations, not by long duration scarcity. Equities sold off at the same time, with the S&P 500 down 3.7 percent on the day, which marks the bond bid as a growth and risk response rather than a celebration of cheaper money.

That sits oddly against meeting pricing. As yesterday's edition reported, November 20 pricing put a 25 basis point Federal Reserve increase in December at 99 percent probability, with increases also priced for the ECB, the Bank of England and the Bank of Japan. Investors are therefore buying Treasuries into a priced tightening cycle, which historically happens when the fear is that the tightening itself, or the conditions causing it, will damage growth.

The dollar barely moved, closing at 103.84 on the index, up 0.03 percent on the day. A flat dollar alongside a 14 basis point fall in the 10 year says the bid was domestic duration demand, not a global rush out of US assets.
