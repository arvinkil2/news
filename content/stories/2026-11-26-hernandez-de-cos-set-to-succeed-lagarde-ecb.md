Pablo Hernández de Cos will become president of the European Central Bank on February 8, according to El País, after the path cleared for him at Thursday's European Council meeting of EU leaders. The Financial Times reported that a majority of euro-area countries endorsed the Spaniard to succeed Christine Lagarde in February. He would be the first Spaniard to hold the post since the ECB was founded in 1998, and its fifth president after Wim Duisenberg, Jean-Claude Trichet, Mario Draghi and Lagarde.

The rival candidacy of Klaas Knot, the former Dutch central bank governor, collapsed at the summit, though the Netherlands backed him to the end rather than withdrawing. Berlin and Paris, the euro area's two largest shareholders in the institution, both supported Hernández de Cos, and the appointment was negotiated as a package: Germany is to take the chief economist seat on the executive board and France the market operations seat, according to El País. Spain now holds three of the EU's top economic posts, with Nadia Calviño running the European Investment Bank and Teresa Ribera as the European Commission's second-ranking official.

Hernández de Cos, who turns 56 in January, governed the Bank of Spain from 2018 to 2024 and is now general manager of the Bank for International Settlements in Basel. He is regarded as fiscally orthodox but sits on the dovish side in monetary policy debates, weighing activity and financial stability alongside inflation, where Knot had the more hawkish profile.

Lagarde leaves on February 7, nine months before her term was due to end, a timing that let President Emmanuel Macron shape the succession before French elections this spring in which Marine Le Pen's far right is polling strongly. Formal steps remain: the European Parliament and the ECB itself must issue opinions, and leaders return to the appointment in December to choose the president and the other two new executive board members formally.
