Natural gas rose 10.19 percent to 3.60 dollars per million Btu, the largest move on the board. Much of the jump is the calendar: the December contract expired on Wednesday and January, the first full winter month, took over the front slot just as the EIA reported the first storage draw of the heating season, 24 billion cubic feet for the week ended November 20 (see today's US gas storage story).

Silver miners rose 4.84 percent to 65.45 dollars, silver futures rose 4.21 percent to 41.74 dollars an ounce and gold miners rose 3.53 percent to 73.89 dollars, with gold futures up 1.68 percent to 3,595.30 dollars and copper up 1.93 percent to 5.44 dollars a pound. The move extends Wednesday's rebound across the metals complex after Tuesday's slide (see today's miners rebound story and the metals rebound story).

Brent crude fell 3.18 percent to 91.60 dollars a barrel on the estate front series. Today's commodities reporting, working from Reuters settlements, records the market the other way: Brent settled 3.5 percent higher at 94.61 dollars on Wednesday after the Houthi claim of an attack on Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery, and was up 0.37 percent at 94.96 dollars at 1211 GMT on Thursday (see today's oil story). The two series disagree, so no single driver is asserted for the table move.

Nvidia fell 3.91 percent to 149.30 dollars. Today's technology reporting carried Nvidia's halt to AI server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries, on a day when the broader semiconductor index fell 0.50 percent and the AI-led equity selloff was also the bid behind falling Treasury yields (see today's Nvidia story and the Treasury yields story).

Meta rose 3.40 percent to 609.89 dollars against the technology tape. No story in today's file explains the move: the only Meta-linked reporting is the CoreWeave loan secured by processors for Meta's data centre contract, a financing story rather than a driver for Meta's own shares (see today's CoreWeave story).

Amazon fell 3.12 percent to 244.43 dollars. No story in today's published file explains the move.

The cross-market read stays defensive. Precious metals and the miners were bid, the dollar was little changed, and Treasury yields fell for an eighth straight session on Wednesday, the 10-year down 5.6 basis points to 4.85 percent, even as growth was revised up to 4.3 percent and core inflation rose to 3.3 percent (see today's Treasury yields story). With US markets shut on Thursday, that risk-off pattern is on hold rather than resolved, and energy remains the split exception: gas prices the winter directly in front of it, while oil waits on Sunday's OPEC+ meeting.
