Natural gas rose 10.68 percent to 3.62 dollars per million Btu, the largest move on the board. Much of the jump is the calendar: the December contract expired and January, the first full winter month, took over the front slot just as the EIA reported the first storage draw of the heating season, 24 billion cubic feet for the week ended November 20 (see today's US gas storage story).

Silver miners rose 4.84 percent to 65.45 dollars and gold miners 3.53 percent to 73.89 dollars, with silver futures up 3.95 percent to 41.63 dollars an ounce and copper up 2.18 percent to 5.45 dollars a pound. The miners' rebound reversed Tuesday's slide across the metals complex (see today's miners rebound story and the metals rebound story).

Brent crude fell 4.22 percent to 90.62 dollars a barrel on the estate front series. This morning's commodities reporting, working from Reuters settlements, records Wednesday the other way, with Brent settling 3.5 percent higher at 94.61 dollars after the Houthi claim of an attack on Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery, then easing on Thursday morning (see today's oil story). The two series disagree, so no single driver is asserted for the table move.

Nvidia fell 3.91 percent to 149.30 dollars. This morning's technology reporting carried Nvidia's halt to AI server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries, on a day when the broader semiconductor index fell 0.50 percent and Amazon fell 3.12 percent to 244.43 dollars, for which this morning's reporting gives no clear single driver. Meta rose 3.40 percent to 609.89 dollars against the technology tape; no clear single driver appears in this morning's reporting.

The cross-market read is defensive rotation rather than a growth signal. Precious metals and the miners were bid, the dollar was little changed, and Treasury yields fell for an eighth straight session, the 10-year down 5.6 basis points to 4.85 percent, even as growth was revised up and core inflation rose (see today's Treasury yields story). Energy is the exception that proves the rule: gas prices the winter directly in front of it, while oil waits on Sunday's OPEC+ meeting.
