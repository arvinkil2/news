Iraq resumed pumping oil through the 960-kilometre Kirkuk-Ceyhan pipeline to Türkiye on Tuesday, ending a 12-year shutdown that began with Daesh attacks in 2014, the oil ministry said. Flows start at 120,000 barrels a day and are planned to rise gradually to 500,000 barrels a day, a route that before the closure carried about 0.5 percent of global supply. Oil Minister Basim Mohammed Khudair said the reopening followed maintenance, rehabilitation work and technical testing, and that the northern route lets Iraq sell crude at market prices without discounts, at a time when its southern export terminals are exposed to the Hormuz disruption.
