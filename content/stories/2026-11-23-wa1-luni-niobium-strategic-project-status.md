WA1 Resources' Luni niobium project has been granted strategic project status by the Western Australian government, providing three years of coordinated support to progress the development through state approval pathways. The designation was awarded by State Development Minister David Michael under the state's Lead Agency Framework, with assistance through the Department of Energy and Economic Diversification, including active case management and coordination between agencies.

The recognition builds on Luni receiving major project status from the Australian government in November 2025, and follows WA1's prefeasibility study, which outlined a pathway for Luni to establish Australia as the world's second-largest primary source of niobium. Brazil currently dominates primary niobium supply, which is why a second source carries strategic weight beyond the project's own economics.

Managing director Paul Savich said the designation came as the company assessed development and funding options after the study. Luni would be Australia's first primary niobium operation, delivering royalties, taxes and regional jobs if built.

Strategic status coordinates approvals; it does not grant them, and it does not fund construction. The next tests for Luni remain the next technical study phase and the funding pathway WA1 is now structuring.
