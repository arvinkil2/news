The Competition and Markets Authority said on November 25 that the undertakings offered by AkzoNobel and Axalta, to divest AkzoNobel's vehicle refinish coatings business in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland, could in principle address the competition concerns from their proposed merger. The CMA will consult on the remedies package and the potential purchaser in due course, and if satisfied will conditionally clear the deal without a phase 2 investigation.

In its phase 1 investigation the CMA found the merger would create the clear market leader in the supply of vehicle refinish coatings in the UK and could reduce competition by removing an important alternative supplier, raising prices and reducing choice for body shops and vehicle owners. The concern was confined to that product market.

The remedy matches the package already agreed with the European Commission, which conditionally cleared the merger at phase 1 on November 6. AkzoNobel has proposed to sell the business, including the necessary brands, assets, contracts and personnel, to a purchaser approved by the CMA. The undertakings were offered on November 18.

AkzoNobel and Axalta said they remain confident the merger will complete by early 2027. The combination would create a global coatings company with about 17 billion dollars in revenue across powder, aerospace, refinish, mobility, marine and protective, industrial and decorative coatings. Shareholders of both companies approved the transaction in August 2026.
