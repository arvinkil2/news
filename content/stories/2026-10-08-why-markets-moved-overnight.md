**Tanker freight (+4.56% to $1,003.76).** The war-risk bid moved from barrels to boats: dirty-tanker rates are up 21.5 percent on the week after a chemical tanker was hit by projectiles off Qatar on Wednesday and three incidents hit Black Sea shipping in three days, widening the war zone beyond Hormuz itself. See today's Hormuz story and the Black Sea story.

**Brent crude (+3.73% to $103.94) and WTI (+3.18% to $91.09).** Crude gave back part of the morning's 5 percent surge but held Brent above $100 as the attack tally kept climbing. The EIA's weekly report (crude stocks down 3.2 million barrels, distillates flat at 12 percent below the five-year average) keeps the distillate deficit as the binding constraint, and the IEA's Wednesday stock-release meeting added no fresh barrels beyond the original pledge. See today's Hormuz, EIA stocks and IEA stories.

**Uranium miners (-3.49% to $38.53).** No story in today's reporting explains the slide; it is a broad continuation of the risk-off sweep across the miner complex on the rates repricing.

**Natural gas (-2.65% to $3.12/mmBtu).** The European Commission said Thursday the EU gas system is flexible enough for winter despite storage below historical norms, citing higher LNG import capacity and lower demand, a supply-reassurance read that undercut the front month. See today's EU gas winter outlook story.

**Rare earth miners (-2.59% to $60.28).** No story in today's files explains the move. This morning's edition linked the complex to the overhang from today's Beijing trade talks and the expiring rare-earth export-control moratorium, but no story file today covers those talks.

**Copper miners (-1.99% to $81.92).** Chilean supply risk is rising just as output sits at a fifteen-year monthly low: workers at Antofagasta's Centinela began an indefinite strike this week, BHP's Escondida is shut after a fatal accident, and a new index puts 5.2 percent of expected output at risk. See today's Chile copper disruptions story.

**DAX (-1.18% to 24,807).** European equities sagged on French debt worries: the Stoxx 600 banks index fell as much as 2.2 percent Thursday, compounding Wednesday's 3.3 percent selloff into the worst two-day drop since March, as France prepares to present its 2027 budget amid widening OAT spreads. See today's EU bank stocks story.

The cross-market read is the same split screen as the morning, with the volume turned up on energy and down on everything else. Physical barrels, freight and diesel are pricing a war that is spreading past Hormuz; financial risk is pricing a Fed that Governor Waller says has an 85 percent chance of hiking by December, with the ECB account at 7:30 AM ET feeding the rates leg. Treasuries themselves are catching a small bid: the 10-year eased 2.9bp to 5.25 percent and the 30-year 4.4bp to 5.62 percent after the strong $39 billion 10-year auction stopped through at 5.30 percent. But long-end yields remain at multi-decade highs, which is why miners and European equities keep bleeding while oil holds its bid.
