Williams told Bloomberg News his view aligns with the median September projection, and that he expects inflation to slow quite a bit toward the 2 percent target next year as tariff and energy price shocks fade, even as AI buildout spending adds price pressure he says he is watching closely. Two-year Treasury yields edged lower after the remarks, erasing an earlier rise of as much as three basis points, while futures pricing still anticipates a December hike and two more in 2027. His stance sets him against colleagues including Cleveland's Beth Hammack and Dallas's Lorie Logan, who have said more than one additional move will likely be needed to return inflation to target.
