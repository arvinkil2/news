Singapore telco Simba did not contest allegations by the Infocomm Media Development Authority that it intentionally used spectrum outside its assignment: the 2,100 MHz band for 4G, and the 2,340 MHz to 2,360 MHz band at about 92 sites for intermittent periods between 2023 and 2026, in instances the regulator says were approved by senior management. In a filing to the Australian Securities Exchange on Tuesday, parent Tuas said Simba also admitted unauthorised deployment of 5G network equipment at two locations in 2023 and failure to comply with an IMDA production order, and that director Richard Tan will resign subject to regulatory approval. IMDA has made no findings and imposed no sanction yet; the possible penalties include a fine of up to $1 million or 10 percent of Simba's annual Singapore revenue, whichever is higher, and suspension or cancellation of its spectrum rights or licence, and the Straits Times reported the spectrum issue contributed to the collapse of Simba's proposed $1.4 billion acquisition of M1.
