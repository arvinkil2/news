## Latest

Saudi Arabia struck back late Wednesday with retaliatory strikes after the deadly Houthi attacks on Abha and Riyadh's King Khalid airports, France24 reported, widening the exchange beyond the coalition's ongoing air campaign inside Yemen. Details of the targets hit were still emerging overnight.

The conflict crossed into Saudi Arabia with deadly effect. Saudi Arabia's civil aviation authority said Wednesday that Abha International Airport and Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport were attacked on Tuesday and Wednesday, killing three people: an Egyptian and an Algerian national at Abha, and a Sudanese national at Riyadh's airport. Thirty-six people were wounded. The statement did not attribute blame, but the Houthis claimed responsibility for a flurry of strikes that included a ballistic missile fired at King Khalid airport, per AFP. The Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed the ballistic missile launch platform used in the Riyadh attack.

The claims go further than the airports. Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree said Wednesday's operations also targeted an Aramco refinery in Rabigh, the Khamis Mushait military base and other sites in Najran and Jizan, warning international airlines to avoid Saudi airspace outside Mecca and Medina. Saudi authorities have not confirmed those claims, and AFP notes the warring parties' claims are impossible to independently verify. Separately, airports in Najran and Jazan were hit on Monday with three lightly wounded. Pakistan and Turkey are moving forces to support Saudi security under the Mecca defence pact.

## Earlier

Yemen's Houthis struck Aden International Airport with ballistic missiles and explosive-laden drones on Wednesday, the country's Transport Ministry said, as fighting between the Iran-aligned group and Saudi-backed government forces intensified around the Bab el-Mandeb strait. One missile landed near the runway minutes before a flight from Cairo was due to land. Authorities canceled the landing and diverted the aircraft to Jeddah, and moved two planes off the runway for fear of further strikes. There were no casualties reported.

The Saudi-led coalition said Wednesday it had intercepted and destroyed a Houthi ballistic missile north of the capital Riyadh, and separately one launched toward the southwestern city of Khamis Mushait, according to Saudi state television. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree claimed the group had also targeted King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh and Abha International Airport with drones and missiles; neither claim had Saudi confirmation.

The airport strike lands on the energy map. Fighting is concentrated on the Dhubab district overlooking Bab el-Mandeb, the shipping route that has grown more important for energy exports since the US-Iran conflict restricted trade through the Strait of Hormuz. Saudi-backed government forces with coalition air support launched a counter-offensive this week to retake territory near the strait and around Mocha that the Houthis captured in September, though whether their advance will hold is unclear. Aden airport is the principal international gateway for the parts of Yemen outside Houthi control.
