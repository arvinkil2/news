RBNZ Governor Anna Breman has overhauled the central bank's top leadership, Bloomberg reported Thursday. Breman, the former Riksbank deputy governor who took the helm December 1, 2025 as the first woman and first foreigner to lead the RBNZ, inherited an easing cycle that had cut the official cash rate to a three-year low of 2.25 percent before the bank pivoted back to hikes this year. Details of the leadership changes are thin and the report is single-sourced, so treat the scope as unconfirmed.

India's September CPI is expected at about 5.4 percent when it prints early next week, per an OilPrice estimate, up from 4.8 percent in August, which would harden the case for the RBI's December hike after Wednesday's move to 5.50 percent. That is a single estimate, not a consensus. The RBI's Rs 36,000 crore G-sec re-issue auction lands Friday with settlement October 12, the first supply test after the hike.

The Bank of England published the minutes of its UK Money Markets Code sub-committee meeting from September 2026, routine plumbing but the kind of record that matters when repo and funding markets get stressed. Fed Governor Michelle Bowman's October 6 speech on modernizing the regulatory and supervisory landscape covered the US side of the same plumbing debate; it was light on rate signals. The IMF published a note arguing that targeted support, not broad subsidies, best protects households when inflation surges, a fiscal-monetary coordination read relevant to the current energy-driven episode. And the South African Reserve Bank published a working paper assessing the anchoring of inflation expectations in South Africa, useful context with the SARB's repo at 7.25 percent and its stance extended into 2027.
