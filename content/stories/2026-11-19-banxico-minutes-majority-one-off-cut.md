Banco de México's Governing Board judged that the disinflation process had continued and, with slack prevailing, that exchange rate pass-through to prices would be limited and no demand pressures were perceived, the minutes of its November 4 meeting show, and on that basis it made a one-off adjustment, cutting the overnight target rate by 25 basis points to 6.25 percent by majority vote. One member backing the cut noted the peso had depreciated about 5.5 percent between meetings, a move not comparable with stress episodes, with the exchange rate still below its 2025 average and pass-through low because inflation expectations remain anchored. The two members who voted to hold at 6.50 percent argued the balance of risks had tilted further upward: one cited persistent core inflation, the eighth month of the Middle East conflict and the risk that peso depreciation reverses the earlier mitigating effect of appreciation, while the other said the debate should have been between pausing and raising the rate, given a rate differential with the United States at its narrowest in years and an expected Federal Reserve increase in December. Forward guidance was unchanged: the board considers that keeping the rate for a prolonged period at the level reached will support convergence of inflation to its target.
