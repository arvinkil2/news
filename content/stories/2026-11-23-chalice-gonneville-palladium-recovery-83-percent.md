Chalice Mining has improved projected palladium recovery at its Gonneville project in Western Australia after research identified how to recover more metal during processing. The work, undertaken with the University of Western Australia, lifts projected overall palladium recovery above 83 percent for Gonneville's first four years of operation.

The improvement came from a PhD project examining how platinum-group metals behave during the separation of copper and nickel concentrates. Early tests showed the metals were not consistently reporting to the same concentrate. Detailed mineral analysis using UWA and CSIRO facilities identified tellurides and bismuthides as the main carriers of the platinum-group elements.

With the carriers identified, Chalice's metallurgical team refined the process: finer grinding to reduce particle size, more time in copper flotation so the platinum-group elements follow the copper, and an added leaching step, using techniques from the gold industry, to capture metal that flotation missed.

Gonneville, about 70 kilometres north-east of Perth, hosts palladium, platinum, nickel, copper and cobalt, and is being advanced as a potential domestic source of platinum-group metals alongside its base metals. The findings now feed into the project's development approach, where recovery assumptions carry directly into the revenue side of any feasibility case.
