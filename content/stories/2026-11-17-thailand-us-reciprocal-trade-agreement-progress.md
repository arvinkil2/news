**Latest.** The Thai government on Wednesday denied that it had agreed to eliminate all tariffs on US goods, contradicting President Trump's account of the Manila signing. Commerce Minister Suphajee Suthumpun said Thailand had not agreed to open its market completely, that access for US goods would be phased in with safeguards such as import quotas for sensitive sectors, and that existing protections, including a requirement to buy locally produced animal-feed maize before importing, would remain. She said the two sides had agreed the substantive elements of the agreement, with legal drafting expected to take about two months before cabinet approval, public consultation and parliamentary consideration, and that an immediate priority was cutting the additional US Section 301 tariff on affected Thai products from 12.5 percent to 10 percent. Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul said Thailand was not disadvantaged by the pact. The opposition People's Party criticised the lack of published terms, saying public concern would persist until the government discloses which products will face zero tariffs and which will not.

Thailand and the United States signed a joint statement in Manila on Tuesday hailing significant progress on an Agreement on Reciprocal Trade, with both leaders expressing confidence the formal agreement would be signed soon. President Trump said Thailand would eliminate all tariffs on US exports and remove substantial non-tariff barriers on American vehicles, medical devices, pharmaceuticals, ethanol and poultry, and that Thailand would purchase an estimated $2.6 billion worth of additional US agricultural products. The two sides also agreed a non-binding memorandum on increased cooperation in rare earth minerals trading.

The Thai government said both countries would now begin their internal procedures to prepare the agreement for signature and entry into force, and a deputy government spokeswoman stressed that Tuesday's signing was not yet the formal agreement, with Thailand still required to hold a public consultation and complete legal procedures. The 2025 framework agreement envisaged a 19 percent baseline US tariff on Thai imports, with final details still to be confirmed.
