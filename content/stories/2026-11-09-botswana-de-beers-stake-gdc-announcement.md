Latest: Botswana has formally selected the Global Diamond Consortium (GDC), led by former De Beers chief executive Gareth Penny, as its preferred bidder for Anglo American's 85 percent stake in De Beers, President Duma Boko announced in his annual State of the Nation address in Gaborone on Monday. "Government has selected the Global Diamond Consortium as its preferred bidder for Anglo American's interest in De Beers," Boko said. Botswana owns the remaining 15 percent and holds a right of first refusal over the sale, so its endorsement clears a major hurdle for a disposal Anglo has pursued for more than two years.

Boko used the speech to set out the ownership claim publicly. "When this transaction is complete, Botswana's shareholding in De Beers will double from the current 15% to 30%," he said. "Botswana will become the single largest shareholder in De Beers, and with that comes greater influence in the strategic decisions for decades." That figure is not yet agreed: the consortium has so far agreed a deal that gives Botswana about 25 percent, a source close to the matter told Rapaport News, and Penny publicly questioned how a 30 percent Botswana stake would fit alongside a consortium holding of at least 50 percent. The 30 percent is also short of the controlling stake Boko had promised when the sale process began, a pledge he acknowledged had drawn criticism.

Neither Boko nor the consortium disclosed financial terms. Under the consortium's offer, Anglo would receive $750 million up front and $250 million in deferred payments, a source told Rapaport News, with a further $500 million capital injection into De Beers still under discussion. Angola and Namibia will take part in the deal alongside Botswana, Boko said, and the consortium's first public statement thanked the governments of Botswana, Angola, Namibia and Oman for their support and partnership. The statement named the consortium's founders as Penny, Saurin Parikh of Antwerp based Diarough and Moshe Klein of New York based Grandview Klein, both De Beers sightholders, and referred to unnamed midstream and downstream partners; it did not mention Pluczenik, Signet Jewelers or Richemont, all previously linked to the bid.

Anglo American said it continues to work with the Botswana government, other governments and the consortium towards agreeing binding transaction documents, and De Beers chief executive Al Cook welcomed Botswana's intention to increase its ownership. No binding agreement, price or timetable has been announced.
