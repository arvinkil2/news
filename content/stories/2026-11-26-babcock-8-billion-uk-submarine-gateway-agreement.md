The Ministry of Defence has awarded Babcock its Gateway Agreement, worth around 8 billion pounds, to transform how submarine maintenance and critical naval base services are delivered, the government announced on November 26. The five-year contract is intended to tackle maintenance bottlenecks so submarines spend less time in dock and more time on operations, including nuclear deterrent patrols and NATO and AUKUS commitments.

The agreement will create about 3,000 new jobs, mainly across Plymouth, Clyde and Bristol, plus around 2,500 apprentice and graduate roles, and will support 8,500 existing jobs at His Majesty's Naval Bases Devonport and Clyde. About 2.4 billion pounds of the spend flows to the wider UK supply chain, including around 1 billion pounds targeted at small and medium-sized businesses.

The Gateway Agreement sits alongside Project Royal Oak, a 26 billion pound, ten-year programme to modernise Devonport, Clyde and Portsmouth into warfighting-ready facilities, and is the second major multi-billion-pound submarine-related deal the MoD has signed since the Defence Investment Plan was published. The new framework carries incentives for faster delivery of maintenance.

Babcock chief executive Harry Holt said the agreement would let the company, in collaboration with the MoD, continue supporting the Continuous At Sea Deterrent, increase submarine availability and develop the skills and infrastructure for the submarine force. The Defence Nuclear Enterprise and its industry partners support over 6,000 UK companies and about 47,000 jobs, with workforce demand forecast to rise to about 65,000 by 2030.
