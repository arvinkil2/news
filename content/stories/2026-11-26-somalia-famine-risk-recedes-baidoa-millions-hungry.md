Millions of people in Somalia still face severe food shortages and malnutrition despite a reduced risk of famine in Baidoa, global food security experts warned on Wednesday. The updated assessment from the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification, the UN backed platform that classifies food insecurity on a five phase scale where level 5 is famine, revised the number of people expected to face severe food insecurity between October and December of this year to 5.3 million, down slightly from the 5.4 million projected in the previous assessment in August.

For internally displaced people in Baidoa, the analysis confirms that through the end of the year they remain in emergency, Phase 4, for acute food insecurity, with acute malnutrition at critical level. But the area is no longer considered at risk of famine, "as the plausible worst-case scenario of extreme El Niño-induced flooding and displacement has not materialised and is unlikely to occur before the end of 2026." The updated findings drew on new data collected since August on food availability, nutrition, deaths and displacement.

Child malnutrition remains the sharpest edge of the crisis. The IPC estimates that another 7,000 children will suffer from malnutrition between July 2026 and June 2027, bringing the total number of children affected or expected to be affected to 1.92 million, including 437,000 children expected to face severe malnutrition.

The report stresses that the humanitarian situation in Somalia remains fragile, with millions in need of food and nutrition assistance, and calls for urgent and sustained support for food security, livelihoods, nutrition, health and water, sanitation and hygiene services, particularly for displaced communities and people in hard-to-reach areas. Status: assessment updated November 25, 2026; the famine-risk downgrade applies to Baidoa's displaced population only, not to the national caseload.
