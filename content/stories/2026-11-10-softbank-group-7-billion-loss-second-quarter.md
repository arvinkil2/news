**Latest.** SoftBank Group shares fell as much as 11 percent in Tokyo on Wednesday, trading as low as 3,465 yen, after the company reported its first net loss in almost two years. Bloomberg tied the drop to the $3.7 billion writedown on its US data center projects and falling AI valuations, and reported the charge covers nonrefundable advance payments made to secure power generation equipment for its 8 gigawatt Ohio project and other US data centers. The fall reverses Tuesday's 13.9 percent close higher, which came before the results were released.

SoftBank Group reported a net loss of 1.12 trillion yen, about $7 billion, for the fiscal second quarter, compared with a net profit of 2.5 trillion yen a year earlier, as investment losses and higher financing costs weighed on earnings. The Vision Fund segment swung to a loss of 81.58 billion yen from a profit of 2.38 trillion yen a year earlier, as declines at listed holdings including Coupang and Grab outweighed a $1.3 billion gain on ByteDance, while revenue of 2.22 trillion yen beat the 2.02 trillion yen LSEG estimate. The company also recorded a 597.2 billion yen loss over the six months from changes to procurement plans for power generation equipment for AI data center projects in the United States, said its cumulative investment in OpenAI stood at $54.6 billion at the end of September with a fair value of $99.6 billion, and its shares closed 13.9 percent higher in Tokyo on Tuesday as Asian technology stocks rebounded with Wall Street.
