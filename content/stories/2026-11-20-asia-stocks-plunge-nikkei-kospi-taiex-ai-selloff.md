**Latest.** Korean treasury bonds rallied as equities fell, Yonhap reported: the 3 year yield closed at 3.642 percent, down 7.4 basis points on the day, and the 10 year yield fell 8.5 basis points to 3.857 percent, with yields lower at every maturity. Foreign investors bought 12,541 contracts of 3 year treasury bond futures, and a Seoul bond dealer cited the Kospi breaking back below 5,000, and foreign buying of short-term treasury futures ahead of the Bank of Korea's policy meeting next week, as the drivers of the strength in bonds.

Asia's stock markets fell sharply on Friday, for a second straight session, as technology shares sold off after Anthropic's record IPO failed to restore confidence in the AI trade. Japan's Nikkei 225 ended 5.06 percent lower at 56,436.65, its lowest close since April 13, Reuters reported. South Korea's Kospi closed down 267.93 points, or 5.21 percent, at 4,869.96, its lowest level in 10 months, and Taiwan's Taiex plunged 6.63 percent to 35,877.63, Aju Press reported.

The selling followed Wall Street on Thursday, where the Nasdaq Composite fell 2.25 percent and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 2.42 percent. In Seoul, Samsung Electronics fell 7.01 percent to 183,100 won and SK hynix fell 5.92 percent to 1,113,000 won. Foreign investors sold a net 1.132 trillion won of Kospi shares and institutions sold a net 951.3 billion won, while retail investors bought a net 1.595 trillion won. The Kosdaq fell 5.71 percent to 645.68.

The pressure was heavy enough for Korean exchanges to suspend program sell orders for five minutes, on the Kospi at 11:54 a.m. and on the Kosdaq at 1:28 p.m., the first time both curbs have been used on the same day since November 6, Aju Press reported. The Korean won weakened to 1,336.2 per dollar, from 1,330.0 the previous day.

Aju Press tied part of the move to a Hunterbrook Media report that newer versions of AMD firmware had appeared in products from IEIT Systems, a Chinese server maker placed on a US trade blacklist in March 2025. The report did not establish any violation of US export laws, AMD said it complies with applicable export controls, and its shares fell 7.72 percent on Thursday. Anthropic opened at 111.50 dollars, above its 83 dollar offering price, and closed at 97.17 dollars, a debut that left the wider AI trade lower rather than steadier.

Friday's closes deepen Thursday's session, when Taiwan fell 4.4 percent and its Financial Supervisory Commission tightened short selling curbs for the second time in ten days. Those curbs took effect on Friday and did not stop the Taiex recording the largest fall of the three markets.
