**Latest.** The Houthis have answered the government strikes with ballistic missile and drone attacks towards Mecca, Riyadh and Saudi Arabia's Eastern Province, Al Jazeera reported on Monday, and have announced they will continue under a stated principle of siege for siege, airport for airport and escalation for escalation. Strategic affairs expert Ali al-Dahab told the network the Houthis now directly and completely control Yemen's western coastal sector, including the coast from Dhubab to the Bab al-Mandeb strait that government forces vacated on Sunday, and that the gains work largely to Iran's benefit. Retired Lebanese brigadier general Hassan Jouni said the fighting now centres on the mountainous triangle linking Aden, the besieged city of Taiz and Bab al-Mandeb, with Houthi forces at Tor al-Baha, about 80 kilometres from Aden, standing in the city's first defensive zone.

**Earlier, November 15.** Houthi forces have advanced to within 60 kilometres of Aden, the seat of Yemen's internationally recognised government, military and government sources said on Sunday, as the government pulled its last forces back from the Bab al-Mandab Strait to defend the capital. Xinhua reported that Houthi fighters seized the Kharaz military base and surrounding mountain positions in Lahj's Al-Madaribah wa Al-Arah district early on Sunday after fierce clashes, and that retreating government troops were building new fortifications along Aden's western approaches. Reuters reported that government forces around the strait had redeployed to al-Suqyah, 150 kilometres west of Aden, that Houthi fighters were positioned just outside Tur al-Bahah in Lahj, about 80 kilometres from Aden, and that the Houthis had released 600 prisoners, according to the International Committee of the Red Cross. The withdrawal marks the apparent end of a six-week government counteroffensive to reclaim the coast at the mouth of the Red Sea.

Yemen's Southern Armed Forces said on Sunday that they were redeploying troops from Dhubab Airport, in the Bab al-Mandab area of Taiz province, to Aden, as part of a broader reorganisation to reinforce front lines and secure the interim capital and neighbouring governorates. Government military spokesman Majed Al-Nuzaili welcomed the move, said defending Aden and lifting the Houthi siege of Taiz were linked responsibilities, and called for international support to secure the Bab al-Mandab Strait for international navigation. In a separate statement, Al-Nuzaili said Yemeni armed forces had carried out 906 strikes against Houthi targets on Sunday, a figure no independent source had confirmed.
