**Latest.** Singapore and the United States have agreed to continue discussions about Megaspeed, Reuters reported on November 16, citing the Straits Times. Singapore's Minister for Digital Development Josephine Teo raised the case when she met US Deputy Secretary of State Christopher Landau in Washington last week, the Straits Times reported, and Singapore's digital development ministry did not immediately respond to a Reuters request for comment. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on Friday that the Treasury, the Pentagon, the State Department and the US Trade Representative's Office had favoured putting Megaspeed on a trade blacklist for months, while the Commerce Department, which controls the list, had not.

Indonesian police raided the office of Megaspeed's local subsidiary earlier this year at the request of US authorities, as part of an inquiry requested by the Federal Bureau of Investigation, Reuters reported on November 16, citing the Straits Times. Police found unopened server racks at the site but no graphics processing units, the Straits Times reported, citing an unnamed Indonesian government source, who alleged that most Nvidia hardware bought by Megaspeed entities was likely transported to China. Shipping records reviewed by Reuters show the Indonesian subsidiary, created in May 2024, imported Nvidia hardware between 2024 and 2026, including H100 processors, in shipments from suppliers including Gigabyte subsidiary Giga Computing; Singapore's home affairs ministry said the US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security carried out a shipment verification check at Megaspeed in Singapore in June 2025. Indonesian police did not immediately respond to a Reuters request for comment, and there has been no public finding that Megaspeed violated export rules.

Megaspeed has broken its silence on the allegations that it served as a conduit for diverting Nvidia artificial intelligence chips to China. In a company statement reported by the Associated Press on November 15 and carried by Yonhap, the Singapore firm said Alice Huang left the company on August 30, 2025 and currently holds no role there, described reports of wrongdoing as claims with no verifiable evidence, and said it is conducting a lawful and legitimate business. The company said it has appointed an outside legal advisory team and will carry out a thorough internal investigation.

Megaspeed declined to comment on the allegations concerning Huang personally, calling them her private matters. The denial leaves standing the tension at the centre of the case: Singapore authorities have investigated Huang on the basis that she was the company's chief executive, board chair and key controlling shareholder, and a research firm's account earlier this year held that employees still treated her as the de facto head of the firm after her reported 2023 step back from its directorship.
