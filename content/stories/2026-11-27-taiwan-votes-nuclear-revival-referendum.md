Taiwan votes on Saturday on a referendum, proposed by the opposition Kuomintang and backed by the Taiwan People's Party, that would end the longstanding nuclear-free homeland policy; it passes only if yes votes outnumber no votes and exceed a quarter of all eligible voters. The ruling Democratic Progressive Party, which shut the last reactor at Maanshan in May 2025, is urging a no vote, even though President William Lai Ching-te said in March that the government would begin restarting the Maanshan and Kuosheng plants, and the Nuclear Safety Commission approved Taipower's Maanshan restart plan on September 24.

The referendum is the island's fourth nuclear vote in eight years, after a 2025 Maanshan restart majority was voided on low turnout. Semiconductor and AI demand is the new pressure: Taiwan's chip industry used more than 4.5 billion kilowatt-hours in September, up 11.55 percent on a year earlier, and S&P Global estimates TSMC alone could rise from about 8 percent of Taiwan's electricity in 2023 to almost 24 percent by 2030.
