Germany's political center is hollowing out. An INSA poll published Saturday put the far-right AfD at 30 percent — a first — with Chancellor Merz's CDU/CSU at a record-low 18 percent, a 12-point gap. Merz's personal approval sat at 10 percent in the Infratest-Dimap survey of October 1. ARD's DeutschlandTrend broadly confirmed the picture (AfD 27, the Union below). [REPORTED polling]

The governance implication is stark: a government polling at 18 percent with a 10-percent-approved chancellor cannot credibly deliver the defense ramp or budget consolidation Berlin has promised. For EU-level politics this matters directly — Germany is the economy whose fiscal posture sets the terms for joint borrowing debates, defense-spending coordination, and the credibility of European issuance frameworks. [ANALYTICAL]

Bond markets have not priced panic: Bunds rallied 6 basis points on Friday to 3.46 percent on the global long-end bid, and Germany remains the euro area's haven even as French spreads blow out. The transmission is slower and structural — a paralyzed Berlin raises tail risk on EU fiscal and defense decisions slipping further. With German 10-year yields at their highest since mid-2009 and the October 29 ECB meeting approaching, the nearer-term spread driver remains France, but the two stories are converging on the same question: whether European sovereign supply can clear at current yields without political credibility behind the fiscal math.
