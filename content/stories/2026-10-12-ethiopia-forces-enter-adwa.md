**Latest (Tuesday, October 13):** Ethiopian government troops reached Zalambessa, on the Ethiopian side of the Eritrean border, on Tuesday after a counter-offensive that has driven Tigrayan rebels from a string of northern towns, Reuters reported, citing six sources including people in touch with residents, a diplomat and a conflict expert. Witnesses and a conflict monitor said Eritrean troops had crossed into Zalambessa last week in support of the rebels, though it was unclear whether they were still there on Tuesday; Asmara denies any incursion. Ethiopia has told the Security Council that Eritrean troops advanced up to 60 kilometres inside its territory, while Eritrea's foreign minister has asked the Council to take action against Addis Ababa under Chapter VII of the UN Charter, and Council members were due to hold a closed-door meeting on the Horn of Africa on Tuesday.

Ethiopian government forces and an allied militia entered Adwa, about 40 kilometres from the Eritrean border, on Sunday, a day after Tigray People's Liberation Front fighters withdrew from the town, residents told AFP via Al Jazeera. One resident said shops were closed and government institutions, including Adwa Hospital, were looted overnight, while another said federal forces had deployed in the town but had not yet reached its centre.

The move follows the federal capture of Mekelle last week and of Adigrat days ago, and Ethiopian Airlines resumed flights to Mekelle on Sunday for the first time since September 23. Ethiopia has accused Eritrea, Sudan and Egypt of backing the armed alliance fighting with the TPLF; Khartoum and Cairo have denied involvement, and Eritrea severed diplomatic ties with Ethiopia over the accusations last week.
