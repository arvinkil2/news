The Trump administration plans to press ahead with efforts to block imports of Chinese data centre equipment, centred on a Federal Communications Commission proposal to ban US imports of new models of Chinese optical transceivers on national security grounds, Reuters reported on Tuesday, citing people familiar with the matter. Amazon, Google, Meta, CoreWeave and Cisco joined Microsoft in lobbying White House officials against the measure, telling them US firms could need up to a decade to comply and raising the risk that China retaliates by banning exports of indium phosphide, a material US makers need to produce their own transceivers. Chinese suppliers hold two-thirds of the global transceiver market according to Counterpoint Research, and shares of US rivals Coherent and Lumentum rose 2.2 percent and 1.5 percent on the report; no final ban has been issued, and the sources said the plan could still be shelved.
