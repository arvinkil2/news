Taiwan's Directorate General of Budget, Accounting and Statistics (DGBAS) raised its forecast for 2026 economic growth to 13.28 percent on Friday, an upward revision of 2.23 percentage points from its August forecast of 11.05 percent. Yonhap reported the new figure would be the island's fastest annual growth since 1978. The agency put 2027 growth at 6.51 percent in its first full outlook pairing for the two years.

The revision rests on another quarter of outsized actual results. Third quarter GDP grew 14.39 percent from a year earlier on the preliminary estimate, slightly above the 14.36 percent advance reading, and the agency revised first quarter growth to 15.44 percent and second quarter growth to 13.31 percent. Exports of goods and services rose 21.65 percent in the third quarter, gross capital formation expanded 26.74 percent, and manufacturing output grew 21.39 percent, led by semiconductors, computers, electronics and optical products.

For 2026 as a whole, DGBAS expects real exports of goods and services to grow 23.96 percent, private fixed capital formation to rise 14.90 percent and private consumption to grow 4.74 percent. The release credits the rapid advance of agentic AI and inference applications, continued AI infrastructure buildout by major global cloud providers, and price rises for memory and IC substrates amid shortages, alongside a pickup in traditional industry exports.

The annual revision of the national accounts lifted 2024 growth to 5.29 percent, from 5.27 percent, and 2025 growth to 8.94 percent, from 8.76 percent. Consumer price inflation is forecast at 2.07 percent for 2026, unchanged from the previous forecast, and 1.96 percent for 2027.

DGBAS flagged expanded United States AI export controls and local resistance to data centre construction as risks to investment costs and timelines in 2027, but said demand for AI infrastructure continues to outstrip supply, which it expects to sustain export and investment momentum even as growth moderates from this year's pace.
