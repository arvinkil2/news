Latest: Washington completed the maritime leg of the rollover on Tuesday. In a notice published in the Federal Register, the US Trade Representative modified the Section 301 action in its investigation of China's maritime, logistics and shipbuilding sectors to suspend the action for a further two months, effective November 9, 2026, with the suspension running through 11:59 p.m. Eastern Time on January 9, 2027. During the suspension no party accrues liability for, or has to pay, the maritime transport service fees under Annexes I, II and III of the April 2025 action, which fall on Chinese-owned, Chinese-operated and Chinese-built vessels, and the added duties on Chinese ship-to-shore cranes and cargo-handling equipment under Annex V.A stay suspended as well. USTR said the step allows further discussion with China; its October proposal to extend drew about 60 public comments, with supporters citing shipping costs and opponents warning the pause weakens incentives to rebuild US shipbuilding.

Latest: Beijing completed its side of the rollover late on Monday. In Announcement No. 58 of 2026, issued with the General Administration of Customs, the Ministry of Commerce said its 2025 Announcements No. 55, 56, 57 and 58 and its own Announcements No. 61 and 62, the October 2025 export control package that carried the rare earth licensing regime, will stay suspended from November 11 to January 10, 2027; the one-year suspension of that package had been due to expire on November 10. In a separate instrument, Order No. 4 of 2026, the ministry extended by two months from November 10 its suspension of the countermeasures it imposed in 2025 on five US subsidiaries of South Korea's Hanwha Ocean under the Anti-Foreign Sanctions Law, citing implementation of the China-US trade consultation consensus.

Latest: Washington formalised its side of the two-month rollover on Monday. A Bureau of Industry and Security final rule published in the Federal Register, effective November 9, extends through January 10, 2027 the suspension of the affiliates rule, which would extend Entity List and Military End User List restrictions to entities at least 50 percent owned by listed parties; the prior suspension was set to end the same day. BIS said it continues to assess the rule's potential impacts on US exporters and its implementation. January 10 is also the date to which Beijing extended its suspension of special port fees on American ships.

China will keep its additional 24 percent tariff on US goods suspended for two more months, the Customs Tariff Commission of the State Council announced on Monday, adjusting the measures set out in its 2025 Announcement No. 4 with State Council approval. The suspension takes effect at 13:01 Beijing time on Tuesday, November 10, and the 10 percent additional rate on US imports stays in place. The commission said the move implements the consensus reached at the Washington meeting between the two countries' leaders. Bloomberg reported that the Transport Ministry, in a separate statement, also extended China's suspension of special port fees on American ships until January 10.

The short term is the signal. The suspension being replaced ran a full year from November 10, 2025, and was due to expire this week; this rollover runs only to January, ahead of next week's APEC summit in China, where President Donald Trump expects to meet President Xi Jinping. In its accompanying statement the commission said China had noted that the United States has repeatedly said it intends to use Section 301 tariffs to replace the emergency-power tariffs struck down by the courts, and that Beijing would monitor that and adjust its own measures as appropriate.
