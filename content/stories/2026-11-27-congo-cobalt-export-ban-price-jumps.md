The Democratic Republic of Congo will suspend all cobalt exports from November 30 until further notice, its strategic minerals regulator ARECOMS said in a notice dated November 23. The suspension covers industrial, semi-industrial, small-scale and artisanal production, and it applies even to shipments authorised earlier that have not left the country by the effective date. Operators holding quotas must complete November export business by November 29.

ARECOMS said it took the decision on November 9 after a significant and sustained decline in cobalt prices since mid year, in a market marked by slowing consumption, rising inventories and speculative trading. The regulator will use the suspension to adjust its quota allocation system so export volumes match global demand. It will evaluate the measure four months after it takes effect, but the review does not end the ban: exports resume only when ARECOMS publishes a separate decision lifting it, and operators who export in breach can be disqualified from future quotas.

The price reaction was immediate. London Metal Exchange cobalt futures recently traded at $43,855 a tonne, up from $33,080 on Monday, a rise of about 33 percent, BMO Capital Markets analysts Helen Amos and George Heppel said on Thursday. Chinese cobalt futures also rose, with the December contract on the Wuxi Stainless Steel Exchange hitting its daily limit on Tuesday at its highest since August 18, Reuters reported.

Congo accounted for 230,000 of the 310,000 tonnes of cobalt mined worldwide in 2025, the United States Geological Survey estimates, and most of it is recovered alongside copper. A halt to shipments can squeeze overseas buyers while cobalt continues to accumulate at home. Glencore, the largest Western producer in Congo, produced 13,700 tonnes of cobalt in the first nine months of 2026, down 52 percent from a year earlier, and has increasingly retained cobalt in solution rather than processing it into saleable hydroxide while focusing on copper, the company said in October.

This is Congo's second export halt in under two years. The first ban ran from February to October 2025, when the regulator replaced it with quotas, and miners have no automatic right to carry unused quotas forward, ARECOMS said in its November 23 notice.
