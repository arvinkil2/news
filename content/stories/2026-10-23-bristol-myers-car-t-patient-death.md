Bristol Myers Squibb said a patient in its Phase 1 trial of zolacabtagene autoleucel, a CD19-targeting CAR-T therapy, developed delayed motor symptoms that did not respond to corticosteroids or intravenous immunoglobulin, then received high-dose chemotherapy, developed septic pneumonia and died the next day, 44 days after the infusion. The trial enrolled 66 patients, 30 of them with highly active relapsing multiple sclerosis; 42.4 percent had cytokine release syndrome events, and five cases of immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome resolved in about two days. Bristol Myers had voluntarily paused its zola-cel studies in September while it reviewed safety data, after Novartis paused its own autoimmune CAR-T trials, and its shares were trading about 2 percent lower after Friday's disclosure, Reuters reported.
