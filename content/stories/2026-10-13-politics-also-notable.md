Madagascar notified the WTO on October 12 that it had initiated a safeguard investigation on October 9 into imports of sugar confectionery and chocolate and cocoa confectionery. Interested parties have 30 days from initiation to identify themselves to the national authority, the ANMCC, and questionnaire responses are due within 30 working days. A safeguard, if imposed after the probe, would temporarily restrict imports found to cause serious injury to domestic producers.

The European Commission published its annual job quality report on Tuesday, calling job quality a priority in the EU. The Press Corner release did not render in full in this run, so no figures from the report are stated here.

In South Africa, the COSATU labour federation reconvened its national congress after the gathering collapsed last month, with reporting through Monday that most of the issues that caused the adjournment had been resolved and that a senior ANC figure walked out during proceedings. The federation is a formal alliance partner of the governing ANC, so its internal fight carries government stability implications.

In the United States, Democrats poured millions into advertising in the Iowa and Ohio Senate races, Bloomberg reported, while crypto aligned donors moved to boost Republican Pete Ricketts in Nebraska, according to Politico via Google News. Democrats also attacked the timing of a federal search of Representative Ilhan Omar's home so close to the election, a dispute already covered in its own story file on October 12.

Japan's Ministry of Finance announced on Tuesday the 20 year Japanese government bonds to be issued in October 2026, a routine auction calendar notice with no policy change attached.

In the Black Sea, drones sank a ship off Bulgaria on Tuesday in a second attack in a week. That incident is covered in full in today's commodities story on the Bulgaria drone attacks and is not duplicated here.
