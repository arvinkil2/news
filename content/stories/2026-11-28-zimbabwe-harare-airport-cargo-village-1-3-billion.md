A planned $1.3 billion cargo village at Robert Gabriel Mugabe International Airport in Harare has been granted National Project Status, the Zimbabwean designation that carries duty exemptions and expedited approvals for strategically important investments. The project is a public-private partnership between the state-owned Airports Company of Zimbabwe and Transvaal Africa, a local logistics, agriculture and aviation firm, acting through a joint special purpose vehicle.

The plan covers about 1,000 hectares at the airport and includes a second runway dedicated mainly to cargo, 142 specialised warehouses, cold-chain facilities, an aviation fuel system, a hotel and a shopping mall. Construction is expected to start in the first quarter of 2027 and finish, in phases, by 2030.

The commercial logic is perishable exports. Zimbabwean horticulture ships through foreign hubs, and dedicated freighter capacity at Harare has thinned; a consolidated cargo precinct with cold chain is meant to bring that traffic, and its handling revenue, home. The Airports Company said the aim is to consolidate cargo operations now scattered around the airport into one site.

What is not yet in place is the money. Accounts of the briefing differ on the funding structure and financial closure was still being finalised, with one report expecting it within 30 days of the announcement. National Project Status lowers the project's cost and approval risk; it does not supply the $1.3 billion.
