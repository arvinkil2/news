Iron Bear Resources released an updated prefeasibility study for its Iron Bear magnetite project in the Labrador Trough on November 23 that lifts the post-tax net present value by US$2.6 billion, or 29 percent, to US$11.6 billion, with no change to the iron ore price assumptions used in the first study in July. The post-tax internal rate of return rises to 17.9 percent from 15.2 percent, and upfront capital falls 5 percent to US$4.0 billion from US$4.2 billion.

The improvement comes from five engineering changes, including a revised crushing and grinding circuit, higher plant throughput and an optimised mining schedule, and from bringing commissioning of the third processing train forward by a year. Planned output rises 12 percent to 25.9 million tonnes a year, split between 7.3 million tonnes of high-grade blast furnace concentrate and 18.6 million tonnes of direct reduction pellets, over a 40-year mine life.

The project rests on a probable ore reserve of 3.3 billion tonnes grading 29.1 percent iron, inside a mineral resource of 13.6 billion tonnes grading 30.03 percent iron, with existing rail access to the export port of Pointe-Noire. The study was prepared to AACE Class 4 standard, so its capital estimate carries a wide accuracy range.

Iron Bear recently regained full control of the project after Vale withdrew from a farm-in agreement, having spent about US$18 million on it. The company plans to register its environmental impact assessment in the first quarter of 2027, estimates it needs about US$50 million to reach a decision to mine, and would then need to secure the US$4 billion development funding, which makes a strategic partner or offtake financing the next real test of the study's numbers.
