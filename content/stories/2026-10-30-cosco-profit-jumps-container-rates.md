Cosco Shipping Holdings' earnings jumped the most in two years in the third quarter, as persistent geopolitical disruption rerouted trade, tied up more of its vessels for longer and pushed container rates higher. Net income rose 81 percent to 17.2 billion yuan, about 2.6 billion dollars, according to a Thursday filing reported by Bloomberg, with transpacific sailings the biggest growth driver.

The mechanism is capacity absorption. Vessels sailing between Asia and Europe continue to route around Africa's Cape of Good Hope to avoid the Middle East, which stretches global supply chains and keeps effective capacity tight. Spot rates for a 40 foot container sit near two year highs, a level that held for most of the quarter, according to Drewry World Container Index data cited by Bloomberg.

Cosco is not an outlier, on the wire's read. Earnings estimates for Maersk and Hapag Lloyd have ticked up since July, Japan's Nippon Yusen raised an outlook that surpassed analyst expectations earlier on Thursday, and Taiwan's Evergreen Marine is set for its most profitable quarter since 2024 when it reports next month. Bloomberg Intelligence analyst Lindsay Chen expects container spot rates to stay where they are, with transpacific rates firmly anchored near post pandemic highs amid resilient US trade flows.

The container result pairs with the tanker market in the site tables, where tanker freight rose 5.62 percent on the day to 949.43 dollars and 5.97 percent on the week, while dry bulk freight eased 0.56 percent. Across both segments the pattern is the same one Scorpio's record product tanker bookings showed yesterday: the scarce asset is available tonnage on a safe route, and the freight line, not the cargo line, is capturing the premium.
