President Donald Trump told a rally in Lubbock, Texas, on Tuesday evening that his administration was officially terminating the Biden-era methane regulations, announcing an Environmental Protection Agency proposal to roll back the 2024 rules that required oil and gas operators to find and fix leaks and to check wells regularly for escaping methane.

The proposal would end the Super Emitter Program, which let EPA-approved third parties flag large leaks for investigation, give relief to marginal wells producing 15 barrels of oil equivalent a day or less, and ease the requirements aimed at phasing out routine flaring. The EPA estimates the changes would save the industry about $45 billion and support an additional 210 million barrels of oil and 1.41 trillion cubic feet of gas between 2027 and 2050.

The plan is a proposal, not a final rule: the White House confirmed the EPA will propose easing parts of the 2024 rule, which must still go through a public comment period, and environmental groups warned the rollback would raise methane emissions.
