President Donald Trump told a rally in Lubbock, Texas, on Tuesday evening that his administration was officially terminating the Biden-era methane regulations, announcing an Environmental Protection Agency proposal to roll back the 2024 rules that required oil and gas operators to find and fix leaks and to check wells regularly for escaping methane.

The EPA's own release lists five proposed changes: separate standards for marginal wells, which it says account for roughly four of every five producing wells in the country; rescinding the Super Emitter Program, which certified third-party monitors to flag large leaks; splitting the closed vent system standard so operational requirements allow for repairs; restoring 95 percent efficient flaring as the benchmark for associated gas instead of requiring it to reach a sales line; and technical flexibility for control devices. The agency estimates the changes would save consumers and businesses about $45 billion and support an additional 210 million barrels of oil and 1.41 trillion cubic feet of gas, a gain The Hill put at 2027 to 2050, citing an official.

The plan is a proposal, not a final rule: the EPA will take public comment for at least 45 days, including a public hearing, after the proposal appears in the Federal Register. The Environmental Defense Fund said the administration was curtailing the federal government's ability to address climate pollution in the largest emitting sectors, The Guardian reported.
