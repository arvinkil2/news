The House Foreign Affairs Committee said on Thursday that the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security approved more than 879,000 shipments of advanced AI chips made by Nvidia and AMD to Chinese companies from December 2025 through May 2026, against requests totalling 3.4 million chips. The approvals included 150,000 chips for Tencent, 75,000 for Alibaba, 43,000 for Baidu and 4,000 for BYD, the committee said, and covered Nvidia's H200 and AMD's equivalent MI325X. All four named buyers sit on the Pentagon's list of alleged Chinese military-backed companies, and committee chair Brian Mast said two further firms named in Nvidia licence applications, Maginfra and New Matrix, are suspected smuggling fronts. It is not clear how many of the approvals resulted in actual shipments.

Nvidia said the approvals were subject to strict conditions and limited volumes and that it has since stopped shipping H200s to China. AMD did not immediately respond to a request for comment, and the Commerce Department did not immediately respond either. The disclosure, made under Mast's authority under the Export Control Reform Act, lands as members of both parties press for stricter export controls on advanced AI chips to China.
