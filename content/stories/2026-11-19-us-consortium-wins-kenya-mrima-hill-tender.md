Kenya's mining ministry has issued a notice of intention to award the Mrima Hill niobium, rare earth elements and other minerals project in Kwale County to the Frontier Minerals consortium, comprising Frontier Minerals LLC, Bunker Hill Mining Corp, American Resources Corporation and ReElement Technologies, after an evaluation that began with expressions of interest in March 2026. The BBC reports the consortium beat seven bidders, including companies from China and Australia, for a site of about 390 acres whose deposits it estimates at more than $60 billion, and that two rival companies intend to challenge the selection, with Australia's RareX alleging the winner missed an earlier submission deadline.

The award is not a licence: Cabinet Secretary Hassan Ali Joho said the successful bidders must first complete community engagement and obtain all consents under the Mining Act, then apply for prospecting licences subject to Mineral Rights Board scrutiny. A joint venture between Fangda Special Steel Technology and Sinohydro won the other two tenders decided the same day, the Kang'ura chromite project in Samburu County and the Kamacabi copper project in Tharaka Nithi County.
