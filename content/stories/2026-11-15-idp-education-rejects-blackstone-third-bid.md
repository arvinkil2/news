IDP Education, the Australian student placement company that co-owns the IELTS English language test, on Monday rejected a revised A$3.00 a share cash proposal from Blackstone that valued it at about A$833.7 million (US$564.5 million). The offer, made by Blackstone Singapore for funds it manages through a scheme of arrangement, was 20 percent above the A$2.50 a share Blackstone offered in September and about 30 percent above its first A$2.30 approach in August, both of which IDP also turned down.

IDP said its board unanimously judged the proposal opportunistic in timing and not in shareholders' best interests, that it substantially undervalues the business and its stake in the IELTS testing network, and that it does not credit the benefits of a multi-year transformation programme that has passed its fiscal 2026 targets. The company said the bid did not warrant further engagement or due diligence access, reaffirmed fiscal 2027 adjusted EBIT guidance of A$95 million to A$115 million, and noted earnings are being held down by student visa restrictions in major destination markets, with volumes expected to contract in fiscal 2027 before returning to growth. Blackstone did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
