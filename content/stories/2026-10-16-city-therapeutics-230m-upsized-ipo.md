City Therapeutics priced its upsized initial public offering at 18 dollars a share, the midpoint of its 17 to 19 dollar range, selling 11,111,111 shares for gross proceeds of about 200 million dollars, the company announced on October 15. The deal was enlarged from the roughly 9.7 million shares first planned. Goldman Sachs, Jefferies and Stifel are joint book running managers, with Oppenheimer also acting as a joint book runner, and the underwriters hold a 30 day option on a further 1,666,666 shares. The offering is expected to close on October 19.

The shares opened at 23 dollars on Nasdaq on October 16 under the ticker CTY, traded as high as 23.88 dollars, and closed at 22.40 dollars, up 24.4 percent from the offering price, for a market value of about 1.2 billion dollars. Renaissance Capital put the market capitalisation at the IPO price at about 1,027 million dollars, and BioCentury reported tight allocations, with roughly half the book receiving nothing.

City is an RNA interference company co founded in 2023 by John Maraganore, the founding chief executive of Alnylam, and its lead programme CITY-FXI targets thrombosis prevention, with CITY-RBP4 in retinal disease and CITY-TFR2 in anaemia behind it. RNAi, silencing a gene by destroying its messenger RNA, is one of the few platform technologies with multiple approved products and a manufacturing playbook investors understand.

That is why the pricing and the aftermarket matter beyond one company. Biotech IPOs have been selective all year, and a deal upsized at the midpoint that then gains a quarter on debut, on the same day Iambic Therapeutics closed up 49 percent on its own debut week tally, tells the private companies in the queue that the window is open for quality, at a disciplined price.
