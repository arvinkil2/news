The European Citizens' Panel on Democratic Resilience has finished its work. Around 150 citizens, randomly selected from all 27 member states, finalised 23 concrete recommendations over the weekend in Brussels and handed them to Executive Vice-President Henna Virkkunen, the European Commission said.

The panel met over three weekends between September and November 2026. Its discussions covered political transparency and accountability, technological sovereignty, artificial intelligence, safe social media platforms and information integrity, alongside media literacy and citizenship education, citizen participation, social cohesion and democratic trust, inequalities and crisis preparedness.

The recommendations group into four priorities: helping citizens take part, through lifelong learning and critical thinking; reducing the gap between citizens and institutions, through stronger transparency and accountability in political decision making; information and technology, through reliable information, safe online spaces and media literacy; and a society that holds together, through solidarity between generations and social groups and inclusive policies.

The Commission said the recommendations will directly inform its work on democratic resilience, building on the European Democracy Shield presented in November 2025. An online debate on the Citizens' Engagement Platform runs from May to December 2026, with Commission follow up action from November 2026 and feedback on outcomes planned for 2027. The recommendations are advisory: what becomes policy depends on which of the 23 the Commission takes up in its Democracy Shield work programme. Status: announced (recommendations delivered).
