Invivyd announced on November 23 that VYD2311 (ibramvibart), its investigational monoclonal antibody for Covid-19 prevention, met all primary and secondary endpoints in an interim analysis of the Phase 3 DECLARATION trial. The analysis covered 2,359 participants randomized to a single 250 milligram intramuscular dose, monthly 250 milligram doses, or saline placebo.

The headline efficacy number needs its qualifier attached. Invivyd said mean neutralizing antibody titers over 90 days were consistent with 70 to 90 percent protection against RT-PCR confirmed symptomatic Covid-19 in both dosing arms. That is a prediction from antibody levels. The trial's actual infection outcomes remain fully blinded while the company continues to gather events intended to support traditional approval, and no detailed adverse event rates were released. The company said safety in both arms was in line with saline placebo.

Invivyd intends to submit a biologics license application in the first quarter of 2027 seeking accelerated approval, based on DECLARATION and on the supportive Phase 3 LIBERTY trial, which compared VYD2311 with an mRNA Covid-19 vaccine. The company expects a single dose to protect for approximately one year, an expectation the 90 day interim data do not themselves establish.

The investment question is regulatory, not statistical: whether the FDA will grant accelerated approval on a titer based surrogate for a prevention product aimed at a broad population, and what confirmatory evidence it will require afterward.
