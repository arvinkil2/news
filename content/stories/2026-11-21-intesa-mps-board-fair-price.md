Monte dei Paschi di Siena's board has approved the issuer's statement that Italian takeover rules require on Intesa Sanpaolo's offer, judging the consideration fair after Intesa raised it in October, while stating that the price does not fully reflect the value of the MPS group, Reuters reported. The statement lands three days before the acceptance period opens on November 24, and it closes the sequence that began when MPS shareholders voted down chief executive Luigi Lovaglio's defence plan on October 29. Intesa said on Saturday that its voluntary offer for all outstanding MPS shares, a proposed 34.5 billion euro acquisition, is now launched on the revised terms.

Each MPS share tendered brings 1.25 euros in cash and 1.664 newly issued Intesa shares. Intesa raised the cash part from 1 euro on October 3, conditional on the defence plan being rejected, and lifted the exchange ratio from 1.6 shares to offset its interim dividend. On the board's calculation, the revised terms carry a 15.3 percent premium to MPS prices before the offer was first announced in June, up from 12.5 percent at announcement, and the board has previously noted that comparable Italian bank deals have paid average premiums of about 30 percent, which is the basis for its position that the price, while fair, sits below the group's full value. The acceptance period runs from November 24 to January 22, 2027, with payment on January 29 and a possible reopening from February 1 to 5. Intesa is aiming for at least 66.67 percent of MPS capital, a threshold it can waive down to 50 percent plus one share.

Two approvals remain open. The European Central Bank authorised Intesa to take control of MPS on October 28, and Consob, the markets regulator, approved the offer document and prospectus on November 8. The competition authority's decision is due by November 30 and is expected to rest on Intesa's remedy package, centred on selling about 635 MPS branches, with most of the bank's central structures, to a new bank bought by the insurer Unipol for up to 3.5 billion euros. The government's golden power review is the second open item: Intesa notified the authorities on October 30, and Reuters reported on November 18 that Italy is leaning toward approving the transaction without conditions, with a formal decision expected within weeks. Intesa puts the combined group's expected savings at about 2.9 billion euros a year before tax by 2029, split broadly between costs and revenues.

The deal would reshape Italian banking. Intesa, already the country's largest bank, would absorb its third largest and create a group serving more than 27 million customers, strengthening its position against UniCredit. Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni's government had earlier sought a third large domestic banking group centred on MPS and Banco BPM; that project failed when MPS shareholders rejected the BPM and Banca Generali offers in October. What decides the outcome now is arithmetic and the two pending rulings: if acceptances fall short of the threshold Intesa is willing to accept, or if either authority attaches conditions Intesa will not carry, the offer can still fail after five months in the field.
