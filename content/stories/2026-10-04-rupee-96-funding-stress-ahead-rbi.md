India's currency and funding markets are tightening in tandem ahead of Tuesday's RBI decision. The rupee has slipped past 96 per dollar, while the Fed's September hike has narrowed the India-US rate differential, adding depreciation pressure. In domestic money markets, INR MIFOR overnight spiked 118bp week-on-week, a sharp tightening of short-term funding conditions.

The mechanism is the classic EM trilemma squeeze: a weaker currency plus a narrower rate gap plus imported energy inflation (Brent above $100) forces the central bank's hand even without a domestic demand overheating story. BofA's pulled-forward call, tightening starting October 7 with the repo reaching 6.25% in H1 2027, effectively says the RBI can no longer look through the currency channel.

The funding-stress angle is underappreciated. An MIFOR overnight spike of this size does some of the RBI's tightening work before it acts, but it also raises the stakes of a Tuesday hold, which would contradict both the 60% poll consensus and the market's own funding-price signal. For global fixed income, India is the template trade: where currency pressure meets energy inflation, the easing cycle ends, and the question is which EM central bank is next.
