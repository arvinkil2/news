**Latest (Wednesday, October 14):** fresh Reuters reporting filled in Tuesday night's Dhahran attack and sharpened the conflict between the accounts of it. The energy ministry said authorities responded to the incident in an administrative building at the headquarters complex late on Tuesday, took the necessary measures, and that there were no injuries or deaths. A company source told Reuters a ballistic missile hit the complex and struck an office tower, damaging its top three floors, and the source and two other people with knowledge of the matter said the building was on fire; six sources told Reuters they heard loud booms near the complex. The coalition said fragments of a missile it had intercepted and destroyed over Dhahran fell on a residential area, without giving further details. That sits against Aramco's account, reported earlier by Bloomberg, that only interception debris reached the site and started parking area fires, with no damage to the headquarters building and no impact on production or operations. The complex hosts a major operations centre overseeing Aramco's network of onshore and offshore oil rigs, pipelines, refineries and export terminals. Brent crude rose above 106 dollars a barrel in Asian trade on Wednesday as the Saudi attacks and stepped up Iranian strikes on Hormuz shipping landed together.

**Latest (Tuesday, October 13, evening):** the coalition confirmed in a Tuesday evening statement that it attacked the Yemen Petroleum Company's Sabah central station in Sanaa and other sites, saying the operation aimed to neutralise Houthi capabilities threatening Saudi Arabia, Yemen and international waterways, and that sites in Sanaa, Hodeidah and Dhamar were being used to supply the group's missile and drone arsenal against the kingdom and Red Sea shipping, Reuters reported. The Yemen Petroleum Company said the Sabah facility held no petroleum stocks at the time, so the strike did not affect oil reserves or supply chains. The Houthis renewed their warning to oil workers in Saudi Arabia to stay away from potential target sites, and President Erdogan told Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman on Tuesday that Turkey would stand by the kingdom, the Turkish presidency said; Turkey and Pakistan signed a mutual defence pact with Saudi Arabia this summer, though Ankara still needs to ratify it in parliament. Washington has so far rebuffed Saudi efforts to bring it directly into the war, Reuters reported.

**Latest (Tuesday, October 13, evening):** the same interception wave that reached Dhahran also hurt people in the capital. Saudi Civil Defence said debris from two intercepted cruise missiles injured four people in Riyadh and damaged 15 homes, the Saudi Press Agency reported via ERT News. In Dhahran, the Energy Ministry said the attack targeted Aramco's headquarters administrative building; the company said a small fire in a parking area was quickly brought under control, with no injuries at the site and operations unaffected.

**Latest (Tuesday, October 13, night):** the Aramco headquarters compound in Dhahran was reached by debris from a missile intercepted overhead on Tuesday night, Bloomberg reported, after a series of attacks across Saudi Arabia in which Dammam, Dhahran and Haradh were among the places targeted. Aramco confirmed debris had reached the site and said falling debris ignited fires in an employee parking area and scattered fires elsewhere on the grounds, all of which were extinguished; the company said there were no casualties, no damage to the headquarters building or its process facilities, and no impact on production or operations. A source with direct knowledge of the matter told Reuters the headquarters had been struck, though it was unclear whether the building was hit directly or by falling debris, and loud explosions were heard in the area; Aramco had not commented at the time of that report. Coalition spokesman Turki al-Maliki said a ballistic missile launched by the Houthis toward Dammam was intercepted and destroyed, and Al Jazeera reported shrapnel from an interception fell in a residential area of Dhahran. The attack landed hours after the Houthis told civilians to evacuate the area around the headquarters, naming it as a target. No oil price reaction had been reported in the accounts read.

**Latest (Tuesday, October 13):** the Saudi-led coalition ordered civilians to evacuate the Yemeni Oil Company's al-Sabah central station in Sanaa on Tuesday, and explosions were heard in the capital shortly after, Al Jazeera reported, with Houthi-affiliated media saying air strikes hit an oil facility in the al-Sabah area. Al Jazeera's correspondent in Sanaa also reported strikes on oil storage tanks at the Ras Isa port and on an oil station in Hodeidah province. Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree answered with a warning of a harsh reprisal against Riyadh and told civilians to evacuate the Aramco headquarters in Dammam, King Khalid International Airport and an Aramco refinery in Riyadh, naming all three as targets. The coalition said it intercepted three drones north of Riyadh on Tuesday, and the Saudi cabinet, chaired by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, said the Houthis would face consequences for the attacks. President Trump said the Houthis were behaving very badly and that he might ask President Erdogan of Turkey to intervene in support of Saudi interests. Yemeni government forces separately said they carried out nine air strikes on Houthi positions in Taiz governorate on Tuesday.

**Latest (Monday, October 12, night):** the fallout from three days of attacks is hardening into a longer shutdown. Saudi authorities said King Khalid International Airport would remain closed until Saturday, Al Jazeera reported, after Saturday's strike killed at least 13 people and Sunday's killed one more. Etihad Airways cancelled all flights to and from Riyadh until next week and suspended Dammam and Jeddah services, Gulf Air cancelled its Riyadh flights until Saturday, and Delta delayed the launch of its direct Atlanta to Riyadh flights planned for October 23. The coalition said Monday night's ballistic missile at Riyadh and two more fired at Khamis Mushait were all intercepted and destroyed. Saudi authorities have opened an investigation into why the air defences failed to stop Saturday's barrage, according to two regional officials who spoke to the Associated Press; an initial inquiry found the Houthis first sent drones to clear a path for a mix of cruise and ballistic missiles. President Trump, asked what he planned to do about the group, said the Houthis were misbehaving very badly but gave no indication Washington would intervene. A Houthi political bureau member, Mohammed al-Bukhaiti, told AFP that US involvement would bring a swift response against US interests in the region, and warned workers at Saudi oil facilities and airports to stay away from sites the group calls legitimate targets.

**Latest (Monday, October 12, evening):** the Houthis claimed a ballistic missile struck King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh on Monday evening, alongside a separate drone operation against the Khamis Mushait air base, and warned international airlines, experts, employees and travelers to avoid Saudi airports, exempting Mecca and Medina. The claim of a hit is the group's account and is not corroborated: the coalition said the missile fired toward Riyadh was intercepted and destroyed. The interception was felt across the capital: Al-Nassr's Asian Champions League match against Neftchi Fergana, scheduled for 9:15 p.m. local time, was delayed by an hour, AFP correspondents reported hearing explosions in Riyadh, and residents received phone alerts warning of a hostile aerial threat and telling them to stay home or in a safe place. The match was played after the announcer said the situation was safe.

**Latest (Monday, October 12, afternoon):** the Saudi-led Coalition to Restore Legitimacy in Yemen said its forces intercepted and destroyed a ballistic missile launched by the Houthis toward Riyadh on Monday, per coalition spokesman Major General Turki Al-Malki, reported by Arab News. Yonhap separately reported three explosions heard over the Saudi capital. No casualties from the intercepted missile were reported in either account.

Speaking at the WPC Energy Congress in Riyadh on Monday, Energy Minister Prince Abdulaziz bin Salman said recent Houthi attacks on Saudi airports and energy facilities were serious and that the kingdom's response must be equally serious. Saudi authorities said a Pakistani man was killed in a Sunday attack on a terminal at King Khalid International Airport, a day after a Houthi missile hit another terminal, killing 13 people and forcing its closure.

The Houthis said the airport attacks retaliated for Saudi operations in Yemen, and the Houthi-run health ministry said a child was killed in a Saudi air strike on a home in Sanaa on Sunday, claims that remain each side's account pending independent confirmation.
