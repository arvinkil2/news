Pennsylvania will invest $371.5 million of state capital in PhilaPort, the Port of Philadelphia, in a programme the governor's office values at more than $837 million in total, Governor Josh Shapiro announced on October 13. The state money is expected to unlock a further $466 million, comprising $266 million of federal support and more than $200 million of private investment.

The centrepiece is a new 2,000 foot berth at Southport Marine Terminal, the port's first new berth in more than 50 years, built using two US Department of Transportation grants. The state allocation breaks down as $145 million for reclamation at Southport Berth II and the North Wharf, $109.4 million for land acquisition, $58.1 million for cranes and equipment, and $31 million for dredging at Southport Berth I. The programme also develops the 152 acre Mustin Yard parcel and upgrades the Tioga Marine Terminal for liquid bulk and breakbulk traffic.

Officials project the expansion will lift container capacity by 122 percent and automobile capacity by 18 percent, create more than 6,000 jobs, and generate $45 million a year in state and local tax revenue. Those are projections from the governor's office, not outcomes.

The base is real but small beside its East Coast rivals. PhilaPort handled a record 889,268 TEU in 2025 and supported more than 8,000 jobs and about $1.2 billion of economic output in Pennsylvania. The bet is that berth capacity, not demand, is what has kept a productive mid Atlantic port at that scale.
