**Latest.** Bahrain's foreign ministry condemned Sunday's drone attack in a statement, saying the kingdom's security is a red line and its sovereignty non-negotiable, EFE reported via RPP. The ministry said Bahrain rejects its territory being turned into a battlefield for settling scores, holds Iran fully responsible for the attacks and their consequences, called them a sabotage of de-escalation efforts and a flagrant violation of international law, urged the United Nations to adopt decisive measures, and reserved the right to legitimate self-defence. The ministry's account said Bahraini air defences intercepted and destroyed the six drones in its airspace, and no casualties or confirmed damage were stated.

**Latest.** Bahrain's defence forces said their air defence systems intercepted the Iranian drone attacks on Sunday before Iran could achieve its goals, describing the attack as a systematic hostile approach, Arab News reported. Saudi Arabia's foreign ministry strongly condemned the Iranian aerial attacks on Bahrain, expressing full solidarity with Manama and support for all measures taken to protect the country's security and stability. Neither statement reported damage or casualties.

Explosions were heard in Bahrain around 4:00 a.m. local time on Sunday and a second series was reported around 6:30 a.m., an AFP journalist reported, as alarm sirens sounded and the interior ministry issued its alerts. The updated AFP report carried the same Revolutionary Guard statement claiming six drones targeted the US air defence radar system at the Al-Riffa helicopter base, and stated no US or Bahraini response to the Sunday claim and no damage or casualties.

Bahrain's interior ministry issued two separate alerts about potential threats in posts on X on Sunday, the first such warnings by the Gulf country since September, urging citizens and residents to remain calm and proceed to the nearest safe location, Reuters reported. The ministry gave no further details on the threats, and no damage or casualties were reported.

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said six of its Aerospace Force drones attacked a US air-defence radar station at the Er-Riffa helicopter base in Bahrain, describing the strike as a second-stage response to US aggression, TASS and RIA Novosti reported, the latter citing Press TV. Neither report stated damage to the radar or casualties, and neither carried a US response to that specific claim. Reuters noted the alerts came two days after the Guard's earlier claim against a US early-warning radar in Bahrain, which the US military denied, saying it had intercepted two Iranian drones with no harm caused.
