**Latest:** The United States widened its Middle East diplomatic drawdown on Friday, reducing staff at its embassies in Jordan, Lebanon, Qatar and the United Arab Emirates after Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton warned Secretary of State Marco Rubio in a one-page memo that Iran had substantially increased its efforts to target American personnel and that lethal plots had reached advanced stages in the Middle East and in certain other countries, the Associated Press reported. AP obtained the memo, which Axios first reported, and three US officials confirmed it was sent. The four embassies are already working under restricted operations and will continue to operate, and their personnel have not been directed to depart, unlike Saudi Arabia, where non-emergency employees and their family members were ordered to leave on Wednesday. When it announced the reductions, the State Department pointed to the growing danger from the Houthis in Yemen and made no direct reference to Iran. A US official confirmed the memo to IANS and said the threat of Houthi attacks and of Iranian terror plots targeting US officials are informing staffing decisions at the region's restricted-operations embassies.

CNN reported early on Saturday that the department is accelerating the departures and is also quietly reducing staffing in Bahrain and Kuwait, and that diplomats called the process chaotic, with many employees and families having only recently returned after being evacuated during the war with Iran earlier this year. Bloomberg reported that an American official linked the departure orders and staffing changes to Iranian efforts to target Americans and to threats from Houthi militants. US officials firmly rejected any suggestion that the drawdown is connected to possible future American or Israeli strikes on Iran, AP reported. President Donald Trump told a rally in Florida on Friday: "We may do another little Operation Epic Fury, or [the Iranians] will agree."

Saudi Arabia's General Authority of Civil Aviation said on Thursday that the two people killed in Wednesday evening's attack on King Fahd International Airport in Dammam were a Saudi citizen and a resident of Indian nationality, raising the toll from the one death reported on Wednesday, and that operations at the airport have resumed and air traffic has returned to normal. The authority said the eight injured were one Saudi citizen, two residents each of Indian, Yemeni and Bangladeshi nationality and one resident of Nepalese nationality, their injuries ranging from minor to severe. Separately, the Saudi-led coalition said four drones were shot down over the Eastern Province early on Thursday, and its spokesman, Major General Turki Al-Maliki, said a drone was intercepted and destroyed in the Riyadh region later in the day.

The Pentagon has been quietly reducing the number of US troops at bases across the Middle East in recent days as regional threats grow, CBS News reported, citing a US official and a senior US military official who linked the drawdown to the expanding conflict in Yemen and direct threats from Iran. Captain Tim Hawkins, a spokesman for US Central Command, told CBS News that US forces regularly move personnel and equipment to optimize readiness and that the command does not discuss the details of such movements for operational security reasons, Interfax reported.

An attack on King Fahd International Airport in Dammam, in eastern Saudi Arabia, on Wednesday evening killed two people and injured eight others, with injuries ranging from minor to serious, Saudi Arabia's General Authority of Civil Aviation said in a statement on X reported by AFP; the authority first reported one death on Wednesday before identifying both fatalities on Thursday. The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted and destroyed five drones in the oil-rich region. AFP noted the Iran-backed Houthis continue to target airports and oil facilities across the kingdom; no claim of responsibility for the Dammam attack was reported in the accounts read.

Earlier on Wednesday, Saudi Arabia's Ministry of Energy said two people, nationals of the Philippines and Nepal, were killed and two others, nationals of Pakistan and India, were injured on Wednesday by falling debris after a drone targeting an installation at the Shaybah oil field, in the east of the country, was intercepted, ERT News reported, citing the Athens-Macedonian News Agency. The ministry's announcement did not say where the drone came from.

The US State Department has ordered non-emergency government employees and their family members to leave Saudi Arabia, spokesman Tommy Pigott confirmed on Wednesday, saying the safety of department personnel and their families is the highest priority and linking the move to Houthi attacks on the country, including on airports. A diplomatic source had told AFP that all American staff and their families were ordered to depart the embassy in Saudi Arabia and that families in the United Arab Emirates, Jordan, Qatar and Bahrain were also told to leave, a wider scope that Pigott said does not apply, as the order covers Saudi Arabia only.

Reuters had reported earlier on Wednesday that the department was preparing ordered departures of some personnel and family members from embassies across the Gulf, citing three US officials, two of whom named a possible resumption of hostilities, particularly with the Iran-aligned Houthis in Yemen, as the reason. The US Embassy in Riyadh issued a security alert the same day warning that Iranian-supported Houthis have attacked civilian airports and other critical infrastructure in Saudi Arabia, that operations at King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh remain suspended, and that the hostilities could escalate rapidly.
