Nearly 150 garment sites across Bangladesh have closed this weekend as workers strike for a higher minimum wage, with dozens of factories shut in the main clothes-producing hubs of Savar, Ashulia and Gazipur, Reuters reported. Workers are demanding a minimum of 30,000 taka (£182) a month against the current 12,500 taka, and on Sunday morning workers blocked the Dhaka-Tangail highway in Gazipur for about an hour before police stepped in; in Gazipur city, police shut 13 factories on Saturday night and 16 more during the Sunday morning unrest after workers walked out.

A government minimum wage board formed on November 1 is due to finalise its recommendations by the end of November, with a revised pay structure expected to take effect from December 1. The garment manufacturers' associations BGMEA and BKMEA are calling for protection for factories, and BGMEA president Mahmud Hasan Khan said factories unable to continue production because of the unrest will remain indefinitely closed from Monday under the labour law's no work, no pay provision.
