Cargo moving along Russia's Northern Sea Route passed 40 million tonnes for the first time on November 17, Rosatom chief executive Alexey Likhachev said, calling it a serious psychological threshold. The previous record was 37.9 million tonnes in 2024; traffic slipped to 37.02 million tonnes in 2025 as delays to Rosneft's Vostok Oil and Novatek's Arctic LNG 2 held volumes back, according to the Gekon consultancy cited by Kommersant.

Most of the cargo is Russian energy heading east, and a large share comes from sanctioned projects, though Rosatom has given no breakdown of this year's total by cargo or sanctions status. Cargo between Russian and Chinese ports came to 5.4 million tonnes in 2025, about 15 percent of the total, and China is the main foreign customer.

Transit traffic, cargo passing through rather than loading or discharging in the Russian Arctic, reached 6 million tonnes by early November, twice the level of a year earlier, according to Arctic Development Minister Alexey Chekunkov. Rosatom's Vladimir Panov said on October 27 that transit would exceed 7 million tonnes this year and that transit container voyages would double to 46, with international container transit above 600,000 tonnes. Independent tracking by Norway's Centre for High North Logistics is more conservative, identifying 104 confirmed transits by September 30 with an estimated 3.7 million tonnes of cargo.

Even at a record, the route carries half the 80 million tonnes that President Vladimir Putin's May 2018 decree set as the target for 2024. Panov said the route would operate year-round from early 2027, when a major shipper begins regular deliveries to Asia. For commodity markets, the significance is structural: a growing share of Russian crude and LNG now reaches Asia through the Arctic, shortening voyages when ice allows and bypassing the Suez route entirely.
