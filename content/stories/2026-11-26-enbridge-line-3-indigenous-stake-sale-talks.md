Enbridge is reportedly in talks with a group of Indigenous communities for the sale of a stake in its Line 3 pipeline network, the Globe and Mail reported, citing unnamed sources. The stake is 6 percent and the sale could fetch over 1 billion dollars, about 1.5 billion Canadian dollars, which would make it the largest transaction involving Indigenous communities in Canada.

The Line 3 system carries about 760,000 barrels of crude a day, linking Alberta's oil sands to Wisconsin, within Enbridge's larger network capable of carrying up to 3.2 million barrels a day. An Enbridge spokesman told the Globe that the company is currently exploring financial partnerships across numerous assets where the economics are favourable for the Indigenous partners and for its business.

The talks follow two US acquisitions by Enbridge this year. In August it bought Salt Creek Midstream's crude gathering business in the Delaware Basin, and in September it agreed to buy Tallgrass Energy's crude transportation business for 2.55 billion dollars in cash, including a 75 percent interest in the 1,050-mile Pony Express Pipeline and a 51 percent interest in the Powder River Gateway system.

No buyer group, financing structure or timetable has been disclosed for the Line 3 stake, and the reported terms remain unconfirmed by the company.
