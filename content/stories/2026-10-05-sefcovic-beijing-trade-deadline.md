## Latest (Friday, Oct 9): 16-point consensus, first-ever Chinese agreement to moderate hybrid exports

The second meeting of the China-EU trade and investment consultation mechanism, held in Beijing on October 8-9 between commerce minister Wang Wentao and EU trade commissioner Maros Sefcovic, produced a 16-point consensus covering trade, investment, export controls and WTO reform, according to the joint statement issued by China's Ministry of Commerce. The centerpiece is an understanding on hybrid and plug-in hybrid vehicle trade under which China agreed to moderate exports, the first time Beijing has accepted export restraint without prior trade tension, Sefcovic said. He told reporters the arrangement, measured against a no-change projection over four years, would more than halve hybrid and plug-in hybrid exports to the EU, preventing "several million" cars from entering the European market, though he declined to disclose the mechanism or numerical parameters until EU leaders review them next week.

The two sides also agreed to make rare-earth and permanent-magnet export licensing smoother and more predictable for European companies, to continue procedures on company price undertakings in the EU's anti-subsidy case against Chinese electric vehicles, and to keep exploring tariff reductions on certain goods under WTO rules. A ministerial-level video meeting is set for January, with the third TIC meeting in March 2027. Sefcovic called the package a blueprint and "the beginning" of rebalancing, saying he is confident leaders will endorse it.

EU trade commissioner Maroš Šefčovič meets Chinese commerce minister Wang Wentao in Beijing on October 8-9 to discuss China's growing trade surplus with the EU, a record €360.6 billion in 2025, up 15 percent from 2024, widening another 10 percent in early 2026. The visit is the deadline Brussels set in June for the first tangible results from its dialogue with Beijing; EU leaders meet October 15-16 to judge what he brings home.

Five days before the talks, China's Ministry of Commerce announced an anti-dumping investigation into p-nitrotoluene imported from the EU, following an application by Chinese producers. The chemical is used in dyes, pharmaceuticals, pesticides and pigments, a specialised product, but the timing places it squarely inside the wider argument over EU-China trade. Beijing has warned it would respond firmly to new EU restrictions on Chinese companies.

Šefčovič will also seek assurances on rare earths and minerals, with the one-year moratorium on extra Chinese export restrictions due to expire in the coming weeks. In June, China assured him existing controls would not disrupt EU supply chains, but German companies are reportedly stockpiling rare earths in case the talks fail. Šefčovič told Euronews last month that without concrete results, "the political interest would be to look for the solution through other instruments", Brussels-speak for the harsher measures EU leaders will weigh on October 15-16.
