New York Federal Reserve President John Williams said on Monday that inflation has continued to run too high and that the Federal Reserve must take actions over time to ensure it returns to the 2 percent goal in a timely manner and on a sustained basis, in remarks at the University of Mount Saint Vincent in New York. Williams said he expects inflation to end this year at about 3.5 percent, get closer to the target in 2027 and reach 2 percent in 2028, and he put current price pressures down to the conflict in Iran, demand for goods associated with the AI build-out and tariffs, whose effects he said have largely abated. He did not address the December meeting directly; CNBC reported that markets fully price a quarter percentage point increase at the December 8 to 9 meeting, and that his remarks were expected to be the last major policy statement from a Fed official before that decision.
