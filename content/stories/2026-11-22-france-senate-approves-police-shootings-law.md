France's parliament has given final approval to a law presuming that police officers and gendarmes who use their weapons acted lawfully, Reuters reported. The Senate passed the text on Friday by 205 votes to 110, in the same wording the National Assembly adopted in July, which completes its passage through parliament.

The law stipulates that when officers use their weapons, they are presumed to have acted within the framework of the law, unless there is evidence to the contrary. It does not expand the situations in which officers may open fire; it shifts the burden of proof. Interior Minister Laurent Nunez has said the presumption ends the automatic placement in custody of an officer who has fired, who would instead be questioned voluntarily where appropriate.

The measure was a long standing demand of far right politicians, and left wing parliamentarians oppose it and seek to challenge it at the Constitutional Council, Reuters reported. Critics, including rights organisations and unions, call it a licence to kill and warn it will make it harder for victims' families to seek justice.

The text can now be promulgated unless the Constitutional Council blocks it. Its status is passed by parliament, not yet in force.
