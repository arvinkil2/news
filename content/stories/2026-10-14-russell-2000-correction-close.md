US stocks closed lower on Wednesday after September consumer prices rose 0.6 percent on the month, the biggest increase in five months, taking annual inflation to 3.7 percent, faster than economists expected. The S&P 500 fell 1.1 percent, its steepest one day fall in more than two months, with concern over the cost of artificial intelligence investment adding to the inflation pressure, the New York Times reported.

The selling was heaviest in small companies. The Russell 2000 slid 1.9 percent, taking its fall from its August high to 11 percent and into a technical correction for the first time since March, Bloomberg reported. Declines were broad, with roughly five stocks in the index falling for every one that climbed, as rising inflation and rising interest rates pushed the riskiest shares below important averages.
