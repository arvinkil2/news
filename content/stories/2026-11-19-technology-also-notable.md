**San Diego ends its Flock contract.** San Diego's city council voted on Monday evening to end the city's contracts with surveillance vendors Flock and Ubicquia, three years after the mayor urged approval of the system. More than 500 cameras will go dark. It is the second time in less than a decade the city has adopted and then rejected the technology: an earlier smart streetlight programme was shut down in 2020, and Ubicquia, which acquired that platform, proposed the 500-camera installation with Flock's automatic licence plate reader software in 2023. Police told the council that vehicle theft has fallen 20 percent since 2023 and that 689 stolen vehicles were recovered with the cameras' help. Flock, valued at an estimated $8.3 billion, said it respects the decision but warned that removing a tool that helps solve violent crimes moves public safety backward. Mayor Todd Gloria said he respected the council's decision and would not veto it. The advocacy group Secure Justice says at least 513 Flock contracts have now ended across the United States.

**Amazon and Google will hold 28.4 percent of Anthropic.** Anthropic's amended S-1, filed on Tuesday, shows Amazon will hold 14.7 percent of the company after the IPO and Google 13.7 percent, together 28.4 percent, all in non-voting Class N shares created from converted notes and a private placement. Amazon will hold 1,920,511,218 Class N shares and Google 1,789,598,269, on the filing's $74 midpoint assumption. Amazon has kept billions of dollars of notes unconverted: converting everything now would take it to 23.8 percent, but it has elected not to waive the ownership cap that limits conversion. A group of tech and chip companies, AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC Partners, agreed to buy $26 billion of the non-voting stock in the placement, with Google buying $10 billion of it.
