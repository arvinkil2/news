The Chicago Department of Aviation broke ground on November 17 on the New Concourse E at O'Hare International Airport, a $917 million project that will add 24 gates in two phases as part of the $8.8 billion ORDNext modernization program.

The first phase, worth $640 million and targeted for completion in 2030, will build 14 gates and about 315,000 square feet of passenger space, including nearly 21,000 square feet of concessions. The second phase, worth $277 million, adds 10 more gates once the O'Hare Global Terminal is completed in late 2033, bringing the concourse to its full configuration in 2034. The two level concourse stretches more than 1,000 feet and is reached mainly through an underground passenger tunnel from Concourse D.

Construction is led by the AECOM Hunt, Clayco and Bowa joint venture, the same team building the $1.45 billion Concourse D, which adds 19 gates and is due for completion in late 2028. An August revision to the construction sequence lets the first phase of Concourse E proceed at the same time as Concourse D, rather than after it.

Aviation Commissioner Michael McMurray said starting Concourse E now adds gates sooner and creates the capacity needed to advance the Global Terminal, which replaces the current Terminal 2, is scheduled to begin construction in 2029 and will nearly double that terminal's size. The design is led by Skidmore, Owings and Merrill with Ross Barney Architects, Juan Gabriel Moreno Architects and Arup.
