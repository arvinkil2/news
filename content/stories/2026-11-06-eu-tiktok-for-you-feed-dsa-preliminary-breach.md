The European Commission preliminarily found that TikTok did not adequately assess how its recommendation algorithm may lead users into "rabbit holes" of repeated content about sadness, isolation or body image, and that its measures to diversify recommendations and its user controls, including the "not interested" button, do not effectively reduce that exposure. The Commission said the algorithm treats repeated watching or liking of such content as positive interest and prioritizes viewing time over a user's choice to see less of it.

TikTok can examine the investigation file and reply in writing, and the European Board for Digital Services will be consulted. Executive Vice-President Henna Virkkunen said the Digital Services Act makes platforms responsible for the negative effects of their recommender systems and that TikTok needs to step up its efforts to protect users' well-being, including minors.
