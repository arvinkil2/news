Asian stocks rallied on Tuesday, led by Taiwan, where the benchmark index jumped more than 9 percent after a 17.5 percent weekly plunge, its steepest since 1991. South Korea's Kospi climbed 7.1 percent, Japan's Nikkei 225 advanced 4.1 percent, and MSCI's Asia-Pacific index excluding Japan rose 4.6 percent, its biggest daily gain since July 31, snapping an eight-session slide.

Chipmakers led the move, with TSMC up 9.9 percent, MediaTek and Delta Electronics each gaining about 10 percent, SK Hynix up 8.9 percent and Samsung Electronics up 8.5 percent. The session followed Wall Street's strongest day since April 2025, where Nvidia surged 31.5 percent, its biggest one-day gain in 23 years, after its board reinstated founder Jensen Huang as chief executive.
