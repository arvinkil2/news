El Niño will keep intensifying to a December peak that could make it one of the strongest since at least 1950, the World Meteorological Organization said Thursday in its monthly climate update, with sea-surface temperature anomalies forecast near 3.7 degrees Celsius above normal for October through December, eclipsing the 2015-16 record of 2.6. The agency put near-100 percent odds on the event persisting through February 2027, and WMO chief Celeste Saulo said it is firmly established and strengthening rapidly, warning of above-average global temperatures and shifted rainfall patterns raising drought and flood risk in different regions. The event is already hitting Central America, where drought is killing crops and livestock.
