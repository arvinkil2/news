Mahanadi Coalfields, the biggest producing unit of state owned Coal India, made a muted stock market debut on Friday. The shares opened at 134 rupees on the National Stock Exchange, just below the 136 rupee issue price, then traded around 137 rupees, leaving the company valued near $9.59 billion, Reuters reported.

The initial public offering raised about $963 million at the top of its 129 to 136 rupee price band, but none of it went to Mahanadi. The sale was entirely an offer for sale: Coal India sold around 662 million shares, a 10 percent stake, with anchor investors taking a portion and about 493 million shares left for other investors. The offer received bids for 736.3 million shares by the final day of bidding on Monday, exchange data showed, and Finimize reported the issue was about 1.5 times subscribed overall, with the retail and non institutional portions not fully booked.

Mahanadi, based in Odisha, accounts for about a fifth of India's coal production and is the third Coal India unit to go public this year, as the government pursues its divestment programme. Because the listing sold down an existing stake rather than funding expansion, its first day price is mainly a valuation marker: the level at which investors will absorb large blocks of state linked coal assets, against which Coal India's future sell downs will be judged.
