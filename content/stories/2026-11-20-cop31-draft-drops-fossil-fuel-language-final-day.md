**Update, November 22 (UK Q&A).** In a question-and-answer page published on Sunday, the UK government named the failed overarching decision the presidency-led "Antalya Pact" and said parties as a whole had objected to it, while a number of negotiation outcomes were still reached, including on climate finance and just transition. London said it had endorsed the COP31 presidency's 35x35 Global Electrification Pledge and a UK-Nigeria low-methane partnership, and confirmed COP31 formally concluded on November 22, with negotiations to continue at COP32 in Ethiopia.

**Update, November 22 (UN reaction).** UN Secretary-General António Guterres said he was “deeply disappointed” that governments in Antalya had failed to reach consensus, and that the outcome did not change the science: “There may be no consensus in the negotiating room. There is no ambiguity in the science.” He said fossil fuels remain the primary driver of warming and that governments had already agreed at COP28 in Dubai to move away from them: “We can’t put out the fire while pouring fuel on it.” UN News reported that Antalya is now the longest-running UN climate summit on record. UN Climate Change Executive Secretary Simon Stiell told the closing plenary, “The world cannot afford a repeat of this moment,” and pointed to one agreement reached at the summit, on a just transition, ahead of COP32 in Addis Ababa.

**Update, November 22 (UK position).** The United Kingdom set out its position in a government statement on Sunday: it does not believe the cover decision proposed by the presidencies meets the moment, and said the text moves backwards on the science when implementation must accelerate, compromising where it should have presented bold collective ambition. London committed to work with partners over the next year on speeding the transition away from fossil fuels and protecting forests, supporting clean electrification, establishing a process on adaptation finance, reforming the COP process, protecting oceans, and supporting mountain regions and glacier dependent communities, and said it would return to COP32 in Addis Ababa ready to deliver on the Paris Agreement.

**Latest.** COP31 ended without a deal on Sunday. After more than two weeks of negotiations that were due to close on Friday and stretched deep into the weekend, COP31 President Murat Kurum, Turkiye's environment minister, told the closing plenary: "It is clear that there are parties in this hall who oppose this text. We recognize that there is no consensus on this document. Therefore, these decisions have not been adopted." The European Union and other countries rejected the final draft, which never mentioned fossil fuels and only recalled the 2023 Dubai pledge to move away from them. The text would have been the first COP outcome to acknowledge that warming is on course to exceed the 1.5 degree Celsius Paris threshold, while calling for faster development of carbon removal technologies to reverse the overshoot. Reuters noted the last major breakdown in UN climate talks was Copenhagen in 2009; the failure does not erase obligations agreed at previous summits.

The COP31 climate summit has gone into overtime, with nearly 200 countries deadlocked over a draft deal that no longer mentions fossil fuels. The two week talks in Antalya, Turkiye, were due to end on Friday, when a late closing session approved some technical matters but not the wider political deal. COP31 President Murat Kurum, Turkiye's environment minister, told delegates the talks were close to the finish line but not there yet, and a third draft is expected on Saturday, Agence France-Presse reported.

The fossil fuel fight is the core of the deadlock. An opening draft from cohost and lead negotiator Australia on Monday called for a faster phase-out of fossil fuels; a slimmer version on Thursday removed every mention of them after Saudi Arabia and other major oil and gas producers objected. The revised text also dropped the proposed $120 billion target for adaptation finance for poorer countries. The European Union said the draft risks turning the summit into a fossil summit, and Panama's environment minister Juan Carlos Navarro, whose country signed a letter with a dozen others demanding a clear transition away from fossil fuels, threatened to walk away.

Other contested points carry over from Thursday's draft: a goal of raising electricity's share of final energy consumption to 35 percent by 2035, without specifying that the power be renewable or clean, and bracketed language on carbon capture that Vanuatu's climate minister Ralph Regenvanu called a false solution. Developing countries remain angry that rich nations are falling short on climate finance, and environmental groups tracking the conference called it on track to be the worst COP ever. Any outcome requires unanimous agreement.

**Update, November 22.** The newest and possibly final draft, released by COP31 co-president and Turkish environment minister Murat Kurum in the early hours of Sunday, does not contain the words fossil fuels, or specifically call for a move away from coal, oil and gas, the Associated Press reported. Kurum gave negotiators only about three hours to make objections or request changes before the draft is presented to what may be the summit's final meeting, where countries will accept, reject or record reservations. Several nations have made serious objections, and adoption needs near unanimous consensus.

The draft references the Dubai agreement but does not repeat its transition language. It includes a specific goal of capturing, reusing and storing one billion metric tons of carbon, and Turkiye's electrification goal of raising electricity's share of total final energy consumption from about 21 percent today to 35 percent by 2035. Deliberations continued on Sunday, nearly 40 hours past the official deadline. The next climate summit is scheduled for Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, in November 2027.

**Update, November 22 (later).** The presidency text was put to nearly 200 delegations for approval, with objections due by 03:00 GMT and the approval session due to begin at 06:00 GMT; the closing plenary then slipped as objections grew. The final ten-page package recalls the 2023 Dubai agreement without repeating its fossil fuel wording, and would be the first COP outcome to acknowledge that the world will overshoot the 1.5 degree Paris limit.
