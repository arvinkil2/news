The European Commission has greenlit Germany's final payment request for 6 billion euros under NextGenerationEU, the European Union's post pandemic recovery instrument, the Commission announced on Wednesday. The decision is a positive preliminary assessment, the Commission's confirmation that the milestones and targets attached to the payment have been satisfactorily fulfilled, which precedes disbursement.

The payment is the last in Germany's recovery and resilience plan, the national programme of reforms and investments funded through the Recovery and Resilience Facility, the centrepiece of NextGenerationEU. Germany's plan channels EU funding into climate and digital investment, including energy and transport infrastructure, alongside structural reforms agreed with Brussels when the plan was approved.

For capital allocation purposes the mechanism matters as much as the amount. This is grant and loan capacity created at EU level and released against verified milestones, not borrowing by Berlin in the market. The final assessment closes the German plan's payment schedule and ends a multi year sequence in which the Commission, not investors, set the conditions and the timetable for a material share of German public investment.

No independent corroboration of the assessment was available in this scan beyond the Commission's own release, which is the document of record for the decision. The remaining step is the formal payment process that follows a positive preliminary assessment.
