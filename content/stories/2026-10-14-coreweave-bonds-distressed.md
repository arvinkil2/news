Latest: The selloff widened from CoreWeave's bonds into the price of default insurance across the AI trade overnight. Reuters reported that five-year credit default swap spreads for CoreWeave have blown out past 1,000 basis points, while Oracle's widened by 26 basis points to a record 288 basis points and SoftBank's by 24 to 424, with spreads for Amazon, Alphabet, Meta and Nvidia also at record-wide levels, if far smaller. CoreWeave's 2032 paper was changing hands at 77 cents on the dollar, down from 79.50 on Wednesday, and its shares, down 15 percent on Wednesday, fell a further 6 percent after the close.

CoreWeave's credit sold off hard on Wednesday and its equity followed. The company's 9.625 percent notes due 2032 traded at 79.50 dollars, down from 85 at the start of the month, lifting their yield to 15.06 percent from 13.42 percent, levels that typically signal distress. The shares fell about 14 percent to near 67 dollars, down 20 percent in October and more than half below their 52 week high, after a chart of the bond yield posted by Chamath Palihapitiya drew a one word reply from Elon Musk: "Yikes."

The balance sheet is the argument. CoreWeave has raised more than 35 billion dollars this year through chip backed loans, high yield and convertible bonds and stock sales, and its debt and lease obligations totalled about 52 billion dollars at the end of June. D.A. Davidson analyst Gil Luria argued the company borrows at around 9 percent while earning about 1 percent on its investments. Peers fell in sympathy, with Oracle and Nebius lower and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index down for a sixth straight session.
