The US Department of Agriculture announced two further measures under its Great American Cotton Plan on October 16, adding crop insurance flexibility and a new trade incentive mechanism to the programme Secretary Brooke Rollins launched in May. Rollins unveiled the initiatives during a visit to Giesenschlag Farms in Caldwell, Texas, and both are scheduled for implementation in 2027.

On insurance, the Risk Management Agency will allow eligible producers, including cotton growers, to purchase multiple endorsements on a single crop insurance policy beginning with the 2027 crop year. The change builds on provisions of the Working Families Tax Cuts Act signed on July 4, 2025, which expanded access to several crop insurance products and improved their affordability. The agency is expected to provide final implementation details to approved insurance providers and agents by November 30, 2026, and until that guidance is published the exact combinations of endorsements available remain subject to clarification.

On trade, the Foreign Agricultural Service is developing a mechanism that links verified purchases of US cotton and textile inputs to preferential tariff treatment for qualifying apparel imports, operating through a tariff rate quota framework. The department identified Bangladesh, Indonesia, Cambodia and Malaysia as the markets involved, all significant apparel manufacturing centres. The announcement did not specify quota volumes, tariff reductions or projected purchase levels, so the commercial impact depends on final programme design.

The measures land on an industry under sustained pressure. The plan's four objectives are increasing domestic cotton consumption, strengthening demand and production, improving international trade and protecting growers against adverse risks, and USDA has framed the effort around regaining the export position the United States lost as foreign competition intensified. Neither measure guarantees higher farmgate prices; their effect will depend on insurance participation, programme costs and whether overseas buyers increase purchases of American cotton rather than redirecting existing sourcing through the new mechanism.
