A record 29 seats in Sarawak's 13th state election were decided without a vote on Saturday, after only one nomination paper was filed in each of them by the time nominations closed at 10am. Election Commission chairman Ramlan Harun announced the figure in Kuching, saying it surpasses the previous record of 19 unopposed seats in the 1996 state election. All 29 seats went to Gabungan Parti Sarawak, the ruling coalition, including Tanjung Datu, Satok, Gedong, Sebuyau, Dalat and Bukit Sari.

The scale is the story. The 29 constituencies hold 632,149 registered voters, about 30.71 percent of Sarawak's 2.06 million electorate, and none of them will vote on polling day, December 12. Ramlan called the outcome part of the democratic process, noting that the commission merely provides the platform and parties choose whether to contest, and said the commission had yet to assess any cost savings from the cancelled polling.

The unopposed wins also raised a constitutional question, whether GPS could claim a simple majority on nomination day itself. Ramlan said the Attorney General's Chambers' position is that the electoral process must be completed before a government can be formed, so any formation waits until after December 12 regardless of the arithmetic.

Sarawak's opposition parties now contest only the remaining seats, and the campaign for them runs to December 12. Status: nominations closed November 28, 2026; 29 seats declared unopposed, polling in the rest on December 12.
