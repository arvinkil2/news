Two crew members were killed and 11 rescued after the Turkish-owned cargo ship Royad Mammadov caught fire and sank in the Black Sea on Monday, October 5, following what Ukraine's president Volodymyr Zelensky said was a Russian drone attack. The 139-metre vessel, owned by MG Shipping 5 and flagged to St Kitts and Nevis, was carrying corn from Ukraine's port of Izmail to Ravenna, Italy, when the fire broke out about 30 nautical miles south of Romania's fishing port of Sfantu Gheorghe, inside Romania's exclusive economic zone.

Romania's Interior Ministry said Coast Guard border police were informed around 11:00 that a fire had broken out aboard a commercial vessel in the country's EEZ. Of the 11 rescued, three were injured and eight were in good condition, according to the Romanian Naval Authority's spokesperson; the injured were evacuated by helicopter. The Constanta Maritime Rescue Coordination Centre coordinated the rescue, deploying the SAR Apollo from Sulina and the SAR Artemis, with a Romanian Coast Guard ship as on-scene coordinator. The vessel later sank.

The two killed were the captain, Rustam Hasanov, and Mahammad Suleymanzadeh, according to Azerbaijan's Foreign Ministry cited by AnewZ. The crew comprised Indian and Azerbaijani nationals.

Zelensky said two Russian drones struck the civilian vessel in neutral waters and confirmed the captain's death. Romanian authorities confirmed the fire, the sinking and the rescue, but did not confirm whether a drone was involved and did not attribute a cause. Turkey has repeatedly raised concerns over attacks on its vessels: its maritime chamber says 226 civilian and commercial vessels have been attacked in the Black Sea since the war began, with the vast majority of incidents reported this year.

This is the second fatal vessel attack in the basin in three days, after Russia's October 3 strike on a Liberian-flagged ship at Odesa that killed one crew member and injured three. Ukrainian authorities say Russian forces also hit a Palau-flagged vessel in the Odesa region on September 28 and a Tanzanian-flagged vessel on September 17, killing its captain, seven seafarer fatalities from vessel strikes in about a month. Black Sea risk is rising into harvest: USDA Crop Progress released October 5 showed corn 23 percent harvested and soybeans 25 percent for the week ending October 4, a normal pace, and the October 9 WASDE will reset Black Sea export estimates against this disruption. Grain loadings face tightening war risk while the physical trade continues; the freight and insurance channel will price it first.
