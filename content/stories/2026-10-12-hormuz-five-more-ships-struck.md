**Latest (Wednesday, October 14):** Iranian officials told Reuters Iran's forces are planning to intensify strikes on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz using new tactics. One official said different offensive tactics had been used for about ten days against vessels Iran considered in violation of its restrictions, and that this had increased attacks on such ships; the officials declined to give details of the new strategy, and Reuters reported Tehran's stated aims as keeping oil prices high and influencing the US midterm elections. Brent crude futures rose 63 cents, or 0.6 percent, to 106.32 dollars a barrel by 0345 GMT on Wednesday, and US West Texas Intermediate rose 66 cents, or 0.7 percent, to 94.80 dollars, after both contracts gained more than a dollar earlier in the session. A strategist at Nissan Securities Investment told Reuters supply concern from the Saudi Houthi conflict is driving prices higher, while expected Russian diesel exports could limit sharp moves in either direction. OPEC said on Tuesday that Saudi Arabia raised oil production last month despite the shipping disruptions, lifting output across the OPEC+ group.

**Latest (Tuesday, October 13):** A container ship and a tanker were struck by unknown projectiles within the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday, the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations said. UKMTO gave no details on damage, casualties or the vessels involved and said authorities are investigating. The brief report, carried by Reuters, is single sourced at this stage and the incident count may be revised as UKMTO publishes its usual detailed notices.

Five more commercial vessels were struck in and around the Strait of Hormuz on Sunday, gCaptain reported, after UK Maritime Trade Operations disclosed four of the incidents in time-late reports on Monday. Four vessels were struck by unidentified projectiles while transiting the strait: an outbound product tanker, an outbound crude oil tanker, another outbound tanker, and a containership sailing inbound; a fifth tanker was hit by a drone near the Sohar anchorage in Oman, where local authorities were helping evacuate the crew.

The reports land on top of the Joint Maritime Information Center's October 11 advisory rating the Hormuz security threat SEVERE, its second-highest category, with US naval figures showing roughly 29 vessel transits a day over the 72 hours to October 10 against a historical average of about 138 a day. UKMTO confirmed one outbound tanker's crew was safe, with the environmental impact unknown; the other reports gave no casualty or damage details.
