Viatris said it will pay Pacira investors $36.50 per share in cash, a 45 percent premium over Wednesday's closing price, and expects the deal to close by the end of 2026. Pacira's two main products, the post-surgical pain drug Exparel and the osteoarthritis knee-therapy Zilretta, are novel formulations of the older non-opioid pain relievers bupivacaine and triamcinolone. The pair booked $692 million in 2025 sales, up about 4 percent, and Viatris said it expects to expand their reach across select markets through its global infrastructure.

Viatris already owns the pain drugs Celebrex and Relpax and could soon launch a fast-acting version of meloxicam if the FDA clears it by a December 27 decision deadline. Leerink analyst Daniel Clark called the transaction "immediately accretive" to revenue and a source of margin expansion into the 2030s, fitting Viatris's strategy of buying on-market commercial assets. Clark added that while the near-term trajectory likely does not change meaningfully, the deal is "steady sources of growth" execution against its capital deployment strategy.

The risk is generic erosion. Patent settlements Pacira struck last year with Fresenius Kabi and a Hengrui Pharma subsidiary allow the two companies to begin marketing small shares of total U.S. Exparel volume as early as 2030, escalating to around 30 percent within three years. Manufacturing patents won in 2024 push Exparel's intellectual property protections to 2044 according to the FDA's Orange Book, but two other manufacturers are challenging those patents. Zilretta's patents expire in 2031. Viatris said it plans to leverage its intellectual property expertise to extend product lifecycles after generics arrive, which is the central bet of the transaction.
