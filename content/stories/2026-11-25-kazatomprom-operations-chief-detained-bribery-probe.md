Kazakhstan's anti-corruption service detained Kuanysh Omarbekov, Kazatomprom's head of operations, and others on Monday as a bribery investigation widened from sovereign wealth fund Samruk-Kazyna to the world's largest uranium miner, the National Security Committee said on Wednesday. Investigators suspect the detainees took part in a corruption scheme allegedly organised by former leaders of Samruk-Kazyna, which managed $88 billion in assets at the end of 2025 and owns 63 percent of Kazatomprom. The miner said its board removed Omarbekov and chief commercial officer Vladislav Baiguzhin on Tuesday, reassigned their duties, and stressed that production and commercial teams remain intact with day-to-day operations continuing as usual.

Kazatomprom's global depositary receipts fell nearly 10 percent to close at $49.80 in London on Wednesday, valuing the company at $12.9 billion. Omarbekov oversaw exploration, drilling, mining and processing for a company whose share of production was about one-fifth of the world's mined uranium last year, and which expects the operations in which it holds stakes to produce 27,500 to 29,000 tonnes of uranium this year. The detentions follow the November 11 arrests of the heads of Samruk-Kazyna and the national railway in the same investigation.
