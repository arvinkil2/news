SpaceX launched Starship on its fifteenth test flight from its South Texas spaceport on Friday evening, attempting for the first time to return both stages to the launch site for tower catches, the double catch Elon Musk had set out before the flight. Less than ten minutes after launch the plan changed: the Super Heavy booster, which carries 33 engines, splashed down in the Gulf of Mexico instead of returning to the Mechazilla tower, and the broadcast host said the change was due to strict safety requirements, with the reasons not yet known.

The Starship ship continued its flight, expected to last about 22 hours and roughly 14 orbits, deploying 26 Starlink V3 satellites along the way. SpaceX still intends to try catching the ship itself for the first time when it returns, which would be a first even with the booster catch missed.
