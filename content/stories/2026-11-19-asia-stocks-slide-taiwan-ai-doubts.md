**Latest.** Taiwan's fall deepened as Thursday's session went on. A later Investing.com report put the Weighted Index down 4.6 percent at 38,347.67, its lowest level since November 6, with 816 stocks falling against 50 advancers and the index now down 9.2 percent on the week. Printed circuit board and optical names hit their daily limits, among them Nan Ya PCB, Kinsus, Browave and Guang Sheng, while memory makers Nanya Technology and Winbond fell more than 7 percent and 5 percent. Foreign investors sold a net 76.56 billion Taiwan dollars of shares on Wednesday, a third straight session of outflows taking cumulative selling above 180 billion Taiwan dollars, and the Taiwan dollar weakened to 32.947 per US dollar, its lowest in more than 19 months. The report tied the rout to Washington's November 15 export controls on advanced chips and to higher US rate expectations after the Federal Reserve minutes and remarks by Governor Waller raising a possible 50 basis point December increase.

Asian stocks fell on Thursday, led by Taiwan, where the Weighted Index dropped 3.9 percent as chipmakers sold off. TSMC fell 2.76 percent, Foxconn lost 3.59 percent and Largan Precision plunged 10 percent, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng fell 1.9 percent on a 6.4 percent drop in Tencent, South Korea's Kospi lost 0.5 percent, Australia's ASX 200 fell 1.08 percent and China's CSI 300 eased 0.49 percent; Japan's Nikkei 225 held a 0.3 percent gain.

Investing.com tied the slide to persistent doubts over the AI trade, noting that Anthropic's IPO, priced at 83 dollars a share above its target range in the largest debut on record, had done little to ease them, and that Nvidia had closed 2.9 percent lower in New York after its post-earnings rally faded.
