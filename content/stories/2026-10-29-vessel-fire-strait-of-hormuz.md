**Latest, October 30:** Two further incidents from Thursday have now been reported alongside the Louise 1 attack, and UKMTO has put the week's pattern in numbers. A company security officer reported that a second tanker was struck by an unidentified projectile at about 1600 UTC on Thursday while completing an outbound transit of the strait, with no crew injuries and no environmental impact, gCaptain reported, citing a UKMTO warning. The vessel reported on fire nine nautical miles north of Khasab, the subject of UKMTO's original advisory in this story, was identified by regional authorities as a dhow that had been involved in a maritime security incident; it listed and sank and its crew was safely evacuated, and no connection has been established with an earlier United States operation that destroyed a dhow suspected of providing intelligence to Iran. UKMTO's October 30 weekly overview recorded 59 of 81 projectile strikes since July 6 along the southern transit route the United States coordinates, 145 conflict related incidents since February 28 including 88 involving vessels in the strait through October 28, and 122 outbound and 155 inbound transits in its latest seven day period, up from 114 and 127 the week before but still about 70 percent below pre-conflict levels. Martin Kelly of EOS Risk Group, who identified the Louise 1, said Iran last used limpet mines against merchant shipping in May and June 2019, off Fujairah and in the Gulf of Oman, and warned that vessels waiting at anchor are exposed to devices placed below the waterline without the crew detecting them.

**Latest, October 30:** The tanker attacked on Thursday 25 nautical miles northeast of Khasab, Oman, was the Panama flagged Louise 1, and the two explosive devices a small boat attached to its hull were limpet mines, according to assessments from three maritime security sources reported by Reuters. The sources called it the first successful limpet mine strike in the waterway since the US war with Iran began in February, after a separate attempt in September in which the devices did not detonate. The fire in the engine room was extinguished, the crew was safe, there was no environmental impact, and the vessel continued to its next port of call, UKMTO said. It was not immediately clear who was behind the attack. Marisks, a Greece based maritime risk group, said the ability of a small craft to approach a transiting tanker, place explosive devices and withdraw is a significant security concern in the strait's congested shipping lanes. Ukrainian forces hit the same vessel with naval drones in the Black Sea in July over what Ukraine's SBU security service said was its role carrying Russian oil as part of the shadow fleet, Reuters reported.

**Earlier, October 30:** Iran's Revolutionary Guards navy said on Friday that it stopped two oil tankers in waters south of the Strait of Hormuz after they ignored warnings and tried to use a route through the waterway that the navy called unauthorised, Reuters reported, via Arab News. The report named no vessels and cited no response or corroboration from UKMTO, the United States, Oman or any shipping source. The claim is an enforcement claim, distinct from the Guards' mine strike claims below, though it echoes the Guards' earlier account, reported by ORF, that tankers had used an unauthorised southern route. It is carried here as Iran's account, uncorroborated.

**Earlier, October 30:** The Revolutionary Guards' spokesman, Hossein Mohebi, challenged the United States military's position that the main transit routes through the strait have been cleared of mines, demanding that the claim be proved with independent evidence rather than a statement, CNBC reported. The challenge came as the Iran-backed Houthis claimed six military operations across Saudi Arabia against airports and oil facilities, a claim Saudi authorities had not confirmed; Saudi officials said air defences intercepted and destroyed a Houthi drone launched toward the Mecca region. Neither development changes the position on the tankers: no independent confirmation of the four reported mine strikes has been reported, and no vessel has been named.

**Earlier, October 30:** A tanker that caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz on Thursday continued its journey to its next port of call, UKMTO said, in Reuters reporting carried by Arab News. The vessel, transiting outbound 25 nautical miles northeast of Khasab, Oman, was attacked by a small boat that attached two explosive devices to its hull; the explosions started a fire in the engine room that temporarily immobilised it. The crew were safe, the fire was extinguished, and there was no environmental impact. The report did not name the vessel, its position differs from the 9 nautical miles north of Khasab in UKMTO's original fire advisory, and whether this is the same tanker first reported on fire on Thursday is not established. It was not immediately clear who was behind the attack.

**Earlier, October 29:** Explosions were heard near Iran's coast in the Strait of Hormuz on Thursday evening, around Sirik and Qeshm Island, where residents reported flashes in the sky, Interfax reported, citing the Fars news agency and local sources. Fars said, on preliminary information, that part of the sounds could have come from warning shots fired at vessels that had broken Iran's rules for passage through the strait, and some sources pointed to possible air defence activity. Separately, Iran's Mehr news agency reported that Revolutionary Guards aerospace forces intercepted an unidentified enemy warplane near Qeshm Island and forced it to leave the area, according to Anadolu Agency; Mehr gave no nationality or aircraft type, and Anadolu said officials had not commented. Neither account is independently confirmed, and both are carried here as Iranian accounts.

A fourth tanker was later reported in flames in the east of the Strait of Hormuz after striking a mine and left unable to manoeuvre, according to the Iranian Army as reported by ORF, Austria's public broadcaster, with agencies. ORF put the Iranian accounts together as at least four tankers on fire after mine collisions, and reported the Revolutionary Guards' version that the first three ships had used an unauthorised southern route with radar and navigation systems switched off and had ignored Guards warnings. No vessel is named in the report, it remained unclear which tankers were involved or under which flags they sailed, and ORF carried no report of casualties. Against the Iranian claims, ORF reported the United States military's position, stated by Central Command after earlier incidents, that all mines on the most important transit routes had been cleared. The fourth tanker claim is uncorroborated and is carried here as Iran's account.

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said on Thursday that three oil tankers struck mines in the Strait of Hormuz in the past 24 hours, according to the Tasnim news agency via Sepah News, as reported by Reuters and carried by Arab News. The claim names no vessels and gives no positions, times or damage reports, and it does not say whether the tanker reported on fire off Khasab is one of the three. Reuters reported no independent confirmation, and no statement from UKMTO, the US Fifth Fleet, the Combined Maritime Forces, Oman or the United Arab Emirates corroborating mine strikes was reported. The claim is carried here as Iran's account, uncorroborated.

The tanker reported on fire off Khasab may be sinking. Splash247 reported that the master of a passing Panama flagged tanker saw the unidentified vessel on fire and mostly underwater, raising concern it had sunk or was sinking, and that another vessel was nearby, potentially assisting, although no rescue operation has been confirmed. Maritime security firm Vanguard described the incident as a suspected projectile strike; UKMTO has not independently confirmed that the vessel was struck by a projectile or that it is sinking, and the ship's identity, flag, ownership and cargo, and the fate of its crew, remain unconfirmed. Splash247 cited International Maritime Organization figures showing 121 confirmed maritime incidents across the Middle East this year as of October 27, with 24 seafarer fatalities.

UKMTO has received a sighting report of a tanker on fire in the Strait of Hormuz, at position 26 27.0N 056 24.6E, 9 nautical miles north of Khasab, Oman, with an incident time of 0205 UTC on Thursday. The advisory, categorised as fire or explosion, says the tanker's master is not responding to VHF calls and that authorities have been notified.

Vessels are advised to transit the area with caution and to report any incidents or suspicious activity to UKMTO. The tanker's name is withheld, no cause has been stated, and Reuters reported that the status of its crew is unknown, with no information yet on damage or environmental impact.
