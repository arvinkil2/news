The Government Accountability Office said on Thursday that the Department of Defense does not have an overarching plan that links munitions production and funding to requirements, and that officials told investigators the department and the services can be reactive to global events rather than driven by total requirements in the absence of one. As an example, GAO said the Army's ramp-up in production of 155 millimetre artillery shells, undertaken to address shortages exacerbated by United States support to allies and partners, was not driven by the department's total munitions requirement. The department has also not designated an office or entity to direct and assess efforts to improve munitions inventories.

GAO also found limited oversight of the department-wide munitions data tool, which draws on 37 data sources: the department has not regularly reconciled the tool's inventory data against the services' own systems and has no data management plan for how the information is aggregated. The report, the public version of a classified study issued on September 24, 2026, recommends an overarching munitions plan with long-term goals, strategies and metrics, designated leadership for its implementation, and consistent timelines for reconciling inventory data. It omits information the department deemed classified or sensitive, including trends in inventories, persistent shortfalls and their effect on support to war plans.
