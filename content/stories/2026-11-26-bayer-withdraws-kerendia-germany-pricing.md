Bayer said on November 24 that it will withdraw Kerendia (finerenone), its heart and kidney drug, from the German market from December 15, in all indications. The company blamed a reimbursement system it said failed to recognise the drug's medical value, leaving terms that made continued marketing in Germany commercially unviable.

The trigger was an October decision by Germany's Federal Joint Committee (G-BA), the health system's highest decision-making body, which concluded that Kerendia offered no added benefit over comparable treatment for patients with symptomatic chronic heart failure with a left ventricular ejection fraction of 40 percent or more. The G-BA had previously, in 2022, recognised a clinical benefit for Kerendia in chronic kidney disease associated with type 2 diabetes, but the withdrawal covers that indication as well.

Bayer stressed that the decision does not affect Kerendia's marketing authorisation or its availability in any other country, and that the G-BA finding did not rest on any new evidence about the drug's quality, safety or efficacy. Kerendia is a key growth product for Bayer: sales rose 73 percent to 977 million euros, about 1.1 billion dollars, in the first nine months of 2026, driven mainly by the United States and China.

The withdrawal is the latest in a series of disputes between large drugmakers and Germany over medicine pricing, in a year when a German health reform raised mandatory rebates on branded medicines. It also lands as German public insurers cite research estimating that raising drug prices in response to United States pressure would add billions to the public health system's costs.
