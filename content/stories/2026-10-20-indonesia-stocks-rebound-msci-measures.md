Indonesia's Jakarta Composite Index rose 4.8 percent on Tuesday, recovering from a six-year closing low, after the stock exchange on Monday excluded stocks with highly concentrated shareholding from its benchmark index and halved the daily down limit on share prices. The measures answer MSCI's warning last week that it may downgrade Indonesia from emerging-market status to standalone status, which could trigger selling by passive funds that track global indexes; Indonesian equities fell nearly 15 percent over the two sessions after that warning, a two-day fall Bloomberg put as the steepest since 1989, leaving Indonesia the world's worst-performing stock market this year even after Tuesday's rebound. The rebound ran against the region, where MSCI's emerging Asia gauge fell 1.4 percent on a 3.7 percent fall in South Korean equities.
