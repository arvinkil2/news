## Latest

The IMF Executive Board completed the sixth review of Ecuador's 48-month Extended Fund Facility on November 23 and approved an 18-month arrangement under the Resilience and Sustainability Facility, the Fund said in Press Release No. 26/377 on November 24. Completion of the review gives Ecuador immediate access to SDR 280.5 million, about 377 million dollars, taking total disbursements under the EFF to close to SDR 3 billion, about 4 billion dollars, against total access of SDR 3.75 billion, about 5 billion dollars. The RSF carries access of SDR 523.275 million, about 703 million dollars, with the first disbursement available after its first review, subject to implementation of the agreed reform measures.

The Fund said all quantitative performance criteria for the sixth review were met, many by large margins, and all indicative targets were met except the target on central government arrears to the domestic private sector, missed by a small margin because arrears were identified and reclassified during a public sector restructuring. Growth is projected at 2.7 percent in 2026, inflation was 1.3 percent year on year in September, the current account surplus is projected at 4.6 percent of GDP in 2026, and gross international reserves are put at 13.0 billion dollars in 2026, at record highs on sustained surpluses. Deputy Managing Director Nigel Clarke, who chaired the Board discussion, said risks have declined since the fifth review but remain elevated, including from a possible strong El Niño.

The International Monetary Fund said on November 9 that its staff had reached agreement with Ecuador on the sixth review of the country's 48-month Extended Fund Facility and on a new 18-month Resilience and Sustainability Facility. Board approval of the review would give Ecuador immediate access to about 280.5 million SDR, roughly 380 million dollars, while the new facility carries access of about 523 million SDR, roughly 710 million dollars, with its first tranche available after its first review and tied to energy-sector resilience, disaster vulnerability, and climate governance reforms.

The Fund said program performance has been strong: all quantitative performance criteria for the sixth review were met, many by large margins, and all indicative targets were met except a small miss on central government arrears to the domestic private sector, which it attributed to arrears identified and reclassified during a public sector restructuring. Growth was 2.1 percent in the first half of 2026 and is projected at 2.7 percent for the year, inflation fell to 1.3 percent in September, and the current account surplus is projected at 4.6 percent of GDP, lifting reserves to historic highs.
