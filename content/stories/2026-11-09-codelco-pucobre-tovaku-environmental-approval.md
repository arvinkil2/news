Chile's Environmental Assessment Service has granted an Environmental Qualification Resolution to the Tovaku copper project in the Antofagasta region, confirming its environmental viability and allowing partners Codelco and Sociedad Punta del Cobre, known as Pucobre, to advance toward construction, Mining.com reported. The 870 million dollar open pit project, spanning the municipalities of Maria Elena and Tocopilla, is held through Minera Puntilla, owned 60 percent by Pucobre and 40 percent by state owned Codelco, and is designed for 46,000 tonnes of copper cathodes a year over a 21 year life using leaching, solvent extraction and electrowinning.

Construction is scheduled to begin in the second half of 2027 and take three years, putting first production around 2030 if the remaining permitting, engineering and construction preparation stay on schedule. The plan includes a 193 megawatt photovoltaic park inside the mining area and a 72.5 kilometre seawater pumping system from Tocopilla.
