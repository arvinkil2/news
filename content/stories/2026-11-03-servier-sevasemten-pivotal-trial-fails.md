Servier announced on November 3 that topline results from GRAND CANYON, the 18-month global placebo-controlled pivotal trial of sevasemten in Becker muscular dystrophy, did not meet the primary endpoint, with no statistically significant benefit on the North Star Ambulatory Assessment, and that secondary endpoints were also not met, although the drug's safety and tolerability profile remained favourable. Servier said the pivotal results are not aligned with certain findings in prior studies, including the Phase 2 CANYON study, and that it is conducting a comprehensive analysis of the dataset, with full results to be presented later. The readout comes five months after Servier agreed to acquire Edgewise Therapeutics' muscular dystrophy business for $1.55 billion upfront plus up to $1.1 billion in milestones, a deal that closed in July.
