The Prime Minister will travel to Brussels on November 20 for the second UK-EU summit, meeting European Council President Antonio Costa and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, the government announced on Monday. Leaders will review progress since the first summit in London on May 19, 2025, which agreed a new strategic partnership, and set next steps on security, illegal migration and crime.

Burnham framed the meeting in cost terms. He said a threadbare Brexit deal had damaged the economy and that he wanted practical steps that cut costs for farmers, food producers and families, reduce paperwork for business and ease pressure on supermarket prices. The EU is Britain's largest market, and food trade paperwork has been the most tangible friction for exporters since Brexit.

No agreement has been announced, and the release promises negotiation rather than outcomes. The political constraint runs both ways: closer food and regulatory alignment lowers trade costs but reopens the sovereignty argument at home, while the EU has consistently linked market access to rules. What to watch on November 20 is whether the communique names specific deals with dates, or stays at the level of intent.

The summit lands three weeks after the October 28 budget, so Burnham will arrive in Brussels with his fiscal position freshly tested in gilt markets. Status: announced meeting, no deals yet agreed.
