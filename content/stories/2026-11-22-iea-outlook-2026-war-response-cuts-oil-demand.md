The Middle East conflict has caused the biggest disruption ever to global oil and gas trade, the International Energy Agency said on Thursday in its World Energy Outlook 2026, and the response to it could permanently lower oil demand.

Cumulative losses have reached 2.8 billion barrels of oil and 60 billion cubic metres of gas supply from the Gulf, the agency said. The Strait of Hormuz, which the conflict has curbed, typically carries about a fifth of global oil and liquefied natural gas supplies.

Under a new Additional Response Case, energy-security concerns speed up electrification, efficiency and fuel switching. Global oil demand in 2035 is then 4.4 million barrels a day lower than under the Stated Policies Scenario, falling to 97 million barrels a day against just over 101 million, and to 85 million barrels a day by 2050.

"In my view, the energy world will never be the same again as it was before the Iran war," IEA Executive Director Fatih Birol said alongside the release. The outlook also records a marker of the demand shift already under way: for the first time, demand for cooling in buildings has exceeded demand for heating, as higher temperatures and rising incomes drive rapid growth in air-conditioner use.
