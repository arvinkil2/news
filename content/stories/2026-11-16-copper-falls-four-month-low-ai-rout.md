London copper fell for a third straight session on Monday, with benchmark three-month metal on the London Metal Exchange down 1 percent to 13,264.5 dollars a metric ton in Asian trading and down as much as 1.4 percent intraday to its lowest since July 8, extending five straight weekly losses, Reuters and Bloomberg reported. Persistent worries about AI investment, a firmer US dollar and expectations of further Federal Reserve rate increases weighed on the metal, and ANZ analysts said the selloff in AI-related stocks likely hurt base metals because reduced AI spending could cut copper demand, while signs of tightness in China could provide some support; US copper was down 0.4 percent to 6.108 dollars a pound by early Sydney afternoon. The fall came hours after China's October activity data missed forecasts across the board, with industrial output up 4.8 percent and retail sales up 0.4 percent, darkening the demand outlook from the metal's largest consumer.
