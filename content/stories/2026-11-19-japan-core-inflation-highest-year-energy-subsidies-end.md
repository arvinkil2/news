Japan's core consumer inflation rose 2.2 percent in October from a year earlier, the highest reading this year and up from 2.1 percent in September, though below the 2.3 percent expected by economists polled by Reuters. Headline inflation rose to 2.3 percent from 2.1 percent, while the core-core measure, which strips out fresh food and energy, eased to 2.2 percent from 2.4 percent.

The increase followed the expiry of household energy subsidies in September. Energy prices climbed 2.2 percent in October after falling 0.6 percent in September, gas prices rose 5.8 percent, and fuel, light and water charges rose 3.4 percent. The government has reportedly approved reintroducing energy subsidies in December. LSEG data put the chance of a 25 basis point Bank of Japan rate increase in December at 72 percent, and the Nikkei 225 fell 2.1 percent after the release while the yen traded at 159.59 per dollar.
