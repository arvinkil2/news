Crude oil rallied on Wednesday after Yemen's Houthis said they had attacked Ras Tanura, Saudi Arabia's largest oil refinery and a major crude export terminal. Brent crude futures settled 3.21 dollars, or 3.5 percent, higher at 94.61 dollars a barrel, and US West Texas Intermediate crude climbed 1.63 dollars, or 2 percent, to 83.68 dollars. Both benchmarks had already gained more than 2 percent on Tuesday.

The rally faded on Thursday morning. Brent was down 82 cents, or 0.87 percent, at 93.79 dollars a barrel at 0939 GMT, and WTI fell 35 cents, or 0.42 percent, to 83.33 dollars, with no WTI settlement due on Thursday because of the US Thanksgiving holiday. PVM analyst Tamas Varga, who attributed Wednesday's move to a rising geopolitical risk premium and short covering ahead of the holiday, put the new Brent trading range at around 88 to 97 dollars, arguing that the expected supply surplus and sticky inflation would cap attempts to push prices significantly higher.

US inventory data supported the rally rather than capping it. Crude stocks rose by 1.1 million barrels to 418.9 million barrels in the week ended November 20, the Energy Information Administration said, less than the 1.5 million barrel build analysts in a Reuters poll had expected.

The claims behind the move remain contested. The Houthis said they attacked the refinery and Riyadh's financial district; the Saudi government and Saudi Aramco did not immediately respond to requests for comment, and the coalition account in yesterday's reporting described interceptions rather than a refinery hit. In the Strait of Hormuz, the semi-official Fars news agency, citing the Iranian Army, said an oil tanker hit a naval mine and caught fire in a restricted zone on Wednesday, after state media said two tankers struck mines in the strait the same day. ANZ analyst Daniel Hynes said the broadening conflict threatens to halt the recovery in oil flows through the strait, with Persian Gulf crude exports up last week but on a declining trend.

Attention now turns to Sunday, November 29, when OPEC+ ministers meet to decide 2027 output policy. Reuters reported that Iraq and Kazakhstan are pressing the group to raise its estimate of their production capacity, in a review that will help determine next year's quotas, and that Russian Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak said the capacity review is set to be completed by year-end. LSEG oil research specialist Zhenqiang Seto noted that the United Arab Emirates is no longer restricted by OPEC+ policies, adding to the uncertainty over how restraint is shared in 2027.
