GlobalFoundries announced Thursday a $2 billion multi-year partnership with TSMC to shore up American manufacturing of advanced semiconductors. Under the deal, GlobalFoundries will produce silicon interposers for TSMC's chip-on-wafer-on-substrate (CoWoS) advanced packaging specification at its Malta, New York fab site. The interposers sit under compute logic and memory in multi-die assemblies such as GPUs and AI accelerators, providing faster, lower-power communication between chips than conventional organic packaging; essentially every chip using high-bandwidth memory relies on an interposer or interconnect bridge to stitch everything together.

The timing matters. When TSMC began producing Nvidia's Blackwell GPUs in Arizona, the final product still had to be round tripped to Taiwan for final assembly, and neither TSMC's own Arizona packaging facility nor Amkor's US CoWoS services are expected online until 2028 or 2029. GlobalFoundries plans to add capacity at Malta to meet demand, but volume production will not begin until the first half of 2028 at the earliest. The partnership is expected to last five years with the option to extend.

**Update (Friday, October 9):** The deal's terms trace to GlobalFoundries' own announcement, confirming the $2 billion figure and the Malta, New York production site. CoWoS remains the most popular but not the only option: Intel Foundry offers both leading-edge process and advanced packaging comparable to TSMC's, compatible with TSMC-produced silicon, and Intel routinely packages TSMC-made chips in its own facilities.
