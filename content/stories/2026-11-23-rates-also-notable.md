Nigeria's Monetary Policy Committee began its two day meeting on November 23 with the policy rate at 23 percent, reset from 26.5 percent in September, the cash reserve requirement for deposit money banks at 45 percent and the standing facilities corridor at plus 50 and minus 300 basis points. Market experts surveyed ahead of the meeting expect a hold while the committee assesses whether disinflation survives festive season and pre election spending. The decision is due on November 24.

The UK Office for National Statistics published new insights on labour market dynamics from its Linked Employer-Employee Data, linking payroll and business data to produce measures of job hires, separations and job to job flows. The statistics are designated official statistics in development, and the release also carried an update on the ONS labour market transformation programme, the long running effort to rebuild the UK's labour force measurement.

Swiss home care hit a record in 2025. The Federal Statistical Office reported home assistance and care services provided 29.6 million hours of care, up 15.3 percent on 2024, outpatient hospital contacts rose 3.9 percent with billings exceeding 10 billion francs for the first time, and inpatient admissions rose 1.8 percent. The figures are a fiscal pressure datapoint for cantons and health insurers rather than a market mover.

Luxembourg's CSSF finalised the country's first vertical risk assessment on international financial sanctions related to terrorist financing and proliferation financing. The document, published on September 24 and carried in the current pool, maps how the financial centre's sanctions exposure runs through its fund and banking sectors.
