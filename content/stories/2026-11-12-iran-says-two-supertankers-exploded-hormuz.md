Iran's Revolutionary Guard navy said on Thursday that two supertankers exploded overnight in the Strait of Hormuz and are burning, after passing along the southern shipping route despite repeated warnings from the Guard's Hormuz traffic control centre that the route is unauthorised and unsafe. The two ships were carrying more than 4 million barrels of oil between them, the statement said, carried by Iran's state news agency IRNA.

The statement named no vessel, flag or owner, gave no casualty figure and stated no cause for the explosions, and no independent source had confirmed the incident as of early Thursday. The Guard navy warned that any ship in the Gulf or the strait that switches off its navigation systems and cooperates with the US military on the southern route would meet the same fate.

The claim is the latest in a series Tehran has made this week, after the Guard said two tankers hit naval mines on the same route on November 8 and Iran said it struck Oman's Didamar Island, which helps guide ships through the corridor, on November 11.
