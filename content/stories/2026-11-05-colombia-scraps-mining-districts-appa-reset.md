Colombia's Ministry of Mines repealed 15 special mining districts covering 5.3 million hectares, nearly 5 percent of the national territory, on October 22, annulling the resolutions that created them, including their technical annexes and maps, and ordering the Colombian Mining Cadastre updated. The districts spanned Antioquia, Boyaca, Cauca, Cesar, Choco, Cordoba, Huila, La Guajira, Narino, Norte de Santander and Valle del Cauca, plus areas on the Pacific coast. The ministry said the districts had produced poor results, inadequate alternatives for mining dependent communities, legal uncertainty and insufficient consultation with regional authorities, and that they conflicted with municipal land use plans and carried no expiry dates or transition provisions.

In parallel, the Ministry of Agriculture revoked Food Production Protection Areas, known as APPA, that had restricted mineral development in farming regions: 15,839 hectares in five municipalities in Cundinamarca's Sabana Centro in early October, designated areas in nine municipalities in southern La Guajira, 17 municipalities across Cordoba on October 23, and, four days later, nine resolutions covering 102,801 hectares in 19 municipalities in southwestern Antioquia. In Antioquia, a technical review found 31,801 hectares, 30.9 percent of the affected territory, overlapped with valid mining titles or areas reserved for their mineral potential. Agriculture Minister Indalecio Dangond said the government intends to eliminate all remaining APPA designations, and the moves follow an August rollback in which the Environment Ministry revoked a reserve in the Santurban paramo.

The industry case is scale. Colombian Mining Association president Juan Camilo Nariño estimates a more supportive climate could unlock $4.7 billion in mine construction, attract $350 million a year in exploration spending and support industry growth of up to 10 percent a year, and the association puts total opportunity at about $4 billion through 2030 across copper, gold, coal and nickel. Deputy Mines Minister Luis Madrinan has said the sector needs to expand by at least 10 percent to support the government's 6 percent growth ambition. The pipeline that could benefit includes AngloGold Ashanti's Quebradona, Cordoba Minerals' Alacran, Libero Copper's Mocoa and Royal Road Minerals' Guintar project, against a production base where Colombia's only significant copper mine, Atico Mining's El Roble, produced 9.2 million pounds in 2025, against 5.5 million tonnes in Chile.

Jurisdiction watch: this is a deregulatory reset, the mirror image of the West African code rewrites covered elsewhere in this edition. The constraints that remain are the binding ones: environmental licensing, as the Gramalote hearing on November 10 will test, community consultation, and security, with illegal gold mining tied to organised crime raising costs for legitimate operators. Repeal changes what may be applied for; it does not shorten the 15 to 20 years a large copper project can take from discovery to production.
