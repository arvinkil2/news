New Zealand's general election ended without a clear winner on Saturday night. With 98.5 percent of votes counted, Prime Minister Christopher Luxon's National Party and Chris Hipkins' Labour Party were each on about 29.3 percent of the party vote, Radio New Zealand reported, a fall for National from 38.1 percent in 2023. National remained the largest party in Parliament, with 42 seats, because it won most electorates.

On election-night figures, National's coalition with ACT and New Zealand First was projected to hold 67 seats in an enlarged 131-seat Parliament, one more than the 66 needed for a majority. Luxon told supporters the coalition had a pathway to forming the next government but that the result was too close to call, and Hipkins did not concede. The Greens took about 13.3 percent, ACT about 10.9 percent and New Zealand First about 9.8 percent, while the centrist Opportunity Party crossed the threshold for the first time with about 6 percent and seven seats.

Several hundred thousand special votes, cast overseas or outside voters' home electorates, are still to be counted, and they have traditionally leaned to the left. The Electoral Commission will publish the official result on 27 November, and Luxon's government continues in caretaker mode until a new government is formed.
