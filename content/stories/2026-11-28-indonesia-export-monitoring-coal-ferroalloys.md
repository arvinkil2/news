State export monitoring firm Danantara Sumberdaya Indonesia (DSI) reassured exporters on Friday that the fee based monitoring platform it aims to launch on January 1 would not disrupt shipments. DSI is part of President Prabowo Subianto's efforts to tighten the state's control over Indonesia's natural resources exports and to tackle issues such as alleged transfer pricing practices and under invoicing.

The firm will monitor exports of palm oil, coal and ferroalloys. Indonesia is the world's top exporter of palm oil, thermal coal and nickel products, so the platform reaches deep into mining supply chains. DSI had not decided on a final fee for its services, but has discussed an indicative fee with industry associations for feedback, chief executive Luke Mahony said at a press briefing on Friday. It aims to announce the fee in December and launch the monitoring system on January 1, Mahony said, with registration from December 15. Palm oil group GAPKI said on Thursday that DSI had proposed a service fee of 0.5 percent of the value of palm oil exports; DSI director Nur Hidayat Udin said the fee's size would not significantly impact exporters' finances or affect prices.

On shipment flow, the existing clearance process would be unchanged and DSI would not replace the role of customs, chief finance officer Sinthya Roesly told the same briefing. "There will be no changes to the customs clearance process itself," she said. "The only difference is that users must first register with DSI and upload their contracts to facilitate pre clearance." The process will not hold up vessels, as analysis of price anomalies typically takes place after vessels have sailed and payments have been received, Mahony said.

As part of its role in tackling under invoicing, DSI will create a reference price to benchmark the value of transactions and monitor price anomalies, he added. Palm oil traders said the indicative fee disclosed by GAPKI was small compared with the export taxes and levies exporters already pay, though traders remained concerned about how the new verification measures would affect the flow of goods.
