American small businesses turned more pessimistic in September, in a survey the Federal Reserve will read as evidence on both hiring and price setting at the economy's base. The National Federation of Independent Business said Tuesday that optimism slumped below its 52 year historical average, dragged down by inflation and by the quality and availability of labour. Its uncertainty index rose 1 point to 90, 22 points above its historical average, and only 7 percent of owners said it was a good time to expand, down 5 percentage points from August.

Labour remains the binding constraint. Twenty six percent of owners ranked low labour quality and availability as their single most important problem, up 3 points from August, 32 percent said they could not fill job openings, 8 points above the historical average, and among the 51 percent trying to hire, 87 percent reported few or no qualified applicants.

Inflation is climbing back up the problem list at the same time. One in five owners named it their top problem, up 4 points from August, with fuel costs cited directly: a Georgia retailer told the federation that fuel prices were hitting delivery trucks and freight surcharges and cutting customers' disposable income. Diesel averaged $6.28 a gallon in the AAA comparison quoted with the survey, up about 72 percent over a year.

The survey lands a day before September CPI, with the August PCE price index, the Fed's preferred gauge, having risen 3.4 percent year on year, and the IMF projecting US consumer prices to rise 3.6 percent this year. NFIB chief economist Bill Dunkelberg said owners are proceeding with caution as they weigh rising costs, interest rates and hiring difficulty, a combination that argues for caution in both directions at the October FOMC.
