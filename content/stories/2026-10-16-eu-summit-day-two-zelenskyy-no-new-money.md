Latest: The summit has closed and the Commission has published its account of the outcome. In her closing statement with Council President Antonio Costa, Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the EU trade deficit with China ran at 1 billion euros a day in 2025 and will be higher in 2026, calling it structural and unsustainable. She set out three pillars: dialogue, where Beijing consultations produced what she described as China's first agreement to moderate hybrid vehicle exports, better market access for several EU industrial and agri-food exports, and more predictability on export controls including rare earths and magnets; fuller use of existing trade instruments, including more safeguards, which leaders supported; and new instruments in December, a DiversifyEU package and a Solidarity Mechanism against coercion and retaliation, plus a further instrument, building on a Franco-German paper, to limit single market access quickly where that is in the Union interest.

On Ukraine, von der Leyen said almost 16 billion euros has been disbursed under the defence leg of the Ukraine Support Loan, and a further 17 billion euros can still be disbursed before the end of the year for budget support if Ukraine delivers reforms, adding that the EU and its member states account for 80 percent of external aid to Ukraine. That conditional figure sits well below Kyiv's ask at this summit: NOS reported that President Zelenskyy asked European allies for 70 billion euros in extra support, for defence, weapons and resilience, ahead of a hard winter in which Russian strikes on the energy sector are expected to intensify and Patriot interceptor stocks are nearly exhausted. On the budget, she said the negotiating box cuts flexibilities by 50 percent against today and would leave 99.5 percent of spending fixed for the next ten years, and that leaders had asked for an ambitious package of new own resources.

Earlier: Speaking in Brussels, President Zelenskyy confirmed a large shortage of PAC-2 and PAC-3 interceptor missiles for Ukraine's US made Patriot air defence systems, saying most of those missiles have for some time been going to the Middle East and that Ukraine is not among the first priority recipients. The missiles Ukraine does receive come from the United States under the PURL programme, paid for by European countries, and Kyiv is negotiating with partner states for whatever batches are available, in lots as small as three to ten missiles at a time. Ukrainian air defence is currently focused above all on protecting energy infrastructure, he said.

Ukraine took the second day of the European Council in Brussels. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy attended in person, and Commission President Ursula von der Leyen appeared with him and Council President Antonio Costa ahead of the session, a deliberate show of alignment a day after Washington temporarily lifted sanctions on Russian diesel, a move covered here on October 15.

European capitals lined up behind Kyiv in public. France's President Emmanuel Macron said Ukraine had to be given the means to protect itself, Poland's Prime Minister Donald Tusk told Ukrainians they were not alone, and Italy's Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni warned that the diesel arrangement hands Moscow legitimacy and revenue while blunting sanctions. Foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas called the deal a gift to Vladimir Putin and asked what he had given in return. Bulgaria's Prime Minister Rumen Radev broke ranks, criticising Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries and welcoming any step that lowers fuel prices.

The solidarity came with little money attached. No fresh decisions on financing Kyiv were planned for this summit, and the use of frozen Russian assets was not on the formal agenda, according to reporting on the draft conclusions. Kyiv arrived asking for more: a senior Ukrainian official said European support still falls short of need and that Ukraine is working on European alternatives to US air defences and Starlink satellite communications. Draft language also covers a ban on moving grain taken by Russia from occupied Ukrainian territory through the EU, and help with Ukraine's winter preparations.

The bloc's own finances consumed the first day and remain open. Leaders are still divided over the 2028 to 2034 budget, worth almost 2 trillion euros, and Costa has taken personal charge of the negotiations ahead of a special summit in late November. On China, draft conclusions would have leaders ask the Commission to keep talking to Beijing while making full use of trade defence measures and adding new protective instruments, against a goods trade deficit of 360 billion euros in 2025. Status: summit concluded October 16, with the closing Commission statement published the same day and covered in the update above.
