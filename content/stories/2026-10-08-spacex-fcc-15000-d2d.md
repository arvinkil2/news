The Federal Communications Commission has approved SpaceX's application to deploy 15,000 high-capacity direct-to-device satellites across nine orbital shells at 326 to 335 kilometers, low enough to reduce latency versus existing constellations. The approval lands about a year after SpaceX filed its plans and follows its acquisition of AWS-4 and H-block spectrum licenses from EchoStar, which lets it stop relying on spectrum leased from mobile carriers. SpaceX says the constellation will enable 5G-like speeds of 150Mbps per user, up from 4Mbps today.

The FCC order requires SpaceX to have half of the authorized satellites launched and operating by October 7, 2032, with the full 15,000 by October 7, 2035. The approval comes days after AT&T, T-Mobile US and Verizon formed a spectrum-pooling joint venture focused on direct-to-device satellite services, setting up a head-to-head between the carrier alliance and Starlink Mobile.

**Update (Friday, October 9):** Trade-press reporting confirms the FCC granted SpaceX's application in part in order DA 26-1078, adopted and released October 6, 2026, clearing the company to use EchoStar spectrum for cellular service inside and outside the US. The grant also lets the satellites keep using T-Mobile's PCS G-block spectrum even as T-Mobile downplays its T-Satellite partnership. The authorization runs 15 years from the date the first new D2D satellite reaches its authorized orbit, and SpaceX must file semi-annual deployment reports. Separately, SpaceX's agreement to buy Grain Management's nationwide 800 MHz spectrum portfolio would add indoor low-band coverage, subject to FCC approval.
