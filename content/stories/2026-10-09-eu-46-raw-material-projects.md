## Latest: Sejourne warns of a crisis in the coming months and names emblematic projects

Executive Vice-President Stephane Sejourne, opening the Commission's announcement on October 9, struck a more urgent tone than the press release, warning that Europe could face a trade crisis in the coming months and saying the bloc is pulling out all the stops to build its own supply chains by 2030. His quote in the release: Europe is stepping up like never before to reclaim sovereignty over critical raw materials, opening mines and developing processing and recycling capacity with the aim of reducing dependency and increasing production in Europe.

Sejourne singled out three projects as emblematic for accelerated permits: Carester's rare-earth plant near Pau in France, a gallium plant in Greece, and a lithium extraction and processing plant in Finland. The Carester plant, expected to be operational in late 2026, could account for about 15 percent of global production of dysprosium and terbium oxides, two rare earths for which the EU is largely dependent on China.

The Commission's release added detail on the pipeline's composition: the 46 projects were selected from 102 applications across 18 member states, of which 92 were assessed against the act's criteria, and they break down into 8 extraction, 11 processing, 19 recycling and 8 integrated projects. Together with last year's lists, they could fully meet the 2030 extraction benchmarks for lithium, nickel, rare earths, magnesium and tungsten, the processing benchmarks for lithium and rare earths, and the recycling benchmarks for lithium, cobalt and rare earths. On financing, the release said Team Europe has mobilised more than 2 billion euros since the RESourceEU plan launched, while ukpulse reported member states have unlocked 1 billion euros for six projects and the European Investment Bank 660 million euros for eight. Of last year's strategic projects, 25 have completed environmental impact assessments and 17 have obtained construction permits. The Commission said a new call for strategic project applications will open toward the end of 2026.

## Latest: KGHM's $2.44 billion copper projects first to win strategic status

The Commission has designated two major investments by Polish copper producer KGHM Polska Miedz as strategic projects under the Critical Raw Materials Act, the company said Friday, making them the first named beneficiaries of the new batch. The projects, worth a combined 9.5 billion zlotys ($2.44 billion), aim to strengthen Europe's copper mining and recycling capacity and reduce dependence on non-EU suppliers.

The Retkow-Grodziszcze mining project aims to extract more than 100 million tons of ore by 2055, yielding nearly 1.5 million tons of copper and over 5,000 tons of silver. KGHM will also transform its Legnica smelter into a recycling facility with an expected annual production of about 135,000 tons of electrolytic copper and 250 tons of nickel. The designation brings streamlined permitting, better administrative coordination and access to preferential financing.

Michal Kozak, an analyst at Polish brokerage Trigon, told Reuters the designation was "directionally good news" for KGHM but cautioned that it was unclear whether it would lead to significant financial support. KGHM shares were up 4 percent at 0905 GMT.

The European Commission has chosen 46 new strategic raw-material projects to benefit from faster permitting and help in bidding for public and private funding, as the bloc races to diversify supply chains and reduce reliance on China. The Commission put the total capital investment needed at about 21.1 billion euros (about 23.7 billion dollars), to be mobilised from public and private sources.

The projects are spread across 16 EU countries and cover 15 of the EU's list of 17 strategic raw materials, including copper, lithium, nickel, cobalt, manganese, graphite, rare earths, magnesium and tungsten. Notable projects include the Zinnwald lithium project in Germany, the Skouries mine in Greece, Arctial's planned aluminium smelter in Finland and the Viscaria copper project in Sweden, with participation from major European industrial groups including Umicore, Aurubis and Leonardo.

The announcement was made on the same day the EU-China Trade and Investment Council holds its final session in Beijing, where securing an extension of the moratorium on Chinese rare-earth export controls is the EU's most time-sensitive ask. The Commission said it had already mobilised more than 2 billion euros in public funding for projects picked last year, but developers warned in August that more projects could fail without urgent financing, and progress has been slow. The 2024 Critical Raw Materials Act sets the targets the pipeline is meant to meet: 10 percent of annual needs mined domestically, 40 percent processed and 25 percent recycled by 2030.
