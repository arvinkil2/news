The North Carolina numbers are the kind that rearrange a map. A WRAL and WSOC poll conducted by SurveyUSA between October 7 and 11 found former governor Roy Cooper leading former Republican National Committee chair Michael Whatley by 54 percent to 40 percent among likely voters, with about 5 percent undecided, in the race for the state's open Senate seat. The survey sampled 900 North Carolinians, including 643 who had already voted by mail or said they were likely to vote, with a credibility interval of 5 percentage points. A separate Trafalgar Group poll of 1,091 likely voters released Monday put the same race much closer, at 48.8 percent to 40.9 percent, which is the honest range to carry: Cooper ahead in every public poll, by somewhere between 8 and 14 points.

The money is voting the same way. Reporting summarised this week said the Senate campaign arm paused spending in North Carolina after pouring 32 million dollars into the race, and that a pro Whatley super PAC cut 6.5 million dollars from its October buy, while Senate Republicans are putting 8 million dollars more into Nebraska, a state a majority party should not need to defend at that price in October. Bloomberg reported a GOP super PAC describing the Senate as the firewall as House hopes fade, a headline level item flagged in the sourcing note.

The ratings moved too. Sabato's Crystal Ball shifted the Kansas Senate race to a toss up, for a seat Democrats have not held since the Franklin Roosevelt era, and reporting on the shift noted Democrats outspending Republicans in Kansas general election advertising, 8.2 million dollars to 7 million dollars by AdImpact figures. Republicans hold 53 seats today, and the battleground list in current reporting runs through Ohio, Michigan, Georgia, Maine, Iowa, New Hampshire, Alaska, Texas and Montana among 35 races. None of this decides November 3, and one poll is not a result. But the pattern across polling, ratings and spending is consistent, and it is the same pattern Tuesday's debate coverage showed on the candidate level: the majority is defending ground it expected to hold comfortably. Status: campaign in progress, early voting beginning this week in North Carolina.
