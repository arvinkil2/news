Canada's current account surplus narrowed to $4.5 billion in the third quarter of 2026, from $6.1 billion in the second quarter, Statistics Canada reported on Friday. It was the first time since 2022 that Canada has recorded two consecutive quarterly current account surpluses. The goods surplus was essentially unchanged at $10.7 billion, with exports up 0.2 percent to $232.3 billion and imports up 0.3 percent to $221.6 billion; the goods surplus with the United States narrowed from $30.9 billion to $30.2 billion. Wider deficits on services, at $1.0 billion, and on investment income, at $1.8 billion, accounted for the smaller overall surplus, and the agency said the investment income deficit grew largely because of higher interest payments on Canadian bonds held by non-residents as bond yields rose.

Foreign investors increased their holdings of Canadian securities by $24.0 billion in the third quarter, including $28.1 billion of debt securities, led by $12.8 billion of federal government bonds and $11.4 billion of private corporate bonds, while reducing their holdings of Canadian shares for a third consecutive quarter. Canadian investors added $5.8 billion of foreign securities, their lowest quarterly total since the first quarter of 2023, and sold $31.1 billion of US shares in July. Foreign direct investment in Canada, at $33.7 billion and driven mainly by mergers and acquisitions, exceeded Canadian direct investment abroad of $31.5 billion.
