The Commodity Futures Trading Commission is investigating whether Kalshi failed to properly notify the agency about dozens of new products that were in the works, and has privately warned the company that charges tied to the launch of certain contracts could land soon, though they could also end in a settlement, Politico reported. The focus is the self-certification process, under which regulated exchanges can begin offering new products as soon as the next day by attesting that they meet the agency's requirements, including that they are not easily manipulated, without waiting for express approval. Kalshi spokesperson Elisabeth Diana said the warning concerned a procedural error involving the submission of a handful of contracts, many of which were not live, and that the company was already addressing it; the CFTC did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
