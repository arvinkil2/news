Friday's close split the mining tape in two. Every miner basket in the site's market tables rose: uranium miners gained 4.39 percent, silver miners 2.63 percent, gold miners 2.16 percent, rare earth miners 1.72 percent, copper miners 1.45 percent and lithium and battery names 1.21 percent. The diversified majors rose with them, BHP up 1.67 percent and Rio Tinto up 0.69 percent.

The metals underneath moved the other way. Gold futures fell 0.94 percent to $4,074.90 an ounce, silver futures eased 0.56 percent to $51.94 an ounce and copper slipped 0.18 percent to $6.24 a pound. Copper's fall extends the week's tariff story: benchmark LME copper fell for a fourth straight session on Friday to its lowest since July, as reported on this site on November 7, even while Shanghai warehouse stocks dropped to their lowest in almost three years.

Uranium is the clearest company led leg. Uranium Energy traded 4.6 percent stronger on Friday morning in New York, in line with the sector basket, after a week in which it signed spot priced supply contracts with two US utilities and its Paraguayan plans were overtaken by that country's political crisis. Ur-Energy, by contrast, fell 7.5 percent on Thursday in Toronto despite setting out a Wyoming expansion path, a reminder that the basket move is not lifting every name.

The divergence has a simple reading and a caution. The simple reading is that equity investors are pricing forward stories, restarts, contracting and jurisdiction contests, rather than Friday's metal settlements. The caution is that miner baskets rising into falling gold and copper futures rarely persist without the metals stabilising: gold futures are still up 4.51 percent on the week and silver up 5.79 percent, so Friday reads as a pause in a strong metals week, not a break. Monday's test is whether the futures or the equities blink first.
