BILL Holdings, the San Jose payments platform used by nearly 500,000 businesses, has agreed to be acquired by TPG in partnership with Corpay for $63.25 a share in cash. The merger agreement, dated November 19 and filed with the SEC on Friday as an exhibit to BILL's Form 8-K, puts the fully diluted equity value at about $6.0 billion and will leave BILL as a wholly owned subsidiary of the buyer vehicle, Blizzard Borrower 2026, Inc., with its shares delisted from the New York Stock Exchange.

TPG will take the majority interest through TPG Capital, its US and European private equity platform. Corpay, the Atlanta spend management company, will invest $1.1 billion for approximately a one-third equity stake and holds an option to acquire 100 percent of the company. The guarantees filed with the agreement name Corpay and TPG funds, including TPG Partners X and T-POP Investment Holdings I, as guarantors, and the buyers have delivered equity and debt financing commitment letters. BILL's board approved the transaction unanimously, and founder René Lacerte will continue to lead the company as chief executive after closing.

Closing is expected in the first half of 2027, subject to approval by BILL stockholders and required regulatory approvals. If BILL terminates the agreement in specified circumstances, including to accept a superior proposal, it owes a termination fee of $180.51 million under the agreement. The filing reports 86,047,711 shares of common stock outstanding as of November 16, 2026.

The $63.25 price is a 31 percent premium to the stock's 60-day volume-weighted average price through November 16, before reports of talks surfaced, according to Finimize, which noted the deal follows activist pressure from Starboard Value, holder of an 8.5 percent stake that had nominated directors. BILL shares fell about 3.7 percent in after-hours trading after the announcement, as investors weighed the premium against the wait to a first-half 2027 closing.
