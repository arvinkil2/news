Trump's 2022 suit alleged Clinton, Comey, Christopher Steele, the DNC and others ran a racketeering conspiracy to discredit him with a false narrative of collusion with Russia in 2016. US District Judge Donald Middlebrooks dismissed the complaint as a political manifesto built on frivolous legal theories, writing that "no reasonable lawyer would have filed it," fined Trump and his lawyer Alina Habba nearly $1 million in sanctions, and the 11th Circuit upheld both rulings in November 2025. The suit had alleged civil racketeering claims that the district court said were filed too late, and the Supreme Court filing now raises whether a president's responsibilities warrant holding off certain legal deadlines while in office: Trump's lawyers wrote that he concluded any private civil suit would consume substantial time and energy and should therefore be postponed until after his term.

The Friday filing is a petition for Supreme Court review of the 11th Circuit decision, filed under an extension Justice Clarence Thomas had granted through October 9; as of Friday night the case had not yet appeared on the Court's docket as a new filing. In the papers, Trump's lawyers argue the lower-court decisions reflect "a deeply troubling trend of federal courts using sanctions and disciplinary threats to punish the president and his lawyers for legitimate legal positions," and say Supreme Court review is needed to protect the presidency. Reuters and CNN independently confirmed the filing on Friday. The Court has not indicated whether it will hear the case, and it accepts only a small fraction of such petitions; a grant of review is distinct from the filing itself, and nothing so far suggests the petition has been granted.
