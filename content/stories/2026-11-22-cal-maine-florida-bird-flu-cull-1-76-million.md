Cal-Maine Foods, the largest egg producer in the United States, said one of its Florida facilities tested positive for highly pathogenic avian influenza. Production at the facility has temporarily ceased while the company follows protocols prescribed by the US Department of Agriculture.

The outbreak will result in the depopulation of approximately 1.4 million laying hens and 360,000 pullets, together 2.7 percent of the company's flock. Cal-Maine said it is coordinating production and distribution across its other locations to mitigate the disruption and maintain continuity of supply.

The company noted that, according to the USDA, avian influenza cannot be transmitted through safely handled and properly cooked eggs, that there is no known risk from eggs currently on the market, and that no eggs have been recalled. According to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, the human health risk to the general public from these viruses is considered low.

Cal-Maine said it will provide updated information in its second-quarter financial report to the Securities and Exchange Commission and does not expect interim updates unless the information is deemed material. The outbreak follows a first quarter of fiscal 2027 in which the company posted a net loss of 58.6 million dollars, against net income of 199.3 million dollars a year earlier, on sales down 41.5 percent to 539.6 million dollars.
