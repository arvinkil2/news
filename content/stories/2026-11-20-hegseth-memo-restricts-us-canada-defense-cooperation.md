## Latest

Canadian Defence Minister David McGuinty said on Saturday, on the sidelines of a security conference in Halifax, that he had seen no change in military cooperation with the United States and had not seen the classified memo Secretary Hegseth reportedly circulated. He said he saw no daylight between the two countries, military to military and otherwise, and that their forces continued working hand in glove on Caribbean drug trafficking operations, integrated missile systems and fighter-jet responses across North America. Canada's defence ministry said it would not speculate on media reports but that the collaborative work continues. Senator Mike Rounds, a Republican briefed by defence officials on the memo, described it as containing no major new measures beyond steps US military officials had taken for years. McGuinty said Canada needed to take greater responsibility for its own security, particularly in the Arctic, and was working on a domestic alternative to Starlink satellite communications.

US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth imposed new restrictions on defense cooperation with Canada in a classified memo distributed last week, the Washington Post reported on Friday, citing US officials familiar with the matter. The officials said the memo's specific contents have not been confirmed, and one said Canada would receive the measure as a slap in the face.

Pentagon spokesperson Sean Parnell told the Post that the United States is taking measures to ensure cooperation with Canada advances America's substantive defense goals and ultimately supports revitalizing the bilateral defense relationship, and that the directive would not affect defense intelligence sharing or cooperation through NATO or NORAD. No Canadian government reaction was reported, and a Five Eyes official told the Post that information shared by the United States through several channels has decreased over the past year.
