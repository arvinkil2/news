**Latest.** The Monetary Authority of Singapore said it will work with the People's Bank of China to restructure the MAS renminbi facility, which is backed by the two central banks' bilateral currency swap arrangement, into an RMB Corporate Facility and an RMB Liquidity Facility. Eligible financial institutions will be able to tap the corporate facility to finance trade with third countries, working capital, capital expenditure and intra-group funding for those activities, at one-, three- and six-month tenors, while the liquidity facility will continue to provide backstop renminbi funding for Singapore's offshore renminbi market. Both facilities will accept Singapore Government Securities and MAS Bills as collateral in addition to Singapore dollar cash, and the two central banks are exploring accepting China Government Bonds. MAS and the People's Bank of China are also exploring a fast payment systems linkage for small-value transfers. DBS Bank, Singapore's second renminbi clearing bank, has started clearing services, and Bank of China's Singapore branch has been included as an MAS primary dealer, MAS said.

The council, co-chaired by Singapore Deputy Prime Minister Gan Kim Yong and Chinese Vice Premier Ding Xuexiang, renewed a memorandum of understanding on trade in services covering sectors including healthcare, eldercare, professional and legal services, and signed a new public health agreement on agency exchanges. Gan said a linkage between the two countries' fast payment systems for small-value transfers is under discussion, which would add to the existing renminbi payment channels between Singapore and China. The 29 deliverables compare with 27 at the 2025 council in Chongqing and 25 in Singapore in 2024, and Ding pointed to science and technology innovation, the digital economy, artificial intelligence and green industries as the next areas of cooperation.
