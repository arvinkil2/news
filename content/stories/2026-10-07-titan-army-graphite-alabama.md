Titan Mining Corporation said Wednesday its subsidiary Empire State Mines has executed a term sheet with the U.S. Army establishing commercial terms for an Enhanced Use Lease to build a graphite processing facility at Anniston Army Depot. Under the framework, the company would develop, finance, build, own and operate the plant, producing purified micronized graphite in phase one and coated spherical purified graphite after customer qualification, with the Army holding a right of first offer on up to 10 percent of annual output.

The company said diligence continues at Pine Bluff Arsenal in Arkansas and Fort Drum in New York, and that the definitive lease still requires agreement on development plans, environmental and regulatory matters, project financing and board approval.

The Army's right of first offer is limited to uncommitted output, a maximum of 10 percent of annual PMG and CSPG production, and does not apply to product committed under offtake, supply or project-financing arrangements, per the term sheet. The facility would integrate with Titan's Kilbourne graphite concentrate operation in New York, which began production in January, the first US graphite concentrate output in decades. Deputy Assistant Secretary of the Army Jeff Waksman said the Army's land and legal authorities let it expedite the critical-minerals industrial base without putting taxpayer dollars at risk. Titan closed down 6 percent on the announcement Wednesday with a $228 million market capitalization; the financing for the commercial-scale build is still to come. Coverage expanded to a third outlet, Mining Technology, on October 8.
