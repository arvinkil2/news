Tanker freight rose 6.29 percent to 618.68 dollars, the largest move in the tables. This morning's commodities reporting covers the tightening physical backdrop: Britain sanctioned eleven more Russian energy ships and the Slavneft, Vostok Oil and Sibur groups on November 12 (see today's UK sanctions story), and the Iran LNG tanker mine report in the Strait of Hormuz was updated overnight with a UN briefing. No single confirmed cargo event in this morning's reporting accounts for the full jump, and the brief says so rather than inventing one.

Broadcom fell 4.76 percent to 347.01 dollars and the semiconductor index fell 3.67 percent, the core of Friday's selloff. Today's technology reporting explains the move directly: the chip rotation that followed Nvidia's leadership change unwound on Friday, with AMD down 8.8 percent and HSBC cutting Nvidia to hold (see today's chip rotation story).

Natural gas fell 3.29 percent to 3.23 dollars per mmBtu. Today's EIA storage story supplies the fundamental leg: US gas storage built 61 billion cubic feet to 3,862 billion cubic feet in the latest week, 75 billion cubic feet above the five-year average, a comfortable cushion heading into winter.

Meta rose 2.90 percent to 641.69 dollars, against the wider technology fall. No clear single driver in this morning's reporting explains the rise; Meta's completed 9.0 billion euro notes offering was already reported on November 12 and is a financing event, not an obvious equity catalyst.

Copper miners fell 2.51 percent and copper fell 1.67 percent to 6.08 dollars a pound. Today's mining reporting covers the second straight day of miner falls, with copper miners leading the sector lower even as physical copper premiums in China stay firm, a positioning and equity move rather than a physical-market break (see today's copper miners story).

Dry bulk freight fell 2.12 percent to 12.93 dollars, moving opposite to tanker freight. No clear single driver in this morning's reporting explains the dry bulk leg; the split itself, tankers bid and bulkers offered, is the signal.

Nvidia fell 2.01 percent to 162.85 dollars, a sixth straight weekly fall, down 12.75 percent on the week. Today's update to the Nvidia board story adds the latest leg: the board approached Arm chief executive Rene Haas, according to the Financial Times reporting carried by Reuters, while the board called any formal engagement premature.

Treasury yields rose again on Friday: the 5-year added 3.6 basis points to 4.98 percent, the 10-year added 5.1 basis points to 5.15 percent and the 30-year added 4.8 basis points to 5.48 percent. Today's rates reporting ties the level to the hawkish policy line on both sides of the Atlantic, with Federal Reserve officials holding that line on Friday and the Bank of England's Huw Pill signalling further rises in 2027 (see today's Fed officials and BoE Pill stories).

The cross-market read is defensive in growth and miners, firm in physical shipping and rates: semiconductors and copper miners fell, the Nasdaq 100 fell 1.22 percent and the Hang Seng fell 1.55 percent, while tanker freight jumped and long yields pushed further above 5 percent. Money is still leaving chip exposure and duration, not energy logistics.
