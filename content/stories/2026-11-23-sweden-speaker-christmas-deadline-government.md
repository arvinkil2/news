Sweden's government formation crisis now has a hard end point. Riksdag Speaker Andreas Norlen told party leaders on Tuesday, November 17, that they have one more week of exploratory talks, after which he will hold fresh individual rounds with all eight party leaders early this week and a third prime ministerial vote is expected before the end of November. "When the Swedish people celebrate Christmas, they should know that Sweden either has a new government or is preparing for an extraordinary election," Norlen said. He said he would not let the process drift past the New Year as the 2018 to 2019 formation crisis did.

The arithmetic has not moved. The four centre-left parties, the Social Democrats, the Left Party, the Centre Party and the Greens, hold 176 of the Riksdag's 349 seats, while the parties aligned with the outgoing right wing government hold 173. But the centre-left bloc is split by a red line: Left Party leader Nooshi Dadgostar insists on cabinet seats, which the Centre Party refuses to accept in a government it would support. Centre Party leader Elisabeth Thand Ringqvist said on November 15 that her party will negotiate first on Social Democratic leader Magdalena Andersson's programme, closing the door, for now, on caretaker Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson's proposed minority government of Moderates, Christian Democrats, Liberals and Centre Party.

Two of the four constitutional attempts are used up. The Riksdag rejected Andersson in mid-October and voted down Thand Ringqvist on November 3 by 324 votes to 25. Kristersson, tasked by the Speaker with exploring a centre-right government, asked for a few more days before being put to a vote. If the third vote fails, a fourth will be held in good time before Christmas; if that fails too, an extraordinary election follows within three months, which would be a first under Sweden's current constitution, in force since 1975.

The deadlock, now in its third month since the September 13 election, leaves Sweden under a caretaker government through the budget season and into the winter, with public impatience rising: a Novus poll published on November 18 put the Moderates ahead of the Social Democrats, 26.5 percent to 24.9 percent. Status: announced (Speaker's timetable); no government formed.
