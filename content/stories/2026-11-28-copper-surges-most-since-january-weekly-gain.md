Copper posted its biggest one day gain in London since January on Friday. Benchmark three month copper on the London Metal Exchange was up 4.78 percent at 12,439.50 dollars a tonne at 0700 GMT and surged as much as 6.45 percent intraday to above 12,600 dollars, after falling below 11,500 dollars earlier in the week. The most active contract on the Shanghai Futures Exchange closed 5.51 percent higher at 93,660 yuan a tonne, having jumped as much as 7.05 percent intraday.

The move puts copper on track for a weekly gain of about 2 percent, its first since the week ended October 9, ending a six week losing streak on the LME. The rebound followed an 11 month low earlier in the week and ran alongside a recovery in global equities after the month's selloff in artificial intelligence stocks, which had weighed on the metal most tied to electrification and data centre demand. Traders who had bet on further declines scrambled to buy back positions, according to Bloomberg reporting carried by Mining.com.

The physical picture does not yet confirm the price. LME copper stocks rose to 337,925 tonnes on Thursday, up about 3 percent from a week earlier, and have risen since mid October, while the cash to three month spread remains in contango, a structure that signals adequate nearby supply rather than a squeeze. Aluminium rose 1.64 percent on the LME on Friday, nickel gained 0.82 percent and lead added 0.43 percent, a broad but shallower base metals advance.

The honest read is a positioning and sentiment rebound from oversold levels, not a verified change in copper demand. Confirmation would need the contango to narrow and exchange stocks to stop rising; until then the rally rests on short covering and a calmer equity market, both of which can reverse quickly.
