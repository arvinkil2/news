**Latest.** NATO's North Atlantic Council, the alliance's top decision-making body, meets in emergency session at NATO headquarters in Brussels on Monday, November 23, to discuss the violation of Polish airspace, with allies set to present proposals for strengthening the protection of Poland's eastern border. Defence Minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz, who discussed the details with NATO Supreme Allied Commander Europe Gen. Alexus Grynkewich on Saturday, wrote on X that Poland would receive "strong support from NATO allies to strengthen the protection of our eastern border" and that the meeting would be an opportunity to "present concrete proposals and identify new possibilities for action". Poland will be represented by Deputy Defence Minister Pawel Zalewski, according to RMF24. Lublin prosecutor Andrzej Boron said the Kh-101 cruise missile recovered near Nowy Lipowiec was manufactured in Russia in the fourth quarter of 2026 and that preliminary forensic findings indicate its warhead detonated above the ground. A Swedish JAS 39 Gripen pilot patrolling Polish airspace received NATO authorisation to shoot down the suspected Kh-101 but did not fire, because severe weather prevented visual or optoelectronic confirmation of the target, RMF24 reported. During his visit to Warsaw on November 21, Secretary-General Mark Rutte pledged that the alliance "will defend every inch of Polish territory".

**Latest.** Washington's public assessment differs from Warsaw's. US President Donald Trump, speaking to Fox News aboard Air Force One on November 19, described Russian missiles entering Polish territory as “little” and said President Vladimir Putin had sent them “by mistake”. The remarks predated the November 20 impacts and concerned earlier incursions; Trump offered no evidence for the accident claim. They came after Prime Minister Donald Tusk criticised Trump for calling Putin “my friend” during their meeting in China, writing on X: “God, protect us from such friends.” Asked about the criticism, Trump defended his relationship with Putin and said he liked Tusk, adding that he had endorsed him and that Tusk “was in 10th place” before winning, an apparent confusion with President Karol Nawrocki, whom Trump backed in Poland's presidential election; Tusk became prime minister after the 2023 parliamentary elections.

NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte called the incident a “grave violation” of Poland’s airspace. “This incident is clearly dangerous, a consequence of Russia’s ongoing war against Ukraine,” Rutte wrote on X, and he is expected to go to Poland this weekend in response. The Associated Press reported a round of European condemnation: British Prime Minister Andy Burnham called the incident “utterly unacceptable”, European Council President António Costa said Russia “continues to spread its threats”, Germany’s Foreign Ministry called it “yet another example of Russia’s ongoing provocations”, and Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha said it showed the threat Russian aggression poses to all of Europe. Britain’s chief of defence intelligence, Lt. Gen. Matthew Jones, said he was confident Russia “does not actively seek war with NATO”, and that his greatest fear was “the risk of a significant miscalculation”. The AP report notes that Poland has the option of Article 4 emergency consultations, which it used last year; no invocation has been reported, and Moscow has not commented on the missiles or the drone.

Prime Minister Donald Tusk has defined the incident as a deliberate provocation by Russia and said it may be connected to allied training taking place on Polish soil. Defence Minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz, noting that allied defence ministers and military chiefs were in Poland for the Coalition of the Willing training for countries supporting Ukraine, said it was no coincidence that the incident happened at the same time. The training, at Nowa Deba in eastern Poland, involves about 2,800 troops from 11 European countries and has been running since November 9; Nowa Deba is about 75 kilometres from Nowy Lipowiec, where the Kh-101 came down in farmland and caught fire. The Polish military said 11 flying objects in total threatened Polish airspace on Friday morning and three crossed it. No casualties or facility damage have been confirmed.

Polish authorities are investigating three impact sites in the country's southeast after Russian munitions crossed into Poland during an overnight attack on Ukraine that Poland's defence minister described as one of the largest since Russia's full-scale invasion began. Defence Minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz confirmed that the object that came down at Nowy Lipowiec, in Lublin province, was a Kh-101 cruise missile. A second site is also believed to involve a missile, and the third most likely a jet-powered drone. Nobody in Poland was hurt and no infrastructure destruction was recorded, he said.

Interior Minister Marcin Kierwinski said operations were under way at three locations, Rudnik nad Sanem, Molodycz and Nowy Lipowiec, in connection with violations of Polish airspace. Infrastructure Minister Dariusz Klimczak said a loud bang had been heard near railway premises at Rudnik nad Sanem, about 50 kilometres west of Nowy Lipowiec, with no visible damage found, and that air traffic controllers had been evacuated from the airport at Rzeszow, the largest city in the region. Prime Minister Donald Tusk convened senior government, military and security officials and said there was currently no danger from the air. President Karol Nawrocki was also informed. Russia's embassy in Warsaw did not immediately respond to a request for comment, and no NATO statement or consultation step had been reported as of Friday morning in Europe.

The munitions fell during a mass Russian strike on Ukraine in which, according to Ukraine's air force, Russia launched 84 cruise missiles, almost all of them destroyed, alongside an unspecified number of ballistic missiles, and more than 400 attack drones were also destroyed. In Kyiv, Mayor Vitali Klitschko said three bridges were damaged. Ukraine's prime minister, Sergii Koretskyi, put the death toll at at least eight, six in Kyiv and two at a railway station in Lviv. The strikes followed a week in which the Kerch bridge between occupied Crimea and mainland Russia was hit three times and President Vladimir Putin warned of a far more painful response. A Russian cruise missile previously crashed in the same Polish region in July, near Tarnawa Kolonia, an incident Warsaw also attributed to Moscow's attacks on Ukraine.
