Japan's economy stalled in the third quarter. Real GDP was unchanged from the second quarter and fell 0.1 percent at an annualized rate, the Cabinet Office's first preliminary estimate showed, against the 0.3 percent annualized growth a Reuters poll of economists had expected and the 1.4 percent annualized pace recorded in the second quarter.

External demand subtracted 0.6 percentage point from growth, more than the 0.4 point drag expected, as higher energy imports outweighed exports, which slipped 0.2 percent on the quarter. Private consumption, more than half of the economy, fell 0.3 percent, a deeper drop than the 0.2 percent decline forecast.

Business investment offset part of the weakness, rising 1.6 percent on the quarter against a 1.5 percent forecast and reversing a 0.9 percent fall in the second quarter, and domestic demand added 0.6 percentage point overall. The GDP deflator rose 2.4 percent from a year earlier, down from 2.6 percent in the second quarter. In the first market reaction, the Nikkei 225 rose 0.4 percent, the yen weakened marginally to 159.76 per dollar, and the benchmark 10-year Japanese government bond yielded 2.885 percent. The Bank of Japan held its policy rate at 1.25 percent in October and is widely expected to raise it to 1.5 percent in December; board member Hajime Takata speaks later on Monday.
