Brazil will not renew the extra subsidy on diesel imports, worth 1.40 reais, about 0.25 dollars, per litre, the Finance Ministry said in a statement on Friday. The subsidy had expired after a 30 day run introduced in October to blunt fuel price spikes linked to the conflict in the Middle East.

A separate baseline subsidy of 2.12 reais per litre remains in effect, extended by ministerial order at the end of October. Combined, the two briefly gave imported diesel support of 3.52 reais per litre. The Planning and Budget Ministry estimated the extra tranche cost the federal budget about 1.6 billion reais during its short run.

Roughly a third of the diesel Brazil consumes is imported, which is why the subsidy was aimed at importers rather than at Petrobras's refineries. The subsidies rest on a provisional measure issued in September that authorised support for producers and importers while instability persists in international fuel markets, and the government has said the cost of the baseline measure is offset by extra oil related revenue, keeping it broadly neutral for the budget.

Officials said the extra measure could be reconsidered if crude oil prices climb again. For now the expiry is a fiscal decision taken while crude has retreated from its October highs, and the pass-through to pump prices, freight rates and food costs is the number to watch in the coming weeks.
