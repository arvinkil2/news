The New South Wales parliament has passed legislation to protect up to 400 jobs at MACH Energy's Mount Pleasant coal mine, allowing operations to continue at current production levels until 2032 while a new planning consent is pursued. The Mount Pleasant Mine Act 2026 follows a High Court ruling that referred the mine's consent back to the Land and Environment Court.

Without the legislation, MACH Energy advised the state government it would have been forced to cut up to 400 jobs at its Hunter Valley operation to comply with the smaller footprint under the previous consent. Under the act, the mine can produce up to 16.5 million tonnes of coal a year until 2032, or until a new consent is granted.

The protection is conditional. MACH Energy must enter a Jobs Guarantee Agreement securing all permanent workers before the legislated consent takes effect, must report quarterly on permanent workforce levels, and faces fines of up to $75,000 per quarter per worker for reductions made without ministerial approval. The resources minister can also direct operations to stop if the guarantee is breached.

The act adds new Scope 3 emissions consent conditions, including limits on coal exports to countries with emissions reduction policies. The NSW Minerals Council welcomed the law as providing certainty for Hunter workers and suppliers, and MACH Energy thanked the local community and the Mining and Energy Union for their support.
