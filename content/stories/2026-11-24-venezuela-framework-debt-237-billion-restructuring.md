Venezuela's government published its long-awaited macroeconomic framework on Tuesday, laying out a full picture of the economy and its debts for the first time in years, Reuters reported. The document puts the country's debt at $237.5 billion and says public debt is in protracted default and unsustainable, citing financial sanctions, falling hydrocarbon revenues and years of macroeconomic imbalance.

The debt pile amounted to 242 percent of GDP at the end of 2025. Eurobond debt totalled $110.1 billion, including $45.9 billion of past-due interest. The government said it planned to adopt a clear and credible restructuring plan that would involve all creditor groups, rather than a piecemeal approach that could leave some bondholders facing larger losses.

The framework expects economic output to more than double over the next five years, to $200.9 billion, and oil production to rise by 9.1 percent a year to 2.9 million barrels per day by 2036. Venezuela has been largely shut off from international finance since US sanctions were imposed in 2017, and re-engaged after the seizure of former President Nicolas Maduro by US forces in January.

Venezuela's sovereign bonds and those of state oil company PDVSA rose more than 4 cents on the dollar after the publication. A group representing international bondholders, the Venezuela Creditor Committee, declined to comment. The country announced plans to restructure in May and promised the figures within a month, but the earthquakes in June delayed the assessment.
