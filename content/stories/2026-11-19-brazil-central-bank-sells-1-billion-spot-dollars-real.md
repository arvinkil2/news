Brazil's central bank sold $1 billion in a spot dollar auction on Thursday to slow a slide that has taken about 15 percent off the real since President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva won re-election in October, its first spot intervention since December 2024. The real, trading at about 5.91 per dollar in New York morning hours and down 0.4 percent on the day after closing a day earlier at its weakest level since April 2025, erased its earlier losses after the auction was announced. The immediate pressure is a fiscal package, PLP 299/2026, stalled in the Senate over a cap on tax offsets worth up to 18.3 billion reais in 2027, and Lula's stated lack of urgency in naming a new finance minister.
