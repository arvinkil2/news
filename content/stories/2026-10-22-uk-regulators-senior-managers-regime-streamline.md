The Prudential Regulation Authority and the Financial Conduct Authority announced parallel consultations on Thursday to significantly streamline the Senior Managers and Certification Regime, the accountability framework introduced ten years ago. Regulator pre-approval would be removed for a range of functions, including chief actuaries and the chairs of risk and audit committees, with firms instead notifying regulators of appointments directly, and the Certification Regime would be removed for around 139,000 individuals below senior manager level who currently face annual certification checks. The regulators expect the changes to save firms around £2 billion over a ten-year period and to cut PRA pre-approvals by 43 percent.

Chief executives, chief financial officers and chairs would still require regulator pre-approval, including fit and proper tests, and the regulators said standards of individual accountability and conduct remain unchanged. Some of the reforms require legislative change, the consultations run until January 22, and policy is expected to follow in 2027.
