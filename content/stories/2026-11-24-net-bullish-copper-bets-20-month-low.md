Speculative investors reduced their net bullish positions in copper futures by nearly 39 percent in the latest reporting week, according to BMO Capital Markets, as selling pressure intensified amid uncertainty over US tariffs and weakening enthusiasm for artificial intelligence investments. It was the eighth straight weekly decline. Net bullish positions held by non-commercial traders in US copper futures fell to 17,777 contracts as of November 17, down from 28,904 a week earlier and at their lowest level since March 2025, BMO analysts Helen Amos and George Heppel said, citing Commodity Futures Trading Commission data released Friday.

Speculators shed 7,268 long contracts and added 3,859 short contracts over the week, the commission's figures show, and BMO said the exercise of put options has amplified the correction. The analysts said conversations at Asia Copper Week in China indicated onshore expectations are for the United States to abandon its tariff policy, a view fuelled by reports that tens of thousands of tonnes of cathode in the United States have been booked for direct dispatch to China.

China's Yangshan warrant premium eased to $450 per tonne on Monday from a record $500 last week, and another 11,225 tonnes of copper were earmarked for removal from London Metal Exchange warehouses in Asia on Friday, with a trader and warehouse manager telling Reuters the metal was destined for China. Traders were preparing to ship an estimated 30,000 to 100,000 tonnes of refined copper from the US West Coast to China, reversing the flow of metal into American warehouses ahead of possible US import tariffs.

Chilean producer Codelco has offered Asian buyers annual contract premiums of $250 to $280 per tonne for 2027, compared with $350 for Chinese buyers this year, Reuters reported, citing people familiar with the discussions. China imported just over 140,000 tonnes of Chilean cathode in the first nine months of 2026, down 38 percent from a year earlier.

A July 2025 White House proclamation imposed a 50 percent tariff on certain semi-finished copper products and derivatives while leaving refined copper exempt pending further review, and the administration is considering duties of 15 percent on refined copper from January 1, 2027 and 30 percent from January 1, 2028. BMO warned downward pressure could persist until Washington signals its intentions or prices fall far enough to curb scrap supply or higher cost leaching production, potentially to around $10,000 to $11,000 per tonne, while its medium term bullish view on copper is unchanged.
