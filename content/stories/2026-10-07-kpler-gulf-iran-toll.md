## Latest

A dozen Iranian attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz were recorded between September 28 and October 2, according to data from a U.S. Navy-led information outlet reported by Reuters on October 8. Tanker traffic through the waterway dropped sharply, with flows on Tuesday at the lowest since late July, according to Kpler. Brent crude traded at $102.50 a barrel, up more than 2 percent from Wednesday's close, while WTI changed hands at $89.96, up 1.9 percent. Gulf of Mexico operators also began shutting in production ahead of a potential hurricane, adding to the supply-side pressure.

Kpler's head of policy and geopolitical risk, Michelle Brohard, said she suspects certain Gulf producers have quietly struck deals to pay Iran a toll, possibly 10 to 20 percent of cargo value, to keep their tankers moving through the Strait of Hormuz as U.S. military escorts look unsustainable. Her claims remain unverified, though IG Group analyst Chris Beauchamp said undisclosed payments appear to be happening under the radar. The suspicion matters because Washington has opposed Iranian transit charges, sanctioned the financial network behind the Hormuz payment system, and warned Tehran against imposing tolls.
