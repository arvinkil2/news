Vanda Pharmaceuticals has settled its patent litigation against MSN Pharmaceuticals and MSN Laboratories over a proposed generic version of Hetlioz LQ, its tasimelteon oral suspension. Vanda reported the settlement, entered on November 16, in a Form 8-K filed on November 20.

Under the agreement, Vanda granted MSN a non exclusive, royalty free license to manufacture, import and market its generic tasimelteon oral suspension in the United States on and after a license effective date in the first quarter of 2040. MSN received limited pre launch rights, including manufacturing and import starting 180 days before that date, and customer and pricing discussions starting 30 days and 14 days before it.

Entry could come earlier in defined circumstances listed in the filing. They include expiry of the licensed patents, a final court decision that they are invalid, unenforceable or not infringed, the launch of an authorized or licensed generic, certain unlicensed third party generic launches that Vanda does not stop, and an FDA declared shortage after a voluntary withdrawal of Hetlioz LQ for reasons other than safety or efficacy.

MSN acknowledged that the asserted patents are valid and enforceable for its product and agreed not to challenge them, subject to exceptions in the filing. The parties will seek dismissal of the Delaware case and a consent judgment and permanent injunction. As required by law, the agreement will be submitted to the Federal Trade Commission and the Department of Justice for review.
