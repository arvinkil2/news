Aspen Neuroscience and Bambusa Therapeutics each filed Friday to raise up to $100 million in initial public offerings, adding two clinical-stage biotechs to the busiest IPO calendar since 2021. Aspen plans to list on Nasdaq under the ticker ASPE with Leerink Partners as lead underwriter; Bambusa plans to list as BBTX with BofA Securities leading a bookrunner group that also includes Evercore ISI, UBS and Cantor Fitzgerald. Bambusa had filed confidentially on August 3.

Aspen, the San Diego biotech, is developing autologous induced pluripotent stem cell therapies for neurodegenerative disease. Its lead candidate sasineprocel, a one-time patient-specific dopaminergic neuron precursor cell therapy delivered to the putamen, is in an open-label Phase 1/2a study in Parkinson's disease and carries both RMAT and Fast Track designations. The company is Gilead-backed and closed a $115 million Series C in late 2025; proceeds will fund ongoing and planned trials, manufacturing expansion and pipeline development. A second program, an autologous gene-corrected microglia therapy for pediatric leukodystrophies, is expected to begin IND-enabling studies in the second half of 2027.

Bambusa, the Boston biotech founded in 2024, is developing long-acting bispecific antibodies for immunology and inflammation. Lead candidate BBT001, targeting IL-4Ralpha and IL-31, is in Phase 1 trials for atopic dermatitis with initial proof-of-concept data reported in July, plus a Phase 2a trial in China for chronic spontaneous urticaria. BBT002, targeting IL-4Ralpha and IL-5, is in Phase 1 to Phase 2a trials for COPD, chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps and asthma. Two preclinical programs, BBT003 for inflammatory bowel disease and BBT004, a B-cell-depleting bispecific for autoimmune disease, round out the pipeline. The lead assets were licensed from BioNTech's Chinese subsidiary.

The filings bring the 2026 biotech IPO count to 29, against 8 in all of 2025, per Renaissance Capital. TRexBio and Retension priced Thursday, and City Therapeutics and Iambic set offering terms the same day as these filings, suggesting the window remains open for clinical-stage companies with defined pipelines even as pricing stays disciplined.
