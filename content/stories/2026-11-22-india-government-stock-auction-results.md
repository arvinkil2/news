The Reserve Bank of India sold 19,000 crore rupees of the 6.53 percent Government Stock 2031 and 14,000 crore rupees of the 7.71 percent Government Stock 2066 at auction on November 20, filling both notified amounts in full. The 2031 stock cut off at a price of 97.66 rupees, a yield to maturity of 7.1116 percent, and the 2066 stock cut off at 98.80 rupees, a yield of 7.8083 percent.

Demand was comfortably ahead of supply. Competitive bids totalled 48,923 crore rupees for the 2031 stock, about 2.6 times the notified amount, and 36,969 crore rupees for the 2066 stock, about 2.7 times cover. Weighted average yields came in just below the cut offs, at 7.1066 percent and 7.8033 percent respectively.

Primary dealers underwrote the full notified amount of both stocks, 19,000 crore and 14,000 crore rupees, and devolvement on them was nil, meaning the auction cleared on market bids alone without the dealers being forced to take stock. Non competitive bids were small, 9.053 crore rupees in the 2031 and 19.276 crore in the 2066, and were accepted in full.

The curve the auction printed is steep by Indian standards: roughly 70 basis points separate the five year area cut off from the 40 year. That term premium is the price of a borrowing programme that leans long, and Friday's cover says investors are still willing to be paid it rather than demanding more.
