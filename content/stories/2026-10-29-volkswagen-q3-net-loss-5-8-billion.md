Volkswagen swung to a net loss of 5.82 billion euros in the third quarter, with an operating loss of 5.6 billion euros against an operating profit of 1.7 billion euros in the same quarter a year earlier, even as group revenue rose 6.2 percent to 80.19 billion euros and deliveries climbed 9 percent to about 2.6 million vehicles. The damage came from the premium brands: Porsche wrote down 4.2 billion euros after reversing its electric vehicle strategy and returning to combustion models, a step that also drove Porsche itself to a quarterly loss of more than one billion euros, and Audi took a writedown of about 500 million euros.

Two structural pressures sit underneath the writedowns. Income from Volkswagen's Chinese joint ventures is shrinking as local makers such as BYD take share and price pressure builds, a hit the company put at about one billion euros in the quarter, and US tariffs imposed under President Trump weighed on results in North America, one of the group's largest markets. For the first nine months of the year Volkswagen still made a net profit of 3.5 billion euros, down 58 percent on a year earlier, and finance chief held the group's full year guidance, but told shareholders to expect no dividend for 2026.
