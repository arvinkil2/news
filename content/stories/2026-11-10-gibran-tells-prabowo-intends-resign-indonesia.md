## Latest

The vice presidency is now vacant, and the succession timetable is being set. Ahmad Muzani, chair of the People's Consultative Assembly (MPR), said on Thursday that the office of vice president has been vacant since Gibran handed his resignation letter to the chamber in person that afternoon, witnessed by the MPR deputy chairs, and that the assembly's task is to fill the vacancy within 60 days at the latest, Antara reported. The MPR leadership will hold a joint meeting on Friday at 14.00 Jakarta time, with all faction leaders and the Regional Representative Council group, to decide four things: the date of a plenary session to fill the office, a letter to President Prabowo Subianto asking him to submit two candidates, a team to verify the candidates' documents, and an ad hoc team to draft the MPR decree.

No parliamentary approval stands in the way. Coordinating Minister for Law Yusril Ihza Mahendra, who said he had read a copy of the letter, said the resignation does not require MPR approval: the letter, five paragraphs long, states in its first paragraph that Gibran is resigning from the office at his own request, and in its fourth asks the MPR to follow up under Article 8, paragraph 2 of the 1945 Constitution, which is a request to act on the vacancy, not a request for approval. Yusril said Gibran cited personal reasons and the voices developing in society, and did not elaborate on either. Prabowo met the MPR and House leadership, including the House's Commission III, and several ministers at the Presidential Palace on Thursday afternoon; Yusril, who was summoned to that meeting, described it as a consultation on how to carry out the follow-up between the presidency and the assembly.

The resignation is now formally before parliament. Ahmad Muzani, speaker of the upper house, said on Thursday that Gibran had handed in his resignation to the chamber and had also submitted a resignation letter to President Prabowo, Reuters reported from Jakarta. Muzani said parliamentary leaders will now convene to discuss the next steps. The report carried no Palace statement, no acceptance and no effective date. Speaking to reporters outside his home in Solo, Central Java, former president Joko Widodo said he would continue to support Prabowo's administration, especially programmes that serve the broader public.

Reuters reported on Wednesday that Gibran's aide Budi Arie Setiadi confirmed the vice president tendered his resignation to Prabowo earlier that day, the first on-record confirmation from Gibran's own office. Two sources at the parliamentary upper house told Reuters Gibran will submit his resignation letter to the chamber on Thursday morning. Senior law minister Yusril Ihza Mahendra set out the sequence that follows: the upper house convenes a session to declare a vacancy, writes to the president requesting two names for a replacement, and then holds an election within 60 days. Neither Gibran's office nor Prabowo's office responded to Reuters' request for comment, and Prabowo, speaking at length at a political party event late on Wednesday, did not mention the resignation. Bloomberg's report that Prabowo has accepted the resignation remains uncorroborated by any on-record statement.

The BBC reported on Wednesday that Gibran has submitted his resignation, citing an aide, in a report carried by MyJoyOnline. The BBC said it is not clear whether Prabowo has formally accepted the resignation, and its report carried no Palace confirmation and no effective date. The aide's account corroborates the submission at the centre of Bloomberg's report below, a resignation letter handed over at a Wednesday meeting, but it does not corroborate Bloomberg's further claim that Prabowo has accepted it, and the Palace has still announced neither an acceptance nor a vacancy.

Bloomberg reported on Wednesday that Prabowo has accepted Gibran's resignation, and that Gibran handed over his resignation letter in a meeting between the two men on Wednesday, citing unnamed people familiar with the matter. The report carries no Palace confirmation, no effective date and no named source, and it goes beyond the Palace's on-record position from Tuesday, when State Secretary Prasetyo Hadi said only that Prabowo had not rejected the intention and had offered advice. No other outlet had reported a resignation letter as of Wednesday morning Toronto time. If the Palace confirms an acceptance, the vice presidency falls vacant and the People's Consultative Assembly elects a successor from two candidates proposed by the president.

Parliament has also acted. Deputy Speaker Saan Mustopa announced on Wednesday that 120 members of the House of Representatives (DPR) have formally proposed a hak menyatakan pendapat (HMP), the House's right to express an opinion, concerning the impeachment of Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka over the diploma issue. The signatories span all eight party factions: PKS supplied 28, NasDem 19, PKB and the Democrats 18 each, PDIP 14, Golkar and Prabowo's own Gerindra 11 each, and PAN one. Faction representatives handed the proposal documents to the DPR leadership at a meeting on Wednesday, and Saan said the leadership has accepted the proposal under Article 211 of the MD3 Law and Article 192 of the DPR Rules of Procedure, to be followed up under the applicable mechanisms. No plenary vote or next date has been announced.

Indonesia's Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka told President Prabowo Subianto on Tuesday that he intends to resign, State Secretary Prasetyo Hadi told reporters at the Palace in Jakarta, confirming the most serious rupture yet inside the government the two men formed after the 2024 election. The two met for about two and a half hours on Tuesday morning. "During the discussion, the vice president expressed his intention to resign in light of the evolving situation," Prasetyo said, referring to recent Constitutional Court and Supreme Court decisions concerning the education documents Gibran submitted as a candidate.

Prasetyo denied reports that Prabowo rejected the intention outright, but stopped short of saying the president had accepted it. No written resignation has been announced, and there is no effective date or formal vacancy. Gibran asked for the opportunity to prove the validity of his documents through proceedings at the State Administrative Court, and Prabowo offered advice during the meeting, Prasetyo said. The Supreme Court recently annulled the validity of an equivalency certificate tied to Gibran's overseas secondary schooling, and the Constitutional Court separately found no convincing evidence that his overseas education met the minimum high-school qualification required to run for office.

The pressure had been building in parliament as well. On Tuesday, 50 members of the House of Representatives from six factions had backed the proposal to exercise the right to express an opinion on the diploma controversy; by Wednesday the filing carried 120 signatures from all eight factions, as reported above. Gibran, the eldest son of former president Joko Widodo, was the decisive asset in Prabowo's 2024 victory, joining the ticket after a controversial Constitutional Court ruling lowered the minimum age for vice-presidential candidates. A widening rift between Prabowo and Jokowi has since become one of the defining tensions of the administration.

For now, the stated position is an intention, not a departure. Whether Gibran files a formal resignation or first litigates the documents question at the administrative court decides the path: a voluntary resignation and a removal under Articles 7A and 7B of the 1945 Constitution are different processes, and the Palace has announced neither.
