Britain's prime minister has authorized an inquiry into the impact of nuclear weapons tests on military veterans after a campaign of about 40 years by survivors who say they were used as guinea pigs for tests that damaged their health and that of their children. Andy Burnham announced the independent panel on Tuesday night on the BBC's Newsnight programme, saying its chairman would be "relentless in his pursuit of the facts." An estimated 22,000 British military personnel witnessed Cold War era nuclear weapons tests in the Indian and Pacific oceans from 1952 to 1967.

"Nuclear test veterans and their families have waited too long for the truth, and that's exactly what the independent panel will establish," Burnham said. The panel will be chaired by Bishop James Jones, who previously led the inquiry into the 1989 Hillsborough disaster, and will follow the same model as the Hillsborough independent panel. It will examine the impact of the testing on veterans and their descendants and investigate allegations of a cover-up. The panel is expected to deliver an interim report before October 3, 2027, the 75th anniversary of Britain's first nuclear weapons test.

Veterans claim the Ministry of Defence intentionally hid data from blood and urine samples that would demonstrate how they were unwittingly used as test subjects to investigate the impact of nuclear explosions on humans, and campaigners link the tests to cancers, blood disorders and miscarriages suffered later. Defence Secretary Wes Streeting said he would not prejudge whether the move would lead to an apology or compensation from the government.

The campaign group LABRATS, which negotiated alongside other veteran organisations, said commitments had been secured on four areas: truth and accountability, a national memorial, education, and research into the health of veterans' descendants. "Crucially, for the first time, this process is being taken away from the Ministry of Defence and placed under the Cabinet Office," the group said. Status: panel established November 24, 2026; terms of reference still to be drafted with the veteran community at the centre.
