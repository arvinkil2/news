Seabridge Gold's KSM project has lost its substantially started designation after British Columbia's Supreme Court quashed the status, requiring the province to reconsider the designation. In 2024 the province's assessment officer ruled the project had substantially started, meaning it would keep its environmental approvals despite ongoing Indigenous and environmental challenges. In June 2026 the court ruled the province had breached its constitutional duty by failing to properly consult the Tsetsaut Skii km Lax Ha Nation (TSKLH).

Seabridge confirmed the ruling in a news release but said the mine's legal status has not changed. Chair and chief executive Rudy Fronk said the company is very confident KSM has more than met the requirements for a substantially started designation. In a separate filing, Seabridge welcomed a news release from the Nisga'a Treaty Nation and the Tahltan Nation stating their support for KSM and for its application for the designation, and said it expects that support to carry weight with the BC Environmental Assessment Office in its reconsideration.

TSKLH legal counsel Ryan Beaton said the decision affirms what the Nation has said for years, that Seabridge and the province have been misinterpreting and downplaying the court's original ruling. TSKLH has said it was not properly consulted on the environmental risks that toxic waste from KSM's proposed tailings management facility poses to waterways on its traditional territory, and it disputes Seabridge's characterization of the Tahltan and Nisga'a as the principal First Nations in the area.

Seabridge shares closed at $25.92 on Thursday, down 29.26 percent from $36.64 the previous day, and fell another 4.32 percent on Friday to $24.80, before regaining 12.10 percent on Monday morning in Toronto. The province must now complete the required consultation and reconsider the designation, and Seabridge says it will seek clarification on the timeline for a new determination.

Located 65 km northwest of Stewart, KSM is one of the world's largest undeveloped gold projects. A 2022 preliminary feasibility study estimated reserves totalling 47.3 million ounces of gold and 7.3 billion pounds of copper, with a net present value of $7.9 billion (C$11.35 billion).
