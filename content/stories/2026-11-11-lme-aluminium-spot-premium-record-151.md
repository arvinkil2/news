Spot aluminium on the London Metal Exchange traded $151 a ton above benchmark three-month futures on Wednesday, the highest premium in data spanning more than 30 years, up from $92 a ton on Monday and $94 a ton on Tuesday. LME inventories have fallen 56 percent this year to just under 230,000 tonnes, their lowest since 1990, and Reuters put available stocks at 207,525 tons on Tuesday, down 51 percent since the war on Iran began disrupting Middle East shipments in late February. Bloomberg reported that much of the remaining stock has come under the control of just two traders, which leaves shorts exposed into the November contract's expiry next week.
