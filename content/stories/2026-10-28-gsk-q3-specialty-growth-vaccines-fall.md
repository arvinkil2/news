GSK's third quarter sales rose 5 percent at constant exchange rates to 8.9 billion pounds, driven by specialty medicines, up 15 percent to 3.9 billion pounds, with HIV sales up 10 percent to 2.1 billion pounds and oncology up 23 percent. Vaccines went the other way, down 2 percent to 2.6 billion pounds: Shingrix fell 5 percent to 0.8 billion pounds and the RSV vaccine Arexvy fell 24 percent to 0.2 billion pounds, partly against tough comparisons a year earlier, while meningitis vaccine sales rose 12 percent. Core operating profit rose 5 percent to 3.1 billion pounds, but total operating profit fell 16 percent after 328 million pounds of major restructuring charges under the company's Accelerate Growth programme.

GSK reaffirmed its full year 2026 guidance at constant exchange rates, with turnover growth of 3 to 5 percent and core operating profit growth of 7 to 9 percent both expected at the upper half of their ranges, and core earnings per share growth of 7 to 9 percent at the lower half. The market focused on the vaccines decline and the restructuring bill: the shares fell as much as 7 percent in London before paring the loss to 3.8 percent, making GSK the second biggest faller on the FTSE 100 on the day, the Guardian reported.
