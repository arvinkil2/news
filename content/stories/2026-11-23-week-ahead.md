## Monday, November 23

No scheduled catalysts on the calendar. Markets reopen after a week in which the Kospi fell 7.95 percent. South Korea’s finance chiefs agreed at an emergency Sunday meeting to prepare the securities market stabilization fund for immediate activation if conditions warrant, and to relax listed companies’ daily share buyback limits for three months from November 24. Nothing on Monday’s calendar settles the week’s European votes or Thursday’s Bank of Korea decision.

## Tuesday, November 24

**Bundestag plenary vote on the 2027 federal budget (politics, with rates exposure).** The Bundestag votes in its sitting week of November 24 to 27 on the 2027 federal budget, after the budget committee approved the draft on November 12. Going in: the approved draft sets total spending at 560.9 billion euros, core borrowing of about 123 billion euros, total borrowing of about 210 billion euros and defence spending of 109.7 billion euros. The vote locks in Germany’s fiscal expansion path and its defence build-out, the largest single driver of euro-area issuance next year. A surprise counts as final borrowing totals materially above the committee draft, cuts or additions to the defence line, or visible coalition dissent that puts passage in doubt. Passage on the committee figures is the expected outcome. Most exposed: Bunds, the euro and German defence names.

## Wednesday, November 25

**US GDP second estimate, third quarter 2026 (rates).** The Bureau of Economic Analysis publishes the second estimate of third quarter GDP, adding corporate profits. Going in: the advance estimate, released October 29, put growth at a 3.4 percent annual rate, with real final sales to private domestic purchasers up 4.9 percent. The Federal Reserve is split after two hawkish dissents on October 28, so a revision to growth or profits feeds directly into the December rate decision. A surprise counts as a large headline revision in either direction, a material change to private domestic demand, or a profits figure that changes the earnings picture markets have priced. A small revision that leaves private demand near 4.9 percent is the expected outcome. Most exposed: Treasury yields, the dollar and December FOMC pricing.

## Thursday, November 26

**European Council summit, Brussels (politics, with rates exposure).** EU leaders meet on November 26 and 27. Going in: the agenda, per this morning’s politics brief, is Ukraine financing for 2027 and the 22nd EU sanctions package on Russia. The financing decision sets the scale and structure of EU support for next year, and a 22nd package would widen the compliance perimeter again after the November 16 sanctions decision. A surprise counts as a financing instrument that uses frozen Russian assets, a financing total well above or below the scale leaders have discussed, a sanctions package with a wider scope than expected, or a failure to reach unanimity. Most exposed: the euro, European defence and energy names, and Ukraine-linked financing.

**Bank of Korea final rate decision of 2026 (rates).** The Bank of Korea makes its final Monetary Policy Board decision of the year. Going in: the base rate was held unanimously at 3.00 percent on October 22, and Governor Shin signalled a tightening stance until inflation nears 2 percent in 2027, with consensus seeing a peak of 3.50 percent. Seoul enters the meeting after the Kospi’s 7.95 percent fall last week and Sunday’s emergency meeting on market stabilization. A surprise counts as a hike at this meeting, which would convert the tightening bias into action before year end, or guidance that drops the tightening bias. A hold that keeps the bias is the expected outcome. Watch the vote split and the updated growth and inflation forecasts. Most exposed: the Korean won, Korean government bonds and the Kospi.

## Friday, November 27

**New Zealand official election results declaration (politics).** The Electoral Commission declares the official results of the November 7 general election, including the roughly 700,000 special votes excluded from the preliminary count. Going in: the preliminary count was too close to call, with the National and Labour party votes tied near 29.3 percent and the governing bloc projected at 66 to 68 seats in a 129 to 131 seat Parliament. The declaration determines the final seat allocation and which coalition combinations are arithmetically possible, ahead of the writ being returned on December 3. A surprise counts as special votes moving a seat total enough to change the available coalition combinations, or first formal coalition statements that rule out a combination the arithmetic allows. Most exposed: the New Zealand dollar, New Zealand rates and coalition-formation risk.

**S&P France sovereign rating review (rates).** S&P holds a scheduled review of France’s sovereign rating. Going in: the review follows the October budget fight, with a deficit target of 5.0 percent of GDP for 2027 and a 54 billion euro adjustment, and an OAT-Bund spread that blew out to about 141 to 152 basis points, the widest since 2011, in reporting this site carried in October. A surprise counts as a downgrade, which would widen euro-area sovereign spreads into the weekend, or an outlook improvement, which would say the fiscal path is regaining credibility. Affirmation with the existing outlook is the expected outcome. Most exposed: OATs and euro-area sovereign spreads.

## Saturday, November 28

No scheduled catalysts on the calendar. Poll publication in Spain is banned from November 24 to 28 under Spanish electoral law, so the final day before the vote runs without fresh public numbers.

## Sunday, November 29

**Spain general election, snap (politics).** Spain votes in a snap general election called by Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez after the Congress of Deputies rejected two government housing decrees in October. Going in: most published polls give the People’s Party and Vox together more than the 176 seats needed for an absolute majority in the 350 seat Congress. The 40dB tracking poll for El Pais and Cadena SER put the right at 192 seats on November 16, with the PP at 31.5 percent, the Socialists at 30.3 percent and Vox at 18.0 percent, while Politico’s poll average has the Socialists pulling even with the PP on votes. A surprise counts as the right bloc falling short of 176 seats, which would reopen the 2023 pattern in which Sanchez assembled a majority after the vote, or a right bloc total well above 192 seats. The new Congress meets on December 23. Most exposed: Spanish sovereign bonds, the IBEX, the euro and EU fiscal-policy coordination.
