France's sovereign risk premium hit a level not seen since the euro-area debt crisis — and then kept going. The spread between 10-year OATs and German Bunds reached 149.17 basis points on Friday, according to LSEG, the highest since January 2012, while the French 10-year yield touched about 4.935 percent and briefly topped 5 percent last week — its highest since 2002. German Bunds, by contrast, rallied on safe-haven flow to 3.46 percent: the divergence is France-specific.

The trigger was the 2027 budget. The government's €43 billion package of cuts and cost savings "did not go down well with investors," in MUFG's Derek Halpenny's phrase — the spread has nearly doubled from ~84bp in early September. On Monday, French yields stayed elevated near their multiyear highs even as U.S. Treasuries eased on the soft payrolls print. And the fiscal story now has a street component: up to 500 high schools closed Monday in the second week of student protests, with a nationwide day of demonstrations called for Tuesday, 24 schools burned or ransacked and ~5,000 arrested.

The milestones keep stacking. Investors now demand more compensation to hold French debt than Italian debt — a reversal of the hierarchy that defined the 2010s. Vanguard called France a "degrading credit" in the Financial Times. This is fiscal-risk repricing, not ECB-policy repricing: with ECB accounts due Thursday and October 29 hike odds at just 26 percent despite 3.8 percent inflation, Lagarde's doctrine that long-end yields are doing the ECB's tightening work has its clearest evidence — at a cost measured in sovereign credibility. France's 2027 Budget Act is presented Thursday with a 5.0 percent of GDP deficit target.
