Asif Merchant, 48, of Pakistan, was sentenced on Tuesday in federal court in Brooklyn to 330 months in prison, 27 years and six months, by US District Judge Eric R. Komitee, for attempting to commit an act of terrorism transcending national boundaries and murder for hire. The Justice Department describes Merchant as a trained operative of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, convicted by a federal jury in March 2026 of traveling to the United States to arrange political assassinations on American soil. By his own admission at trial, Merchant joined the IRGC, was recruited in about 2022 and met a handler in Tehran about 15 times, then flew to the United States in April 2024 and paid $5,000 to purported hitmen who were in fact undercover FBI agents, after an acquaintance he approached reported him to the FBI.

Merchant believed the ultimate target of the plot would be Donald J. Trump, the department said, and stated that other targets could have been then President Joe Biden and former UN Ambassador Nikki Haley; he admitted the plot was motivated by Iran's desire to avenge the January 2020 US killing of IRGC commander Qasem Soleimani. Attorney General Todd Blanche said an Iranian agent who came to the United States to coordinate an assassination attempt against President Trump has been justly sentenced. Tehran has denied targeting Trump or other US officials or plotting attacks against them, Deutsche Welle reported.
