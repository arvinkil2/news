The European Commission accepted legally binding commitments from Sanofi on Wednesday to end its investigation into whether the French drugmaker disparaged Fluad, the flu vaccine CSL Seqirus makes for older patients, in France and Germany, where it competes with Sanofi's own Efluelda. Under the pledges, which run to 2030, Sanofi will not use material aimed at healthcare professionals in either country to criticise national vaccination recommendations or to present Efluelda as safer than its rival, and over the next two years it will publish a statement on its French and German websites clarifying that national recommendations treat the two vaccines as equivalent for elderly people. Accepting the commitments spares Sanofi formal charges and a possible fine of up to 10 percent of global annual turnover.
