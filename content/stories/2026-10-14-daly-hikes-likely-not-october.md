San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly told Reuters in an interview on Wednesday that the Federal Reserve will probably need to raise short-term rates further to rein in inflation that remains too high, but she signalled there is no need to do so at the October 27 to 28 meeting, saying the timing of further adjustments is still being deliberated and there is no urgency to decide on a limited set of information. Daly linked the September headline inflation reading of 3.7 percent mainly to gasoline prices, described core inflation as sticky in line with her expectations, and said a stable labour market lets the Fed concentrate on price stability. Earlier on Wednesday, Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee took a more anxious tone in a Yahoo Finance interview reported by Reuters, calling the September CPI data discouraging and pointing to services inflation above 5 percent showing little sign of improvement, while stopping short of prescribing a near-term policy response.
