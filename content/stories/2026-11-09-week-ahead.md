## Monday, November 9

**FOMC minutes, October 27 to 28 meeting (rates).** The Federal Reserve publishes the minutes of the meeting that held the target range at 3.75 to 4.00 percent, the first account of how close the decision was and of the discussion that set up December. Going in: futures price a December hike at about 77 to 78 percent after Friday's payrolls beat, up from about two thirds on Thursday, and the 10 year Treasury yield closed Friday at 4.86 percent. The October hold drew dissents in favour of a hike, so the minutes are read for the size of that camp and for any stated condition for moving in December. A surprise counts as language that makes December conditional on a soft CPI print, which would cut hike pricing before Tuesday's release, or language that treats a hike as the default unless growth breaks, which would firm it. Most exposed: US Treasuries, the dollar and December rate pricing.

## Tuesday, November 10

**US CPI, October (rates).** The Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes October consumer prices at 8:30 a.m. Eastern, the week's largest release and the last CPI the Fed sees before December 8 to 9. Going in: consensus is headline inflation up 0.3 percent on the month, down from 0.6 percent in September, and core up 0.2 percent, down from 0.3 percent. The Cleveland Fed nowcast is lower, at 0.24 percent headline and 0.21 percent core, and RBC is at 0.3 percent for both. No annual rate will be published for the main indexes, because October 2025 prices were not collected during the government shutdown, so the monthly figures carry the whole signal. A surprise counts as core at 0.4 percent or above, which would push December hike pricing toward certainty and lift the front end, or headline and core both at 0.1 percent or below, which would reopen the hold case. Most exposed: US Treasuries across the curve, the dollar and equity index futures.

**Reserve Bank of Australia meeting minutes, November 3 (rates).** The RBA publishes the minutes of the meeting that raised the cash rate to 4.85 percent, its fifth increase this year. Going in: the November 3 decision followed September inflation at 4.3 percent year on year, above forecast, with the quarterly trimmed mean up 1.0 percent against the bank's August forecast of 0.8 percent, and a labour market that lost 39,300 jobs in September. The minutes are read for the December discussion and for how the board weighed the jobs fall against the inflation miss. A surprise counts as an explicit December hike signal, which would lift the Australian dollar and the front end, or language that frames November as the last move, which would do the opposite. Most exposed: the Australian dollar, Australian government bonds and December RBA pricing. The RBA decides next on December 8 to 9.

## Wednesday, November 11

**Bank of Japan Summary of Opinions, October 29 to 30 meeting (rates).** The Bank of Japan publishes the summary from the meeting that held the overnight call target at 1.25 percent and pointed to December 18 as the likely date of the next increase. Going in: JGB yields sit at multi-decade highs, the yen closed the week near 158 to the dollar on this site's rates reporting, and the December meeting is the priced event, with the bank's Bond Market Group meetings on December 7 to 8 as the last sounding before it. A surprise counts as opinions that open a January or later path instead, which would ease December pricing and press the yen, or a larger group in favour of moving sooner. Most exposed: JGBs, the yen, global term premia and carry trades.

**China CPI and PPI, October (rates).** The National Bureau of Statistics publishes October consumer and producer prices, a paired release that has split wide this autumn. Going in: Lundgreen's preview expects consumer inflation to ease to 0.8 percent year on year from 0.9 percent in September, with prices flat on the month, while producer inflation rises to 5.2 percent from 5.0 percent, with monthly growth slowing to 0.4 percent from 1.2 percent. September producer inflation was the highest since June 2022 on energy and chemical costs, while food prices held consumer inflation down, so the gap between the two series is the release. A surprise counts as consumer inflation at or above 1.2 percent, which would say demand is firmer than the September miss suggested, or producer inflation below 4.5 percent, which would say the energy pass-through is already fading. Most exposed: the offshore yuan, Chinese equities, industrial metals and the PBoC easing path.

## Thursday, November 12

**US retail sales and PPI, October (rates).** The Census Bureau and the Bureau of Labor Statistics publish October retail sales and producer prices on the same morning, the demand and pipeline test two days after CPI. Going in: retail sales consensus is about 0.4 percent on the month, and producer prices are expected up 0.4 percent on the month after September's 1.3 percent jump, with the annual rate seen near 6.6 percent, up from 6.3 percent. Sales are reported in current prices, so part of any gain is price rather than volume, and the control group is the cleaner read on consumption. A surprise counts as a second producer print above 0.8 percent, which would confirm pipeline pressure after CPI, or a fall in the control group, which would cut against the payrolls picture of a firm economy. Most exposed: US Treasuries, the dollar and consumer shares.

**OPEC Monthly Oil Market Report (commodities).** OPEC publishes its monthly report with the secretariat's 2027 demand forecast and its first full read on October compliance, a week after the November 1 meeting held December quotas. Going in: Brent closed Friday at 98.32 dollars, down 16.42 percent on the week, WTI at 90.83 dollars, and money managers cut 78,492 lots of combined net length in the week ended November 3, before the election day fall. Quotas have been paper-only while Gulf exports run below normal, so the compliance and export figures matter more than the headline demand number. A surprise counts as a cut to the 2027 demand forecast, which would extend the crude selloff, or compliance data showing exports recovering faster than the quota path, which would press diesel and tanker rates as well. Most exposed: Brent and WTI, product cracks and tanker freight.

**EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report (commodities).** The Energy Information Administration publishes weekly US crude, gasoline and distillate stocks, delayed a day by the Veterans Day holiday. Going in: US distillate stocks sat at a 2003 low on the last print on this site's commodities reporting, diesel margins have been rising with freight costs, and the crude tape is still absorbing last week's 16 percent Brent fall. A surprise counts as a distillate draw that takes stocks to a new low for the cycle, which would lift gasoil cracks into winter, or a large crude build, which would say the price fall is reaching physical balances. Most exposed: diesel and gasoil, refining margins and product tanker demand.

## Friday, November 13

**USDA WASDE and Crop Production (commodities).** The Department of Agriculture publishes the World Agricultural Supply and Demand Estimates with updated US corn, soybean and wheat ending stocks and the South American outlook, the first WASDE since the November harvest updates. Going in: Friday's closes were wheat at 591.2 cents, corn at 441.5 cents and soybeans at 1,193.8 cents, and the report follows two weeks of flash sales to China recorded on this site. A surprise counts as a cut to US soybean ending stocks on export demand, which would extend the soybean bid, or a larger corn carryout than the harvest data implied, which would press corn and wheat together. Most exposed: corn, soybeans and wheat futures, and fertilizer and farm equipment shares.

**IEA Oil Market Report (commodities).** The International Energy Agency publishes its monthly report with 2027 demand and supply balances and any revision to its estimate of the Hormuz supply loss, one day after OPEC's report, so the two agencies' balances can be read against each other. Going in: the agency's previous report had trimmed 2026 demand growth, tanker freight remains far above year ago levels on the Gulf Coast to Asia route, and Egypt has been deferring LNG cargoes at current prices. A surprise counts as a larger Hormuz loss estimate, which would rebuild part of the risk premium crude lost last week, or a 2027 surplus large enough to cap any recovery. Most exposed: Brent and WTI, LNG pricing and tanker freight.

**ECB Monetary Policy Account, October 29 meeting (rates).** The European Central Bank publishes the account of the meeting that held the deposit facility at 2.50 percent, with the staff discussion of French spreads and the December decision as the items markets will trade. Going in: the Bank last raised in September, a Reuters poll reported on this site points to December for the next move, and French bonds remain the pressure point in the euro area curve. A surprise counts as an account that records a live December hike discussion with named support, which would lift the euro and peripheral yields, or language that treats higher long yields as a substitute for a hike, which would do the opposite. Most exposed: Bunds, French and peripheral spreads and the euro.

## Saturday, November 14

**Nvidia GTC keynote (technology).** Jensen Huang delivers the GTC keynote, the company's first full roadmap update in this calendar's framing since its September quarter earnings, three days before its third quarter results on November 17. Going in: Nvidia shares closed Friday at 186.64 dollars, up 2.78 percent on the day but down 8.8 percent on the week, with semiconductors down 13.13 percent on the week after the post-election selloff. Investors are watching for the Rubin ramp schedule, networking and sovereign AI orders, and for any demand figure that tests the order visibility Huang has cited at previous events. A surprise counts as a roadmap delay or a demand figure below the level the market carries into earnings, either of which would extend the semiconductor selloff into Tuesday's results, or a new platform or sovereign order large enough to reset it. Most exposed: Nvidia, semiconductor shares, data centre suppliers and AI infrastructure credit.

## Sunday, November 15

No scheduled catalysts on the calendar. The week closes with December pricing for the Federal Reserve set by Tuesday's CPI and Monday's minutes, and with Nvidia's earnings on November 17 as the next dated test after Saturday's keynote.

## Also on the tape

The calendar carries twelve catalysts, but the week's supply and earnings calendar is wider. The US Treasury sells 125 billion dollars of refunding debt: 58 billion dollars of three year notes on Monday, 42 billion dollars of ten year notes on Tuesday, the same session as CPI, and 25 billion dollars of thirty year bonds on Thursday, all settling November 16. Tuesday also brings the Bank of Canada summary of deliberations, Copom and Riksbank minutes and Japan's thirty year auction, and CoreWeave reports after the close. Cisco and Applied Materials report on Thursday. Friday's CFTC positioning release is the first to capture the November 4 crude collapse in full. Monday brings Philippine third quarter GDP, with a median poll of 2.7 percent, and 55 billion pesos of bill auctions, and the ITU plenipotentiary conference opens the same day. US bond markets are closed on Wednesday for Veterans Day while equities stay open, which thins trading around the BoJ and China releases.
