Australia has imposed targeted Magnitsky-style human rights sanctions and travel bans on four Sudanese individuals and targeted financial sanctions on one entity, Foreign Minister Penny Wong announced on November 5. The measures name the leaders of both warring parties: Abdel Fattah Abdelrahman al-Burhan, commander in chief of the Sudanese Armed Forces, and Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo Mousa, commander of the Rapid Support Forces. Dabanga reported the other two individuals as Al-Tijani Al-Tahir Karshoum, the RSF-aligned de facto governor of West Darfur, and RSF field commander Hussein Barsham, with financial sanctions on the El Bara Bin Malik Brigade, an armed group aligned with the army.

Wong said the two forces have committed serious human rights violations and abuses, including attacks on civilians, torture, sexual and gender-based violence and extrajudicial killings, and that those responsible must be held to account. The sanctions build on Australian measures against 13 individuals under the UN Security Council's Sudan framework, and Canberra issued a class humanitarian permit so relief work is not caught by the restrictions. Australia says hundreds of thousands of people have been killed and more than 14 million displaced since the war began on April 15, 2023, and that it has provided more than $67 million in humanitarian assistance.
