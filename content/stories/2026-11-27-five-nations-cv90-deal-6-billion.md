BAE Systems said it has been awarded contracts worth a total of $6 billion in a joint procurement with Finland, Lithuania, the Netherlands, Norway and Sweden for the delivery of more than 400 CV90 combat vehicles. The agreement covers the CV9035 MkIV Nordic Edition and was awarded under separate purchase order agreements that refer to the same framework agreement defining the vehicle configuration.

BAE called the deal a world first, five nations procuring a major defence item together under one agreement. The programme also includes technical publications, training, spare parts, special tools and test equipment. A distributed supplier network in the participating nations will produce the vehicles and components, which the company said transfers technical knowledge and strengthens security of supply for infantry fighting vehicles in the region.

Finland's share is the only national portion detailed so far. Finnish Defence Minister Antti Hakkanen said on November 26 that Finland will buy 160 vehicles for 2.8 billion euros, with an option for up to 100 additional vehicles and spare parts, Reuters reported. The Finnish vehicles will begin entering service in 2030 and will gradually replace Finland's Soviet-made BMP-2 infantry fighting vehicles.

The common configuration is the point of the structure. BAE said a shared vehicle across the five armies creates synergies in development, spare parts, logistics and sustainment, and makes crew transitions and maintenance simpler between the participating forces. Tommy Gustafsson-Rask, general manager at BAE Systems Hägglunds, said the procurement underscores the trust placed in the CV90 family, now expanded with Lithuania, as the backbone of modern mechanized forces in the region.
