CrowdStrike and Zscaler said on Monday they have signed a definitive agreement under which CrowdStrike will acquire Zscaler in an all-stock transaction with an enterprise value of about $43.9 billion, based on CrowdStrike's closing price on November 20. Zscaler shareholders will receive 1.135 CrowdStrike shares for each Zscaler share, an implied value of about $260.41 per share and a premium of roughly 34 percent to Zscaler's last close. Both boards approved the deal unanimously. Existing CrowdStrike shareholders are expected to own about 84.1 percent of the combined company, and Zscaler shareholders about 15.9 percent.

The transaction is expected to close in the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal 2028, subject to approval by Zscaler shareholders and required regulatory approvals. Jay Chaudhry, Zscaler's founder and chief executive, and his affiliates, holding about 34 percent of Zscaler's shares, have signed voting agreements in support. After the close, Chaudhry will keep leading the Zscaler business and join CrowdStrike's board. The two companies will operate independently until then.

The strategic logic is consolidation at the two control points of corporate networks. CrowdStrike's Falcon platform secures endpoints, workloads and identities; Zscaler's Zero Trust Exchange sits inline on the connections between users, applications and, increasingly, AI agents, processing more than 750 billion transactions a day across more than 200 locations. CrowdStrike said the combined company will start with more than $10 billion of annual recurring revenue, of which Zscaler contributes $3.9 billion, and estimated the acquisition expands its addressable market by more than $185 billion in calendar 2029. About 8,000 Zscaler customers do not yet buy from CrowdStrike, and the two already share more than 2,000 customers. CrowdStrike targeted revenue synergies reaching about $1 billion in annualized run rate within 30 months of closing, and $500 million of annualized cost synergies within 18 to 24 months, and said the deal is immediately accretive to free cash flow per share before any synergies.

The announcement came with CrowdStrike's third-quarter fiscal 2027 results, which the company called the best quarter in its history. Revenue rose 28 percent to $1.58 billion, annual recurring revenue grew 27 percent to $6.26 billion, and net new ARR of $419.2 million was a record. Cash flow from operations reached $665.2 million and free cash flow $474 million, both records. CrowdStrike raised its full-year guidance, to total revenue of $6.08 billion and ending ARR of about $6.71 billion.

Zscaler's own results, published the same morning in its Form 8-K, showed the business CrowdStrike is buying still growing at about 20 percent. Revenue for the quarter ended October 31, 2026 rose 20 percent to $949.3 million, annual recurring revenue grew 21 percent to $3,882 million, and net new ARR was $111 million. Operating cash flow was a record $541.0 million and free cash flow $416.5 million, while the GAAP net loss widened to $27.4 million from $11.6 million a year earlier and non-GAAP net income rose to $208.4 million.

Investors marked the two stocks in opposite directions. Zscaler shares jumped more than 13 percent in premarket trading on Monday while CrowdStrike fell about 5 percent, according to Reuters, the standard pattern for a large all-stock acquisition at a full premium. Jefferies, which had assessed the rumoured combination last week, said it would make CrowdStrike a more formidable competitor to Palo Alto Networks across several categories, while flagging that a deal of this size would draw regulatory scrutiny. That scrutiny, and the shareholder vote, are now the gating items between announcement and close.
