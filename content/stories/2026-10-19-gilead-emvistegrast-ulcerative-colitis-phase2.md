Gilead Sciences has put the full Phase 2 SWIFT dataset for emvistegrast on the record at United European Gastroenterology Week in Barcelona, and it confirms and extends the result STAT first reported on October 19. At the 200 milligram once daily dose, 31 percent of patients with moderately to severely active ulcerative colitis achieved clinical remission at week 12, against 8 percent on placebo, a 23 point placebo adjusted difference. Clinical response, the trial's main goal, was reached by 66 percent on the drug against 37 percent on placebo.

Emvistegrast (GS-1427) is an oral selective alpha4beta7 integrin inhibitor, in effect the gut selective mechanism of Takeda's injectable Entyvio (vedolizumab) in pill form. Endpoints News noted that the 31 percent remission rate sits above the remission rates Entyvio itself posted in its own ulcerative colitis trials, with the standing caveat that cross trial comparisons are not head to head evidence. The drug was generally well tolerated, with overall side effect rates comparable to placebo and no serious side effects linked to the study medication, Reuters reported.

The forward commitment is the new commercial fact. Gilead said it plans to start a late stage monotherapy study of the pill in 2027. BMO Capital Markets analysts called the data "an incremental positive for Gilead's inflammatory strategy that establishes emvistegrast as a credible oral alpha4beta7 option," in a field that also includes Abivax's oral obefazimod.

The sourcing note from yesterday's edition is now closed. The October 19 story ran on STAT's interview based account alone and was graded C with the limitation flagged. The UEG presentation and Reuters' report of the company's figures supply the second independent source and the full response, remission and safety numbers, so the story is regraded B. What is still missing is the peer reviewed or fully published dataset, including endoscopic outcomes and the lower dose arms.
