The US Food and Drug Administration on Monday approved Wilserdly (giredestrant), a once-daily oral tablet used alone after surgery in adults with estrogen receptor-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer, the most common subtype of the disease. Wilserdly, an oral estrogen receptor antagonist that blocks estrogen signalling and breaks down the receptors, is the first new hormone therapy for early-stage breast cancer in more than 20 years, the FDA said. The approval, granted to Genentech, a member of the Roche Group, covers stage I, II or III disease at intermediate to high risk of recurrence following surgery.

In the randomized lidERA Breast Cancer trial of 4,170 adults, Wilserdly reduced the risk of invasive breast cancer recurrence or death by 30 percent compared with standard hormone therapy (tamoxifen or an aromatase inhibitor), with 140 patients on Wilserdly experiencing an event against 196 on standard therapy. At three years, 92.4 percent of patients taking Wilserdly were alive and free of invasive breast cancer, against 89.6 percent on standard therapy. The FDA granted the application Priority Review, and overall survival results are still being collected under a postmarketing commitment.
