The Bank of Korea's Monetary Policy Board held the base rate at 3.00 percent on Thursday, in line with market expectations, Yonhap reported. The board maintained its tightening stance but paused to assess the effect of consecutive 25 basis point hikes in July and August, which had lifted the rate from 2.50 percent, while consumer inflation held at 2.9 percent in September, above the bank's 2 percent target, and growth outperformed on semiconductor exports.

Attention now turns to the year's final meeting on November 26, with markets expecting a possible further hike and a possible upward revision to the bank's 3.3 percent growth forecast for 2026. The next inputs are the third quarter GDP advance estimate on October 27 and October inflation on November 2, and a sharply stronger won, which touched its firmest intraday level since October 2024 on Wednesday, was among the factors Yonhap cited as reducing the urgency of a move this month.
