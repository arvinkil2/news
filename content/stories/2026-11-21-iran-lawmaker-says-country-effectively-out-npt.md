## Latest

Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Iran's nuclear doctrine has not changed and its official positions remain the same as before, in an interview with the state-run Iran Daily newspaper published on Saturday and reported by Agence France-Presse. He said the doctrine question is broad and serious, with supporters and opponents inside Iran, and that China and Russia also oppose a change in Tehran's doctrine. The remarks are the government's public line against calls, mostly from hard-liners since the wars with Israel and the United States, to shift the doctrine toward developing a nuclear weapon as a deterrent. Iran continues to bar IAEA inspectors from its nuclear sites, citing security concerns.

Abolfazl Zohrevand, a member of the National Security and Foreign Policy Committee of Iran's Majlis, told a TASS correspondent that Iran had in fact ceased to be a member of the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty and of the International Atomic Energy Agency after strikes on its nuclear facilities and the killing of its nuclear scientists, and that Iranian public opinion fully supports withdrawal. He said Iran had been pushed out of both bodies and did not need to announce anything, presenting the position as his own assessment of where matters stand. The TASS report carries no formal withdrawal decision or announcement by the Iranian government.
