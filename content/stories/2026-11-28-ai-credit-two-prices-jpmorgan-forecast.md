The US investment-grade market is pricing two different worlds at once. The ICE BofA US Corporate index carried an option-adjusted spread of 98 basis points on November 24, according to Federal Reserve data, barely wider than the 93 basis points of November 18. Oracle, which still holds an investment-grade rating, saw its five-year credit default swaps reach 356 basis points, a record high and up from under 80 basis points a year ago, while its bonds yielded about 9 percent this week. CoreWeave's five-year default insurance costs 1,534 basis points, implying more than a 70 percent probability of default within five years.

The supply behind the repricing is set to grow. JPMorgan strategists projected on November 25 that companies will sell $450 billion of investment-grade bonds in 2027 to fund artificial intelligence, up from $368 billion this year, with AI capital spending expected to nearly double to $1.4 trillion in 2027 before flattening in 2028. The Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis reported on November 10 that borrowing linked to advanced computing made up 31 percent of US nonfinancial investment-grade issuance, against under 6 percent three years earlier.

The primary market showed the strain at the top of the credit scale this week, not the bottom. CDP Infrastructures, the financing entity of La Caisse, paused a 1 billion Canadian dollar five-year sale despite an AA rating from Morningstar DBRS, after the spread it would have had to pay widened by at least five basis points, and Canadian corporate spreads reached their widest in a year. In Europe, Deutsche Pfandbriefbank postponed a sterling bond, the first pulled high-grade deal of 2026 in that market; GlobalCapital reported that the crossover index widened 20 basis points between the deal's announcement and its withdrawal, and the lead managers said the backdrop worked against them.

The divide is also visible inside the index itself. PIMCO noted on November 18 that Amazon, Alphabet, Meta and Nvidia make up about 35 percent of the AA-rated segment of the US corporate index, while the extra spread available in BBBs over AAs is the thinnest on record, even though Oracle is the largest issuer in that BBB segment. The practical consequence is that an investment-grade fund is now, in part, an AI fund, and the index average no longer describes the price the marginal borrower pays.
