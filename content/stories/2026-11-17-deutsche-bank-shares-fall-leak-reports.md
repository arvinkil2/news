Deutsche Bank shares fell 5.2 percent in early trade in Frankfurt on Tuesday after the bank responded to a series of media reports based on leaked internal documents, saying the incidents reported date back many years and that it had improved its controls. "The matters date back many years, in some cases as much as two decades," the bank said in a statement, adding that it is a different bank today and pointing to record profits in 2025 and recent quarters under chief executive Christian Sewing.

The reports, published under the name Inside Deutsche Bank, rest on a leak of more than 15 million files, including more than 10 million emails, internal investigations, board minutes and compliance records covering the bank's activities from the early 2010s onward. Whistleblowers passed the material through a lawyer to journalists Josy Joseph of India and Khadija Sharife of South Africa, and more than 200 journalists at more than 50 outlets, including Der Spiegel, the Financial Times, the New York Times and Le Monde, have been evaluating it since early 2026 in an investigation coordinated by the South African non-profit Barracuda Media. The consortium says the files show the bank repeatedly missed warning signs of possible money laundering and sanctions violations, gave powerful clients preferential treatment and let business considerations override its own compliance regime, including in its courting of Russian clients who ended up on sanctions lists after the 2022 invasion of Ukraine.

Among the specific findings reported by the Financial Times and other consortium members, the bank flagged 582 million dollars in transactions linked to Epstein in suspicious activity reports filed with US regulators in 2019, and in 2017 a client relationship manager mistakenly sent the year-end tax statements of 1,586 accounts, including accounts belonging to Donald Trump, to another client's tax adviser, who alerted the bank and deleted the file. Deutsche Bank said it "acknowledges its error of onboarding Epstein in 2013 and regrets our historical connection with him," and said it had cooperated with regulators, law enforcement agencies and the US Congress.

The bank said it had invested 7 billion euros in its control environment between 2019 and 2025, and that "given the legacy nature of these matters, any potential financial impact is not expected to be meaningful, if there is any impact at all." The whistleblowers' lawyer confirmed they also provided material to the US Department of Justice, the US Financial Crimes Enforcement Network and Britain's HM Revenue and Customs. Deutsche Bank has paid more than 20 billion dollars in penalties and settlements since 2000 and operated under US deferred prosecution agreements in 2015 and 2021, and its offices have been raided at least four times this year, according to the consortium.
