South Korea's Defense Ministry on Friday proposed military talks with North Korea to mutually confirm the precise location of the border and discuss restoring communication channels, Deputy Minister for National Defense Policy Kim Hong-cheol announced, with three-way talks involving the United Nations Command proposed as an alternative and no date or venue set. The proposal follows a landmine blast in the demilitarized zone in September that injured three South Korean soldiers: Seoul's military concluded North Korea buried mines south of the Military Demarcation Line, a finding Pyongyang rejected, and Kim said North Korean troops building roads and fences and laying mines in the zone have repeatedly crossed the line. A similar proposal last November drew no response, and the offer lands days after Kim Yo Jong said North Korea had moved new troops into firing position along the border and would put border loudspeakers on standby, while South Korea's Joint Chiefs chairman Jin Yong-sung ordered a firm response if the North used Seoul's actions as a pretext.
