China's two largest rare earth producers more than doubled their profits in the first nine months of 2026, Mining.com reported with files from Reuters. China Northern Rare Earth, the country's biggest light rare earth firm, reported net profit of 3.65 billion yuan, and heavy rare earth producer China Rare Earth Resources and Technology reported 408 million yuan, with both set to beat analysts' full year estimates on the report.

The gains were attributed to tighter supply controls and rising prices, including a 62 percent rise in neodymium-praseodymium prices since controls were first introduced, to 719,000 yuan. The two companies were the only producers given production quotas in 2024, and in 2025 Beijing extended the quota system to imported materials and required regular reporting on strategic mineral flows.
