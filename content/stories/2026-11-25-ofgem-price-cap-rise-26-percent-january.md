Ofgem on Wednesday set the energy price cap for 1 January to 31 March 2027 at a level 26 percent above the current quarter, taking the annualised figure for a typical household paying by direct debit for gas and electricity from 1,723 pounds to 2,166 pounds. The regulator attributed the increase to the continued conflict in the Middle East pushing up international gas prices, and said gas wholesale prices have roughly tripled since the conflict started, driving a 33 percent rise in gas bills in the January cap against October, while electricity bills rise 19 percent.

The cap affects around 20 million households on standard variable tariffs, while around 10 million households on fixed tariffs are unaffected while their deals run. Ofgem said the government has also announced that around 20 million households will receive 100 pounds towards their energy bill from January, and the regulator has written to suppliers, networks and brokers reminding them of their obligations to support customers who are struggling this winter.
