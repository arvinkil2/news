Diamondback Energy and KKR announced on Tuesday an agreement to jointly acquire Five Point Infrastructure's 70 percent stake in Deep Blue Midland Basin, the full-cycle water infrastructure platform in the Midland Basin formed as a joint venture between Diamondback and Five Point in 2023. Diamondback already holds the remaining 30 percent, so the two buyers will jointly own 100 percent of Deep Blue, which manages more than 2.2 million barrels of water per day across sourcing, transportation, storage, recycling and disposal. KKR's investment will be funded primarily through KKR Global Infrastructure Investors V, financial terms were not disclosed, and the transaction is expected to close in the fourth quarter of 2026, subject to regulatory approvals.

Update, November 13: Reuters, citing a person familiar with the transaction, and Bloomberg, citing its own source, put the cash consideration for Five Point's 70 percent stake at about 2.1 billion dollars, implying a value of about 3 billion dollars for all of Deep Blue. Truist Securities estimated the split at about 602 million dollars from Diamondback to lift its holding from 30 to 50 percent and about 1.51 billion dollars from KKR for the other 50 percent, assuming the same pro rata price. The companies themselves have not disclosed terms.
