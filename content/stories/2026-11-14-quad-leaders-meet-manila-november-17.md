**Latest.** President Trump flies into Manila on Tuesday afternoon for the Quad leaders meeting, his first, two years after the group last met at leaders level, Reuters reported from Manila. He will skip the East Asia Summit, join a US-ASEAN "Special Summit" and meet Philippine President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr. on Wednesday, and is also expected to hold talks on Tuesday with Thai Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul, with a joint statement expected on reciprocal trade negotiations. At the ASEAN summit on Monday, Chinese Premier Li Qiang hailed what he called "truly extraordinary" China-ASEAN economic achievements and committed Beijing to free trade. A Japanese foreign ministry official said last week the Quad leaders were expected to show unity, with maritime security, export restrictions, critical minerals supply chain diversification and AI among the potential discussion points.

Leaders of the United States, Japan, Australia and India are expected to meet in Manila on Tuesday, November 17, shortly after President Trump arrives on the last day of the ASEAN summit, two people familiar with the planning told Reuters. It would be the first Quad leaders meeting since the four met in Delaware in September 2024, and the first since President Trump returned to office. Critical minerals supply is expected on the agenda, along with free and unimpeded commercial shipping lanes for energy supplies and attacks on commercial ships, and the meeting follows the Quad foreign ministers' October 31 talks in Atlanta, where they agreed to respond jointly to China's control of critical minerals supply chains.
