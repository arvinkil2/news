The FBI is investigating an apparent breach of the National Crime Information Center, the shared database that connects federal, state and local law enforcement, according to two people briefed on the matter cited by NBC News, with CNN reporting the intrusion was discovered in recent days. The bureau had no comment, and it is not known how the intruders got in or whether records were taken. The reports follow a cyberattack earlier in the week on a Department of Agriculture system used to process payments to farmers, which the department disclosed to employees on Thursday.

Update, November 22: the Associated Press reported on Saturday that the FBI is investigating an apparent hack of the database, confirmed by a person familiar with the matter who was not authorized to speak by name, and the bureau again declined to comment. The NCIC holds records on fugitives, missing persons, criminal history data and stolen property. President Donald Trump addressed the reports in a Saturday interview with Fox News, saying, "We just heard about it and I will say that it's not good," while adding that as a result of the breach "maybe we'll learn things that we would have never learned otherwise because, you know, you had the Russia, Russia, Russia hoax." A former FBI official told CNN the concern is not only whether hackers gained access to sensitive information but whether they could compromise the accuracy of the information officers rely on. In September, the FBI reportedly told agents and support staff that their personal information had been stolen in a cyberattack, which would make this the second reported intrusion involving an FBI system in recent months.
