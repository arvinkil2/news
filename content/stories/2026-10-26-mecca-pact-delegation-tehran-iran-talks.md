Top officials from the three Mecca Pact countries, Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan, met Iran's leaders in Tehran on Monday in their first high-level contact since the kingdom became a pressure point in the wider Iran conflict, Reuters reported. Saudi Arabia was represented by Defence Minister Prince Khalid bin Salman, Pakistan by army chief Field Marshal Asim Munir and Turkey by intelligence chief Ibrahim Kalin, and Iran's foreign ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei said the regional security discussions would include the repercussions of US and Israeli military action against Iran, developments in the Palestinian territories and Lebanon.

The meeting, proposed during Munir's visit to Tehran last week and described by Saudi deputy minister Rayed Krimly as part of the pact countries' efforts to de-escalate the crisis, comes as Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia have spiralled and both Washington and Tehran have drawn up plans to restart the wider war, possibly after the November 3 US midterms. No outcome from the talks had been reported as of Monday morning.
