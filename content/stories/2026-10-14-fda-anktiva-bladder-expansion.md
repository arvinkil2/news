The Food and Drug Administration on Wednesday approved an expanded indication for nogapendekin alfa inbakicept-pmln (Anktiva, from Altor BioScience, an ImmunityBio subsidiary), used with BCG, for adults with BCG-unresponsive non-muscle invasive bladder cancer. The label previously covered patients whose disease included carcinoma in situ, with or without papillary tumours; it now also covers patients with high-grade papillary-only tumours, making Anktiva the first treatment approved specifically for that group.

Unusually, the expansion rests on evidence borrowed from the related subtype. The supporting trial, cohort A of QUILT-3.032, enrolled carcinoma in situ patients and produced a complete response rate of 62 percent, with 58 percent of responses lasting at least 12 months and 40 percent at least 24 months. The FDA concluded that the clinical and laboratory similarities between the two tumour types justified extrapolating that efficacy to papillary-only disease, and said it is finalising draft guidance on using carcinoma in situ evidence to support approvals of this kind.
