**Latest (Saturday afternoon, October 10):** Simon strengthened further to a Category 4 storm with winds up to 150 mph, the National Hurricane Center said Saturday, with the storm about 115 miles south-southwest of Manzanillo and expected to approach or move just inland of Mexico's west-central coast late Saturday or early Sunday. Authorities issued a hurricane warning for parts of the Pacific coast south of Puerto Vallarta, including the area around Manzanillo, Mexico's largest Pacific port, and a tropical storm watch toward Lázaro Cárdenas. The NHC warned of 10 to 20 inches of rain through Sunday, life-threatening flooding and mudslides in steep terrain, and a dangerous storm surge with large destructive waves near landfall.

Hurricane Simon gained strength into a powerful Category 4 hurricane early Saturday, with maximum sustained winds of 225 kilometers per hour (140 mph), the National Hurricane Center in Miami reported, as it moved toward Mexico's Pacific coast. The center forecast 10 to 20 inches of rain across parts of southwestern Mexico, with some areas receiving up to 30 inches, and urged residents to take immediate preparedness measures.

Jalisco authorities suspended classes Friday and Monday in 22 coastal municipalities and began evacuating dozens of people from risk zones. The tourism sector in Puerto Vallarta, a destination popular with American travelers, was told to make sure workers and visitors are in shelters or safe areas. Forecasters warned Friday of flooding and dangerous landslides along the Jalisco coast and in parts of Michoacán, Colima and Nayarit.
