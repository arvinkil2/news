**Latest:** The injured count rose to 54, with 16 people killed, official media reported, in an Associated Press update published early on Friday. The quake also collapsed 20 livestock sheds alongside at least 339 homes, the Nagqu media office said. The US Geological Survey recorded at least seven aftershocks of magnitude 4.3 to 5.2. More than 1,600 people from the local county were deployed on rescue work, with about 1,000 prefabricated housing units and some 3,000 tents and folding beds sent to the region, more than 300 police officers dispatched and almost 350 fire and rescue personnel mobilised, Xinhua said, and China's leader Xi Jinping called for all-out rescue and medical relief efforts. Communications were down in some areas, trains through the region were halted, rockfalls closed a section of national highway 317 to all but emergency vehicles, and intermittent snow was expected in the quake region over the next three days, Xinhua said.

Earlier: The earthquake killed 16 people and injured 32 others, Xinhua reported, citing Nagqu City's emergency management bureau. As of 2:25 a.m. on Friday, 11 of the injured were in serious condition and 21 had minor injuries, Xinhua said. The quake collapsed at least 339 homes in Nagqu, the Nagqu media office said, citing city government officials, search and rescue efforts continued, and police and fire rescue teams were building tents to house people displaced by the quake, the Associated Press reported. A level-II earthquake disaster emergency response was activated in the area, Al Jazeera reported. Some houses in the villages closest to the epicentre showed cracks, the state broadcaster said, and a string of smaller tremors of around magnitude 3 and 4 have been recorded in surrounding regions since the main quake, China Earthquake Networks Center data showed. Reuters described it as the strongest earthquake to hit China since January 2024.

A magnitude 7.0 earthquake struck the Tibetan plateau at 14:33 UTC on Thursday (22:33 local time), with an epicentre at 32.336 degrees north, 93.714 degrees east, about 183 kilometres east-northeast of Nagqu and 50 kilometres north of Suo, at a shallow depth of 10 kilometres, the US Geological Survey said. The German Research Centre for Geosciences (GFZ) independently reported the same magnitude and depth for a quake in Tibet.

The epicentre is in a remote, high-altitude area: Nagqu is the nearest city of size at roughly 184 kilometres away, and Lhasa is about 389 kilometres away. No casualty or damage reports had emerged in the first hour after the quake, and the USGS had received no felt reports, though the survey notes its automatic solution may be revised.
