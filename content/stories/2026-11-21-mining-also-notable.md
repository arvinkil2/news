Fewer mining projects moved closer to production in the quarter to September 30 even as companies reported a record number of drill holes, S&P Global data show. Companies raised $9.81 billion (C$14.1 billion) in the quarter, 28 percent less than a year earlier. The pattern, heavy drilling funded by thinner financings, leaves more projects competing for development capital just as metals prices fall.

Gold futures fell 4.62 percent to $3,515.40 an ounce in the latest market tables, down 9.54 percent on the week, and Mining.com and the Northern Miner both reported miners' production costs rising at a near record pace as the price approaches $3,500. Gold miners fell 5.0 percent on the day in the tables and silver miners 5.59 percent, extending a week in which every mining subgroup lost more than 11 percent.
