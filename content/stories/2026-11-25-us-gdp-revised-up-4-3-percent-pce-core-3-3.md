US real gross domestic product increased at an annual rate of 4.3 percent in the third quarter of 2026, according to the second estimate released today by the Bureau of Economic Analysis. That is a revision up of 0.9 percentage point from the advance estimate of 3.4 percent, and well above the roughly 3.5 percent survey consensus recorded ahead of the release. Real GDP had increased 2.2 percent in the second quarter.

The contributors to the increase in real GDP in the third quarter were increases in investment, consumer spending and government spending, partly offset by a decrease in exports, the BEA said, while imports, which are a subtraction in the calculation of GDP, increased. The revision primarily reflected upward revisions to investment and consumer spending, led in investment by intellectual property products, notably software, and in consumer spending by goods, partly offset by a downward revision to health care services.

Real final sales to private domestic purchasers, the sum of consumer spending and gross private fixed investment, increased 5.8 percent in the third quarter, also revised up 0.9 percentage point. Real gross domestic income increased 4.4 percent, compared with 2.3 percent in the second quarter. Profits from current production increased $206.0 billion in the third quarter, compared with an increase of $384.0 billion in the second quarter. On prices in the GDP accounts, the price index for gross domestic purchases increased 2.7 percent, revised down 0.3 percentage point, the PCE price index increased 2.1 percent and the core PCE price index increased 2.5 percent, both unchanged from the advance estimate.

In the separate October personal income and outlays release, also published today, personal income increased $201.8 billion, or 0.7 percent at a monthly rate, disposable personal income increased 0.8 percent, and personal consumption expenditures increased $63.8 billion, or 0.3 percent. Real PCE decreased $5.8 billion, less than 0.1 percent at a monthly rate. The PCE price index increased 0.3 percent in October from the preceding month, and the index excluding food and energy also increased 0.3 percent. From a year earlier, the PCE price index increased 3.8 percent and the core index increased 3.3 percent, against a consensus expectation of 3.2 percent for the core annual rate.

Together the two releases describe an economy growing much faster than forecasters expected in the third quarter, with domestic private demand at 5.8 percent, while the Fed's preferred inflation measure remains more than a percentage point above its 2 percent target on an annual basis. They are the last tier-one US prints before the Federal Open Market Committee meets on December 8 to 9, and they arrive after Treasury yields have fallen roughly 20 basis points in a week, a rally priced for a softer data outcome than the BEA delivered.
