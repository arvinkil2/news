Moscow came under a drone attack on Wednesday night, according to the city's mayor, Sergei Sobyanin, whose successive updates put the number of drones shot down on approach to the capital at 96 in total, 72 in his first tally and a further 24 later. Russia's aviation authority, Rosaviatsiya, restricted arrivals and departures at Vnukovo, Domodedovo and Zhukovsky airports; the Zhukovsky restrictions were lifted after about an hour, while the other two airports were handling flights only by prior coordination, and two aircraft flying from Turkey to Moscow turned back.

The claims are Moscow's own, carried by Russian state wires and reported by Ukrainska Pravda, and Ukraine has not confirmed launching the attack. The Telegram channel Astra reported explosions heard near Naro-Fominsk, southwest of the capital, and Moscow region authorities had issued a drone danger warning at about 10 p.m. local time.

## Latest

Russian officials have since put the full scale of the overnight attack far higher. TASS reported that 613 drones were launched toward the Moscow region, and Sobyanin said air defences destroyed 231 of them on approach to the city, with the remainder neutralised on distant approaches between Wednesday evening and Thursday morning, Al Jazeera reported. That is more than double the 96 downed on approach in his earlier updates on the same night.

No damage, casualties or airport closures from this attack were reported in the Al Jazeera account, and it cited no Ukrainian statement and no independent corroboration of the figures. It described the raid as the latest in a series of major drone assaults on the Moscow region, following a record attack in September in which air defences reported shooting down more than 1,600 drones. Separately, it said Russia attacked Kharkiv, Ukraine's second largest city, damaging an educational institution, citing Kharkiv's mayor, Ihor Terekhov.
