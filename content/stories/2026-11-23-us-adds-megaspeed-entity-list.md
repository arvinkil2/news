**Latest:** Singapore is continuing to investigate Megaspeed, Prime Minister Lawrence Wong said on Tuesday at a press conference in Canberra, where he was on an official visit. Singapore's inquiry is focused on whether Megaspeed breached Singapore laws, Wong said, and Singapore is continuing discussions with US authorities, whose own investigation is also ongoing. Wong said Singapore had previously investigated Alice Huang, the Chinese businesswoman who formerly led Megaspeed, and released her because there was no basis for further prosecution. US and Singaporean law enforcement jointly conducted an on-site end-use check at Megaspeed in June 2025, Reuters reported. Megaspeed did not immediately return a request for comment; a week ago the company said it conducts legitimate and lawful business, including complying with US export controls.

The US Department of Commerce has added seven Southeast Asian companies to the Entity List, including Singapore-based Megaspeed International Pte. Ltd., the cloud computing firm at the centre of the alleged diversion of Nvidia chips. The final rule, filed by the Bureau of Industry and Security on Monday, also lists Apex Enterprise Solutions and Swiftdata in Singapore, MSFusion, PT Megaspeed International and Speedmatrix in Malaysia, and Megaspeed (Thailand) Co., Ltd. in Thailand.

The End-User Review Committee found reasonable cause to believe the entities pose a significant risk of diverting advanced computing commodities to unauthorized end uses or end users, the rule says. Each listing carries a licence requirement for all items subject to the Export Administration Regulations, with applications reviewed under a presumption of denial. Alibaba, whose Singapore subsidiary Apex Enterprise Solutions is among the seven, said it would pursue all legal avenues and that Apex had never diverted advanced computing chips, according to Reuters reporting carried by CNBC.
