In an October 9 order, the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission accepted PJM Interconnection's definition of a large load, an end-use customer with a cumulative peak of 50 megawatts or more at a single electrical site, and a Large Load Registry that takes effect October 12 and requires load-serving entities to report the large loads they serve. The commission rejected the rest of PJM's August filing as one package, including the Interim Resource Adequacy Service, under which new large loads that had not brought their own new capacity could be curtailed in emergencies, and the related Bring Your Own New Capacity rules.

FERC said parts of the tariff appeared to reach into retail regulation, which sits outside its authority, and that PJM had not shown the curtailment treatment was not unduly discriminatory, while giving PJM guidance on how a revised proposal could be refiled. PJM has warned it will be short of the capacity needed for its reliability requirement from the 2027/2028 delivery year, largely because of data centre load growth.
