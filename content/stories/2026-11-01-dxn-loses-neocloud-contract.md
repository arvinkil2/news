DXN's first AI customer has walked away. The Australian modular data center builder announced in its first quarter FY27 update that its US listed neocloud customer has cancelled its contract for an AI high performance computing module, citing a strategic shift in its business. The deal, signed in June, was worth 8.8 million Australian dollars (about 6.19 million US dollars) for a 1.36 megawatt liquid cooled module offering densities up to 150 kilowatts per rack, destined for the United States. DXN said the customer is also unlikely to proceed with a larger follow on campus opportunity that had been discussed.

The financial damage is cushioned by the contract itself. DXN has recognised 100,000 Australian dollars of revenue from the deal and expects to be paid 4.2 million (about 2.95 million US dollars) for work progressed to date, roughly 48 percent of the contract value. The rest of the quarter was strong on its face: total revenue of 3.4 million Australian dollars, up 340 percent on the same quarter last year, with 2.9 million from the modular business, and a contract backlog of 30.8 million (about 21.7 million US dollars).

Managing director Shalini Lagrutta said the company is disappointed its maiden AI HPC customer changed direction, but that the contract terms protected DXN's position and the design work and US relationships built through the programme have opened further opportunities in the US market, with a step change in customer enquiries during the quarter. DXN has taken a temporary lease on about 1,000 square metres of additional factory space in Seven Hills, New South Wales, is seeking a permanent east coast manufacturing site, and is establishing capacity in Johor, Malaysia and Jakarta, Indonesia, all due live next year.

Read against the modular sector's expansion claims, the episode is the risk case in miniature. A 1.36 megawatt single customer deal was the company's AI flagship; its cancellation costs nothing like the headline value thanks to progress payments, but it leaves the AI pipeline to be rebuilt from enquiries. For small builders riding the neocloud wave, backlog quality, meaning named customers, staged payments and cancellation terms, matters more than backlog size.
