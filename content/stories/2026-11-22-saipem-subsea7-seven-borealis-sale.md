Subsea7 has started the process of finding a buyer for Seven Borealis, its rigid pipelay and heavy lift vessel, after Brazil's competition authority CADE approved the company's merger with Saipem on November 4 subject to the divestiture.

CADE's merger control agreement requires the vessel, which can work in water depths beyond 1,500 metres including Brazil's pre-salt fields, to be sold to an independent purchaser the regulator approves in advance. The agreement also bars the merged company from reacquiring the vessel, requires it to keep tendering for deepwater subsea projects in Brazil, and protects the execution of projects already contracted for the ship. Subsea7 confirmed the sale process in its third quarter results and is considering a sale and leaseback so the vessel can keep working on commitments that run into 2028.

The companies now expect the merger to close in early 2027, later than the second half of 2026 previously targeted. Thirteen of the 16 antitrust authorities reviewing the deal have approved it; reviews remain open at the European Commission, in Australia and at the Economic Community of West African States. Egypt gave conditional approval on November 9, with margin limits on local bids, subcontracting of up to 5 percent of awarded work to independent contractors and annual compliance reports for three years.

Under the binding agreement signed in July 2025, Subsea7 merges into Saipem, which will be renamed Saipem7 and headquartered in Milan, with shareholders of each side owning about half. The combined group will have revenue of approximately 20 billion euros, a backlog of 43 billion euros and previously announced annual synergies of about 300 million euros. Separately, Subsea7's Seaway7 unit plans to sell its heavy transportation business, six semi-submersible vessels, to HEA Energy, a divestment the merger agreement permits and which triggers a special dividend of 105 million euros.
