The gas rally that began on Monday extended into a fourth session on Tuesday. In the estate market tables, natural gas rose 9.66 percent to 3.64 dollars per million British thermal units, up 8.07 percent on the week, the largest one-day gain in the commodity complex. In Europe, the Dutch TTF front month rose for a fourth consecutive session, trading at 89.79 euros per megawatt-hour on Tuesday morning, up 1.41 percent, after Monday's opening jump of 8.6 percent to 96.075 euros.

Storage is the reason forecasts carry this much weight. Gas Infrastructure Europe data put EU storage at 68.9 percent full on November 23, against a five-year average of 89 percent, and EnergyReader put the figure at 69.17 percent on November 22, holding about 781 terawatt-hours. Europe entered the heating season with its lowest storage in over a decade and has already withdrawn about a tenth of the gas injected during the summer filling season, so a cold spell in early December draws directly on a thin buffer.

The same weather is tightening Asia. Japan's nationwide day-ahead power price rose 6 percent in the week of November 16 and spot power reached its highest since August 2022, with snowfall expected to lift gas demand in the Chubu and Hokkaido regions. The JKM marker for Northeast Asian LNG stood at 28.64 dollars per million British thermal units on November 24, and analysts cited by OilPrice expect Japan to overtake China as the world's largest LNG importer this year for the first time since 2022, putting Europe and Asia in direct competition for flexible cargoes through the winter.

In the United States, the December Henry Hub contract expires today, November 25, and that expiry is amplifying the move. Finimize reported December futures settling up 4.4 percent at 3.323 dollars on Tuesday after a rough two days, and Ritterbusch and Associates warned that the combination of expiry and choppier forecasts can produce an exaggerated move if severe cold arrives, because traders must roll positions into January and adjust hedges quickly. The January contract, the first full winter month, is where the market will price December weather once the roll completes.
