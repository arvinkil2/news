Latest: Polls closed at 7pm New Zealand time and the Electoral Commission's preliminary count, with 16.3 percent of results in, puts the governing National Party on 30.55 percent of the party vote (301,164 votes) and a projected 43 seats, ahead of Labour on 28.93 percent (285,115 votes) and 35 seats. Behind them, the Greens are on 12.99 percent (16 seats), ACT on 11.11 percent (13), New Zealand First on 9.82 percent (12) and the Opportunity Party on 5.39 percent (6), while Te Pāti Māori's five electorate wins against 0.44 percent of the party vote lift the projected parliament to 130 seats.

On those numbers neither bloc can govern alone: National plus ACT hold 56 seats and Labour, the Greens and Te Pāti Māori hold 56, against the 66 needed in a 130-seat parliament, so New Zealand First's projected 12 seats decide which side forms the government. The early count is partial and the projection will move through election night; the Commission aims to have preliminary results from 95 percent of polling stations by the end of the night, with the official result, including ballots cast abroad, expected by November 27.

Polling stations opened at 9am on Saturday across New Zealand and close at 7pm, closing a voting period that began on October 26. More than 1.6 million voters, out of nearly 3.8 million in total, had cast ballots by the end of Thursday, according to the Electoral Commission, with cost of living and the state of the economy the backdrop to the final day.

Opinion polls ahead of the vote put the governing centre-right National Party and the opposition centre-left Labour Party neck and neck, with neither on course to win enough seats to govern alone under the mixed-member proportional system.
