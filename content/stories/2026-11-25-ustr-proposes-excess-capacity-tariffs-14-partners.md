## Latest

Brazil and the European Union rejected the proposals on Wednesday evening. Brazil's government expressed its "outrage" at the separate proposal for a 10 percent duty on finished Brazilian aircraft and a new rate on out-of-quota sugar, and said the measures aimed to pressure Brazil into changing regulations on its Pix payment system and digital trade. "The USTR's unilateral decision constitutes an unacceptable action within the ongoing bilateral negotiation process. Brazil will not yield to such threats," President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva's government said, adding that it was not ruling out reciprocal measures and would keep seeking a negotiated solution without giving up its right to challenge the duties at the World Trade Organization. Brazil had asked on Tuesday for a WTO dispute panel on the July Section 301 tariffs, a request the United States blocked the same day, Reuters reported. Embraer shares were down over 3 percent on Wednesday afternoon; the company said it was reviewing the announcement and defended the zero-tariff regime for the global aerospace industry in place since the 1980s. USTR will hold a hearing on the Brazil duties on January 14, 2027.

The European Commission said it rejected the premise of the findings against the bloc. "We strongly reject the premise that Europe is a source of structural overcapacity. To the contrary, Europe is among those most exposed to overcapacity," spokesman Olof Gill said, adding that the Commission expected any measure to respect in full the 2025 deal capping US duties on the bloc at 15 percent.

The Office of the United States Trade Representative on Wednesday determined that the acts, policies and practices of 14 economies relating to structural excess capacity and production in manufacturing are unreasonable and burden US commerce, and proposed additional tariffs on each of them for public comment. The 14 economies are China, the European Union, Japan, Korea, Taiwan, India, Vietnam, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand, Cambodia, Bangladesh, Singapore and Switzerland. USTR made no determination on Mexico or Norway, the other two economies in the investigation opened on March 11, 2026.

The proposed headline rates are highest where no trade agreement caps them. Vietnam faces up to 35 percent, falling to 20 percent if it reaches a reciprocal trade agreement with the United States. India faces up to 25 percent, or 17 percent with an agreement. Singapore faces up to 20 percent, or 10 percent with an agreement. China faces up to 20 percent on most goods and up to 10 percent on the products in Annex A of its Federal Register notice, a structure USTR said accounts for the 20 percent rate in the Kuala Lumpur Joint Arrangement reached by President Trump and President Xi on October 30, 2025; Reuters reported that if the US-China truce, which expires on January 10, unravels, China's rate would rise to 40 percent.

Where framework agreements already exist, the proposals track them. The European Union, Japan, Korea, Taiwan and Switzerland each face a proposed 15 percent rate, net of most-favoured-nation duties and existing Section 301 tariffs. Bangladesh, Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia and Thailand each face up to 18 percent. USTR said the new duties would not stack on top of the tariffs of 10 or 12.5 percent imposed in July on 60 trading partners over forced-labour enforcement, and Greer told CNBC the caps were consistent with the trade agreements reached over the past year, Reuters reported. In a separate action the same day, USTR proposed a new 10 percent tariff on Brazil's finished Embraer commercial aircraft and higher duties on Brazilian sugar imports above quota, Reuters reported.

India, which faces up to 25 percent, gave its first official response late on Wednesday. The Commerce and Industry Ministry said it had taken note of the USTR notice and was examining it, that India had cooperated constructively throughout the investigation while maintaining that no Indian sector has structural excess production capacity, and that any trade concern should be resolved within the bilateral trade agreement negotiations. New Delhi said it remains committed to working with Washington to complete that agreement, in line with the February 2, 2026 announcement and the February 7 joint statement.

Greer said the targeted practices include industrial plans, production and export incentives, preferential lending, financial repression and inadequate labour protections, and that statements at the G7, G20 and WTO had produced no change. China's Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun rejected the finding, saying the so-called overcapacity issue is a false issue and an excuse for political manipulation, Reuters reported. Written comments and requests to appear are due by January 5, 2027, and USTR will hold hearings on January 25, 2027, so no duty can take effect before late January.

Markets had moved before the announcement rather than on it. Asian chip-heavy markets sold off sharply on Wednesday in anticipation of the findings, with Taiwan's Taiex down 4.94 percent and Korea's Kospi down 5.8 percent, as covered on this site, and the prospect of new barriers weighed on US technology shares through the session.
