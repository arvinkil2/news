Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu said on Monday that France's planned minimum wage increase will take effect in December instead of January, as the government seeks to defuse a cost-of-living crisis that has sparked some of the country's worst civil unrest in years. Labour Minister Jean-Pierre Farandou said the rate will rise by 1.6 percent in December rather than in the usual January inflation adjustment, bringing the net monthly minimum wage to 1,502 euros, up 24 euros from the level set in June. Lecornu called it the second-biggest increase in 20 years.

Unions rejected the move within hours. The CFDT, which had sought a 100 euro increase, called the rise insufficient through its head Marylise Leon, while CGT head Sophie Binet, whose union demanded about 150 euros, described it as crumbs and called new protests for November 17. The unrest began over underfunded schools in September and widened into broader anger over living costs, including riots in major cities on October 17, five months before a presidential election.
