SK hynix has formally declined to confirm the largest rumored memory investment of the year. In a Form 6-K furnished to the SEC on November 27, the company addressed an October 29 report in the Maeil Business Newspaper that its US subsidiary Solidigm is pursuing an investment of approximately 60 trillion won to build a new NAND flash memory production facility in the United States. SK hynix furnished a first clarification on the same day as that report.

The filing states that Solidigm is reviewing various measures to strengthen its competitiveness and expand its production capacity, but that no matters have been determined as of the date of the report. SK hynix committed to make a further disclosure either when specific details are confirmed or within three months of November 27.

That three-month commitment gives the rumor a dated test. Either a confirmed plan, with a site, a capacity figure and a spending schedule, appears by late February 2027, or the company must update the market again on a proposal it has so far declined to endorse.

Solidigm is the former Intel NAND and SSD business that SK hynix acquired, and it is the group's main US-based NAND operation. A US production expansion on anything near the reported scale would change the geography of NAND supply, which is currently concentrated in South Korea, Japan and China, and would sit alongside the group's existing US commitments in advanced packaging.
