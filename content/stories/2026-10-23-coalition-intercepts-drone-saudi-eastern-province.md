**Latest:** Yemen's Houthis said on Saturday they had struck Saudi Arabian oil installations and airports, and the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted two missiles, Reuters reported. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said the targets were King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, an Aramco facility in Yanbu, the Rabigh oil refinery, crude oil pumping stations and the East-West pipeline, King Fahd International Airport in Dammam, an airport in Najran and an air base in Khamis Mushait. Earlier on Saturday, Saudi Civil Defence briefly issued warnings of possible danger for Taif, Yanbu, Mecca and Jeddah, then said about ten minutes later that the threat had passed. There was no immediate confirmation from Saudi authorities, and Reuters said it was unclear whether the reported attacks on the kingdom hit their targets or were intercepted.

Earlier on Saturday, coalition spokesman Brig. Gen. Turki Al-Malki said two ballistic missiles launched toward Taif and Yanbu, in western Saudi Arabia, were intercepted and destroyed, TASS reported on Saturday. Yanbu, on the Red Sea coast, is one of the kingdom's main oil export outlets. The statement followed the overnight interceptions of a missile aimed at Riyadh and a drone in the Eastern Province, and the TASS report gave no time for the western interceptions and carried no casualty or damage figures for them.

The Saudi-led coalition said early on Saturday that air defences intercepted and destroyed a ballistic missile launched by the Houthi militia toward Riyadh, in a statement issued at 4:49 a.m. local time and carried by the Saudi Press Agency, Gulf Business reported. An AFP journalist in the capital heard two explosions in the early hours, after Saudi Civil Defense activated its early warning system to alert residents to a possible threat, Deutsche Welle reported, in Spanish, with AFP and Reuters. The coalition's initial announcements reported no casualties or damage from Saturday's interceptions, and no Houthi claim of responsibility had been reported in the sources read. Gulf Business also reported that on Friday the coalition had destroyed two ballistic missiles targeting Riyadh and Khamis Mushait, along with two drones launched toward the kingdom's southern region.

Coalition spokesman Brig. Gen. Turki Al-Malki said in a statement on X that a drone was intercepted and destroyed in the Eastern Province, in an announcement the coalition issued at 3:35 a.m. local time. Shortly past 4 a.m. local time, Saudi Civil Defense sounded alarms warning of potential incoming aerial threats across Riyadh, Najran and the southern governorate of Wadi Al-Dawasir, and two loud blasts were heard across the capital minutes after the alert, Arab News reported.

Saudi Arabia has faced sustained drone and missile barrages in recent weeks, including a strike on Riyadh's international airport, and cross-border attacks have surged since the breakdown in July of an uneasy four-year truce in Yemen, the report said.
