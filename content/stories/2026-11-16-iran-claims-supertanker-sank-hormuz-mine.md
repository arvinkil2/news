Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said on Sunday that two oil tankers travelling along an unauthorised route in the southern part of the Strait of Hormuz struck naval mines, causing massive explosions and fire, and that one of them, a supertanker carrying over two million barrels of crude oil, sank in the southern waters of the Persian Gulf. The statement, carried by Iranian state television, named neither vessel and gave no flag, crew information or environmental assessment.

No independent source has confirmed either mine strike or the sinking. The Guards posted video of what they said was the sunken tanker on fire, but Seatrade Maritime News reported that no vessel can be discerned in the footage, which shows a large fire and smoke on the water. The UK Maritime Trade Operations centre said on Sunday that it was aware of a report by Indian shipping authorities of an attack on a tanker in the Gulf, with the crew safe and the damage and environmental impact unknown, but it was unclear whether that report concerned the same incident.

The tanker attack that has been confirmed from Sunday is a separate one. Maritime security firm Vanguard Tech identified the ship attacked while approaching the strait as the Al Dhannah, a very large crude carrier managed by ADNOC Logistics and Services, struck in the steering gear room at the stern before heading back towards Dubai port, Seatrade reported. Iran says the northern corridor through its waters, used with authorisation from its Persian Gulf Strait Authority, is the only allowable route through the strait, and the Guards' navy has warned tanker owners and crews not to approach the restricted area. A further confirmed strike, on a tanker leaving the Gulf on Monday morning, is covered separately in this edition.
