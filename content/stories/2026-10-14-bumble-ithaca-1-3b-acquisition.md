Bumble has agreed to be taken private. The company announced October 13 that it signed a definitive agreement with Ithaca Holdings, an all-cash deal valuing Bumble at $1.3 billion. Shareholders will receive $6.05 per Class A share, a premium of about 138 percent to the unaffected closing price on October 9 and about 119 percent to the 30-day volume-weighted average price. An independent special committee ran the strategic review and recommended the deal; the board approved it unanimously, with founder Whitney Wolfe Herd recused, and affiliates of Blackstone and Wolfe Herd have already delivered the shareholder consent needed to approve it.

The discount to history is the story. Bumble went public in 2021 at a valuation of roughly $14 billion on the strength of its women-first model. In the second quarter of 2026 it reported revenue of $211 million, down 15.2 percent year over year, with paying users down 16.4 percent to about 3.2 million. The swipe model that built the category is losing users across the industry, and Bumble has been testing AI-driven features and in-person formats, including its Plans product for real-world meetups, without reversing the trend.

Wolfe Herd will stay actively involved alongside Ithaca's Ravi Gupta and John Hegeman. Going private removes the quarterly earnings treadmill while the company attempts the product reset, but it also crystallizes the loss for public investors who held from the IPO. For the technology beat, the signal is consumer: the largest dating platforms are now restructuring assets, not growth stories, and the next model for the category will be built under private ownership.
