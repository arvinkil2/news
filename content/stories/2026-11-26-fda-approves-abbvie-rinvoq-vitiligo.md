The FDA has approved AbbVie Rinvoq (upadacitinib) 15 mg once daily for adults and pediatric patients aged 12 and older with nonsegmental vitiligo, AbbVie announced on November 23. The oral JAK1 inhibitor is the first systemic therapy approved in the United States for the condition, which AbbVie estimates affects nearly 3 million United States adults. The approval is Rinvoq's tenth United States indication.

The decision rests on Viti-Up-1 and Viti-Up-2, two replicate global Phase 3 trials that randomised 614 patients aged 12 and older, 308 and 306 respectively, 2 to 1 to upadacitinib or placebo for 48 weeks. At week 48, at least 75 percent facial repigmentation (F-VASI 75) was achieved by 25 percent of upadacitinib patients against 6 percent on placebo in Viti-Up-1, and 23 percent against 7 percent in Viti-Up-2. The other co-primary endpoint, at least 50 percent total-body repigmentation (T-VASI 50), was reached by 19 percent against 6 percent and 21 percent against 6 percent, all statistically significant. In the open-label extension, facial response rates among patients continuing treatment rose to 55 percent and 47 percent by week 76 in descriptive analyses without a placebo comparison.

Treatment-emergent adverse events were reported in 81 percent and 70 percent of upadacitinib recipients against 60 percent and 62 percent on placebo, most commonly upper respiratory infection, acne, nasopharyngitis and headache. No adjudicated major cardiovascular events, venous thromboembolism, gastrointestinal perforations, active tuberculosis, lymphoma or nonmelanoma skin cancer were reported in the placebo-controlled period. The label carries the JAK inhibitor class warnings, including serious infections and higher mortality and cardiovascular risk in patients aged 50 and older with a cardiovascular risk factor.

Until now the only approved drug option for nonsegmental vitiligo in the United States was topical ruxolitinib cream, whose use is limited to 10 percent of body surface area. A once-daily pill removes that ceiling, which is why the approval matters commercially as much as clinically for AbbVie's immunology portfolio.
