Latest: President Donald Trump responded on Thursday by accusing Iran of inventing the attacks to influence oil prices ahead of the November 3 midterm elections. "Iran is doing everything they can to drive up the price of Oil prior to the U.S. Midterm Election," Trump wrote on Truth Social. "They are even making up fake hits, mines, and everything else, because more Oil is coming through the Hormuz Strait now than it did even prior to the beginning of the Conflict." UK Maritime Trade Operations, which tracks attacks on commercial shipping in the region, has issued no new incident reports corresponding to the Guards' claims. In its October 16 weekly assessment, however, UKMTO acknowledged it was struggling to establish the true number of attacks because most targeted vessels were not reporting incidents directly to it, so the absence of reports does not by itself settle whether the strikes occurred. Neither Tehran's claim that seven vessels were attacked nor Trump's assertion that the incidents were fabricated has been independently established.

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Navy said on Thursday that four vessels it described as involved in illegal oil transfers in the northern Persian Gulf were simultaneously hit and caught fire after its forces tracked them on Wednesday, and that three more vessels collided with mines west of the Strait of Hormuz. The Guards also warned the US military that continued provocations could lead to a wider response against its regional bases.

No vessel was named in the statement and no independent confirmation was available overnight. The claim extends a pattern this site has tracked all week, in which the Guards announce mine and missile strikes on unnamed ships while maritime assessors, most recently the Joint Maritime Information Center on October 18, report no substantiation for the mine claims specifically.
