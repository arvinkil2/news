Colombian security forces stormed an Exito supermarket in the northern city of Cartagena and liberated the people held inside, ending a stand-off of about 15 hours. The siege began around 8 in the morning on Tuesday, when armed men entered the supermarket in La Castellana, a residential neighborhood, and took a group of about 30 people hostage.

According to President Abelardo de la Espriella, the gunmen had meant to rob an armored car that had already left with the previous day's proceeds. They then seized the hostages and demanded 7 billion pesos, about 2 million dollars, and a means of escape. During the day at least five hostages were released from the supermarket cafeteria, whose windows had been covered with paper, and riot police were deployed around midnight after family members and onlookers outside became restless.

The operation turned shortly after 1 in the afternoon on Wednesday, when explosions were heard and special forces entered the building. The security forces killed one of the hostage takers and detained the remaining four, De la Espriella said in a social media video, describing an "extraordinary joint operation between the National Police and the military forces." One agent was reported injured in the rescue, according to Caracol Radio. The president said the gunmen "were given the opportunity to surrender and release the hostages, but they rejected that opportunity, so we took decisive action."

The next morning the prosecution said the hostage takers' arms and explosives were fake, and former hostages said the men had spent most of the day drinking. All of the hostages were freed: about ten had been released during the stand-off and the remainder in the storming. Status: siege resolved November 25, 2026; the detained men face prosecution, and the casualty account rests on official statements.
