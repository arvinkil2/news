**Latest (05:04 ET):** The freight boom now has a policy counterweight under discussion. Splash247 reported that Washington is considering exemptions allowing blacklisted tankers linked to Russia, Iran and Venezuela to carry oil and petroleum products not themselves subject to US sanctions, with companies needing Treasury approval to employ the vessels; a US official told Reuters the aim is to release additional tanker capacity and bring down record freight rates, and Energy Secretary Chris Wright raised the possibility at the World Petroleum Congress in Riyadh this week. No formal easing has been announced. Asian shipping stocks sold off hard on Thursday: COSCO Shipping Energy fell more than 13 percent in Hong Kong, and its Shanghai listed shares and China Merchants names hit their 10 percent daily limits. Clarksons Research estimates 1,010 oil tankers totalling 121 million dwt are individually sanctioned, about 17 percent of global tanker capacity, but SEB and Singapore broker Sentosa questioned how much genuinely usable mainstream capacity relief would add, given the age of the ships and the EU and UK sanctions that would remain.

Tanker rates have hit all time highs as Middle East producers tie up vessels in ship to ship transfers outside the Strait of Hormuz, with supertanker rates from the Gulf to Asia reaching 1.4 million dollars a day earlier this month. Freight from the US Gulf Coast to Europe now totals about 46 million dollars a voyage, or 24.50 dollars a barrel, against 6.5 million dollars before the war, and the Americas to Asia route costs over 80 million dollars.

Western Canadian Select fell to 74 dollars a barrel last week before recovering to 79.50 dollars, with Ecuador's Oriente and Colombia's Vasconia also down sharply, even as Russian Urals fetches close to 120 dollars. With few ships available and heavy grades in strong demand for diesel, refiners in Europe and Asia are holding back from purchases and the glut in the Americas looks set to persist.
