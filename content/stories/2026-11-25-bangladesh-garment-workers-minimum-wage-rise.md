Bangladesh's government-appointed garment wage board has agreed to raise the monthly minimum wage to 17,500 taka ($136) from 12,500 taka ($100), with the increase taking effect in December and reaching pay packets in January. Workers had demanded 30,000 taka ($240), and the labour representative on the board, Babul Akhter, said workers were not completely satisfied with the outcome while urging them to return to their factories.

The garment sector employs four million people and accounts for about $38 billion a year, four-fifths of Bangladesh's exports. The manufacturers' association BGMEA said the protests had cost the industry an estimated $428 million, with orders worth $254 million moving to other countries.
