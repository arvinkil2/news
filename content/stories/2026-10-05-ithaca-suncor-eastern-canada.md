Suncor Energy agreed Sunday to sell its interests in three oil projects off Canada's east coast to London-based Ithaca Energy for C$1.2 billion — about US$842 million — in upfront cash, plus a contingent payment of up to C$350 million tied to future oil prices. The deal transfers Suncor's 48 percent interest in Terra Nova, 40 percent in White Rose and 38.6 percent in West White Rose.

The headline price is only part of the economics. Ithaca will assume the investment commitments and future liabilities attached to the assets — including a regulatory well-compliance programme at Terra Nova estimated at about C$500 million starting in 2027, and abandonment and lease liabilities totaling roughly C$1.4 billion. For Ithaca, the acquisition is a significant geographical expansion — its first international transaction, establishing a presence in Canada's offshore eastern region — and the company expects it to be immediately accretive to adjusted EBITDAX, free cash flow and dividend per share.

For Suncor, the sale is portfolio concentration: CEO Rich Kruger said it "further focuses our efforts on opportunities that generate the greatest long-term shareholder value." The buyer-seller logic is clean — Suncor exits the mature, liability-heavy Atlantic offshore; Ithaca, a North Sea operator, bets its late-life asset expertise travels across the Atlantic.
