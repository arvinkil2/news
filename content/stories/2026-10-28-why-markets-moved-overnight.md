Natural gas was again the largest move in the site market tables, up 13.53 percent to 3.06 dollars per million British thermal units in the midday tables, updated at 12:39 ET, and up 9.75 percent on the week. No story in today's edition gives the move a new fundamental driver. This morning's edition read the rebound as short covering against the record money manager short position of 599,731 contracts reported last week, with short covering labelled as interpretation, not a reported cause, and nothing published since adds a cause. Today's gas story, QatarEnergy extending its LNG force majeure to the end of 2026, prices the European benchmark, a different market from the United States contract in the tables.

WTI crude rose 3.48 percent to 103.38 dollars a barrel, up 6.41 percent on the week. Today's reporting supplies the driver. Reuters, carried in today's API crude inventories story and today's OPEC+ story, reported oil up more than 2 dollars after Yemen's Houthis said they had hit Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery, the 550,000 barrel a day plant Saudi authorities did not immediately confirm was struck. The inventory leg was supportive rather than decisive: the American Petroleum Institute estimated crude stocks fell 2.031 million barrels last week against expectations for a build. OPEC+ is expected to hold December output targets steady at its meeting on Sunday, in today's OPEC+ story, so no offsetting supply decision is in front of the market.

Brent crude fell 1.35 percent to 112.34 dollars a barrel in the midday tables, while staying up 3.23 percent on the week. That direction is opposite to WTI in the same tables and opposite to the wire reports carried in today's stories, which had Brent up between 1.3 and 1.8 percent in European trade to about 115.90 dollars. No story in today's edition explains the fall shown in the tables, and none is invented here: the divergence between the tables and the morning's wire levels is recorded as it stands.

The Nikkei 225 rose 1.73 percent to 63,268 points, while remaining down 1.26 percent on the week. Today's Bank of Japan preview is the Japan context in the edition: the bank is expected to hold its policy rate at 1.25 percent at its October 29 to 30 meeting and to judge that underlying inflation has effectively reached its 2 percent target, with most economists in the Reuters poll expecting a rise to 1.50 percent this quarter. No story in today's edition attributes the Nikkei's rise to a specific company or policy driver, and none is claimed here.

Tesla fell 1.57 percent to 401.05 dollars, giving back part of a 3.77 percent weekly gain. No Tesla story was filed in today's edition, so no cause is offered for the move.

Broadcom rose 1.53 percent to 347.57 dollars. The only Broadcom item in today's file is in the infra-deals also notable roundup: banks are considering about 21 billion dollars of bond financing for the Broadcom organised chip financing behind Anthropic's compute build, reported by Bloomberg and flagged single-sourced in the roundup, as an update to the 63 billion dollar Northstar structure covered on this site on October 27. Today's reporting does not establish that the item caused the rise, and it is recorded as the day's Broadcom news, not as an explanation.

Soybeans rose 1.24 percent to 1,244.5 cents a bushel. No story in today's edition explains the move. Today's World Bank Commodity Markets Outlook forecasts food commodity prices rising about 10 percent in 2026, but that is an annual forecast published today, not a driver for one session's trade, and it is not presented as one here.

Semiconductors rose 1.15 percent to 516.94 dollars, and the sector's move has named company news behind it in today's edition. SK hynix set a shareholder return programme of about 100 trillion won, Teradyne reported third quarter revenue up 70 percent on AI test demand, UMC raised its capital budget, and Vertiv reported sales up 61 percent with guidance raised, each in its own story today. The cluster is a results story, not a single headline.

The cross-market read is an energy and positioning tape on top of a quiet policy wait, neither broad risk-on nor risk-off. The S&P 500 rose 0.18 percent and the Nasdaq 100 rose 0.12 percent at midday, gold fell 0.53 percent, and silver fell 1.11 percent. The 10 year Treasury yield stood at 5.03 percent in the tables, up 3.9 basis points on the day and still down 11.4 on the week, and the dollar index was little changed at 102.25. The Bank of Canada has already moved today, raising its overnight rate to 2.50 percent in this morning's decision, covered in today's Bank of Canada story. What remains is the Federal Reserve at 14:00 ET, where today's Treasury yields story puts a 91 percent probability on a hold today with a December hike almost fully priced, so Chair Kevin Warsh's language, not the morning's energy moves, decides the close.
