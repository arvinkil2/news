Brent crude touched 110 dollars a barrel on Friday for the first time since May, and December Brent settled at 109.82 dollars, up 6.42 percent on the day, while November US crude settled at 101.26 dollars, up 6.53 percent, back above 100 dollars. The move caps a week in which Brent climbed from about 105 dollars overnight through 108 and 109 dollars during Friday's session as the tanker war widened on two fronts at once.

In the Gulf, Iran's Revolutionary Guard said it struck two oil tankers near Larak Island and that the Strait of Hormuz is closed, according to Iranian state media cited by CNBC. The claim extends a campaign in which attacks on merchant ships in the strait have already reached a new high, with 149 confirmed incidents since March 1 in today's earlier reporting. No US official corroboration of the tanker strikes was reported.

In the Black Sea, Ukraine's security chief Vasyl Malyuk said Ukrainian missiles and sea drones hit two tankers, and said Ukraine would now strike Russian refineries systematically because the Kremlin has refused a ceasefire on energy infrastructure, describing the refineries as legitimate targets that are nonetheless civilian targets. The refinery threat lands on a Russian fuel system already short of diesel: Russia tightened diesel exports from late September as drone damage mounted, before fully lifting an earlier export ban in June, and European diesel prices were already elevated going into Friday's session.

The two fronts compound. Hormuz risk lifts the crude benchmark and the freight rates that move it, while a sustained Ukrainian campaign against Russian refineries tightens the diesel supply that Europe and Asia compete for, which is why the price response ran to more than 6 percent in a single session rather than the smaller war premium of earlier weeks.
