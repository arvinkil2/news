Gadi Eisenkot, the centrist Yashar leader tasked with forming Israel's next government, has signed coalition agreements with two parties this week, the right-wing Yisrael Beiteinu of Avigdor Liberman on Tuesday and the left-leaning Democrats of Yair Golan on Thursday. Together the three parties hold 36 seats in the 120-member Knesset, well short of the 61 needed for a majority.

Under the Democrats' agreement, the party receives four portfolios, including an expanded national security ministry currently held by Itamar Ben Gvir, plus labour, communications, and culture and sport, which will include a new directorate for democracy. The deal was signed hours after Yashar resumed talks that Golan had frozen in protest at Yashar's parallel negotiations with Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud over a possible unity government.

Those unity talks are the other track. Yashar and Likud have held four rounds exploring a rotation government with Eisenkot first as prime minister, and on November 27 Eisenkot accused Likud of stalling the talks to exhaust his mandate and force a repeat election; Likud says it remains open to unity and accuses Eisenkot of building a left-wing coalition. Naftali Bennett's Beyachad party, with 12 seats in Eisenkot's wider change bloc of 48, has not signed and Bennett still prefers a unity government with Likud.

The arithmetic leaves Eisenkot needing the ultra-Orthodox parties, Arab-majority list support or abstention, or a Likud deal. His bloc aims to sign all agreements by December 2 and swear in a government before the December 6 deadline, which President Herzog can extend by up to 14 days. Status: two agreements signed, November 2026; government not yet formed.
