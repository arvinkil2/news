Novo Nordisk also announced a change of finance chief alongside the results. Karsten Munk Knudsen, chief financial officer since February 2018, will step down at the end of December 2026, and Luigi La Corte, currently finance chief at Swiss dermatology company Galderma, will succeed him, Reuters reported. The quarter also carried 15 billion kroner in impairment charges, including 4.6 billion on the failed cardiovascular drug ziltivekimab, 4 billion on the scrapped obesity drug monlunabant and 4.1 billion on the manufacturing network, which left reported operating profit down 9 percent despite the adjusted gain, Fierce Biotech reported.

Novo Nordisk reported third quarter sales of 79.2 billion Danish kroner (about $11.9 billion), up 4 percent year on year and ahead of FactSet estimates of 73.6 billion kroner, with adjusted operating profit of 31.7 billion kroner against 28.6 billion expected, CNBC reported. Sales of the company's Wegovy pill beat estimates, though CNBC gave no separate figure for them.

The company raised its full year outlook for both adjusted revenue and operating profit growth to between negative 2 percent and 2 percent at constant exchange rates, from negative 6 percent to flat previously, an outlook it had already raised once in August. Reuters also reported the third quarter beat and the raised full year profit and sales outlook.

Morning update, November 4: Reuters reporting with company compiled consensus puts the detail behind the beat. Sales of the Wegovy pill, launched in the United States in January, were 5.39 billion kroner (about 810 million dollars) in the quarter, up 53 percent from the second quarter and ahead of the 4.62 billion kroner consensus. Adjusted sales were 79.20 billion kroner against 73.22 billion expected, and adjusted operating profit was 31.72 billion kroner against 28.39 billion expected, up 27 percent at constant exchange rates. This was the third guidance raise of the year, and the company expects free cash flow of 60 billion to 70 billion kroner for 2026.

The same reporting carries the price decision that will shape 2027. Novo will cut the United States wholesale acquisition cost of Wegovy by about 50 percent and of Ozempic by about 35 percent, to 675 dollars a month, effective January 1, 2027, and management said the cuts will hit 2027 cash flow. Chief executive Mike Doustdar declined to give 2027 guidance before February, citing loss of semaglutide exclusivity in some international markets, continued pressure in China and a less favourable gross to net effect than in 2026, and noted that the pill's prescription curve has flattened in recent weeks, which he attributed to seasonality.
