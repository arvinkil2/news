Brazil has designated 27 critical minerals in a push to develop its mining sector at home and reduce reliance on exporting raw materials. The National Council for Industrialization of Critical and Strategic Minerals approved the list on November 5, placing rare earths, graphite, lithium, nickel, cobalt, copper, manganese, silicon and uranium in the high priority group, eight minerals including tungsten, vanadium and titanium in the medium group, and ten including niobium, iron and gold in the lower group. Only the listed minerals, in nationally registered projects, will be eligible for government support, and the classifications will be reviewed every four years.

The council also created a fund, FGAM, that can receive up to R$2 billion (about $364 million) from the federal government to reduce investment risk, alongside a federal programme with an annual funding limit of R$1 billion between 2030 and 2034. The mines and energy ministry said the list would function as the compass of Brazilian mineral policy, and President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva said the government wants to work with international partners on processing rather than control the industry outright.
