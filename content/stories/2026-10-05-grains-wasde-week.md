USDA's weekly Crop Progress report, released 4:00pm ET on Oct 5 for the week ending Oct 4, showed 23 percent of the corn crop and 25 percent of the soybean crop harvested, both ahead of the five-year average pace, per IndexBox's writeup of the NASS data. Corn was 83 percent mature across 18 states covering 91 percent of 2025 acreage. Corn condition slipped to a season low of roughly 56 percent good-to-excellent, continuing the slide from 57 percent the prior week.

Futures were mixed into the report. December corn settled essentially flat at 497.5c, November soybeans added 2.75c to $12.81 (+0.2 percent), and December SRW wheat rose 8.5c to 691.5c (+1.2 percent), with wheat drawing support from Black Sea risk after the weekend corn-vessel strike. Harvest pressure is the near-term weight on row crops; the counterweight is yield uncertainty into Friday's WASDE and Crop Production reports at 12:00pm ET.

The yield debate is wide open. StoneX came out Friday at 182.1 bushels per acre and a 16.115 billion-bushel crop, above the USDA's September estimate of 178.5. If the USDA moves that direction, the market may struggle to hold $5 corn. A cut toward the Pro Farmer Crop Tour's 173.2 would likely be the first thing that turns the market back around. High temperatures in many areas this year may have stressed plants and capped yield potential, though late-season heat also reduced some disease pressure. Managed-money corn longs were already being cut after the September 30 stocks report, Friday's WASDE decides whether that unwind continues or reverses.

One caution: Successful Farming's headline ("Corn Harvest Trails Average") appeared to conflict with IndexBox's "ahead of average" characterization, but the Successful Farming page was inaccessible, so the discrepancy could not be resolved.
