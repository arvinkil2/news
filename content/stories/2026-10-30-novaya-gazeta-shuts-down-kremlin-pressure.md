Novaya Gazeta has announced it will shut down, with editor-in-chief Sergei Sokolov saying in a video statement that on November 9 the paper will switch off its servers and leave the offices it has occupied for 30 years. The paper's statement opened with the words "They've killed us," and Sokolov blamed the Russian authorities and special services for forcing the editorial team to stop work. The announcement came two weeks after Sokolov was questioned by investigators in Moscow over alleged unlawful use of personal data, in a criminal case against his colleague Oleg Roldugin, who has been detained and charged with unlawful access to computerized information, an offence carrying up to 10 years in prison, and has pleaded not guilty. Founded in 1993, the paper was one of the few critical outlets left in Russia; its former editor Dmitry Muratov dedicated his 2021 Nobel Peace Prize to six colleagues killed for their work, among them Anna Politkovskaya.
