IonQ posted third quarter revenue of 173.8 million dollars, up 336 percent year on year and its sixth consecutive record quarter, Datacenter Dynamics reported in its roundup of the quantum sector's third quarter results. The company raised its full year revenue guidance to between 505 and 515 million dollars, up from the 280 to 290 million dollars it had previously expected, and said it expects its quantum computing segment to grow 110 percent year on year.

The composition of the quarter matters as much as the headline. Revenue from IonQ's quantum platform was 90.3 million dollars, up 127 percent year on year, while SkyWater Technology, the chipmaker IonQ acquired earlier this year for 1.8 billion dollars, contributed 83.5 million dollars. Net income was 564 million dollars, down from 1.05 billion dollars in the same quarter a year earlier. Remaining performance obligations more than doubled to 1.2 billion dollars.

The rest of the listed sector remains at a far smaller scale. D-Wave posted revenue of 4.5 million dollars, up 21 percent, with a net loss of 48.2 million dollars and quarterly bookings of 1.9 million dollars, down 19 percent. Finland's IQM reported revenue of 8.9 million euros, up 51 percent, with 42.2 million euros of new orders in the quarter and a backlog of 102.5 million euros. Quantinuum grew revenue 233 percent to 9.1 million dollars but widened its adjusted EBITDA loss to 80 million dollars. Rigetti's revenue fell to 1.4 million dollars from 1.9 million, and it forecast full year revenue of 20 million dollars contingent on a customer acceptance in India in the fourth quarter. Pasqal, in its first report since its SPAC merger and August 28 Nasdaq listing, posted revenue of 3.3 million euros, down from 5.7 million a year earlier, with no quantum processing unit sales in the quarter.

The spread between IonQ and the field is now the sector's central fact. IonQ's raised guidance implies a full year above half a billion dollars, an order of magnitude beyond any peer's quarterly run rate, but nearly half of its third quarter revenue came from a conventional semiconductor foundry business it bought, not from quantum computing itself. Whether the market prices IonQ as a quantum company or as a foundry with a quantum segment attached will turn on how that mix evolves.
