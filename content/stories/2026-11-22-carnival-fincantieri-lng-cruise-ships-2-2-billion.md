Italian shipbuilder Fincantieri said on Friday it had signed a contract with Carnival Corporation to build two next-generation dual-fuel cruise ships for Carnival Cruise Line, in a deal worth more than 2 billion euros, about 2.2 billion dollars. The contract is subject to financing and other customary conditions.

The vessels will be built at Fincantieri's Marghera shipyard and delivered in 2033 and 2035. Each will have a gross tonnage of about 130,000 and around 1,650 guest cabins, smaller than the line's newest megaships and designed to reach more destinations. Both ships will be powered primarily by liquefied natural gas, with environmental and digital technologies intended to improve efficiency and reduce emissions, and Fincantieri said the design could be used across multiple Carnival brands in future.

"With 12 new ships now on order for delivery through 2039 across Carnival Cruise Line, Princess and AIDA, we have a strong pipeline that supports our measured approach to growth," Carnival chief executive Josh Weinstein said. The two ships add to the three-ship Ace-class programme already under way for Carnival Cruise Line at Fincantieri's Monfalcone yard, where construction of the first 230,000-tonne vessel began in July 2026.
