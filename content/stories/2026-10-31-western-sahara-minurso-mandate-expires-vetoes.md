Latest: the mission has now ended. Secretary-General António Guterres said on Sunday that MINURSO's mandate expired at midnight on Saturday after the Security Council failed on Friday to agree on its future, and that the mission's operations have concluded, the United Nations said in a statement published by UN News. A liquidation period began on Sunday, with a smaller team reporting to the Department of Operational Support overseeing the disposal of UN equipment and the return of troop-contributing countries' assets. Guterres said the mission's departure "does not abrogate" the people's "inalienable right to self-determination", that the UN will keep working towards a peaceful resolution through his Personal Envoy, Staffan de Mistura, and urged all concerned to exercise maximum restraint. When the mandate ended the mission was headquartered in Laayoune with nine team sites and about 200 civilian staff, military observers and staff officers from over 35 countries, and Guterres paid tribute to the 20 MINURSO personnel who lost their lives in its 35 years. Morocco welcomed the decision, Reuters reported, while the Algeria-backed Polisario Front expressed concern.

Russia vetoed the United States draft at the Security Council on Friday, despite 11 of the 15 members voting in favour and three abstaining. The draft would have extended the mission for a year, made Morocco's autonomy plan the only basis for talks, scaled back its military component and renamed it MINUSO, dropping the reference to a referendum from the name of the UN Mission for the Referendum in Western Sahara. A rival Russian draft to extend the mission for a year on existing terms then drew only three votes in favour, one against and 11 abstentions, short of the nine needed to pass.

Morocco's royal palace welcomed the failure to renew, saying the mission's personnel and equipment should leave in the coming days and that Morocco remains committed to the ceasefire. Polisario representative Sidi Omar thanked Russia for the veto, which he said protected Sahrawi self determination, while Oxfam warned that losing the mission's monitoring could put refugees at risk.
