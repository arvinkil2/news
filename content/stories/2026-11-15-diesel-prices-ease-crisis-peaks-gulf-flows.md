Global diesel prices, elevated since the start of the Iran war and the blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, have fallen sharply in recent weeks as Gulf exports recover and global oil supplies get back on track for the first time since February, France 24 reported on Sunday. In the United States, the national average retail price fell below 6 dollars a gallon on Saturday.

Europe shows the same pattern in harder data. The IRU's weighted average EU diesel price fell by more than 4 percent in the first twelve days of November to 2.09 euros a litre on November 12, its lowest level since early September and 8 percent below the September record. It remains 28 percent above its February 27 level, and prices have stayed above 2 euros a litre since the end of July.

The easing is a price story more than a supply story. Crude delivered in Europe has fallen by almost a third since early October as Middle East exports recovered, while diesel has fallen by only about a tenth, the IRU noted, and US retail diesel remains 58 percent above its February level in its data. The EIA has raised its forecast for US retail diesel in 2027 to 5.03 dollars a gallon from 4.49 dollars.

The International Energy Agency's arithmetic has improved by a notch: refinery runs, at a seasonal low of 81.3 million barrels a day in October, should reach 84.7 million in December, and the agency now expects the global diesel balance to move from a small deficit in the third quarter to a surplus of about 20,000 barrels a day in the fourth. Winter demand is the test that remains, with heating season starting against inventories that are still unusually low.
