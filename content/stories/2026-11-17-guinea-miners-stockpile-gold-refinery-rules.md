Gold miners in Guinea have been stockpiling bullion they cannot export since rules requiring local refining took effect, industry sources told Reuters, after a 90-day deadline to export unrefined gold expired on October 6. Three mining executives and two lawyers familiar with the matter said bureaucracy and a slow start to domestic refining had left producers with inventories worth tens of millions of dollars, tying up working capital, and one executive said his company had stockpiled more than 20,000 ounces over six weeks and doubled its insurance cover.

The miners affected include South Africa's AngloGold Ashanti, Russia-backed Nordgold and Australia's PDI Gold, the sources said. Guinea's Nimba Gold Refinery was inaugurated on November 1, and Mines Minister Bouna Sylla told Reuters it had begun processing gold, that a major UAE-based refiner had been appointed to operate it, and that the government was discussing commercial arrangements with mining companies. Guinea produced about 69 metric tons of gold in 2025.
