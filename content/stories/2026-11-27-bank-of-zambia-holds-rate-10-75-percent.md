Zambia's central bank left its benchmark lending rate unchanged at 10.75 percent on Friday. Governor Denny Kalyalya said policymakers judged the current stance appropriate, with inflation forecast to remain within the bank's 6 to 8 percent target band, Reuters reported. Most economists had expected the hold.

Inflation was 6.4 percent in November, inside the target range even after September's aggressive easing, when the bank cut the policy rate by 2.5 percentage points from 13.25 percent. The bank nudged up its average inflation forecast for this year and warned that El Nino could hurt harvests and limit hydroelectric output, which would raise food and electricity costs.

The decision leaves a gap of more than four percentage points between the policy rate and inflation, a signal that the bank does not intend to let price growth drift above target while it supports the economy after the large September cut. The risk it named is a supply-driven one: food and power shocks can lift inflation even when domestic demand is weak, which would keep rates higher for longer than the September cut implied.

Zambia also has IMF staff-level backing for a new 1.5 billion dollar support programme. Programmes of that kind tend to lower the extra yield investors demand to hold a country's debt, but the benefit depends on inflation credibility holding, which is what Friday's pause is designed to protect.
