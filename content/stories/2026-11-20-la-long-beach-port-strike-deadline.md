**Latest.** The Pacific Maritime Association and ILWU Local 26 have reached a tentative contract agreement covering about 250 watchmen at the ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach, averting an immediate strike at the busiest US trade gateway, FreightWaves reported. The agreement, reached under federal mediation, remains subject to ratification by Local 26 members, and the existing contract will be extended while that process proceeds, the PMA said. No terms or timeline for ratification were disclosed.

The tentative settlement follows more than a year of bargaining. The previous contract expired in July 2025, and the latest extension deadline had been set for 12:01 a.m. on November 21. Before the settlement, the PMA said its offer included a $3,200 one-time bonus and wage increases that would lift average annual earnings for full-time watchmen from more than $170,000 to $198,000, with fully employer-paid healthcare and higher pension benefits. Local 26 watchmen provide security at six container terminals across the two ports, and there were concerns that other union workers honouring picket lines would have disrupted cargo operations.

More than 110 US trade associations, including the National Retail Federation, the US Chamber of Commerce and the National Association of Manufacturers, had urged the Pacific Maritime Association and ILWU Local 26 to stay at the table and keep cargo moving through Los Angeles and Long Beach. Local 26 members work security at terminals handling about 85 percent of cargo at Long Beach and 33 percent at Los Angeles, according to the PMA.
