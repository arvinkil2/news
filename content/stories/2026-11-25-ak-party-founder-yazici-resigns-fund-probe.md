Hayati Yazici, a senior member and founder of Turkish President Tayyip Erdogan's ruling AK Party, said on Tuesday he was resigning from the party's leadership as a criminal probe into alleged market manipulation and insider trading widened to include his son. Yazici served as a minister from 2011 to 2014 and was previously Erdogan's personal lawyer.

His son, Mustafa Yazici, was questioned recently by authorities over suspected manipulative transactions on the Borsa Istanbul stock market. Authorities have imposed a travel ban on him and appointed trustees to manage companies linked to him. Yazici said neither he nor his son committed any wrongdoing or profited from the trading, and that his position in the party had been turned into an instrument in a social media campaign against him and the AK Party. "I will never allow this to happen. Therefore, I have decided to step down from the positions I currently hold," he said on X.

The probe began after Turkish investment funds that had concentrated their holdings in thinly traded stocks were unable to meet investor withdrawals from September, prompting regulators to liquidate 131 funds and launch a criminal investigation that has involved several senior officials and members of government, Reuters reported.

The case has already reached beyond the party. Fatma Betul Sayan Kaya, a former minister and senior AK Party member, was detained this month on suspicion of financial and other offences over highly profitable stock trades made just before the crisis triggered a major stock market selloff. A prosecutor last week requested authorisation to investigate Sahap Kavcioglu, a former head of the banking watchdog and former central bank governor, over suspected wrongdoing; Kavcioglu resigned this month, and senior banking watchdog officials have been jailed pending trial as part of the investigation.
