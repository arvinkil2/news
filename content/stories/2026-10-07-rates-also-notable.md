## Latest

The Federal Open Market Committee's minutes for the September 15-16 meeting, released at 2pm ET, confirmed a unanimous 12-0 vote to raise the federal funds target range 25 basis points to 3.75-4.00 percent, the first hike in three years under Chair Kevin Warsh. Most participants assessed that another increase "would likely be appropriate by year end," with no dissent and views differing only on whether a higher path was warranted on risk-management grounds. The minutes also disclosed that nominal Treasury yields rose about 35bp across the 2-10 year curve over the intermeeting period, driven by a higher expected policy path, geopolitical developments, uncertainty around Treasury's buyback program, and heavy private debt issuance to fund AI infrastructure; staff estimated August PCE inflation at 3.8 percent (3.4 percent core) with unemployment at 4.1 percent.

The hawkish tilt landed about where markets stood: October 27-28 hike odds sat near 20 percent and December near 82 percent after the minutes, barely moved from the morning, with September's +29,000 payrolls print having already killed October pricing. One notable aside: participants judged financial conditions supportive of growth despite higher long-term yields, with the exception of housing, where mortgage rates have climbed to a three-year high.

The $39 billion 10-year reopening (CUSIP 91282CRF0, 4.625 percent coupon, maturing August 2036) stopped at a high yield of 5.300 percent with a 2.77x bid-to-cover, above the 2.52 ten-auction average. The real relief was the buyer mix: indirect bidders took 80.3 percent, direct bidders 17.1 percent, and primary dealers just 2.5 percent, a dramatic snapback from Tuesday's 3-year sale where foreigners took only 57.6 percent. September's 10-year had stopped at 4.834 percent, so the market absorbed a 47bp yield climb in a month without a demand wobble.

Market reaction was muted: the 10-year yield sat near 5.28 percent late afternoon, up about a basis point after touching 5.37 percent intraday; gold fell about 1.2 percent to roughly $4,136.60; and the dollar index held around 102.3, its highest since March 2025. The $22 billion 30-year auction lands Thursday.

## Earlier

The Federal Open Market Committee published the minutes of its September 15-16 meeting at 2pm ET today, the meeting that delivered the first Fed hike in three years. The preview flagged the discussion around the decision, any dissent, and the staff's view on the inflation path after September's +29,000 payrolls print against roughly 85,000 expected. Fed speakers this week: Governor Michelle Daly said AI demand shows no sign of slowing in an Axios interview, while Jeff Schmid called beating inflation "frustrating" and said there is a slow way to go.

The Treasury auctioned $39 billion of 10-year notes today after Tuesday's $58 billion 3-year sale drew the highest yield since 2006 with the weakest foreign bid since February; $22 billion of 30-year bonds follow Thursday.

The ECB publishes its monetary-policy accounts Thursday, and France takes its first legislative act on the 2027 budget the same day after Tuesday's 14bp OAT rally. The UK sold its 91-, 182- and 364-day T-bills; Japan's Finance Ministry released September market operations and monetary base data; the South African Reserve Bank published its end-September gold and foreign exchange reserves; and the ECB's quarterly survey of credit terms in euro-denominated securities financing and OTC derivatives (SESFOD) is in the field.
