**Latest, October 28, press conference:** Chair Kevin Warsh announced a change to the meeting calendar. The FOMC will still meet eight times a year, but in 2027 one meeting will be devoted to broad strategy discussion, with no vote on the federal funds rate and no policy statement expected, and in 2028 two meetings will take that form, leaving six rate setting meetings that year. Warsh said he does not anticipate a policy decision at the strategy meetings and that the change allows a focus on longer term deliberations. Bloomberg reported that he first proposed fewer policy meetings in July, on the argument that more data can accumulate between decisions.

On the economy, Warsh said growth has strengthened, with first half growth revised up to about 2.4 percent and the third quarter likely above 3 percent, led by consumer spending, with business investment rising at about a double digit pace and capital spending historically high. He said the Committee's discussion focused mainly on the price stability side of its mandate, that inflation is too high and that energy and related prices have moved higher again in recent weeks, and that its job is to make sure inflation does not broaden out. Employment conditions are positive, he said, and more information will help the Committee determine appropriate policy steps; he declined to call financial conditions tight. Bloomberg reported after the press conference that bond traders were pricing roughly a 90 percent probability of a rate hike in December.

**Latest, October 28:** The Federal Open Market Committee held the target range for the federal funds rate at 3.75 to 4.00 percent, the level set by September's hike, and continued its policy of maintaining ample reserves. The statement was approved by a 10 to 2 vote. Lisa D. Cook and Beth M. Hammack voted against, preferring to raise the range by a quarter of a percentage point at this meeting. Two dissents in favour of an immediate hike, on a decision markets had priced at 91 percent for a hold, mark this as a hawkish hold rather than a pause in the tightening path.

The statement's account of the economy gave the dissenters their ground. Economic activity is expanding at a strong pace, domestic spending has been resilient, productivity growth is strong and capital investment is robust, in the Committee's words, while uncertainty remains elevated in part because of geopolitical developments. Job gains have kept pace with the workforce and the unemployment rate has changed little, a firmer labour reading than the soft September payrolls figure that had weakened the growth case going into the meeting.

On inflation, the Committee said it remains elevated, that gathering more information will help it determine the stance needed to return inflation to the 2 percent goal in a timely way, and concluded, in full: "The Committee will deliver price stability." The implementation note kept the interest rate on reserve balances at 3.90 percent and the primary credit rate at 4.00 percent, both effective October 29. The next decision is on December 9, and pricing going into today put a December hike as almost fully priced; nothing in the statement argues against it. Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference follows at 14:30 ET.

Treasury yields rose on Wednesday morning as the Federal Open Market Committee prepared to deliver its decision at 14:00 ET. In Asian trade the two year yield rose 1.9 basis points to 4.755 percent and the 10 year rose 3.3 basis points to 5.025 percent, according to Tradeweb levels reported by Dow Jones, and in European morning trade the 10 year was up 1.8 basis points at 5.010 percent, with euro area and UK yields also higher on the day.

The rise is a cheapening into the event, not a change of view. At the latest close in this site's market tables, the five year yield stood at 4.84 percent, up 1.6 basis points on the day and down 9.5 on the week, the 10 year at 4.99 percent, up 0.7 on the day and down 12.4 on the week, and the 30 year at 5.30 percent, up 0.4 on the day and down 12.0 on the week. The dollar index stood at 102.39, up 0.07 percent on the day and 0.6 percent on the week, after touching its highest since October 14 on Tuesday, Reuters reported.

Pricing for today is settled and pricing for December is the argument. Money markets put a 91 percent probability on no change today, according to LSEG data reported by Dow Jones, matching the 91 percent implied by CME FedWatch in Reuters' reporting, and a hike is almost fully priced for December. Amundi's Alessia Berardi, quoted by Dow Jones, put the consensus case plainly: patience should not be confused with complacency, the labour market looks less strong and market rates remain high, but the inflation outlook has not changed, so the Fed still has accommodation to remove.

Beyond the decision, supply is the next test. Investors are looking ahead to the Treasury's quarterly refunding announcement next week, and Deutsche Bank strategists noted that the Treasury's questionnaire for primary dealers ahead of that announcement, asking about structural changes in investor demand and expectations for future auction sizes, was an important step toward a discussion of adjusting long end issuance after the selloff that took 10 and 30 year yields to 24 year highs earlier in October.
