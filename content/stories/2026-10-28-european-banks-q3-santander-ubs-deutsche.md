Europe's big banks reported together on Wednesday, and the common thread is that the rate cycle is still paying them. Santander, the euro area's most valuable lender, said it added 17 million customers year on year, leading to underlying profit of 11.2 billion euros in the first nine months of 2026, up 16 percent, on revenue up 8 percent, in its own results release filed with the SEC. For the third quarter alone, underlying profit reached 3.8 billion euros, up 19 percent, while attributable profit of 3.43 billion euros was down 2 percent after higher loan loss provisions, and the bank reiterated its full year targets, including a CET1 ratio between 12.8 and 13 percent.

UBS reported net profit of 2.69 billion dollars for the quarter, up 8 percent from a year earlier and broadly in line with the 2.71 billion dollar company compiled consensus, Reuters reported, with pretax profit up 27 percent and revenue up 5 percent to 13.4 billion dollars on strong wealth management inflows in Asia Pacific and the Americas and a stronger investment bank. The Swiss bank repurchased 1.8 billion dollars of shares in the quarter and plans to complete its 3 billion dollar buyback by February, ahead of its previous mid 2027 target, even as management argued against proposed tougher Swiss capital requirements imposing undue costs. RBC analysts called the result mixed, with wealth management revenue below expectations.

Deutsche Bank posted a 2 percent rise in profit, beating forecasts, as strength in retail and corporate banking offset weakness at the investment bank, where fixed income and currency trading revenue fell 3 percent, less severe than the 5 percent decline at US peers. Chief executive Christian Sewing said the bank is firmly on track to meet its 2026 objectives. BNP Paribas, reporting the same morning, lifted net income 13.5 percent to 3.5 billion euros and announced a 1.3 billion euro buyback.

The contrast inside the same results season is HSBC, whose shares fell more than 5 percent on Tuesday after its third quarter print, covered on this site yesterday. The pattern across the week is expectations, not direction: lenders that beat a high bar were rewarded, and the one that merely met it was sold, which says more about how much rate driven earnings are now assumed than about credit quality in any of these books.
