**Latest.** Jensen Huang is weighing a return as Nvidia chief executive after resigning last week and has talked with confidantes about potentially returning, the Financial Times reported, via Investing.com. An ally said supporters were assembling a war room of Silicon Valley executives to advocate for his return, would seek White House backing, and that the effort had Huang's support; Elon Musk and Epic Games chief executive Tim Sweeney backed a public letter demanding the board bring Huang back, and Altimeter Capital founder Brad Gerstner and Atreides Management's Gavin Baker, both Nvidia shareholders, wrote to the board on Friday asking it to leave all options on the table, including Huang's return. The board has approached Arm chief executive Rene Haas as a possible successor but said no formal process has started, and the company, now led by interim chief executive Colette Kress, faces quarterly results on Tuesday; neither Nvidia nor Huang gave a direct statement on the return report.

**Latest.** The scrutiny has widened to Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick. Bessent said on CNBC on Friday that Treasury, the War and State departments and the US Trade Representative had favoured putting Megaspeed on the Commerce Department's entity list for months, and that Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security "has the pen" on that decision, The Hill reported. Lutnick has agreed to testify before the Senate Banking Committee on December 10, Senator Chris Coons said Lutnick had let the American people down, and Senator Ron Wyden and Representative Richard Neal have told Lutnick to preserve Commerce records on the H200 export approvals, writing that there appears to be a profound lack of due diligence in the approval process.

**Latest.** Bessent has taken the fight to Nvidia's board. On Fox News's Sunday Morning Futures he said the company needed a dramatic shakeup after Huang's removal, and that as the board conducts its search for a new leader it should go outside the company. He said he wants to determine whether Nvidia knowingly overlooked warning signs of shell companies, many based in Singapore, that funnelled chips to China, questioned the company's culture and whether it pressed seemingly illicit sales to lift revenue and earnings, and said he needs answers within the next 10 days from the board and outside counsel.

**Latest.** Megaspeed said on Sunday that Alice Huang left the company effective August 30, 2025, and has no current role there, Reuters reported via Investing.com. The Singapore cloud firm did not say whether she still controls it. Megaspeed said media reports about its business, supply chain and global operations are not supported by verifiable evidence, that it conducts legitimate and lawful business, that it has hired outside lawyers for a thorough internal investigation, and that its supply-chain controls are designed to comply with US export control requirements.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC there is "a lot of leakage in the system" of US export controls, and that the real question in the Nvidia diversion case is whether the company knew its customers and checked that it was not selling to what he called obviously pass through shell companies. He said that if restricted GPUs were reaching China that way, Nvidia's internal controls were not robust, criticised general counsel Timothy Teter for pressing Commerce officials over the Singapore detention of Megaspeed's Alice Wong rather than doing more oversight, and said anyone at the company responsible should be held accountable. Bessent added that he does not favour tighter export rules over enforcing the existing ones, and that he is confident Nvidia's board will investigate thoroughly.
