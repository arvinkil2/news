**Latest.** President Vladimir Putin said on Thursday that Russia will respond to the bridge strikes in a way he called far more painful than what Kyiv did to the bridge, speaking after his meeting with Presidents Trump and Xi in Shenzhen, Fox News reported, citing Reuters. Putin said the road section suffered serious damage and that Russia will restore the bridge, and he said he had discussed a potential stoppage of attacks at sea and against energy facilities with his Security Council and asked for further thought on it, before adding that after the strikes on the Crimean Bridge there will be no agreements. A Russian official told the Associated Press that the latest strike halted rail traffic, and President Zelenskyy arrived in Berlin the same day for talks with more than 40 partners, including Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff, on ending the war.

**Latest.** With the road span down, only trains can carry people and goods between Crimea and Russia for the foreseeable future, and road traffic must divert to the coastal M-14 highway, which is exposed to Ukrainian drones and has limited capacity, RFE/RL reported. Engineers told the report that rebuilding the road span would take six months to more than a year, with about a year the best case, and that clearing the blocked shipping channel could take a week or two if salvage work is not contested. The report also said Ukrainian forces reportedly damaged another major bridge on November 17, on the route linking Crimea to Rostov-on-Don via Taganrog, and described scattered panic buying and fuel queues in Crimea.

**Latest.** Rail traffic over the bridge was temporarily suspended after the latest attack, Deutsche Welle reported, citing Crimea's Russian installed governor Sergei Aksyonov, who later said traffic was set to be restored starting from around 4 p.m. local time on Thursday. TASS reported, citing Deputy Prime Minister Marat Khusnullin, that it may take a few months for some sections of the bridge to become operational; the report did not specify which sections. No Ukrainian claim for the latest attack was quoted in the DW report.

Latest: the bridge was attacked again on Wednesday, for the third time this week, The Guardian reported. Crimea's Kremlin appointed governor, Sergei Aksyonov, wrote on Telegram that traffic had been temporarily halted after the new attack, that all relevant services were on site and that an assessment of the damage was under way. The Guardian put this week's damage at a 227 metre section of the road span destroyed, with the rail corridor apparently still operable despite some damage, and noted the strike landed hours before Presidents Putin, Xi and Trump meet in China. Ukraine has not claimed responsibility for Wednesday's attack in the report; President Zelenskyy had hailed this week's earlier strikes as an unprecedented operation, and an unnamed source in Ukraine's armed forces told the paper the attacks were designed to embarrass Putin on the world stage.

Earlier: debris from the collapsed road span has fallen into the Kerch-Yenikale Canal, the only navigable passage connecting the Sea of Azov with the Black Sea, Splash247 reported. Andrey Sizov, managing director of Russian agricultural consultancy SovEcon, warned that removing the collapsed structure could substantially delay any resumption of commercial shipping; the Sea of Azov normally accounts for about 25 percent of Russian grain exports, according to SovEcon, but shipping through the strait has been suspended since July following repeated drone attacks, and no official timetable for clearing the channel has been confirmed. The railway section was also damaged, although Russian authorities subsequently reported the resumption of limited train services.

The maritime battle widened the same night. Russia's defence ministry claimed on Wednesday morning that its forces had struck port infrastructure at Izmail on the Danube and an unnamed general cargo ship sailing towards Pivdennyi, allegedly carrying dual-use military supplies; Moscow provided no vessel identification and the claims have not been independently confirmed. Ukrainian officials separately reported Russian strikes on energy infrastructure in the southern Odesa region, leaving parts of Izmail without electricity. Ukrainian drones also struck the Gazprom Neftekhim Salavat refinery in Bashkortostan on Tuesday, a complex capable of processing about 10 million tonnes of oil a year; Russian regional authorities said operations were continuing without critical damage, although footage showed fires around the facility.

Latest: BBC Verify has now verified the collapse Kyiv claimed earlier on Tuesday. Ukraine's operation ran in seven stages over several weeks, with the Security Service and the General Staff first phasing out air defences, radars, electronic warfare assets and other designated targets, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said. Drones hit the bridge on Monday night and visibly damaged the road arch, and final strikes in the early hours of Tuesday brought the weakened arch down. BBC Verify verified video filmed from a camera on board a Ukrainian made Flamingo missile approaching the main span, and daylight images show the arch, about 227 metres long and weighing thousands of tonnes, collapsed into the strait with about 200 metres of roadway below the sea. The parallel rail arch remains standing, although it may also have been damaged; all traffic was initially suspended and trains were later allowed to resume. Two engineers told BBC Verify the damage to the main span is catastrophic, with one estimating that reinstating full road traffic will take over a year. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said the bridge will be rebuilt. The BBC report gave no casualty figures, and noted the fallen arch may have blocked the shipping channel used by Sea of Azov ports including Mariupol and Berdyansk.

Earlier: Ukraine attacked the Kerch Bridge for a second consecutive day on Tuesday. Crimea's Moscow-installed governor, Sergei Aksyonov, said traffic on the bridge was suspended after the attack, and Al Jazeera reported that a Ukrainian drone attack killed at least two people in Crimea. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy confirmed the attack, published footage that he said showed the bridge's main arch and part of its roadway collapsed, and said the strike answered Russian attacks on Ukraine's infrastructure. The collapse claim is Ukraine's own and has not been independently verified; Russian officials had said on Monday that the bridge suffered only minor damage.

Russia struck inside Kyiv the same night. Mayor Vitali Klitschko said the North and South bridges were hit and damaged and that the subway system was also damaged, with water and power supplies partially cut. Both bridges were closed to traffic, though they remain standing, and heating was cut to hundreds of apartment buildings on the Left Bank, Al Jazeera reported from Kyiv. Agence France-Presse, reporting the same closures, said several critical infrastructure systems were damaged and water and heating supply was interrupted in some districts, and carried no casualty report for the Kyiv strikes. Russia's Defense Ministry said it shot down 1,441 Ukrainian drones overnight, a figure from a party to the conflict that AFP ranks as the second highest daily total this year on its tally of official Russian statements, and said its own strikes also targeted facilities producing cruise missiles and drones, transport and energy sites, and port infrastructure.

Earlier: a second wave reached Moscow on Monday evening. Moscow Mayor Sergey Sobyanin said air defences destroyed another 23 drones flying towards the capital, with emergency services working where debris fell, and TASS, counting from Sobyanin's own messages, put the total shot down on the approach to Moscow at 180 since 8 p.m. Moscow time on Monday. The TASS report carried no mention of casualties or of specific damage. Ukraine has published no statement on this wave and no independent count is available, so the scale rests on Moscow's account alone.

Earlier on Monday, Russia's Defense Ministry said its air defenses shot down 1,336 Ukrainian drones across 16 regions overnight, including over Crimea and the waters of the Sea of Azov and the Black Sea, a count above the previous record of 1,254 it recorded on October 29. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said the Crimean Bridge suffered only minor damage, that an assessment was under way, and that traffic on the bridge continues, updating the earlier restriction. Local authorities in Belgorod region said one person was killed and another injured by drone attacks there.

Ukraine attacked Russia with a large drone barrage early on November 16, hitting the Crimean Bridge and, according to Bloomberg, likely an oil refinery as well. Crimea's Moscow-installed governor, Sergei Aksyonov, said on Telegram that traffic on the bridge would remain interrupted until about midday, that services were working on the consequences of the attack, and that an emergency coordination centre had been set up in Kerch; the ferry service between Crimea and the port of Kavkaz was still operating.

Neither report gave a verified count of drones or described the extent of any damage to the bridge, and no Ukrainian official statement was carried in either. Bloomberg reported the strike came after US-brokered peace talks ended without a breakthrough.
