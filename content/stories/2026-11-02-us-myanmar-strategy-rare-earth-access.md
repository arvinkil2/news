Latest: Myanmar's military-led government is urging the United States to deepen its re-engagement and rapidly boost investment in the country's rare earth mines, Reuters reported on Tuesday. In a newsletter published on Monday by Washington lobbying firm DCI Group on behalf of Myanmar's information ministry, the government called minerals the natural place for the relationship to grow and said the strategy document's aim of at least three major commercial or investment deals valued above 5 million dollars each is modest given Myanmar's resources and America's stated ambition to compete with China. The main production sites in northern Kachin are controlled by the Kachin Independence Army, a rebel group fighting the military, and Washington, which froze Myanmar's assets at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York and imposed sanctions after the 2021 coup, reversed course by holding direct talks in September; the State Department did not immediately respond to Reuters' request for comment.

The State Department's integrated country strategy for Myanmar says the US mission will facilitate at least three multistakeholder forums producing concrete agreements or policy proposals advancing fiscal federalism and territorial demarcation, which the document says will create opportunities to access Myanmar's rare earth resources and combat scam centers, Reuters reported on Monday. The strategy also aims to secure at least three major commercial or investment deals valued above $5 million each for US companies.

Reuters reported last month that US officials have quietly held talks with Myanmar's nominally civilian government, a departure from years of policy aimed at isolating the military, and that the talks have included how to gain access to rare earth minerals located mainly in conflict-affected areas and currently exported to China. The strategy document does not spell out whether the US intends to lift sanctions or address other compliance concerns that deter investment, and the State Department did not respond to Reuters' request for comment.
