**Latest:** The United States is set to add Singapore-based chip firm Megaspeed to its trade blacklist on Monday, a source told Reuters, in a move that would bar US companies from shipping goods, software and technology to Megaspeed without a licence that is likely to be denied. Megaspeed is closely tied to the espionage investigation involving Nvidia: its controlling shareholder is Alice Huang, the Chinese businesswoman US authorities have reportedly concluded was a Chinese intelligence asset, an allegation China has called groundless and which Reuters said it could not independently verify. Nvidia lists Megaspeed as an official cloud partner. The blacklist report rests on a single unnamed source, and no response from Megaspeed, Nvidia or the Commerce Department is reported.

**Latest:** Jensen Huang is scheduled to testify publicly before the Senate Banking Committee on December 3, according to a notice on the committee's website cited by TechCrunch, hours after the board reinstated him as chief executive. The committee oversees export control policy, and the public hearing follows bipartisan calls for Huang to testify about his relationship with a woman suspected by the US government of working as a Chinese spy and about possible involvement in chip-smuggling operations. Before his reinstatement, Huang had already agreed to appear on December 1 before the House Foreign Affairs Committee, the Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party and the House Intelligence Committee, TechCrunch reported.

**Latest:** Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer said on Monday that Democrats will wield their full power to investigate any wrongdoing by the Trump administration, Jensen Huang, other Nvidia executives and anyone, in his words, selling out US national security for financial gain, The Hill reported. In a post on X on Monday morning, Schumer said the administration's rush to reinstate Huang underscores the need for Congress to act, and accused President Trump of putting his own financial interests and those of his billionaire allies before national security. The Hill named no committee, subpoena or hearing in connection with the pledge, and said it had sought comment from the White House and Nvidia.

**Latest:** President Donald Trump said on Monday that he had spoken to Nvidia's board directly and indirectly and had convinced them to reinstate Huang, The Guardian reported, quoting remarks he gave to CNBC. "I've been speaking to the board directly and indirectly, and I felt very strongly about it," Trump said, adding that he felt more strongly as he saw what was happening with the company, which he called a very important part of the country. The Guardian's report adds that he assured the board on national security risks. The claim goes beyond his public appeal of November 20: the president is now describing private contact with the board before its decision, not only support after it.

**Latest:** President Donald Trump welcomed the reinstatement on Monday, writing on Truth Social that the board had taken the correct and appropriate decision and that the United States government has no reason to believe Huang's return represents a national security risk, El País reported. Trump said Attorney General Todd Blanche had confirmed the government has no evidence Huang engaged in any illegal conduct, called Huang a great patriot and a national treasure, and said Nvidia must continue its internal investigation into compliance with United States export controls. El País also reported that Nvidia's own announcement said the board decided unanimously to allow Huang's return after a process that included consultations with the Trump government, and that the board is confident the reinstatement poses no national security risk.

**Latest:** Nvidia shares opened about 33 percent higher at $167.08 on Monday and the Nasdaq Composite opened up 7.30 percent at 24,269.75, Yonhap reported from New York, as markets reacted to the news of Huang's return after a sharp fall in AI-related stocks last week.

Nvidia's board of directors reinstated Jen-Hsun Huang as the company's president, chief executive officer and a member of the board on November 22, effective immediately, the company disclosed in a Form 8-K filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission on Monday. The filing states that, following the reinstatement, Colette M. Kress will continue to serve as executive vice president and chief financial officer. Kress signed the filing in that capacity on November 23. A press release dated November 23 announcing the reinstatement is attached to the filing as Exhibit 99.1.

The reinstatement reverses the position the board took last week. Huang resigned earlier this month after reporting on his relationship with a Megaspeed executive whom US intelligence officials believe gathered intelligence for Beijing. Kress then served as interim chief executive, and board chair Harvey Jones said on the November 17 earnings call that the board had unanimously accepted Huang's resignation and did not intend to reinstate him, CNBC reported. The board had also hired Heidrick & Struggles to run a chief executive search, and the firm had contacted Arm Holdings chief executive Rene Haas about the job, Bloomberg News reported on November 18.

President Donald Trump publicly intervened on November 20, asking Nvidia to reinstate Huang, calling him one of the brightest minds in the world and saying the company should give him a second chance. The filing gives no reason for the board's change of position, and it does not address the investigations and congressional inquiries that followed Huang's resignation, including a reported SEC examination of the company's accounting and the administration's questions about chip sales to China.
