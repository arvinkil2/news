**Latest:** Jensen Huang apologized to Nvidia employees at an all-hands meeting on Tuesday for the conduct that led to his brief ouster, Bloomberg reported, via Investing.com, citing people familiar with the meeting. Huang told staff he had let them down and acknowledged violating the company's code of conduct, calling the gathering the hardest company meeting he had ever attended. He thanked chief financial officer Colette Kress for leading the company during his nearly two-week absence, said he was grateful for a second chance, and told staff the board had reinstated him after evaluating the facts and determining there was no national security threat, adding that Nvidia remained apolitical. The Wall Street Journal separately reported that Huang is undergoing daily therapy. Nvidia shares fell more than 7 percent on Tuesday after surging nearly 31 percent on Monday, Investing.com reported.

**Latest:** Attorney General Todd Blanche pushed back on reporters' questions on Tuesday about whether the Justice Department had investigated Huang, The Hill reported. Asked directly, Blanche said he would not discuss his communications with the president, and that if the department had had information that Huang was committing crimes, he would have told the president. Pressed again on whether Huang had been investigated, he asked what that meant and said the reporting he had seen did not suggest Huang himself was under investigation. He declined to share intelligence findings about Alice Huang, noted that the department has hundreds of export investigations running at any time, and called Nvidia a good corporate citizen. The Justice Department and the Commerce Department have confirmed they are investigating Megaspeed, without detailing any probe into Huang or Nvidia.

**Latest:** Former Vice President Mike Pence attacked the reinstatement in posts on X on Tuesday, The Hill reported, saying Huang had to go and should have stayed gone, and calling on Congress to investigate how Nvidia's most advanced semiconductor technology reached companies doing business with China and to impose tougher export controls. Pence said allegations that Nvidia engaged in the sale of sensitive semiconductors to companies transferring the technology to China were totally unacceptable. He joins other Republicans breaking with the president on the decision, including former House Speaker Newt Gingrich, who called for Congress to dig into the matter as a national security issue.

**Latest:** The political isolation of the president's position widened on Tuesday, CNBC reported. Republican Senator Kevin Cramer said on CNBC that the risk that a Chinese spy could hold information about a key individual in the supply chain was not worth taking, and that guardrails would be needed around Huang. Democratic Representative Jamie Raskin asked when a president had ever called into a corporate board meeting and essentially dictated a chief executive choice. CNBC reported that bipartisan lawmakers are racing to pass export-control bills before the end of the year, that Republicans will open the investigations next week before ceding power to Democrats in January, and that Elon Musk was among the few prominent voices backing the return, writing on Monday that the mistake had been corrected.

**Latest:** Jensen Huang admitted to Nvidia's board, during a meeting before his reinstatement, that he had had more affairs beyond the two that have been reported in the news, The Wall Street Journal reported, via TechCrunch. It is not clear which affairs Huang admitted to in that conversation. The Journal also reported that Attorney General Todd Blanche told President Trump and Vice President JD Vance over the weekend that the Justice Department was not investigating Huang and that there was no evidence he was a national security risk, after which both contacted the board.

**Latest:** Nvidia shares fell 3.9 percent in premarket trading as of 4:40 a.m. ET on Tuesday, CNBC reported, after the stock closed 30 percent higher on Monday following Huang's reinstatement. Other United States semiconductor stocks were also lower in premarket trading, with Micron, Marvell Technology, AMD and Arm all down more than 3 percent. Mizuho analysts upgraded the stock on Monday, writing that Huang's return restores investor confidence in the AI trade, while Bank of America warned that reinstatement should not be confused with the end of scrutiny and that attention around governance, China and export controls, customer relationships and compliance would persist. Senator Elizabeth Warren called the reinstatement reckless in a statement on Monday.

**Latest:** The Asian rally broadened beyond Seoul as the session went on. South Korea's Kospi was up about 7 percent, Taiwan's Weighted Index surged 9.1 percent and Japan's Nikkei 225 rose 4.6 percent, Investing.com reported. TSMC jumped nearly 10 percent, SK Hynix rose 7.9 percent and Advantest, a Nvidia supplier, gained 15 percent in Tokyo. The figures are intraday and single-sourced.

**Latest:** South Korea's Kospi surged in early trading on Tuesday as Asian markets reacted to Huang's reinstatement, rising 290.94 points, or 6.18 percent, to 4,999.48 at 09:03 in Seoul after opening up 3.64 percent at 4,880.16, and briefly recovering the 5,000 level, Yonhap reported. The Kosdaq rose 21.41 points, or 3.37 percent, to 656.86 at the same time. The figures are intraday, not closing levels.

**Latest:** The Entity List rule itself, read in full from the Bureau of Industry and Security's public inspection copy, says the End-User Review Committee added the seven companies because of "the significant risk of these entities being or becoming involved in the diversion of advanced computing commodities to unauthorized end uses or end users." Each listing carries a licence requirement for all items subject to the Export Administration Regulations, with licence applications reviewed under a presumption of denial. The final rule takes effect on its public inspection date, ahead of formal Federal Register publication on November 25, and a savings clause lets shipments already en route on that date proceed under the previous rules if completed within 30 days.

**Latest:** The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security has added seven Megaspeed-linked companies to the Entity List, in a final rule filed for the Federal Register on Monday (document 2026-23999, scheduled for publication on November 25). The seven are Megaspeed International Pte. Ltd., Apex Enterprise Solutions (Singapore) Private Limited and Swiftdata Pte. Ltd. in Singapore; MSFusion Sdn. Bhd., PT Megaspeed International and Speedmatrix Sdn Bhd in Malaysia; and Megaspeed (Thailand) Co., Ltd. in Thailand. The listing requires a US government licence for exports of items subject to the Export Administration Regulations to any of the seven. The New York Times reported that the decision followed weeks of debate inside the administration, with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent favouring quicker action while Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick's department held the export-control decision. The rule is a risk finding that names neither Nvidia nor Huang, and Megaspeed remains under a Justice Department criminal investigation into alleged smuggling of AI chips to China, the Times reported.

**Latest:** The United States is set to add Singapore-based chip firm Megaspeed to its trade blacklist on Monday, a source told Reuters, in a move that would bar US companies from shipping goods, software and technology to Megaspeed without a licence that is likely to be denied. Megaspeed is closely tied to the espionage investigation involving Nvidia: its controlling shareholder is Alice Huang, the Chinese businesswoman US authorities have reportedly concluded was a Chinese intelligence asset, an allegation China has called groundless and which Reuters said it could not independently verify. Nvidia lists Megaspeed as an official cloud partner. The blacklist report rests on a single unnamed source, and no response from Megaspeed, Nvidia or the Commerce Department is reported.

**Latest:** Jensen Huang is scheduled to testify publicly before the Senate Banking Committee on December 3, according to a notice on the committee's website cited by TechCrunch, hours after the board reinstated him as chief executive. The committee oversees export control policy, and the public hearing follows bipartisan calls for Huang to testify about his relationship with a woman suspected by the US government of working as a Chinese spy and about possible involvement in chip-smuggling operations. Before his reinstatement, Huang had already agreed to appear on December 1 before the House Foreign Affairs Committee, the Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party and the House Intelligence Committee, TechCrunch reported.

**Latest:** Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer said on Monday that Democrats will wield their full power to investigate any wrongdoing by the Trump administration, Jensen Huang, other Nvidia executives and anyone, in his words, selling out US national security for financial gain, The Hill reported. In a post on X on Monday morning, Schumer said the administration's rush to reinstate Huang underscores the need for Congress to act, and accused President Trump of putting his own financial interests and those of his billionaire allies before national security. The Hill named no committee, subpoena or hearing in connection with the pledge, and said it had sought comment from the White House and Nvidia.

**Latest:** President Donald Trump said on Monday that he had spoken to Nvidia's board directly and indirectly and had convinced them to reinstate Huang, The Guardian reported, quoting remarks he gave to CNBC. "I've been speaking to the board directly and indirectly, and I felt very strongly about it," Trump said, adding that he felt more strongly as he saw what was happening with the company, which he called a very important part of the country. The Guardian's report adds that he assured the board on national security risks. The claim goes beyond his public appeal of November 20: the president is now describing private contact with the board before its decision, not only support after it.

**Latest:** President Donald Trump welcomed the reinstatement on Monday, writing on Truth Social that the board had taken the correct and appropriate decision and that the United States government has no reason to believe Huang's return represents a national security risk, El País reported. Trump said Attorney General Todd Blanche had confirmed the government has no evidence Huang engaged in any illegal conduct, called Huang a great patriot and a national treasure, and said Nvidia must continue its internal investigation into compliance with United States export controls. El País also reported that Nvidia's own announcement said the board decided unanimously to allow Huang's return after a process that included consultations with the Trump government, and that the board is confident the reinstatement poses no national security risk.

**Latest:** Nvidia shares opened about 33 percent higher at $167.08 on Monday and the Nasdaq Composite opened up 7.30 percent at 24,269.75, Yonhap reported from New York, as markets reacted to the news of Huang's return after a sharp fall in AI-related stocks last week.

Nvidia's board of directors reinstated Jen-Hsun Huang as the company's president, chief executive officer and a member of the board on November 22, effective immediately, the company disclosed in a Form 8-K filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission on Monday. The filing states that, following the reinstatement, Colette M. Kress will continue to serve as executive vice president and chief financial officer. Kress signed the filing in that capacity on November 23. A press release dated November 23 announcing the reinstatement is attached to the filing as Exhibit 99.1.

The reinstatement reverses the position the board took last week. Huang resigned earlier this month after reporting on his relationship with a Megaspeed executive whom US intelligence officials believe gathered intelligence for Beijing. Kress then served as interim chief executive, and board chair Harvey Jones said on the November 17 earnings call that the board had unanimously accepted Huang's resignation and did not intend to reinstate him, CNBC reported. The board had also hired Heidrick & Struggles to run a chief executive search, and the firm had contacted Arm Holdings chief executive Rene Haas about the job, Bloomberg News reported on November 18.

President Donald Trump publicly intervened on November 20, asking Nvidia to reinstate Huang, calling him one of the brightest minds in the world and saying the company should give him a second chance. The filing gives no reason for the board's change of position, and it does not address the investigations and congressional inquiries that followed Huang's resignation, including a reported SEC examination of the company's accounting and the administration's questions about chip sales to China.
