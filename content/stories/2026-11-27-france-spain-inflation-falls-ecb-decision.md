## Latest

Bundesbank president Joachim Nagel said on Friday that the European Central Bank may have to raise interest rates further as soon as next month, because euro-area inflation is likely to stay above 3 percent for some time next year. "We're assuming that in 2027 we could remain durably above 3%," Nagel, who heads Germany's Bundesbank and sits on the ECB's Governing Council, told an event in Frankfurt, as reported by Reuters. "This means that, on the monetary policy side, we might have to do even more. We'll be meeting again in December." His warning points beyond the December 17 decision and lands on the same day as the softer French and Spanish inflation prints reported below.

Inflation fell more sharply than expected in France and Spain in November, as declining fuel prices pulled the annual rates down in both countries. In France, consumer prices were 3.2 percent higher than a year earlier on the harmonised measure, down from 3.7 percent in October and below the 3.6 percent economists polled by The Wall Street Journal had expected, the national statistics office said on Friday. The national measure eased to 2.9 percent from 3.4 percent. In Spain, annual inflation dropped to 4.1 percent from 4.9 percent in October, against a consensus near 4.7 to 4.9 percent, with core inflation easing to 2.6 percent from 3.1 percent.

The French release also carried weaker activity data. Third-quarter gross domestic product was revised to a 0.2 percent contraction, from a first estimate of a 0.1 percent fall, and consumer spending was flat in October. Services inflation in France fell in November on a sharp decrease in prices for accommodation and transport services, suggesting limited spillover so far from energy costs into the rest of the economy.

The readings land three weeks before the European Central Bank's December 17 decision. The ECB has raised its key rate twice this year, to a deposit rate of 2.50 percent, and investors are positioned for a third increase next month. The central bank has said it will decide on a data-dependent basis, and Friday's figures point to weaker inflationary pressure than the October euro area reading of 4.0 percent had suggested.

Two qualifications cut the other way. European natural gas prices remain near multiyear highs, which will keep household energy bills elevated through the winter, and the European Commission said last week that it expects French inflation to peak at 4.3 percent in the first quarter of 2027 as energy costs pass through into food and services. The ECB's own consumer survey, conducted from October 1 to November 2, found households expecting prices to rise 3.5 percent over the coming year, up from 3.1 percent in September. The euro area flash estimate for November follows on December 1.
