## Latest

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has welcomed the prospect of Europeans at the table. After brief talks with Witkoff and Kushner in Dublin, where he was attending a European summit, Zelenskyy said he was pleased at the prospect of further rounds of talks with European participation, and that the sides had decided to work on an agenda covering de-escalation steps and ceasefires, Reuters reported. "It's important that everything is unblocked," he said. Witkoff, speaking on Russian state television after the Kremlin meeting, said Putin had agreed to the European seat: "The Europeans will join at the next meeting, which we consider to be a very important moment because they are our partners and it's important to us that they take part." Putin said Moscow had never opposed dialogue with Europe and that it was Europe that broke it off, and warned that a European idea of sending Western troops into Ukraine after a peace deal would trigger a resumption of the war. Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov, in his own briefing, still did not confirm that Europeans would join the next round, as reported below. Each side's account here is its own stated position.

The Kremlin has not confirmed the European seat Witkoff announced. In a briefing after the talks, Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov did not confirm that Europeans would join the next round and called the current European stance on Ukraine a recipe for endless war, the Associated Press reported. Putin also proposed a joint Russian-Ukrainian humanitarian mission to Kostiantynivka in eastern Ukraine, saying Russia would need five days to clear mines around the city, where he said 400 to 500 residents remain, and that the mission would show who controls the city, a control claim Ukraine has disputed in the past. Ushakov said the US envoys welcomed the idea and would be in touch; Kyiv has previously worried that such missions could let Moscow regroup. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, whom the envoys were due to meet in Dublin, called the US approach of discussing a new European security order with Russia a bit strange. Each side's account here is its own stated position.

President Vladimir Putin took a hard line in the Kremlin talks with US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff on Thursday, The Guardian reported. Putin refused to soften Russia's territorial demands and insisted on full control of the four Ukrainian regions Moscow claims to have annexed, ruled out accepting foreign peacekeeping troops in Ukraine, calling such a deployment a "continuation of war", and warned that Russia would target any foreign troops deployed to Ukraine. After the meeting, Witkoff confirmed that European representatives would join the next round of talks, and Putin said a four-way dialogue would follow once Europe appoints a chief negotiator authorised to speak on its behalf. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas said a focus on a European security architecture was a distraction without a ceasefire. The envoys were then due in Dublin to debrief President Volodymyr Zelenskyy.

The European Union's two presidents have set out the Dublin position in writing. In a joint statement after meeting President Zelenskyy at the European Political Community summit, European Council President António Costa and Commission President Ursula von der Leyen welcomed this week's Abu Dhabi talks and the renewed momentum in the peace process, said the European Union was present at the talks and will continue to be involved in the format, and said the bloc will keep providing considerable financial and military assistance. They said the next European Council, on November 26 to 27, will discuss Ukraine's urgent financing needs for 2027 on the basis of the Commission's proposal, and that the Commission will in parallel present its 22nd package of sanctions against Russia. On winter support, the statement said the Commission is mobilising 500 generators from the rescEU reserves, helping establish emergency support centres, and supporting the delivery of four floating bridges from Norway and 200 buses.

Britain has widened the pressure track while the Moscow meeting runs. The UK government announced on Thursday that it has sanctioned Slavneft, making Britain the first country to have sanctioned all ten of Russia's major oil producers, in a package that also covers shadow fleet vessels carrying Russian oil and LNG, major coal companies, and Rosneft's Arctic project LLC Vostok Oil. Prime Minister Andy Burnham, arriving at the European Political Community summit in Dublin after COP31 in Türkiye, said peace talks have progressed but now is not the time for complacency, and called on leaders from around 50 countries to keep pressure on President Putin to approach negotiations seriously and agree a full and immediate ceasefire. Burnham is expected to co-chair a small group meeting on Ukraine at the summit on Thursday afternoon alongside President Zelenskyy, and to press for robust, legally binding security guarantees as part of any peace process.

The meeting in Moscow was set for noon local time. Kushner and Witkoff landed at Vnukovo airport on Thursday morning, where Dmitriev met them at the plane and accompanied them to central Moscow, and they were due to meet Putin at 12:00 local time (09:00 GMT), the BBC and Al Jazeera reported. The US delegation brought Putin an updated proposal for a peace plan, Al Jazeera reported from Moscow, and the central ask on format is unchanged: Kushner said the envoys would ask Putin to give his blessing to inviting European representatives into future discussions, and urged Europe to identify one person to speak on its behalf, while Moscow has opposed European involvement. Kushner said on Tuesday the US had seen some openings and some opportunities and would try to make some progress at the meeting. The envoys are scheduled to meet Zelenskyy in Dublin on Friday. Overnight, while the diplomacy moved, Russia launched ballistic missiles and combat drones at Ukraine: a woman was killed and five people injured when a drone hit a residential block in Kyiv, Ukrainian officials said, 89 of 123 combat drones were shot down, and strikes on energy infrastructure caused power outages in nine regions. Those figures are Ukrainian officials' account as reported by Al Jazeera. Ukraine's military said it has struck Russia's five largest oil refineries since October 30.

The diplomacy has reached Moscow, and Kyiv has put a battlefield problem on the record the same morning. US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner arrived in Moscow on Thursday, greeted by Russian presidential envoy Kirill Dmitriev, and were expected to meet President Vladimir Putin to advance the issues discussed in Abu Dhabi, The Guardian reported in live coverage; Russia's deputy foreign minister Sergei Ryabkov said it was too early to say the necessary trust had been restored between Russia and Ukraine. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy posted on X that Ukraine's army is once again experiencing significant disruptions to Starshield's operations over Ukraine, that the problem has run for several days along the entire front line and in the occupied territories, and that systematic speed restrictions are being imposed on the system, limiting it to 161 kilometres per hour. He said Ukraine's military has investigated and concluded the restrictions are systematic rather than random errors, that interception systems cannot reach sufficient speed to stop Russian jet-powered drones in time, and that Kyiv is establishing the circumstances with the American side. The Starshield account is Zelenskyy's stated position; no independent corroboration had appeared in the coverage read.

From Moscow the envoys were expected to fly to Dublin to debrief Zelenskyy on Thursday evening on the margins of the European Political Community summit. Ireland's Taoiseach Micheál Martin said the objective was to debrief the Ukrainian president on the envoys' engagement with Putin, welcomed a potential prisoner swap discussed in Abu Dhabi as a significant measure in itself, and stressed the need for a clear, coherent European voice in the talks. France's Europe minister Benjamin Haddad said the EU will approve new sanctions on Russia on Monday covering 150 additional entities and 50 banks, with France proposing the bulk of the additions, according to Reuters reporting carried in the same coverage.

Washington has widened the brief for Thursday's Moscow meeting. The United States has asked Russia to submit a new proposal for a future security agreement with Europe, one that includes Russian steps to ease tension with the continent, Axios reported on Wednesday, citing two sources with knowledge of the matter, in reporting carried in full by ENM News. US officials describe the steps they want as symmetrical: concrete moves that would reassure Europe that Moscow does not intend to launch another attack. The White House position, as reported, is that a Europe-Russia security arrangement and a Russia-Ukraine peace deal should be negotiated in parallel, with any security implementation coming only after a settlement ends the war, that Washington will not accept a security deal unless both Europe and Ukraine are on board, and that Moscow will not be allowed to veto Europe's security choices. The Americans also want President Vladimir Putin to open the door for European representatives to join the next round of peace talks. Russia's delegation head Vladimir Medinsky has said Moscow wants a broader security settlement with Europe as part of any end to the war, but Russia has not been willing to bring Europeans into the talks, and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen has said Europe will only discuss a new security architecture after the war ends. Envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff meet Putin in Moscow on Thursday and President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Dublin on Thursday night, where the prisoner exchange numbers are also to be finalised. The request is attributed to unnamed sources and no US or Russian official had confirmed it on the record in the reports read.

The BBC's wrap of the round, published on Wednesday, confirms the conclusion reported below and adds the negotiators' own characterisation. It describes the talks as ending without a breakthrough and without a hint of one, though the American side said it saw some openings, and frames the round as centred on Europe's post-war security architecture: reassurance for Kyiv that allies would come to its aid if it were attacked again, against Moscow's wish to limit NATO's influence in eastern Europe. Ukrainian negotiator Davyd Arakhamia called this round one of the hardest, saying the Russians came with very strict directives, and urged a second round as soon as possible, with no date set. The BBC also reports the sequencing split directly: Kyiv and the Europeans want a ceasefire before a security framework is discussed, while the Kremlin insists fighting stops only after an agreement is reached. A German government spokesperson said agreements cannot be implemented if no consensus can be reached among NATO members. Each side's characterisation is that side's stated position.

The two day talks concluded with an agreement in principle on a new prisoner exchange and no wider deal. Russia's chief negotiator Vladimir Medinsky said the sides had agreed on a new, large scale prisoner exchange, with concrete details to be announced later, and US envoy Jared Kushner called it the biggest humanitarian release of prisoners, with the numbers to be finalised on Thursday, Deutsche Welle reported in live coverage carrying AP, AFP, Reuters and dpa reporting. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy hailed what he called an important humanitarian arrangement on preparations for a new exchange. No number and no exchange date were given, and no ceasefire, territorial agreement or signed text was reported. Ukraine's chief negotiator Rustem Umerov said the sides had stated their positions directly and significant differences remain, that the first steps must be an end to the war and security guarantees for Ukraine, and that difficult work lies ahead. Ukrainian negotiator David Arakhamia spoke of good momentum and hoped another session could be held as soon as possible. Zelenskyy also pressed for Europe to be represented at every stage of the diplomacy, and Ukrainian, US and European representatives met separately after the formal talks with Russia ended.

Jared Kushner said after the talks closed that he and Steve Witkoff will travel to Moscow on Thursday, November 12, to meet President Vladimir Putin, discuss the Abu Dhabi results and try to make further progress, and will then go to Dublin to meet President Volodymyr Zelensky, Vedomosti reported. Kushner called the second day difficult and substantive, said there is neither sympathy nor trust between the leaders, and set the sequencing for what comes next: no agreement on Ukraine's postwar security will be concluded before agreements on territory, settling the territorial disagreements will be the hardest part of the negotiations, and resolving them is a necessary condition for any security agreement after the conflict ends. He said the American side is working on arrangements that would ensure Ukraine's sovereignty and security and a durable peace in Europe. Those statements are the US negotiator's account as reported by a Russian newspaper; neither Washington nor Kyiv had published its own account of the closing session in the sources read at the time of this update.

The second day of the talks has ended. Vedomosti, in Russian, reported on Wednesday, citing a RIA Novosti source in the negotiating group, that the trilateral session in Abu Dhabi concluded after less than two hours behind closed doors, with Vladimir Medinsky leading the Russian delegation, Rustem Umerov the Ukrainian delegation, and Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner representing the United States. TASS sources told the paper a new prisoner exchange could be one outcome of the meeting; no exchange was announced as agreed, and the Russian side was expected to speak to the press afterwards. Politico had reported the territorial question was expected to be a key topic of the second day. This account of the closing session is Russian-side reporting via state wires; no joint statement had been published and neither Kyiv nor Washington had confirmed the day's proceedings at the time of this update.

President Volodymyr Zelensky said on Tuesday, at a press conference during his visit to Denmark, that the Abu Dhabi talks discussed a large prisoner exchange of 1,000 for 1,000, possibly 1,500 for 1,500, and that the Americans would take that agenda to Russia, according to UNIAN reporting, in Ukrainian. No exchange was agreed. He said Wednesday, November 11, is the main day of the talks and will cover de-escalation steps, energy, the Black Sea, the exchange again and a possible trilateral meeting at leaders level. The Russian delegation raised territory on Tuesday, which Zelensky said is a question for leaders, and he said the Russian side told the room it had occupied half of the Donbas territory Ukraine controls, a claim the Ukrainian team disputed in the room and which the Americans present, including Supreme Allied Commander Europe General Alexus Grynkewich, confirmed was not the battlefield picture, according to Zelensky. That battlefield account is Ukraine's stated position. Espreso TV, in Ukrainian, also reported that Ukraine has shared a winter plan covering energy, diesel and bridge protection with its Northern European partners, and that Foreign Minister Andriy Sybiha said Ukraine proposed a truce in energy and in the Black Sea, with Russia's response awaited.

President Volodymyr Zelensky said after the first day of sessions that constructive and substantive negotiations took place, covering practical steps toward ending the war, the release of prisoners, security guarantees for Ukraine and concrete discussion of further negotiations to come, according to Yonhap News Agency reporting of his social media statement. He added that killings on the battlefield continue and that Ukraine is defending itself against Russia's invasion, and in a separate post the same morning he urged partners to put more pressure on Russia, citing overnight air strike damage. Russia's Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov rejected the European proposal that a ceasefire come before discussion of European security, saying a ceasefire would let the Ukrainian side make up for military losses. Both characterisations are the respective governments' stated positions; no joint text from the room has been published.

The trilateral track resumed in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday, November 10, with Russian and Ukrainian envoys meeting under US mediation for a two day round expected to cover ceasefire monitoring and territory. The talks are reported, not announced in a joint communique: no agenda or delegation list has been published by the hosts, and the round's opening was confirmed by wire reporting rather than by an official programme.

Ahead of the session with Russia, the European partners, Ukraine and the United States met and issued a joint statement, published by the European Council and the European Commission on Tuesday morning. The statement says the participants coordinated on efforts to end the war and establish conditions for a durable peace, including support for robust security guarantees as part of any settlement. It says any future arrangements must respect sovereignty, territorial integrity and independent decision making, and must provide credible security guarantees for Ukraine.

The statement's strategic core is institutional. The participants reaffirmed a shared commitment to a strong post war European security architecture and said a strong NATO will remain central to Euro Atlantic collective defence. They said they would keep working in close partnership through the negotiating process, with the objective of moving toward broader multilateral discussions involving European partners. Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said he and Commission President Ursula von der Leyen had coordinated positions before the talks, and von der Leyen said the EU would be present alongside its partners.

Moscow's position, stated by the Kremlin and reported on Monday, is that the Abu Dhabi format remains trilateral: Russia, Ukraine and the United States, with the Europeans running their own coordination alongside rather than sitting in the Russia session. That distinction matters beyond protocol. A settlement negotiated in a trilateral room and guaranteed in a European one leaves the guarantee's legal and military form to be settled later, and the joint statement's language on robust guarantees is a coordination position, announced, not a negotiated text with Russia.

The round has now closed with no document published. What to watch is whether any agreed text on ceasefire monitoring or a prisoner exchange follows in the coming days, any statement on territory, and whether the broader multilateral format the Europeans propose is taken up or left as an aspiration. Until a document emerges, the Abu Dhabi round is a resumed process, not a breakthrough, and claims of concessions from any side should be treated as that side's narrative.
