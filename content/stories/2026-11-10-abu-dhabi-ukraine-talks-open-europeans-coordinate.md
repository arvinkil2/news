## Latest

Jared Kushner said after the talks closed that he and Steve Witkoff will travel to Moscow on Thursday, November 12, to meet President Vladimir Putin, discuss the Abu Dhabi results and try to make further progress, and will then go to Dublin to meet President Volodymyr Zelensky, Vedomosti reported. Kushner called the second day difficult and substantive, said there is neither sympathy nor trust between the leaders, and set the sequencing for what comes next: no agreement on Ukraine's postwar security will be concluded before agreements on territory, settling the territorial disagreements will be the hardest part of the negotiations, and resolving them is a necessary condition for any security agreement after the conflict ends. He said the American side is working on arrangements that would ensure Ukraine's sovereignty and security and a durable peace in Europe. Those statements are the US negotiator's account as reported by a Russian newspaper; neither Washington nor Kyiv had published its own account of the closing session in the sources read at the time of this update.

The second day of the talks has ended. Vedomosti, in Russian, reported on Wednesday, citing a RIA Novosti source in the negotiating group, that the trilateral session in Abu Dhabi concluded after less than two hours behind closed doors, with Vladimir Medinsky leading the Russian delegation, Rustem Umerov the Ukrainian delegation, and Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner representing the United States. TASS sources told the paper a new prisoner exchange could be one outcome of the meeting; no exchange was announced as agreed, and the Russian side was expected to speak to the press afterwards. Politico had reported the territorial question was expected to be a key topic of the second day. This account of the closing session is Russian-side reporting via state wires; no joint statement had been published and neither Kyiv nor Washington had confirmed the day's proceedings at the time of this update.

President Volodymyr Zelensky said on Tuesday, at a press conference during his visit to Denmark, that the Abu Dhabi talks discussed a large prisoner exchange of 1,000 for 1,000, possibly 1,500 for 1,500, and that the Americans would take that agenda to Russia, according to UNIAN reporting, in Ukrainian. No exchange was agreed. He said Wednesday, November 11, is the main day of the talks and will cover de-escalation steps, energy, the Black Sea, the exchange again and a possible trilateral meeting at leaders level. The Russian delegation raised territory on Tuesday, which Zelensky said is a question for leaders, and he said the Russian side told the room it had occupied half of the Donbas territory Ukraine controls, a claim the Ukrainian team disputed in the room and which the Americans present, including Supreme Allied Commander Europe General Alexus Grynkewich, confirmed was not the battlefield picture, according to Zelensky. That battlefield account is Ukraine's stated position. Espreso TV, in Ukrainian, also reported that Ukraine has shared a winter plan covering energy, diesel and bridge protection with its Northern European partners, and that Foreign Minister Andriy Sybiha said Ukraine proposed a truce in energy and in the Black Sea, with Russia's response awaited.

President Volodymyr Zelensky said after the first day of sessions that constructive and substantive negotiations took place, covering practical steps toward ending the war, the release of prisoners, security guarantees for Ukraine and concrete discussion of further negotiations to come, according to Yonhap News Agency reporting of his social media statement. He added that killings on the battlefield continue and that Ukraine is defending itself against Russia's invasion, and in a separate post the same morning he urged partners to put more pressure on Russia, citing overnight air strike damage. Russia's Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov rejected the European proposal that a ceasefire come before discussion of European security, saying a ceasefire would let the Ukrainian side make up for military losses. Both characterisations are the respective governments' stated positions; no joint text from the room has been published.

The trilateral track resumed in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday, November 10, with Russian and Ukrainian envoys meeting under US mediation for a two day round expected to cover ceasefire monitoring and territory. The talks are reported, not announced in a joint communique: no agenda or delegation list has been published by the hosts, and the round's opening was confirmed by wire reporting rather than by an official programme.

Ahead of the session with Russia, the European partners, Ukraine and the United States met and issued a joint statement, published by the European Council and the European Commission on Tuesday morning. The statement says the participants coordinated on efforts to end the war and establish conditions for a durable peace, including support for robust security guarantees as part of any settlement. It says any future arrangements must respect sovereignty, territorial integrity and independent decision making, and must provide credible security guarantees for Ukraine.

The statement's strategic core is institutional. The participants reaffirmed a shared commitment to a strong post war European security architecture and said a strong NATO will remain central to Euro Atlantic collective defence. They said they would keep working in close partnership through the negotiating process, with the objective of moving toward broader multilateral discussions involving European partners. Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said he and Commission President Ursula von der Leyen had coordinated positions before the talks, and von der Leyen said the EU would be present alongside its partners.

Moscow's position, stated by the Kremlin and reported on Monday, is that the Abu Dhabi format remains trilateral: Russia, Ukraine and the United States, with the Europeans running their own coordination alongside rather than sitting in the Russia session. That distinction matters beyond protocol. A settlement negotiated in a trilateral room and guaranteed in a European one leaves the guarantee's legal and military form to be settled later, and the joint statement's language on robust guarantees is a coordination position, announced, not a negotiated text with Russia.

The round has now closed with no document published. What to watch is whether any agreed text on ceasefire monitoring or a prisoner exchange follows in the coming days, any statement on territory, and whether the broader multilateral format the Europeans propose is taken up or left as an aspiration. Until a document emerges, the Abu Dhabi round is a resumed process, not a breakthrough, and claims of concessions from any side should be treated as that side's narrative.
