The United Kingdom announced on Tuesday a sanctions package against Ansar Allah, known as the Houthis, and four senior officials of the Yemeni movement, citing the group's resumption of conflict inside Yemen and across the region and its involvement in serious human rights abuses. The Houthis as an organisation are now subject to an asset freeze and director disqualification under the Yemen (Sanctions) (EU Exit) Regulations 2020, a step that goes beyond the arms embargo the group already faces under United Nations sanctions.

The four individuals, designated under the Global Human Rights and International Humanitarian Law Sanctions Regulations 2026, are subject to an asset freeze, a travel ban and director disqualification. They are Abdulkarim Amir al-Din Hussein al-Houthi, the Houthi authorities' interior minister in Sana'a since 2019; Abdul Hakim Hashem Ali al-Khaiwani, head of the Houthi Security and Intelligence Service; Mohammed Moufleh, a Houthi appointed judge; and Sarim al-Din Muffadal, a senior Houthi prosecutor.

The government said al-Houthi has been responsible for police, security forces and detention facilities in whose custody detainees have been subjected to torture and other cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment, including electric shocks, beatings and sexual violence, and that Muffadal is reasonably suspected of having directly perpetrated, or knowingly permitted, sexual violence including rape against women detainees. Moufleh was designated over a serious violation of an individual's right to liberty and security of person. These are the UK government's stated grounds for designation, not findings of a court.

"The Houthis' renewed aggression targeting our partners and innocent civilians is unacceptable and they must be held to account," Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband said. The government said the Houthis have threatened the peace, security and stability of Yemen through escalation inside the country, attacks on international shipping in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden, and missile and drone attacks against regional partners. The UK now counts 16 UN and UK sanctions in place on Houthi persons. Status: enacted (designations announced and in force November 24, 2026).
