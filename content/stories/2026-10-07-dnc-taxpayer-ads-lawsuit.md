**Latest (Sunday, October 11):** a federal judge temporarily barred the ads. Manhattan-based
US District Judge Jennifer Rochon ruled Sunday that the Trump administration may not
use taxpayer money to pay for the political television advertisements, in the lawsuit
brought by watchdog group Common Cause and joined by a public-employee union and an
Alabama state-representative candidate, per Reuters. The plaintiffs had argued the spots
were political propaganda designed to help Republican candidates in the November 3
midterms rather than public service announcements, and had asked Rochon to block them
before the weekend's football games and Saturday Night Live. The administration had
already told the court Saturday it was planning to stop running the ads, and the Justice
Department had asked for the case to be dismissed as moot; the judge's order locks in
the halt. Democratic Party officials are separately challenging the ads in federal court
in Washington. Trump said the ads were "positive promotion for our great U.S.A." and the
White House said his MAGA PAC would fund future airings but would not reimburse taxpayers
for ads that already ran. DOJ did not immediately respond to Reuters' request for comment.

The Democratic National Committee on Wednesday sued President Donald Trump's administration over television advertisements that promoted his political message at taxpayer expense, calling the spots illegal government-sponsored propaganda. The complaint, filed in federal court in Washington, accuses Trump of personally directing the campaign and asks the court to declare the ads illegal and block further federal spending on them. The spots began airing in September and have already cost more than $12 million, according to AdImpact, with $20 million in Department of Homeland Security funding tapped to pay for them. Trump defended the ads Monday as "positive promotion for our Great U.S.A." and said his MAGA Inc. super PAC would pay for them going forward, but a fifth ad aired Tuesday marked "paid for by the U.S. Government," promoting his military actions in Venezuela, and he told reporters he had not committed to reimbursing money already spent. DNC Chair Ken Martin called it misuse of taxpayer dollars in "a last-ditch attempt to save Republicans in November."
