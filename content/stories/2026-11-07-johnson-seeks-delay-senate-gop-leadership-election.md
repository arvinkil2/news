Senator Ron Johnson of Wisconsin is leading an effort to postpone the Senate Republican leadership elections scheduled for Tuesday, November 10, arguing that holding a closed door vote so soon after the party's midterm losses would be political malpractice. Johnson has gathered at least five signatures from colleagues, the threshold under conference rules at which a written request forces a meeting of the full Republican conference, and he plans to keep collecting names through the weekend before sending his letter on Monday, November 9, to conference chairman Tom Cotton.

The stated conditions are two: ample opportunity to discuss what happened on Tuesday, and knowing who will actually be in the conference, a reference to the Senate races still uncalled on Friday, Mississippi among them. Senator Mike Lee of Utah said he would sign, writing that the conference has no business holding leadership elections until both conditions are met. Johnson himself was blunt about his odds, saying he did not know that he would be able to stop the vote. A petition forces the meeting; it does not itself postpone anything, and Republican senators and aides said they did not currently expect the election to move past Tuesday. Senate Republicans defeated a similar call to delay in 2022.

The target is not named in the letter, but the context is. Majority Leader John Thune of South Dakota is expected to win another term as Republican leader, which from January would make him minority leader of a conference that lost at least seven Senate seats on Tuesday, with two more contests uncalled on Friday and trending Democratic. The push began after an adviser to President Trump publicly questioned whether Thune should remain leader. Johnson, who chairs the Senate Budget Committee, framed his demand as a postmortem: you cannot, he said, just elect the leadership team by acclamation after a defeat of this size.

Status discipline matters here. What has happened is a signature drive that has reached the procedural threshold and a letter planned for Monday. What has not happened is a delay, a challenger to Thune, or any vote. The conference meeting the signatures can force is the next step, and the leadership election remains scheduled for Tuesday, November 10, in the Old Senate Chamber, by secret ballot.
