Israeli caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu renewed his call on Saturday for a national unity government with his rival Gadi Eisenkot, saying in a video statement that a meeting between them on Thursday had strengthened his conviction that only such a government can guarantee Israel's security, and inviting Eisenkot to meet "in one room, without preconditions". Eisenkot, the former army chief whom President Isaac Herzog has tasked with forming a government, dismissed the appeal within minutes, saying Netanyahu knows he lost the election and that the call for negotiations while talks are already under way is a trick that does not work on him. Neither side won a majority in the October 27 election: Netanyahu's right-wing and religious bloc holds 53 of the Knesset's 120 seats, Eisenkot's four-party bloc 48, and Arab-led parties a record 19; Eisenkot has 28 days, extendable by 14, to form a government, and the Knesset elects its speaker on Tuesday.
