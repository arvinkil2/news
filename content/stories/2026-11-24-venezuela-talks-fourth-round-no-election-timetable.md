A fourth round of political negotiations between the Venezuelan government and a sector of the opposition concluded in Caracas on Monday, November 23, ending after ten days of discussions without major breakthroughs on core democratic demands. The US backed negotiation process is aimed at establishing a stable political transition following the January 2026 ouster of former president Nicolas Maduro.

The delegations were led by National Assembly President Jorge Rodriguez for the government and Dinorah Figuera, representing former members of the 2015 National Assembly, for the opposition. At the close of the round the two sides announced a series of administrative and procedural accords, eight secondary measures in all, including commitments to ongoing legal reforms and general pledges toward democratic coexistence.

What the round did not produce is the core of the transition. Negotiators did not finalise a schedule for renewing the National Electoral Council, the body that runs elections, and did not establish a definitive timetable for future presidential elections. No immediate mass releases of political detainees were announced at the closing session. Bloomberg, reporting on the negotiations on Tuesday under the headline "Key Electoral Reform Lags," reached the same conclusion from the electoral side: the reform steps that would make a credible presidential vote possible remain behind the rest of the process.

The delay matters because of the sequence Washington has set out. US officials have described a path running from a new Supreme Justice Tribunal and a new electoral council in early 2027 to a declaration that the presidency is vacant, which would trigger an election within six months. None of those steps has been completed: candidacies for the expanded 32 member supreme court closed on November 18 after a week's extension, and the day the new judges are named has been stated variously as December 11, the first week of December and mid December. Opposition estimates for the presidential election itself range from July 2027, in Washington's plan, to late 2027 or early 2028.

At the conclusion of Monday's meeting, Jorge Rodriguez confirmed that both delegations will pause formal sessions and reconvene in Caracas in December 2026 to continue discussions. Status: reported (round concluded; electoral commitments unresolved).
