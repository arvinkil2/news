Meta is re-evaluating the 2030 timeframe for its net zero emissions goal, the company said in its 2026 sustainability report, published this week. "We are re-evaluating our timelines, sequencing, and interim milestones, including the 2030 timeframe for our net zero emissions goal and our near-term intensity targets, so our approach reflects current operating realities while keeping progress transparent year over year," wrote Rachel Peterson, vice president of data centers, and Blair Swedeen, global head of net-zero and sustainability, in the report's introduction.

A Meta spokesperson told TechCrunch the company remains committed to net zero but is re-evaluating the 2030 timeline and interim milestones, and will keep investing in emissions reductions and reporting progress annually. No replacement date was given.

The report's own figures explain the retreat. Meta's greenhouse gas emissions have more than doubled in the last four years despite years of clean power procurement, and its data centre emissions increased more than 12 times between 2024 and 2025, before the Hyperion data centre in Louisiana, with its multibillion-dollar bet on natural gas generation, is even online. Meta left RE100, the corporate renewable energy initiative it had belonged to for a decade, in July, after the group began requiring more rigorous reporting toward renewable energy targets.

The company framed the change as a response to demand it did not forecast. When the goal was set, Meta said, it anticipated steady growth and falling clean energy costs; instead, demand for AI infrastructure arrived on a timeline measured in quarters rather than decades, and meeting it has meant building at a pace and scale the industry has never attempted. The consequence reaches beyond Meta: voluntary targets carry no penalty for being missed, and the first hyperscaler to drop a dated target lowers the reputational cost for every peer carrying the same tension between AI capacity and emissions.
