**Latest (Tuesday, October 13):** Pope Leo's surgery at Rome's Gemelli hospital removed the lower lobe of his right lung using a video-assisted technique, and the tissue examined during the operation confirmed lung adenocarcinoma, as pre-operative tests had suspected, his surgeons said in a statement released by the Holy See Press Office just before 10 p.m. Rome time. The pope spent about three hours in the operating theatre, the operation itself lasted about 90 minutes, and the Vatican said it was successful with no complications; he woke normally and returned to his room on the hospital's tenth floor. He is expected to spend a week in hospital recovering, and the Vatican has cancelled all his audiences through October 20.

Pope Leo told pilgrims in St. Peter's Square on Sunday that a recent health check detected a localized nodule in his lung at a very early stage and that, on his doctors' advice, he will undergo surgery to remove it in the next few days. The pontiff described the growth as well-contained; the Vatican press office said his recovery was expected to take about a week.
