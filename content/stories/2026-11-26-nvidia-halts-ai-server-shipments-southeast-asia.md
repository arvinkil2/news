## Latest

Singapore has not confirmed the halt. Deputy Prime Minister Gan Kim Yong, asked about the Financial Times report at the Asia Future Summit in Singapore on Friday, November 27, said "We have not confirmed it," and said the government is engaging local businesses to assess the impact on them. Singapore's ministries had earlier said they are engaging the US authorities on the scope and implementation of the new controls.

The same US restrictions are now delaying flagship models inside China, Reuters reported on Thursday, citing people familiar with the matter. Z.AI, also known as Zhipu AI, has yet to release its GLM-5.5 flagship, planned for this month at roughly 3 trillion parameters, and the new computing restrictions could push it into the first quarter of 2027 or later, two people said. Moonshot AI internally expects the interval between its major releases could stretch from six months to a year or longer if its access to overseas computing is cut off entirely, putting its Kimi K4 around the middle of 2027, one person said, and The Information reported last week that Moonshot had scrapped a $150 million deal to reach Nvidia Blackwell chips through an Indonesian data centre. Reuters also reported that safeguards by OpenAI and Anthropic against distillation, training models on answers from more capable models, have slowed both labs, and that the Commerce Department rule covers overseas data centre arrangements above a threshold of roughly 750 Nvidia H100 chips, with existing operators given 90 days to obtain licences.

Nvidia has told its manufacturing partners to halt server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries amid tighter US export controls, the Financial Times reported on Thursday. Partners such as Dell and Super Micro, which build servers using Nvidia's advanced AI processors, have told buyers in Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia that Nvidia will not allow their orders to be shipped, the FT reported, citing three people with knowledge of the matter.

The report follows new US Commerce Department rules introduced last week aimed at stopping Chinese AI developers from reaching advanced chips through cloud computing contracts with data centres in Southeast Asia. Cloud providers in Asia now face a 90-day deadline to prove their services do not reach restricted entities.
