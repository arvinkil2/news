Prime Minister Mark Carney told reporters in Cambodia on Monday that Canada could turn to Chinese automakers if its trade negotiations with the United States fail to preserve an integrated North American auto industry, saying Canada's auto workers must be fully deployed one way or the other: "It's not leverage. But action, reaction." He said talks with Washington remain his priority and that the two sides are making progress, but that the outcome could determine whether Canada pursues alternatives involving European, Chinese or Japanese automakers instead.

Any Chinese investment would have to involve substantial manufacturing in Canada, with proper labour standards and domestic supply chains that expand over time, Carney said, not the assembly of imported parts. Under a January agreement, Canada cut its tariff on Chinese electric vehicles from 100 percent to 6.1 percent, initially allowing up to 49,000 vehicles a year, in exchange for lower Chinese tariffs on Canadian farm goods including canola. Carney is due to meet President Xi Jinping in Shenzhen this week, where auto manufacturing is expected to be discussed.
