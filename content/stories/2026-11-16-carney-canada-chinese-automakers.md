## Latest

Canada and China agreed to ease tariffs on each other's goods at talks between Prime Minister Mark Carney and President Xi Jinping in Shenzhen, held ahead of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit there, with Ottawa announcing the deals on Tuesday. China is to reduce tariffs on Canadian pork, fish and seafood; in exchange, Canada will prolong its current tariff exemptions for Chinese steel and aluminum for another two years. The two leaders also agreed to cooperate more closely on economic and financial matters, and to work together in education and environmental protection. No specific tariff rates were given for any of the measures.

Carney hailed "greater predictability and stability" in relations with China and said Canada had already made faster-than-expected progress toward its goal of boosting exports to China by 50 percent by the end of this decade. Xi said cooperation between the two countries would benefit not only them but also Asia-Pacific and the world. The agreements extend the rapprochement the two leaders set in motion in Beijing in January, when they struck a deal cutting tariffs on Canadian agricultural products while Chinese electric vehicles gained easier access to the Canadian market. Critics in Canada warn of security and values risks in closer ties to Beijing; Canadian intelligence believes China interfered in at least two federal elections, which Beijing denies.

Prime Minister Mark Carney told reporters in Cambodia on Monday that Canada could turn to Chinese automakers if its trade negotiations with the United States fail to preserve an integrated North American auto industry, saying Canada's auto workers must be fully deployed one way or the other: "It's not leverage. But action, reaction." He said talks with Washington remain his priority and that the two sides are making progress, but that the outcome could determine whether Canada pursues alternatives involving European, Chinese or Japanese automakers instead.

Any Chinese investment would have to involve substantial manufacturing in Canada, with proper labour standards and domestic supply chains that expand over time, Carney said, not the assembly of imported parts. Under a January agreement, Canada cut its tariff on Chinese electric vehicles from 100 percent to 6.1 percent, initially allowing up to 49,000 vehicles a year, in exchange for lower Chinese tariffs on Canadian farm goods including canola. Carney is due to meet President Xi Jinping in Shenzhen this week, where auto manufacturing is expected to be discussed.
