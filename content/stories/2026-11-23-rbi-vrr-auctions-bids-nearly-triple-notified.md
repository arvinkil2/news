The Reserve Bank of India allotted both of its Monday variable rate repo operations in full on November 23, after banks bid close to three times the notified amount in each. The 2 day operation took bids of 137,612 crore rupees against 50,000 crore notified and allotted 50,003 crore at a cut off rate of 5.64 percent and a weighted average rate of 5.65 percent. The 4 day operation, flagged in yesterday's forward calendar, took bids of 151,982 crore against the same 50,000 crore notified amount and allotted 50,005 crore at a cut off of 5.62 percent and a weighted average of 5.63 percent.

The demand follows the pattern set on Friday, when the second 3 day operation was allotted 25,007 crore rupees on bids of 48,040 crore at a weighted average of 5.61 percent. Bid cover near 2.8 and 3.0 times on Monday says banks want term funding over the week rather than relying on the overnight window alone.

The RBI's money market operations statement for November 22 shows why. Banks placed 106,401 crore rupees in the standing deposit facility at 5.25 percent on Sunday while drawing 28,576 crore at the marginal standing facility at 5.75 percent, and net liquidity from outstanding operations stood at an injection of 12,479 crore rupees once the day's operations were counted. Durable liquidity was in surplus by 702,107 crore rupees as of October 31.

The pricing matters more than the volumes. Weighted average rates of 5.63 to 5.65 percent sit inside the corridor and close to Friday's 3 day result, so the heavy bidding reads as balance sheet positioning into month end rather than funding stress. The RBI meets demand at those rates instead of forcing banks toward the 5.75 percent ceiling.
