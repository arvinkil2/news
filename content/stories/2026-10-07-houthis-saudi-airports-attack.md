**Latest (11:48am ET Thursday):** The Houthis claimed a ballistic missile struck King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh on Thursday, killing three people and injuring several, the third time this week they have claimed to target the airport. Saudi authorities had not confirmed the claim; Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Saree said on X the missile "accurately struck its target and disrupted airport operations," and warned "for the last time" that all international airlines must stop flying through Saudi airspace. FlightRadar24 showed numerous delayed arrivals and departures at Riyadh airport, and some Riyadh schools told parents of shelter-in-place orders, though no public alert was issued. Coalition spokesman Turki al-Maliki put the retaliation figure at 82 Houthi military targets destroyed, missile launch sites, command centers and weapons depots across Saada, Marib, Al Jawf and Al Hudaydah; the Saudi-backed Yemeni government announced a major counteroffensive the previous day, claiming to push Houthis from Red Sea coast areas and around Mocha. Reuters reported Saudi Arabia wants to eliminate the Houthis and is seeking support, even logistical or defensive, from Turkey and Pakistan. The UN says the conflict has displaced more than 200,000 within Yemen.

**Earlier:** The exchange is continuing. The Saudi-led coalition said late Wednesday it had attacked more than 80 Houthi military sites across the governorates of Saada, Hodeidah, Jawf and Marib in retaliation for the airport attacks. The Houthis claimed on Thursday that they had launched another ballistic missile at Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport, a claim Saudi authorities had not confirmed. The Gulf Cooperation Council called for a "firm and deterrent" international response, and UN Secretary General Antonio Guterres condemned the cross-border attacks.

Saudi Arabia announced Wednesday that Houthi missile and drone attacks on Riyadh international airport and Abha airport in the kingdom's southwest killed three people, one Egyptian, one Algerian and one Sudanese national, and wounded 36, some seriously, alongside material damage. The Houthis had claimed responsibility for the strikes earlier in the day, after warning airlines that Saudi airspace was "not safe"; flight-tracking data showed traffic at Riyadh airport disrupted for days beforehand.

The Saudi-led coalition vowed the attacks would not go unpunished, saying it destroyed 82 military targets in retaliation, intercepted incoming fire and destroyed the ballistic-missile launch ramp used against Riyadh. The strikes cap a week of sharp escalation: Wednesday's clashes in western Yemen killed at least 62, and more than 330 fighters have died since Monday, according to military sources on both sides.
