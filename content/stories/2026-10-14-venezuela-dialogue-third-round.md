The third round of dialogue between representatives of Venezuela's governing chavismo and a sector of the opposition began on Wednesday in Caracas, behind closed doors in a capital hotel, with the political and civil guarantees for a future election at the centre of the agenda, El Nacional reported, citing EFE sources who said the talks would run for several days with no closing date set. Washington has also flagged renewal of the Supreme Court magistrates and changes to the National Electoral Council as part of the institutional reform agenda, and the State Department said Secretary of State Marco Rubio had urged continued progress on political freedoms and electoral reform as the basis for free, fair and credible elections.

The opposition delegation is led by Dinorah Figuera, who met Rubio in Washington last Friday; Maria Corina Machado and Edmundo Gonzalez Urrutia are not taking part. The first two rounds, in August and September, each ran about a week and covered reforms and political opening measures, and critics of the process argue Washington weighs access to Venezuelan oil resources more heavily than a democratic transition, a charge the article reports without a US response.
