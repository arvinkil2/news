**Latest, October 29:** Lagarde has now given her reason. Asked at her press conference about the ECB's constraint of neutrality and about how much France's political situation weighed on her decision, with the French presidential election due in April, she said she cannot remain neutral, or be comfortable in that neutrality, ANSA reported. She said truth is very often damaged and contradicted by distorted representations, and that from the position she has held for more than twenty years, at the International Monetary Fund and then the ECB, she has things to say that she cannot say in her current post. She added that her voice and her action could help on principles she cares about, in many parts of the world and not only in Europe. In a short farewell video she called the ECB a rock, said she leaves it ready for the future, and clarified that the two plasters visible on her face were from a small dermatological operation this week, not a health problem.

Christine Lagarde will leave the European Central Bank on February 7, 2027. The ECB said on Thursday that Lagarde had informed the Governing Council and António Costa, the President of the European Council, that she will step down on that date. Her successor will be appointed by the European Council under the nomination process in Article 283 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. The Governing Council thanked her and gave her a long standing ovation, the bank said.

The departure is early. Lagarde's eight-year term was due to run to October 31, 2027, and while she had not fully denied reports that she might leave ahead of it, telling La Croix this month that she did not rule out going a few months early, Thursday's announcement fixes a date almost nine months ahead of the term's end and starts the succession process immediately. Wire reports said Costa confirmed the search for a successor begins today.

The reaction from the finance ministers' side was swift. Eurogroup President Kyriakos Pierrakakis thanked Lagarde for what he called her outstanding contribution to the stability of the euro area, and said the Eurogroup will act swiftly to prepare the Council of the EU's recommendation to the European Council on the appointment. That recommendation, after consultation with the European Parliament and the ECB's Governing Council, is the formal route to naming the next president.

The contest now opens against the backdrop this site set out on October 25. Reuters' succession explainer puts the frontrunners as Pablo Hernández de Cos, the former Bank of Spain governor who now runs the Bank for International Settlements, and Klaas Knot, the former Dutch central bank chief. The presidency is not the only seat in play: Isabel Schnabel is leaving the executive board for the International Monetary Fund, chief economist Philip Lane's term ends on May 31, and Croatia's Boris Vujčić has already taken the vice presidency. Succession at the ECB is a package negotiated by euro area leaders, and France's hand in that negotiation is weakened by its own bond market, with the French 10-year spread over Germany near 170 basis points, its widest since 2011.

The announcement lands on a policy day. The Governing Council announces its rate decision at 13:15 GMT today, with a hold in the deposit rate at 2.50 percent widely expected and a further increase priced for December. Lagarde will still chair the December meeting and the one after it. What changes today is the horizon: every statement she makes from now on is read as the position of a departing president, and the market's question shifts from what the ECB does next to who will be setting policy from February.
