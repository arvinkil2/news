## Latest

The toll has risen. Deutsche Welle reported that President Volodymyr Zelenskyy confirmed 14 people were killed in the strike on Dnipro station, up from the 13 reported earlier in the day, and called the attack horrific. Ukraine's Air Force said it intercepted three drones in the daytime attack and that a fourth evaded its defences and hit the station. DW gave no updated wounded figure for Dnipro. No Russian statement on the strike had been reported.

Ukrainian officials said a Russian jet-powered drone struck Dnipro railway station on Thursday, hitting a platform where train No. 866, the Dnipro to Zaporizhzhia service, was standing. Oleksandr Hanzha, head of the Dnipropetrovsk regional military administration, said 13 people were killed and at least five wounded, and Mayor Borys Filatov said many people had been killed and wounded on what he called a civilian electric train. Ukraine's Air Force had reported a jet-powered drone over the city at about 10:50 a.m. local time.

Ukrzaliznytsia, the state railway, said people were on the platform despite an announced heightened danger level, and that rescuers, medics and police were working at the scene. No Russian statement on the strike, and no independent confirmation of the casualty figures, had been reported in the coverage read in this run.
