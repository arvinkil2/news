Altria Group confirmed recent press speculation on Thursday and said it is considering a possible cash offer for Imperial Brands, the London listed maker of Davidoff, Gauloises and JPS cigarettes. Altria said it is also in discussions with a third party over that party's possible acquisition of part of Imperial Brands' business if a deal goes ahead. No agreement has been reached, Altria gave no financial terms, and there is no certainty that an offer will be made. Under the UK Takeover Code, Altria now has until 5:00 p.m. London time on December 24, 2026 to announce a firm intention to make an offer or state that it does not intend to do so, unless the Takeover Panel extends the deadline.

Imperial shares rose 14 percent in early afternoon trading in London to 34.47 pounds, a level last seen in July 2017, and led the FTSE 100 risers. The shares had already jumped earlier on Thursday after Bloomberg reported that Japan Tobacco was studying a move for Imperial, speaking with advisers and weighing a joint purchase with a partner that would split Imperial's assets by region. Reuters reported that Imperial's market value was 22.77 billion pounds, about $29.8 billion, and that a combination with Japan Tobacco could create a group worth more than $99 billion, while noting it could not immediately verify the Bloomberg report.

Whether the third party in Altria's talks is Japan Tobacco has not been confirmed by any of the companies. The structure now in public view, one buyer taking Imperial whole and selling on parts, or two buyers dividing it by geography, would need agreement on which regional businesses each party keeps, and would face competition review in several markets. Imperial did not immediately respond to a request for comment on Altria's statement and declined to comment on the Japan Tobacco report.
