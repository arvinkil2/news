The Trump administration asked the Supreme Court on Monday to stop California counting mail ballots from more than 20,000 voters on a Department of Homeland Security list of suspected noncitizens, until their citizenship is confirmed. The emergency request came one day before the midterm elections, after a federal judge rejected the same request last week and a federal appeals court rejected it on Friday.

California says the list is unreliable, could include thousands of US citizens, and could disenfranchise eligible voters, and state officials say separating the ballots would be nearly impossible because voting has been under way since early October. District Judge David Carter, in rejecting the request last week, raised concerns about the reliability of the data behind the list.
