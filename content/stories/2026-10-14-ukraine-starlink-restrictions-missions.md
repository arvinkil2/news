**Latest (Saturday 09:45 ET):** President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has given Kyiv's fullest account yet of the Starlink limits, and it contradicts the report carried below that full function had been restored. Answering journalists online on Saturday, he said Ukraine sent vehicles fitted with Starlink into European Union countries and drove them at 180 kilometres per hour and above, with the service working and the runs recorded, while inside Ukraine the speed cap of 161 kilometres per hour remains and the interceptor drones that work with Starlink against jet powered Shahed drones do not operate because of it. The restrictions therefore apply on Ukrainian territory and not in the European Union, he said, calling the limit a planned operation. He said SpaceX is not responding to Ukraine's military or its defence ministry, that officials at various levels describe the limits as technical problems, and that talks continue at military and diplomatic levels. The account is Ukraine's, reported by Ukrainska Pravda via European Pravda, and the test results have not been independently verified.

**Latest (Saturday 07:42 ET):** President Trump has now denied the episode personally. Speaking to reporters at the White House on Friday, he said he did not order the restrictions, did not think Elon Musk did either, and had heard about the matter through the media, adding that, from what he understood after checking that morning, the service had not been slowed down at all and was probably functioning. That denial sits directly against the Reuters account below, which attributed the order to the White House, and against President Zelenskyy's position, restated to the Financial Times, that the limits were deliberate and not a technical problem. A Ukrainian military source told the Financial Times that the system's full functionality has since been restored and the speed limits lifted, a claim reported by CNN and not independently confirmed.

**Latest (Thursday 20:42 ET):** The order to restrict Starshield came from the White House itself, Reuters reported, citing two American officials and one Western official speaking anonymously. Pentagon leadership received a White House instruction to slow the system for Ukrainian forces, the officials said, though they did not say who made the decision or who was responsible for carrying it out. The temporary restriction was decided after President Donald Trump began demanding that President Volodymyr Zelenskyy stop attacks on Russian oil refineries. A senior American official denied the president's involvement, saying Trump never made such a request, while the White House did not answer whether the request could have come from another administration official. After the unexpected restrictions on Tuesday and Wednesday, Ukrainian officials called the White House, the State Department and the Pentagon, and were told in Washington that it had been a White House decision, one Ukrainian official said. The account sits against earlier reporting by Axios that the administration had no involvement and sought to place responsibility on SpaceX. Ukrainian officials and military personnel say the limits significantly complicated strike missions against Russian forces and logistics in occupied territory, and restricted the use of interceptor drones against Russian Shahed drones.

**Latest (Thursday 19:04 ET):** The demand for an investigation is now formal. A group of senators, including four Republicans, has sent a letter demanding that the Pentagon's inspector general investigate Ukraine's Starlink outage, The Hill reported on Thursday. The letter turns Senator Thom Tillis's Wednesday pledge into a written request: Tillis had said senators would ask the inspector general to initiate an investigation and report back to Congress, arguing that Ukrainian civilians and American diplomatic and security personnel were placed at unnecessary risk without warning. Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth has still issued no public statement on the Starshield restrictions.

**Latest (19:53 ET):** The intelligence dispute now has a paper trail. United States intelligence officials warned Ukraine's embassy in Washington on Monday that all intelligence sharing would stop immediately if Ukrainian strikes on Russian energy facilities continued, the Washington Post reported, in a report carried by UNIAN. A source familiar with the matter told the Post that sharing had not in fact been halted, at least not in full, though the threat stands, and described the warnings as delivered so widely that calls reached everyone with a Ukrainian sounding name. Separately, US officials told the New York Times on Wednesday that the United States continues to provide intelligence to Ukraine. That formal position sits against President Zelenskyy's claim that sharing had halted, and against the missed American missile warnings Kyiv reported on two consecutive nights.

**Latest (17:13 ET):** Elon Musk has said the curbs were not his decision: the military Starshield network is owned and controlled by the United States government, with limits set by the US military and ultimately by the president, the Guardian reported. That statement moves the argument from SpaceX to Washington. It also reported the first quantified military cost Kyiv has put on the limits: adviser Serhiy Beskrestnov said interception of jet powered Shahed drones had already fallen from over 90 percent to about 60 percent, a Ukrainian figure that is not independently verified. On intelligence, the two presidents now contradict each other in public: President Donald Trump denied cutting intelligence sharing on Tuesday, while President Volodymyr Zelenskyy told NPR that US officials had threatened his advisers with future limits, and said Russia attacked for a second night without the customary American missile warning.

**Latest (16:56 ET):** The limits began to ease on Wednesday afternoon, and the argument around them widened. Former defence minister Mykhailo Fedorov said speeds up to 160 kilometres per hour were being restored on the military service, thanked Elon Musk, and urged SpaceX to remove the Starshield speed limit in full, according to The Hill. In Washington, Republican Senator Thom Tillis publicly pressed Musk to confirm the disruption had been an unintended and temporary glitch rather than a deliberate choice, warning that intentionally weakening Ukraine's self defence could be read as helping President Vladimir Putin. The Hill reported no direct reply from Musk or SpaceX to Tillis. In Kyiv, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy went further than his advisers had in the morning: he said the United States had halted intelligence sharing with Ukraine, leaving it without American warning of incoming missile strikes, called the Starlink limit a serious mistake that would cost lives if attacks continued without the service, and said Russia was already exploiting the restrictions, the Guardian reported. Those intelligence claims are Zelenskyy's, stated as he addressed them to President Donald Trump, and are not independently confirmed in the reporting opened.

Starlink terminals used by Ukraine's military and other national security agencies lost connectivity on Tuesday evening when they travelled at more than 100 miles per hour, while ordinary satellite communication terminals kept working. Serhii Sternenko, an activist with close ties to the military who first reported the outage, said the speed at which connectivity cut out later fell lower still, and Ukrainian and Russian outlets put the effective cap well below the figure in the first reports.

Serhiy Beskrestnov, a military technology adviser to President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, said the armed forces "did not receive a warning about these restrictions and this led to the failure of a number of missions", and that the disruption appeared targeted rather than coincidental. No specific mission, unit or place was named in the reporting, and Poland's foreign minister Radoslaw Sikorski publicly appealed to Elon Musk over the restrictions, according to Ukrainian and Russian reporting alike.

Why the terminals were capped is not established. Sternenko alleged SpaceX acted at the Trump administration's request, and the Guardian linked the outage to Washington pressing Kyiv to halt long range strikes on Russia's oil industry immediately, with President Donald Trump blaming those strikes for high diesel costs. SpaceX gave no statement in the morning reporting, so the link between the cap and the refinery dispute remained an allegation from the Ukrainian side at that point; Musk's later statement, in the Latest update above, attributes the limits to the US military rather than to SpaceX.

The restrictions landed on a hard night regardless. Zelenskyy said Russia launched 39 ballistic missiles and 120 attack drones against Kyiv and Kharkiv late on Tuesday, hitting power plants and substations and leaving at least 600,000 people without power, and the Guardian reported it was the second night running that Russian ballistic missiles arrived with no advance warning from the United States.
