**Latest (Tuesday fixing, Beijing):** China held its benchmark lending rates for a seventeenth consecutive month. The National Interbank Funding Center, authorized by the People's Bank of China, announced on October 20 that the one-year loan prime rate is 3.00 percent and the five-year and above rate is 3.50 percent, both unchanged from the previous month and effective until the next release. The last adjustment was in May 2025, when both tenors were cut by 10 basis points, and the seven day reverse repo rate that anchors the quotations has also been unchanged since that cut.

The outcome matched expectations exactly. All 22 market participants in a Reuters survey published on Monday expected the one-year rate to stay at 3.00 percent and the five-year rate at 3.50 percent, and CME Econoday's consensus showed a zero basis point expected change on both tenors. The unanimity followed Monday's third quarter growth print of 4.5 percent from a year earlier, ahead of the 4.4 percent consensus, as surging exports offset weak domestic demand. Wang Qing, chief macro analyst at Golden Credit Rating, told Jiemian News the growth rebound and faster development in high technology manufacturing left aggregate monetary policy holding steady, with countercyclical effort channelled through structural tools instead, and he saw little chance of a loan prime rate cut in the near term after the September 29 package of structural measures, including a 0.25 percentage point cut to the pledged supplementary lending rate and expanded relending quotas for agriculture, small business and private enterprises.

The hold remains a choice rather than an absence of pressure. China's new home prices fell again in September, extending a slump of more than three years, and long-dated Chinese bond yields hovered near a 15-month low. Speaking at the Financial Street Forum in Beijing on Monday, People's Bank of China Governor Pan Gongsheng said the near to medium term direction is to give interest rate regulation a larger role, including reforming loan pricing benchmarks away from relatively single reliance on the loan prime rate toward more diversified anchors, a shift Wang Qing said is already visible in some provinces, where banks have begun issuing loans priced off the DR money market rate. The loan prime rate is calculated monthly from submissions by 20 designated commercial banks to the People's Bank of China and is charged to banks' most creditworthy clients, with the five-year tenor the reference for mortgages.
