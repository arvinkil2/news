Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook will not take part in a White House hearing into mortgage fraud allegations against her unless the process is changed, her lawyer Abbe Lowell signalled in a letter dated Monday and released on Tuesday. Lowell told White House counsel Will Scharf that Cook has no legal obligation to participate in an unfair hearing and has a duty to the Federal Reserve to decline any process that would undermine its independence, calling the three member committee of inquiry a facade designed to obscure a predetermined decision to fire her.

The committee, created by President Trump in October and including White House economic adviser Kevin Hassett, had planned to question Cook on November 5 before pushing the closed door session back to December 14. Lowell objected that Hassett has prejudged the case in public comments and that the panel wants Cook's documents immediately while promising to show her its own evidence only seven days before the hearing. Cook, who denies listing more than one property as her primary residence to secure better mortgage terms, remains willing to appear if the White House seats an unbiased panel and implements fair procedures, her lawyer wrote.
