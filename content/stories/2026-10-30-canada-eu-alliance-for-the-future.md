Latest: the European Commission's own summit release, dated October 30, lists the deliverables underpinning the Alliance for the Future. The EU and Canada concluded negotiations for a Digital Trade Agreement to complement CETA, an agreement between Canada and the European Investment Bank on investment in critical raw materials is being finalised, and the two sides also reached a modernised agreement on competition cooperation and an agreement on digital credentials, digital identity wallets and trust services. They agreed to explore cooperation on Galileo, IRIS², EU GOVSATCOM and Arctic connectivity, and set a people-to-people goal of expanding Horizon Europe and Erasmus+ cooperation. The release describes the associate-membership concept discussed at the summit as an association without accession, built on closer alignment on policies, programmes and governance.

Prime Minister Mark Carney and Commission President Ursula von der Leyen presented the Alliance for the Future in Montreal as the deepest possible partnership between Canada and the 27 member EU, open to other like-minded countries and framed as a hedge against unpredictable geopolitics and the weaponization of trade. Canada agreed to contribute to a 90 billion euro support loan for Ukraine, with no Canadian amount given, and the two sides announced an agreement bringing Canada into Europe's supercomputing network, giving Canadian researchers access to European machines for training AI models. Leaders said future discussions would work toward a more integrated market, reduced trade and travel restrictions and supply chain certainty, and both leaders again used the term associate membership for Canada's proposed role, with a clearer scope promised at a 2027 summit despite resistance from some EU member states.
