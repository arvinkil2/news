**Japan bill supply stays routine.** The Ministry of Finance published the result of its November 27 treasury discount bill auction and the notice for the next sale on December 4, alongside the issues reopened through the November 27 liquidity enhancement auction. All three are routine operations in the JGB calendar; the rates-relevant event remains the Bank of Japan's December 17 to 18 meeting, for which Tokyo core inflation of 2.9 percent, reported yesterday, is the key input.

**RBI liquidity operations continue.** The Reserve Bank of India published the result of its three-day variable rate repo auction held on November 27, part of the routine liquidity management that has run alongside this week's government bond sales. The full results of Friday's bond auction, including the trimmed 2029 line, are in today's updated RBI story; the central bank's next decision is on December 4.

**Mexico's sector accounts.** INEGI's institutional sector accounts for the second quarter of 2026 show non-financial corporations and households generating 78.7 percent of GDP between them, 44.1 percent and 34.6 percent respectively, with general government at 8.1 percent and financial corporations at 5.2 percent. In a separate release, INEGI put the contribution of nonprofit institutions and volunteer work at 3.4 percent of GDP in 2025, generating 1,150,260 million pesos and 3,604,897 jobs, 9.1 percent of total employment.

**India GDP lands Monday.** The National Statistics Office releases July to September GDP on November 30, four days before the RBI's December 4 decision. India Ratings estimates growth of 7.6 percent year on year, matching the median of a Reuters poll of 43 economists, against 7.8 percent in the April to June quarter and the RBI's own projection of 7.2 percent; India Ratings raised its full-year FY27 forecast to 7.2 percent from 7.1 percent. A print at or above the poll would strengthen the hand of those calling for a 50 basis point hike next week.

**US exchanges amend halt rules.** The New York Stock Exchange, NYSE American, NYSE Arca and Nasdaq each filed immediately effective amendments to their trading-halt and limit up, limit down rules, published in the Federal Register dated November 30. The filings are procedural, harmonising halt mechanics across the venues, and carry no direct rates implication; they are noted because halt behaviour in stressed sessions is part of the market-plumbing backdrop to any December volatility.
