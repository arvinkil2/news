President Donald Trump has said he wants to stop US data centre companies from developing overseas. "We're going to keep it all in this country," he said on Friday, November 20. "We're not going to allow our companies to build data centers outside of our country."

He said he would impose a "tremendous tax" on companies that build data centres abroad. The size of any tax was not shared, no policy document has been published, and how a ban or tax would work in practice is unclear. Trump noted he was taking his own aides by surprise with the announcement.

The exposure is broad. Many of the world's largest data centre developers are US-based with significant international footprints: Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Meta and Apple have all developed facilities abroad, and colocation companies including Digital Realty, Equinix, Vantage, Stack, CyrusOne and Iron Mountain hold sizable international portfolios.

The remarks follow US midterm elections in which data centres were high on voters' agenda and Republicans took a beating, and they sit alongside tighter US controls on where advanced computing capacity may be built. Until a measure is drafted, this is a stated intention rather than a rule, and it is reported here on a single source.
