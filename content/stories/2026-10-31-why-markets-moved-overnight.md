Semiconductors fell 8.24 percent to 471.51 dollars, the largest move in the market tables. Today's semiconductor story ties the fall to Intel's collapse and Amazon's cash outflow turning an earnings week into a sector wide repricing of AI spending (see "Semiconductor stocks plunge as AI spending fears spread").

Silver futures fell 5.93 percent to 49.10 dollars an ounce and silver miners fell 5.58 percent to 71.25 dollars. Today's gold and silver story reports investors selling metals to cover losses in sliding technology stocks, with spot silver breaking below 50 dollars for the first time since November 2025 (see "Gold hits one year low as miners lose most ground").

Uranium miners fell 5.57 percent to 33.73 dollars. Today's uranium story reports Cameco cut production guidance and raised cost guidance in its third quarter results even as the long term uranium price held at 97 dollars a pound (see "Uranium shares slide as Cameco misses and costs rise").

Broadcom fell 5.48 percent to 326.08 dollars. It fell as part of the same semiconductor move, named alongside Nvidia, down 4.22 percent, in today's semiconductor story (see "Semiconductor stocks plunge as AI spending fears spread").

Gold miners fell 5.21 percent to 77.09 dollars, copper miners fell 5.19 percent to 71.96 dollars and rare earth miners fell 5.12 percent to 49.99 dollars. The gold miners follow the precious metals selloff in today's gold and silver story; for copper miners and rare earth miners, no story in today's edition explains the move beyond the broad miners selloff, and none is claimed here.

Every large fall sits in technology or the miners that fund and supply it, while WTI crude rose 2.08 percent to 104.12 dollars and tanker freight rose 2.05 percent on Hormuz risk, and Treasury yields eased, with the 10 year down 6.0 basis points to 4.93 percent.
