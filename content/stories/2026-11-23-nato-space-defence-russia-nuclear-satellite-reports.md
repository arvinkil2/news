NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte said on Monday that the alliance’s defence commitment extends to space, after German media reported that Western intelligence services have concluded Russia plans to launch a satellite armed with a nuclear weapon into orbit this year.

Asked about the reports at a joint press conference in Berlin with German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, Rutte said NATO is constantly assessing its deterrence and defence on the ground, at sea, in the air and in space, across the whole of alliance territory. “I can assure you, we will always make sure that we defend every inch of NATO territory, and that includes making sure that we are safe when it comes to the space element,” he said, in NATO’s own transcript of the press conference. Rutte did not confirm the satellite report itself. He said the alliance has response options that are not always visible to the public.

Merz said the reports were not entirely new but were no less alarming. “They reveal the level of escalation Russia is evidently prepared to pursue,” he told reporters, citing drone incidents along Poland’s eastern border as further evidence. European leaders are expected to discuss the reports at a meeting in Brussels later this week, AnewZ reported.

The underlying report, a joint investigation by German broadcasters NDR and WDR and the newspaper Süddeutsche Zeitung published on Monday, says intelligence shared among NATO members in early November indicated that a satellite named Sputnik-S could be launched within 60 days. The outlets say the satellite was assembled several years ago and is stored in the Moscow region, that Germany’s BND intelligence service and armed forces have exchanged intelligence on it, and that the Bundeswehr has briefed senior officials, including Defence Minister Boris Pistorius, in recent weeks. German military officers consider a December launch plausible, the reports say.

Reuters reported the same conclusion on Monday: NATO members’ intelligence services have concluded that Russia plans to launch the satellite this year. Reuters noted that US officials have alleged for years that Russia is developing a nuclear weapon capable of destroying entire satellite networks, including SpaceX’s Starlink system, which Ukrainian troops use. Russia denied similar US assertions in 2024.

A nuclear detonation in low Earth orbit could disable satellites in the affected zone, with disruption to navigation, telecommunications and weather forecasting. Placing a nuclear weapon in orbit would breach Article IV of the 1967 Outer Space Treaty, which bans nuclear weapons and other weapons of mass destruction in orbit around Earth and to which Russia is a party.

Russia rejected the reports. Its embassy in Berlin said it was unaware of any such plans and dismissed “all unfounded allegations regarding the space activities of the Russian Federation” as part of a propaganda campaign, adding that Russia’s space activities follow its international obligations and serve peaceful purposes. No intelligence document or satellite imagery supporting the assessment has been made public.
