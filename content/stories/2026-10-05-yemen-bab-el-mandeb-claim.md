The Yemen theater of the Iran war escalated sharply on Monday. The Houthis' military spokesman Yahya Saree said the group had struck King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, the Aramco refinery in Rabigh and military sites and smaller airports in southern Saudi Arabia with drones, ballistic and cruise missiles, in response to Saudi-led coalition attacks earlier in the day. A Houthi-affiliated coordination center separately warned airlines that Saudi airspace "will be a theater for operations," language that puts civil aviation over the kingdom on notice for as long as Riyadh backs Yemen's government forces. The claims are reported, not independently verified.

On the ground, Yemen's Saudi-backed government announced a strategic offensive to retake the capital Sanaa, held by the Houthis for more than a decade, and claimed more than 1,000 strikes plus control of the Bab el-Mandeb strait. Maj. Gen. Majid al-Nujaili, spokesman for the government forces, said the Giants Brigades and the National Shield Forces had "succeeded in securing Bab el-Mandeb," with 1,122 precision strikes claimed against Houthi positions. Reuters, citing military and government sources, reported government forces had seized much of the Dhubab area. The Saudi-led coalition has formally joined the operation, with spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki al-Malki saying 100 fighter jets were taking part in the "Dawn of Yemen" operation. None of it is independently verified: Houthi-aligned media called the territorial claims "fictitious," and the announcement of 100 coalition warplanes flying dawn sorties could not be confirmed. What is confirmed is heavy fighting: Houthi forces effectively encircled Taiz after cutting its supply route to Aden, and fighting in Taiz province killed more than 70 people in a single day on Sunday, according to sources on both sides cited by AFP.

The day's most structural development came from Riyadh, where Turkey, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia announced a new mutual defense alliance with a "rapid deployment" of Turkish and Pakistani troops to the kingdom, per a joint statement. The pact formalizes a Sunni-bloc security architecture around the Red Sea and Gulf energy corridors and opens a new channel for defense procurement, basing agreements and escalation management. The UN Secretary-General said he was "deeply concerned" by the sharp escalation and condemned continued Houthi cross-border attacks on Saudi Arabia.

In the Strait of Hormuz, the US military said Monday it had destroyed 13 commercial ships over the past 12 weeks for violating the US Navy blockade, which resumed nearly three months ago under President Trump's direction, or for belonging to the IRGC's shadow network; since July 14 it has disabled three non-compliant ships and redirected 130 vessels. The UK Navy's maritime trade center reported three tankers struck by unknown projectiles over the weekend and one tanker turned back Monday after an Iranian Revolutionary Guard threat. Washington also approved a $1B precision-rocket sale to the UAE and $400M in Patriot services for Kuwait, both announced as proposed sales pending congressional notification.

The market read-through is mixed but tense. Brent hovered near $100 a barrel Monday. Maritime tracker Kpler said non-Iran Middle East oil exports surpassed pre-war levels in September, with 40 percent of flows now bypassing Hormuz, mostly through Saudi and Emirati pipelines and the Red Sea, the very route the Yemen fighting now threatens. Saudi Aramco's CEO called global stockpiles "scarily thin" at a London energy forum, days after G7 nations agreed to release inventories. Iran's president called negotiations with the US "meaningless" after three rounds of talks were followed by renewed attacks, and Tehran says Hormuz stays closed until Washington meets its conditions. Inside Iran, the judiciary hanged two men Monday over the January protests, continuing a wartime rise in executions.
