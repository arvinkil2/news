The European Central Bank publishes the accounts of its September meeting on Thursday, and the tension they have to resolve is the sharpest in the institution's recent history. Euro area flash inflation for September came in at 3.8 percent year on year — the highest in three years, which MUFG reads as energy-driven — yet markets price only a 26 percent chance of a quarter-point hike at the October 29 meeting, and up to three hikes over twelve months.

The reason is Lagarde's doctrine: long-end yields are doing the ECB's tightening work. The evidence is France — a 149-basis-point OAT-Bund spread, the widest since the 2012 debt crisis, pricing governance failure rather than monetary policy. With the deposit rate at 2.50 percent and the French 10-year near 5 percent, the transmission is running through fiscal-risk premia, not the policy rate.

The week's euro area data give the accounts their context: services PMIs on Monday showed business activity expanding at its fastest pace in more than three years, retail sales land Tuesday, German industrial production Wednesday. If the accounts read hawkish into 3.8 percent inflation, the 26 percent hike pricing has room to run — and French spreads are the amplifier.
