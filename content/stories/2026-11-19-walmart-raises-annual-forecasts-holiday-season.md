Walmart slightly raised its annual targets on Thursday, betting that demand for lower-priced groceries and growth in its delivery business will support sales through the holiday season. The retailer now expects fiscal 2027 net sales to grow 4.9 to 5.2 percent, up from its previous forecast of 4 to 5 percent, and adjusted earnings of $2.84 to $2.89 a share, up from $2.80 to $2.87; analysts on average had expected sales growth of 5.5 percent and earnings of $2.88 a share, according to LSEG data cited by Reuters.

In the reported quarter, total revenue rose a better-than-expected 5.2 percent to $188.84 billion, while operating expenses jumped 5 percent to $40.01 billion and membership fee revenue rose by double digits in the three months through October. Reuters described the outlook as muted: shoppers are concentrating spending on groceries and essentials and seeking value elsewhere, the company has cut prices on thousands of items this year, and freight and logistics costs have surged, squeezing margins across the retail sector. Rival Target also raised its annual forecast on Wednesday after another strong quarter.
