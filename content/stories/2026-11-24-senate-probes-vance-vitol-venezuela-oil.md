A US Senate committee is investigating whether Vice President JD Vance played a role in the awarding of a lucrative Venezuelan oil deal to commodities giant Vitol, according to a letter reviewed by Reuters. Senator Maggie Hassan, a New Hampshire Democrat, wrote to Vance that she has "serious concern that you may have used your position as Vice President to help a major political donor secure lucrative business opportunities involving Venezuelan oil."

Hassan will become chair of the Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee in January when the new Congress is seated. She is seeking documents from Vance's office by December 14, among them communications between Vance or his staff and Addison or the trading firm.

The investigation follows a Reuters report that detailed a meeting between Vance and Vitol trader John Addison in December 2025, during a trip Vance made to Montana's Yellowstone Club, a week before the United States invaded Venezuela in January and captured Nicolas Maduro. According to a person familiar with the matter, who was given an account of the discussion by Addison, Vance was particularly interested in how quickly Venezuelan crude could be sold if the US hypothetically gained control of the country's oil supplies, and Addison assured him Vitol was well placed to do that job. At the time Addison was a gasoline trader for Vitol with no expertise in trading Venezuelan crude, according to Vitol; he retired from the firm earlier this year.

Both Vance and Addison have denied having the meeting. Hassan wrote in her letter: "Despite denials from your office and Mr. Addison, flight records place his private jet and your government plane at the same Montana airport on the day of the reported meeting." Vance's press secretary, Luke Schroeder, said in a statement: "The alleged airport meeting never happened." Reuters noted that its original report did not specify that the meeting happened at the airport. Vitol and Addison were not immediately available for comment.

What is not disputed is the sequence that followed. Addison has confirmed to Reuters that on January 5, two days after the operation that ousted Maduro and killed up to 100 people, US Energy Secretary Chris Wright called him at his desk in Houston to ask whether Vitol wanted the job of selling Venezuela's oil. The Department of Energy confirmed Wright called Addison on January 5 to discuss Venezuela. Within days, Vitol and its rival Trafigura landed special licences to sell Venezuela's oil, according to the two firms, and the companies have since earned hundreds of millions of dollars marketing the crude, according to estimates by independent experts. The United States handled more than 12 billion dollars of Venezuela's oil revenue in the first nine months of this year, according to a report made public by the State Department earlier this month.

Addison had been an early backer of Vance, contributing 300,000 dollars in 2022 to the Protect Ohio Values Political Action Committee to help Vance's successful bid for one of Ohio's US Senate seats, Federal Election Commission records show. Later, Addison and his wife, Shannon, contributed 7 million dollars in 2024 to political action committees to help secure Trump's second term in the White House, according to the same records.
