**Latest.** Lenders backing the SpaceX debt package sought safeguards that would prevent Elon Musk from sending the Nvidia chips securing the financing into space, the Financial Times reported on Thursday, citing people familiar with the matter. The lenders' concern, as reported, is that chips deployed in orbital data centres would be difficult to repossess and sell after a default. The FT said it remains unclear whether the proposed restriction was included in the final loan documents, and that SpaceX has said the chips would be used in data centres on Earth.

SpaceX moved to issue at least $32 billion of debt to fund its artificial intelligence chip purchases on Thursday, at yields topping 8 percent, Bloomberg reported, in the long-awaited investor sale of the financing assembled by Apollo Global Management and Goldman Sachs behind the company's roughly $40 billion GPU lease programme.

The sale is structured in seven parts and sits inside a special purpose vehicle, so the borrowing stays off SpaceX's own balance sheet and the company rents the graphics processors from the vehicle instead, according to reporting by Bloomberg and Semafor citing people familiar with the matter. The bulk of the funding, about $35.5 billion across the wider package, was expected to price at rates of 7.75 percent and 8.3 percent, while riskier parts accounting for less than $3 billion could price at a blended rate above 9 percent. Apollo was to lend close to $20 billion of the deal upfront and then syndicate or sell it to other investors. Apollo declined to comment and SpaceX did not respond to a request for comment in the accounts read.

The pricing lands well above SpaceX's existing borrowing costs. Both reported rates sit more than a percentage point above where the company's outstanding bonds trade, and the accounts attribute the level to a debt market that has grown choosier about AI infrastructure exposure, with investors fretting over the growing debt loads carried by companies in the buildout. The sale is nonetheless the market's first full test of demand for the largest single GPU financing assembled this year, after weeks in which reported price talks were reopened as credit conditions worsened.

The underlying obligation is the one document in the record that SpaceX itself has filed. In a Form 8-K published on October 26, the company disclosed a binding commitment letter, dated October 24, to enter into a master finance lease with an Apollo-managed lessor, under which the lessor acquires GPU pods and related equipment and leases them to SpaceX for terms of about five years, with aggregate principal payments expected to total approximately $40 billion. The filing said the lease's fixed rate would be determined at a future pricing and that the master lease itself had not been executed, with entry expected before the end of 2026 and equipment deliveries, for Nvidia Vera Rubin hardware, in the first half of 2027. Thursday's issuance is that pricing arriving: what remains unconfirmed by any filing is how the final tranches, allocations and rates compare with the ranges reported this week.
