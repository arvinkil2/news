The South African Reserve Bank kept its policy rate unchanged at 7.25 percent on Thursday. Four members of the Monetary Policy Committee preferred no change, while three favoured an increase of 25 basis points. The bank said near-term inflation is above 5 percent, mainly because of fuel costs, with fuel inflation around 40 percent and more than a third of all inflation coming from transport, and it assessed inflation risks to the upside.

Governor Lesetja Kganyago said high inflation prints above 5 percent are expected to persist into early next year before easing, that growth is seen picking up to about 2 percent annually over the forecast period from just over 1 percent now, and that the bank would not hesitate to raise rates if needed to return inflation to its 3 percent target. Its energy and fiscal risk scenarios both showed higher inflation, lower growth and at least two hikes. The next statement is due on 28 January.
