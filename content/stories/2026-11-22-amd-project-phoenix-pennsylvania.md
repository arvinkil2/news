A large-scale data center campus is being planned in Pennsylvania with chip firm AMD earmarked as the end user. AMD has been identified as the intended tenant of the proposed Project Phoenix development in Mount Carmel Township in Northumberland County, on land along Arcos Drive north of Route 61 and west of Route 54, near Kulpmont and Marion Heights.

The project is the subject of a conditional use application submitted by Fit Energy USA Inc. for approximately 351 acres of former mining property, which Fit Energy acquired from Susquehanna Coal Company in April this year. Application documents hosted on the township's website suggest data center firm 5C is the intended operator: 5C PHL01 Inc. is negotiating an agreement to purchase the undeveloped property from Fit Energy, and AMD is listed as the intended end user.

The first single-story building could total 160MW of IT capacity across 482,895 square feet. The first overall phase could see two buildings totaling 1.5 million square feet, with four more totaling 1.85 million square feet developed last, alongside on-site generation. According to the project website, primary power will come from fuel cells, and the liquid-cooled campus will use closed-loop cooling and air-cooled chillers to reduce water use.

The filing connects to a partnership announced earlier this year: 5C and AMD announced a strategic collaboration in July to accelerate the development of the next generation of gigascale AI campuses, with few details provided at the time. 5C, formed last year when Hypertec's cloud division was spun out and merged with 5C Data Centers, has facilities in development across Ohio, Arizona, North Carolina and Tennessee that could reach more than 2GW at full build-out. AMD is separately set to lease gigawatts of capacity across the US from Core Scientific and 50MW from Riot in Texas, a pattern of securing AI capacity through partners' balance sheets rather than its own construction program.
