Artemis Gold's Blackwater mine in central British Columbia produced 69,546 ounces of gold in the third quarter of 2026, with 12.4 million tonnes mined, 10 percent more than the same quarter in 2025. Year to date production reached 205,532 ounces, and the company held full year guidance of 265,000 to 290,000 ounces, with all-in sustaining costs of $1,048 an ounce in the quarter keeping Blackwater among the lowest cost gold mines in its peer group.

The growth projects are the story. Phase 1A, which lifts processing capacity by 33 percent to 8 million tonnes a year at a cost of about $120 million, was 77 to 80 percent complete at the end of the quarter and is on track for tie-in and commissioning in December. The crushing circuit has already been proven at the 8 million tonne a year rate. The much larger EP2 project, budgeted at $1.44 billion, would take throughput to 21 million tonnes a year and annual production above 500,000 ounces; early works finished ahead of schedule, major works construction began on August 1, and completion is targeted for the second half of 2028. Studies are also running on debottlenecking both expansions toward 25 million tonnes a year and on a possible Phase 3.

Management tied the expansion path to the acquisition of Vista Gold, which adds the Mt Todd development asset in Australia. The company frames the sequence as Blackwater past 500,000 ounces a year within two years, with Mt Todd providing a pathway toward one million ounces a year across the portfolio.

The near term test is execution in a softening tape: Phase 1A commissioning lands in December, in the same quarter the gold price has fallen toward $3,500. With costs near $1,048 an ounce, Artemis has more margin buffer than most developers, but the EP2 commitment of $1.44 billion will be financed against whatever gold price prevails in 2027.
