The Panama Canal handled 13,951 transits in fiscal 2026, which ran from October 2025 through September 2026, up 4.1 percent from 13,404 a year earlier, with tonnage reaching 521 million PC/UMS tons, up 6.5 percent from 489.2 million, and total revenue rising 16.6 percent to B/. 6.6 billion, the Panama Canal Authority reported on Wednesday. AFP reported that the 521 million tons is a record, above the 518 million tons set in fiscal 2022, and that administrator Ilya Espino de Marotta attributed a surge in traffic to the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, for which the canal raised its daily transit limit from 36 to 41 vessels for a period. El Niño drought then cut into that gain: the authority reduced daily transits to 32 in early October as its reservoir lakes fell, and on Wednesday it reinstated its Last-Minute Reservation service for the Panamax locks, citing water conservation and rainfall nearing average levels.
