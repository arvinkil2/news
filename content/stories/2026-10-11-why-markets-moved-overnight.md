Copper miners rose 4.29 percent, the session's biggest move, a second straight day of gains as the tape kept repricing Chilean supply risk around the Centinela strike (see the Centinela strike story). Copper itself added 2.65 percent to $6.69 a pound, part of the same metals bid.

Corn held at 479.8 cents, down 4.1 percent, still digesting the October WASDE, which raised the US corn yield to 181.2 bushels an acre and projected 16.034 billion bushels of production against 1.849 billion in ending stocks, burying the drought-damage narrative (see the WASDE corn shock story). Wheat followed, down 1.79 percent to 671 cents.

Dry bulk freight fell 4.07 percent to 13.89, the weakest of the commodity groups this morning; no clear single driver in the reporting. Tanker freight eased 1.43 percent but remains up 14.35 percent on the week on the Hormuz disruption.

Silver rose 3.36 percent to $61.05 and silver miners added 2.56 percent, with gold up 1.43 percent to $4,216.30 and gold miners up 2.95 percent. No clear single driver in the reporting beyond the broad metals bid that has run for two sessions.

Amazon gained 3.29 percent, leading the megacaps as the AI trade bounced back on Friday, with Microsoft up 2.38 percent alongside it. No clear single driver in the reporting.

Energy's midday read is all setup, not movement: Brent and WTI sat at $104.72 and $91.85 while natural gas added 1.64 percent to $3.22, with Hurricane Isaias damage still limiting Gulf supply and the Saturday emergency SPR exchange (see the SPR exchange story) working through. The Riyadh airport exchange escalated again Sunday morning (see the Riyadh strike story), so crude and freight face the most headline risk when futures reopen Sunday evening.

The cross-market read: metals bid while the dollar barely moved (DXY +0.07 percent) and rates sat still (US 10Y +1.3bp to 5.24 percent), a combination that points to commodity-specific and positioning flows rather than a macro rates impulse. Grains are the mirror image, still digesting the bearish WASDE supply print. Equities leaned risk-on in the US and Europe (DAX +1.13 percent, FTSE +1.06 percent, Hang Seng +1.79 percent) while semiconductors lagged, down 5 percent on the week.
