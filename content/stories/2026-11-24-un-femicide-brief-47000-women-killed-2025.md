Forty seven thousand women and girls were killed intentionally during 2025 by an intimate partner or family member, according to new data published on Tuesday by the UN Office on Drugs and Crime and UN Women. The 2026 Femicide Brief, released to mark the International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women, reveals that home remains the most dangerous place for a woman or girl, a pattern that has barely shifted in years. Sixty per cent of the 78,000 total femicides in 2025 were committed by an intimate partner or family member, an average of five women every hour, or 129 per day.

In no region are women or girls safe from femicide. Africa recorded the highest estimated number of intimate partner and family related femicides, with 22,100 victims, and the highest rate, at 2.9 victims per 100,000 female population, followed by the Americas at 1.4 per 100,000, Oceania at 1.1, Asia at 0.6 and Europe at 0.5. Asia recorded 15,100 victims, the Americas 7,400 and Europe 2,100.

"For far too long, we have seen alarming statistics," said UNODC Executive Director Monica Juma. "Frightening attitudes, social norms, and the failure of institutional safeguards create an environment in which killing a wife, daughter, mother, or sister becomes 'tolerable'." UN Women Executive Director Sima Bahous said: "We must ensure women can report violence safely, access protection, and receive support before it is too late. When warning signs go unreported, cases are not properly investigated, women and girls are not protected and perpetrators are not brought to justice, the consequences can be fatal."

While homicide rates globally have declined, femicide rates have done so at a slower pace. Between 2010 and 2025, homicide rates declined nearly twice as fast as femicide rates in the Americas, and about 1.5 times as fast in Europe. A separate UN Women report, Access to Justice for Women Survivors of Violence, drawing on data from 54 countries, finds that nearly one in three women worldwide have experienced physical or sexual violence, yet only seven per cent report it to police.

The agencies note that though the 47,000 women and girls killed in the private sphere in 2025 is lower than the 2024 estimate of 50,000 victims, the change is largely due to improvements in data availability at the country level and is not indicative of an actual decrease, and that the recorded cases mean the true toll is likely higher. The brief opens this year's 16 Days of Activism campaign, running from November 25 to December 10 under the theme "UNiTE to End Violence Against Women and Girls: Ensure justice, protect survivors, end impunity."
