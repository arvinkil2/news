Developer Marimaca Copper (TSX: MARI; ASX: MC2) has secured investment committee backing from a Sprott unit for a proposed funding package to help build its main copper project in northern Chile. Marimaca signed a non-binding term sheet with Sprott Resource Lending for up to $450 million (C$646.6 million), including $425 million in senior secured debt, according to a company statement on Tuesday. Sprott will also make an equity investment of up to $25 million.

The first $100 million of the loan would become available after a construction decision, subject to final agreements and other conditions, and Sprott must still complete financial and legal due diligence. The 7.5 year loan would carry a minimum interest rate of 8.5 percent, priced at 5.75 percentage points over the secured overnight financing rate with a floor of 2.75 percent on the benchmark. Principal repayments would begin on March 31, 2031. The lender would require neither copper price hedges nor payments tied to production.

The Marimaca oxide project, about 45 km from Antofagasta, would cost $587 million to build and produce an average of 43,000 tonnes of copper cathode a year over 13 years, according to Marimaca's 2025 feasibility study. The planned open pit operation would use heap leaching and sits near highways, power lines and the port of Mejillones. Marimaca had $127 million in cash and equivalents at September 30, received environmental approval in November 2025 and is pursuing the remaining construction permits. It still has to arrange the rest of the construction financing.

National Bank Financial analyst Andrew Dusome pointed to a targeted final investment decision in mid 2027, and expected a first resource estimate for the nearby Pampa Medina discovery in the first half of next year. Marimaca shares dropped 1.8 percent to C$5.86 on Tuesday afternoon in Toronto, valuing the company at about C$794 million.
