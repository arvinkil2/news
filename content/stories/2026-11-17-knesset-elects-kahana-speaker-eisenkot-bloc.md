The Knesset elected Matan Kahana as speaker on Tuesday, approving the ally of Gadi Eisenkot by 66 votes to 52 over Ofir Katz, the candidate backed by Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc, after a debate that ran for seven hours. Kahana, who replaces the Netanyahu ally Amir Ohana, was backed by the Jewish-led opposition parties and the Arab-led parties, the same alignment that has supported Eisenkot since Netanyahu and his allies lost their majority in the October 27 election.

Eisenkot, who has up to five more weeks to form a coalition, said the appointment reflected the majority in favour of change, and his bloc has pledged to use its control of the chamber to advance bills setting prime ministerial term limits, opening a state inquiry into the October 7 attacks and reversing laws that curbed the judiciary. Analysts quoted by AP noted the speaker can make the legislative process much faster or much slower, which matters while the coalition talks run against their deadline.
