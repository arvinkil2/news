"If you ask whether we're on a path back to 2 percent, my sense is that we are not right now, and we need to address this," Chicago Federal Reserve President Austan Goolsbee told CNBC's Squawk Box on Friday. He said price pressures are spreading beyond energy and tariff costs into services and medical care, which are unlikely to cool significantly in the near term, and pointed to the data centre construction boom driven by the artificial intelligence race as a source of strong demand that is lifting prices.

Goolsbee said the Fed cannot treat inflation as temporary and look through it if demand is overheating, and that it could take more aggressive action if the data show inflation worsening. Even a rapid fall in crude prices would not fully relieve energy pressure, he added, because refining capacity constraints would keep diesel and jet fuel prices elevated.
