PepGen's experimental oligonucleotide drug PGN-EDODM1 repaired, on average, 46.5 percent of the splicing errors caused by myotonic dystrophy type 1 after four monthly doses in six treated patients, with four of the six above 50 percent, and the company said the results were the highest splicing correction yet seen for a multi-dose DM1 drug. Side effects were judged mild to moderate, with nausea most common and no sign of cumulative kidney damage over 16 weeks, though dosing in the United States remains under a partial FDA hold dating to last year and results from a higher dose cohort are expected in the first half of next year. The same day, PepGen filed a preliminary prospectus supplement to sell $125 million of common stock and pre-funded warrants, raising money into a share price that rose more than 90 percent on the data.
