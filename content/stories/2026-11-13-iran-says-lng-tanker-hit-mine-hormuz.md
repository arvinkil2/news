**Latest.** A second tally has put the same October peak on the record and added the month's traffic scale. Lloyd's List Intelligence counted 37 ship attacks around the Strait of Hormuz in October, the highest monthly total since the war began, and 418 transits by non-Iranian tankers, also a monthly record, Yonhap News Agency reported. At least 14 attacks had been confirmed in November by the time of its report, and Lloyd's assessed that Hormuz traffic was recovering but not back to normal, with the risk of further deliberate attacks very high.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards navy said a tanker carrying liquefied natural gas struck a naval mine and suffered an explosion in the Strait of Hormuz on Friday while using what it described as an unauthorized and dangerous route, Iranian state media reported, according to Reuters. No details on the tanker were immediately available, and no independent source has confirmed the incident.

The claim follows Iranian media reports on Thursday that four oil tankers were hit by explosions in the waterway after ignoring warnings and entering areas Iran considers restricted. At least 10 ships were hit in the first 10 days of November, after 37 attacks in October, the highest monthly tally since the conflict began in late February, according to data from the Washington Institute for Near East Policy cited by Reuters.
