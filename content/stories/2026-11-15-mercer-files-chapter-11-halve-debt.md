Mercer International, the Vancouver-based forest products group, has filed for prepackaged Chapter 11 protection in the United States to restructure its balance sheet. Mercer and one non-operating subsidiary, Mercer International (Texas) LLC, filed their cases in the US Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas on November 9. Its operating subsidiaries are not part of the proceedings and the mills are to keep paying suppliers in the ordinary course.

The numbers frame the plan. Funded debt at filing was 1,642.8 million dollars; the plan swaps about 1.1 billion dollars of bonds for shares and targets funded debt of about 700 million dollars by December 31. The disclosure statement gives about 122 million dollars a year of cash interest before the restructuring and an expected reduction of about 100 million dollars.

The plan has its creditors behind it. Mercer said it has a restructuring support agreement with holders of more than 88 percent of the outstanding principal of its 12.875 percent senior notes due 2028 and its 5.125 percent senior notes due 2029. Under the proposed allocation, senior note holders would take 91.5 percent of the new common stock and existing shareholders 8.5 percent.

Mercer makes pulp, lumber and cross-laminated timber at mills in Germany, Canada and the United States, with capacity to make 2.1 million tonnes of pulp a year. President and chief executive Juan Carlos Bueno called the filing a planned step toward completing the previously announced recapitalisation. The restructuring remains subject to court confirmation of the plan and other conditions, and Mercer expects to complete it by the end of 2026.
