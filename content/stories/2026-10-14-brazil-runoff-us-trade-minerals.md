Brazil's runoff campaign has settled into a trade argument. Senator Flavio Bolsonaro leads President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva after a first round in which he took about 47 percent to the incumbent's about 45 percent, and the relationship with Washington is one of the clearest divides between them. Brazil and the United States set up a working group last month to drive negotiations over tariffs and a broader bilateral trade framework, and US access to Brazil's critical minerals and rare earths has emerged as a key point of contention as Washington tries to reduce dependence on China for those inputs.

Analysts quoted by Reuters warn the negotiation itself is now hostage to the result: Washington may prove reluctant to concede anything to Lula if it expects to negotiate with Bolsonaro instead. Lula's approach has been to defend Brazilian sovereignty after US pressure over the prosecution of the challenger's father, former president Jair Bolsonaro, who is serving a 27 year sentence over a coup plot, while prioritising multilateralism and closer ties with the BRICS bloc and the Global South. The challenger is expected to campaign on cost of living, crime and the promise of better deals with the Trump administration.

The economic stakes run in both directions. Brazil's next president must manage tensions between its largest trading partner, China, and its second largest, the United States, while shaping mineral development, industrialisation and regional policy. A Bolsonaro victory would extend a run of right wing wins in Latin America aligned with Washington; a Lula re election would preserve distance from the United States and deepen the China relationship.

Bloomberg's October 14 explainer frames the same stakes for the October 25 vote. No new polling or official development was reported on Tuesday; this is a material update to the runoff picture, not a new event. Status: campaign in progress, vote on October 25.
