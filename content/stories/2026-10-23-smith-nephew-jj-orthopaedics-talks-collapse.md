Smith & Nephew's talks to combine with Johnson & Johnson's orthopaedics division fell apart after the London-listed group refused to agree to its former chief executive, Namal Nawana, leading the merged business, the Financial Times reported, citing people familiar with the matter. The talks were at an advanced stage and the companies were aligned on the main financial terms and structure, but Smith & Nephew said it would only agree if its own chief executive, Deepak Nath, remained in charge; Nawana left in 2019 after 18 months when the company could not meet his pay demands. The deal would have valued Johnson & Johnson's hip and knee replacement unit at as much as 22 billion dollars, almost double Smith & Nephew's 9.4 billion pound (12.58 billion dollar) market capitalisation; a Smith & Nephew spokesperson declined to comment and Johnson & Johnson could not be reached outside business hours.
