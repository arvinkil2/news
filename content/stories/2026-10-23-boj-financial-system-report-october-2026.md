Japan's financial system has been maintaining stability on the whole and no major financial imbalances have been seen, the Bank of Japan said in its semi-annual Financial System Report, adding that banks have sufficient capital and stable funding to withstand stress equivalent to the global financial crisis. This edition refines its macro stress testing around a fall in expectations for AI investment profitability, amplified mainly by portfolio adjustments in the foreign non-bank financial sector, where private funds have become prominent providers of financing for data centre investment that hyperscalers cannot cover internally. The Bank also noted that its stock price indicator continues to deviate upward from trend, that valuation losses on yen-denominated bonds have been increasing at regional banks and shinkin banks as rates rise, and that the yield gap on Tokyo commercial real estate has been declining.

Two domestic findings sit behind the stable headline. Housing loans with maturities longer than 35 years are growing as borrowers stretch terms to hold monthly payments down. The Bank also says lending to foreign funds and data centres, while still a limited share of major banks' overseas lending, has become a focus segment because of its higher spreads, and that banks in that business need to manage portfolio quality carefully.
