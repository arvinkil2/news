The US Food and Drug Administration on Monday approved Welgemda (ribitol), the first medicine for limb-girdle muscular dystrophy type 2I/R9, a rare inherited muscle disease caused by mutations in the FKRP gene, for adults and pediatric patients weighing at least 30 kilograms. The oral solution, taken twice daily, is manufactured by ML Bio Solutions Inc. and distributed by BridgeBio Pharma, Inc., and it is the first therapy approved for any form of limb-girdle muscular dystrophy.

The approval rests on a randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind study of 112 patients with genetically confirmed disease, in which patients on Welgemda showed significant improvements in walking speed on the 100-metre timed test and in lung function at a planned 12-month interim analysis, the FDA said. The most common adverse reactions were gastrointestinal effects and joint pain, and the drug carried Orphan Drug, Fast Track and rare pediatric disease designations.
