President Donald Trump said on Thursday that Canadian jet maker Bombardier is investing 270 million dollars in the United States, in a Truth Social post crediting Senator Roger Marshall of Kansas and Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton for the deal. Trump said the investment would create thousands of jobs in Kansas, Texas and Indiana. Bombardier did not respond to a request for comment on the investment details announced by Trump, Reuters reported.

The announcement closes a pressure campaign. Trump threatened in September to ban the company from the country, a threat that became a political issue in Kansas, where Bombardier employs 1,500 people in Wichita and where Marshall faces a tough re election race in the November 3 elections. In February Trump had threatened to decertify Bombardier aircraft over a certification dispute involving rival Gulfstream Aerospace; that threat never materialised because Transport Canada ultimately approved the jets. At a speech in Wichita on Monday, Trump said he did not plan to hurt Bombardier and would take care of its workers.

Bombardier's own confirmed step is smaller: the company announced a 50 million dollar investment in Wichita last week. Whether the 270 million dollars includes that amount, over what period it would be spent, and on which facilities, was not stated in the announcement or confirmed by the company.

The pattern is now familiar in North American industrial policy: a tariff or certification threat against a foreign manufacturer with a large US workforce is withdrawn in exchange for a headline investment number. The capital allocation is real only to the extent the company later commits it in its own filings, which is the document to watch.
