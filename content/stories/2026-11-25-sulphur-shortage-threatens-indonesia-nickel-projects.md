A sulphur shortage threatens projects that would add more than 600,000 tonnes of nickel capacity in Indonesia's battery-materials sector, Wood Mackenzie said on Wednesday. Indonesia had relied on the Middle East for about 80 percent of its sulphur imports before the closure of the Strait of Hormuz; imports fell 29 percent year over year in the first half of 2026 and average prices nearly tripled.

Indonesian plants use sulphur to make the acid that extracts nickel and cobalt from ore in high-pressure acid leach processing, and sulphur accounts for roughly half the operating cost of those plants, the International Energy Agency estimates. Wood Mackenzie research associate Ziyi Liu said supply constraints are unlikely to ease quickly, because logistics normalisation takes time even if conflict-related disruptions subside and the fertiliser sector competes for the same supply. The consultancy also reported production cuts of 30 to 45 percent at smelters in the Indonesia Morowali Industrial Park in late September as drought cut water supply and barge deliveries of coal.
