Latest: Oil rebounded for a second day on Friday but stayed on track for its steepest weekly fall since 2020. Brent futures for January were up 31 cents, or 0.3 percent, to 98.60 dollars a barrel by 1129 GMT and WTI was up 48 cents, or 0.5 percent, to 91.00 dollars, Reuters reported, putting Brent on track for a 16 percent weekly decline and WTI for 13 percent. OilPrice, earlier in the session, had Brent at 99.42 dollars and WTI at 91.24 dollars, on course for weekly losses of about 12 percent on its contract basis. Prices fell 8 percent on Wednesday alone after the Democratic midterm sweep, then recovered as tanker attacks in the Gulf and Hormuz surged, with UKMTO data showing attacks on oil carrying ships still running close to record pace and Reuters reporting at least five vessel incidents in the Persian Gulf since Sunday. Rystad analyst Janiv Shah said the balance is loosening toward oversupply while quality and routing frictions persist around Hormuz.

Latest: Oil fell for a fourth day on Thursday, Brent down 80 cents, or 0.85 percent, to 93.82 dollars a barrel and West Texas Intermediate down 1.20 dollars, or 1.36 percent, to 86.92 dollars, after both benchmarks tumbled more than 8 percent on Wednesday to settle at their lowest since August 31, Reuters reported. Traders read the Republican midterm losses as reducing the risk of further escalation of the US-Israeli war on Iran: two sources close to Gulf government circles said a weakened President Trump has greater incentive to seek a deal and a full scale assault now looks less likely. JPMorgan said the resumption of Gulf supply has pushed the global market back into surplus, estimated at 1.1 million barrels a day in September and widening to 1.4 million in October, and analysts said crude shipments through the Strait of Hormuz have recovered to close to pre-war levels, though at a higher cost. Eurasia Group cut its probability of a deal to 10 percent from 20 percent and raised a continued standoff to 30 percent, and warned escalation would likely push Brent into a band of 110 to 125 dollars.

Latest: President Trump said he has no immediate plans to launch strikes on Iran after the midterm rout. "We seem to be getting everything we want," he told the Financial Times, adding that the United States is still prepared to escalate if necessary: "I'm ready, we're ready. We're locked and loaded if we need to go, but there may be no reason for it." Iran's parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf read the election the other way, saying Americans had voted "NO TO WAR" and warning Washington of a "nightmare" if mass attacks restart. Bloomberg reported that Qatar and Pakistan mediated talks have made little progress in recent weeks, with Qatari officials expecting feedback from both sides on new proposals within a day or so.

Latest: The Energy Information Administration confirmed a fifth straight weekly fall in US crude stocks, but a much smaller one than the industry data had pointed to. Commercial crude inventories fell 865,000 barrels to 409.8 million barrels in the week ended October 30, 4 percent below the five year average, against a Reuters poll expectation of an 877,000 barrel build and the American Petroleum Institute's 5.27 million barrel draw. The tightening was in products: distillate stocks fell 3.6 million barrels to 99.3 million, their lowest level since May 2003 on Dow Jones reporting of the EIA data, against an expected 100,000 barrel drop, and gasoline fell 222,000 barrels to 209 million against an expected build. Refinery runs rose 199,000 barrels a day with utilisation up 1.7 points to 94.5 percent, and total products supplied averaged 21.2 million barrels a day over four weeks, up 4.2 percent on a year earlier.


Oil traded both ways on Wednesday as investors tried to price a changed Washington against an unchanged physical market. Brent futures rose 76 cents, or 0.7 percent, to 104.36 dollars a barrel by 0730 GMT and West Texas Intermediate gained 63 cents to 96.72 dollars, reversing earlier declines, Reuters reported. Invezz, tracking the later European session, had Brent slipping back about 1.26 percent to near 102.20 dollars, and the site market tables at midday put Brent at 94.34 dollars, down 8.94 percent on the day and down 18.19 percent on the week, as the selloff extended into the US session.

The political trigger was the midterm result. Democrats captured both chambers of Congress, which traders read as a constraint on funding or expanding the war with Iran and a reason to trim the supply risk premium. Priyanka Sachdeva of Phillip Nova told Reuters that conflicting signals from President Trump, with strikes continuing alongside statements downplaying escalation, had made it difficult to build directional conviction, and that some profit taking after the October rally was justified.

The physical data pulled the other way. Market sources citing American Petroleum Institute figures said US crude stocks fell by 5.27 million barrels in the week ended October 30, against a Reuters poll expectation of a rise of about 880,000 barrels. Gasoline stocks rose by 1.43 million barrels and distillate stocks fell by 1.19 million barrels, a combination Haitong Futures described as broadly supportive, with diesel inventories still tight.

Underneath the price action, the Hormuz constraint has not lifted. A senior Iranian official told Reuters on Tuesday that Tehran and Washington remained far from a deal, the United States had carried out defensive strikes on Qeshm Island, and reported oil throughput through the strait remains well below pre conflict levels. The official EIA inventory report, due at 10:30 a.m. EST on Wednesday, is the next test of whether the API draw is confirmed.
