Ethiopia ordered six Sudanese diplomats to leave the country within 48 hours on Tuesday and closed Sudan's consulate in Asosa, a town near the border, according to Ethiopian and Sudanese officials. The move came two days after Sudan complained to the UN Security Council and the African Union's Peace and Security Council that Ethiopia allows the Rapid Support Forces to use its territory to launch drone attacks on civilian infrastructure, an accusation Ethiopia denies.

Ethiopia accuses Sudan in turn of backing rebels linked to the Tigray People's Liberation Front in its northern Tigray region, which Khartoum denies. Amjad Farid, a political and foreign affairs adviser to Sudan's Transitional Sovereign Council, said Khartoum remained committed to defending its territory while making every effort to avoid open war with Ethiopia. A direct confrontation would strain Sudan's military, which is still fighting the RSF in a civil war that began in April 2023.
