Copper plunged to its lowest level since April on Wednesday, as speculators abandoned bets tied to the artificial intelligence boom and a surging dollar and fading prospects of US tariffs on refined copper overwhelmed signs of acute physical shortage in China. Comex copper for December delivery fell as much as 6.7 percent to $5.5835 a pound ($12,309 a tonne) in late morning trade in New York, a fifth straight daily fall that left the contract down 16 percent in three weeks and 19 percent below the record settlement of $6.8885 reached on September 9. The year's gain, which stood at 22 percent at the peak, has been wiped out.

In London, three-month copper on the London Metal Exchange was down 3.4 percent at $12,772.50 a tonne in official trading and later extended its loss to as much as 5.6 percent, also the lowest since April. The most traded contract in Shanghai shed 3.4 percent to 97,920 yuan a tonne, and tin, the other metal most closely tied to AI hardware, slumped more than 7 percent in London to its lowest in almost eight months.

The selling spread to the producers. Eleven of the largest copper exposed miners lost a combined $49 billion, more than 6 percent of their market value, by early afternoon in New York, taking their losses to $112 billion over the past week and $186 billion, or 20 percent, over the past month. BHP fell 6.5 percent, Southern Copper 5.2 percent, Freeport-McMoRan 6.7 percent, Rio Tinto 5.1 percent and Glencore 6.5 percent, while Anglo American fell 8.0 percent, Teck Resources 7.4 percent, and Antofagasta and Lundin Mining each fell more than 9 percent.

Analysts pointed to a complete recalibration rather than a single trigger. Managed money net long positions on Comex have fallen from more than 80,000 contracts six weeks ago to about 35,000, close to a 14 month low, speculative positioning has in places flipped net short, and trend following funds have accelerated the decline as chart levels gave way. Sentiment took a further knock late on Tuesday when Stan Druckenmiller, among the best known backers of copper's AI trade, told a conference in Austin that he is now short the metal. At the World Copper Conference Asia in Shanghai, Mercuria's head of metals and mining research, Nicholas Snowdon, said copper demand today is closer to a recessionary-like environment than a growth environment.

The rout sits oddly against the physical market. The Yangshan premium paid to import copper into China jumped by about a fifth to a record $360 a tonne on Wednesday, Shanghai Futures Exchange stocks dropped 18 percent last week to 27,642 tonnes, the lowest since December 2023, and spot treatment charges sank to a record minus $247.20 a tonne on November 6, meaning smelters pay miners to process their concentrate. Chile's copper commission Cochilco expects national output to fall 8.4 percent this year to 4.94 million tonnes, the lowest in 23 years. None of it stopped the financial selling, which follows the slide that took London copper to a four month low on November 16.
