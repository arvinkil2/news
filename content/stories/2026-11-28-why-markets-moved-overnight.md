Silver futures rose 7.78 percent to $43.17 an ounce, the largest one-day move in the tables, and silver miners rose 7.44 percent alongside. Friday's official settlements confirm the direction: COMEX silver settled 5.35 percent higher at $43.985 and spot silver gained 6.74 percent on the week, a positioning rebound after the prior week's slide (see the updated silver and metals story).

Gold miners rose 7.16 percent, ahead of gold futures themselves, which gained 2.94 percent to $3,639.60 an ounce. COMEX gold settled at $3,681.10, up 2.44 percent on the day, and the gold-silver ratio compressed to 83.6 as silver outran gold for a second session.

Copper rose 3.46 percent to $5.52 a pound and copper miners gained 4.57 percent. LME copper rose as much as 6.5 percent intraday, its biggest move since January, and posted its first weekly gain since early October, even as LME stocks climbed to 337,925 tonnes in contango (see today's copper story).

Natural gas shows up 6.79 percent in the tables at $3.49 per mmBtu, but that reading is a contract-roll artefact, not a rally: the January contract fell about 3.8 percent on milder weather forecasts (see today's US natural gas story).

Uranium miners rose 5.27 percent and Tesla rose 5.22 percent. Neither has a clear single driver in this morning's reporting.

Semiconductors rose 4.76 percent as technology shares broadly advanced, with the Nasdaq 100 up 2.61 percent. This morning's technology reporting offers context rather than a Friday trigger: TSMC's share of global foundry revenue reached 75 percent in the third quarter (see today's TSMC story).

The pattern is a metals-led risk-on close: miners outperformed the metals they dig, technology rose with them, and energy was quiet, with Brent settling at $95.44 for its first weekly gain in November ahead of Sunday's OPEC+ meeting. Bond yields rose about 9 basis points on the day but fell on the week, and with the Fed in blackout and the December hike all but fully priced, the next test is the December 4 payrolls report.
