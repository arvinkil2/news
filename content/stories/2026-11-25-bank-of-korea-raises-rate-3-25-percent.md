The Bank of Korea raised its base rate by 25 basis points to 3.25 percent at its final meeting of the year on Thursday, resuming tightening after a hold in October and following consecutive hikes in July and August, with the move in line with economist expectations. Consumer inflation was 2.8 percent year on year in October, with core inflation also at 2.8 percent, both above the bank's 2 percent target, while third-quarter GDP grew 3.4 percent year on year.

In its accompanying outlook, the bank raised its 2026 growth forecast to 3.9 percent and put 2027 growth at 4.1 percent, Yonhap reported, after the economy expanded 3.6 percent in the first nine months of the year on an AI-led semiconductor export cycle.
