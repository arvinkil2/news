**Latest (Monday, October 12):** AFP reporting, relayed by Arab News, adds detail to Saturday's strike on the Jabal Al-Uqaydat site. Survivor Abdelaziz Youssef told AFP a jet appeared around 8:00 am and struck five times; another survivor, Sharaf Ali, said thousands of people were working at the remote site when it was hit and that the dead are still being counted because survivors scattered in different directions. Emergency Lawyers put the preliminary toll at 72 civilian miners and said the attack occurred within Sudanese territory, arguing the repeated attacks reflect an Egyptian desire to expand control over Sudan's natural resources. This was the second strike on Jabal Al-Uqaydat this year: airstrikes, reportedly by Egypt, killed 23 workers at the informal mining site in June, after which the Egyptian army said it had conducted a "large-scale operation" against illegal gold mining within Egyptian territory, detaining 223 people including 136 foreign nationals. The site sits near the disputed Halayeb triangle, claimed by both countries, along a 1,200-kilometre border Egypt treats as a major security threat. Egypt has issued no official comment on Saturday's strike.

At least 72 Sudanese artisanal gold miners were killed in an airstrike near the Sudan-Egypt border on Saturday, the Sudanese rights group Emergency Lawyers said, per a Reuters relay. The group blamed the Egyptian air force and said such a cross-border strike would violate international law; Egypt did not comment. Unverified images circulating online showed dozens of bodies in rubble, and the strike comes as artisanal gold has become a key cash source for both sides of Sudan's war, much of it smuggled into Egypt.
