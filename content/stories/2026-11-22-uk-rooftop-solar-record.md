Britain has fitted more than 270,000 rooftop solar systems in 2026, the most in any year in the country's history, with over a month of the year still to run, the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero announced on November 20. The figure comes from MCS, the body that certifies small scale installations, and it is the sixth consecutive year the annual record for certified installations has been broken.

The announcement is as much about where panels are going as how many. Cardiff's Principality Stadium switched on more than 3,250 panels in 2026, enough to power more than 50 matchdays a year. Ascot Racecourse has installed more than 1,200 panels, expected to meet about 9 percent of its annual electricity needs. New Spitalfields Market has fitted 2,730 panels in what the City of London Corporation calls London's largest rooftop solar installation, and Aldi has put 19,000 panels on its new 500 million pound distribution centre in Leicestershire, capable of powering the entire site at peak generation. Lincolnshire Co-op has installed panels at 53 sites over two years.

Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh said Britain's rooftops are powering a solar revolution, with households and communities generating their own electricity and taking control of their bills.

The department said its official data confirming the record will be published in December. The 270,000 figure covers MCS certified small installations; larger commercial arrays sit outside it.
