The Alaska Senate race's ballot dispute has moved from allegation to court calendar. A judge has fast tracked new motions to dismiss the Justice Department's lawsuit concerning the candidacy of a second Dan Sullivan, a namesake challenger whose presence on the ballot Democrats allege was arranged to confuse voters in the race between Republican Senator Dan Sullivan and Democrat Mary Peltola. Separately, the department has issued subpoenas in the race, The Hill reported, as part of its scrutiny of the alleged ballot stacking scheme.

The procedural acceleration matters more than the filings themselves. With voting on Tuesday, a fast tracked dismissal track means the court, not the campaign, now sets the tempo of the dispute, and any ruling lands either in the final days of voting or over the count itself. Alaska's governor has warned publicly that the episode exposes a structural flaw in the state's ballot access rules, a point both parties' lawyers are likely to reuse whatever the judge decides.

The case sits inside a wider pattern this cycle: the Justice Department's election year interventions have already drawn a restraining order in California barring threats against election officials, and Alaska Republicans and Democrats are each accusing the other side of engineering the ballot. The department's theory and the defendants' answers remain allegations until the court rules.

Status: reported litigation, motions pending on an expedited track. No ruling had been reported when this edition closed.
