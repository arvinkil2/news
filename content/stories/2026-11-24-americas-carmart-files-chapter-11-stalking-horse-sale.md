America's Car-Mart, Inc. and certain subsidiaries filed voluntary petitions for Chapter 11 relief on November 24, 2026 in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas, and are seeking joint administration of the cases. The company will continue to operate as a debtor in possession, and its first-day motions seek authority to pay employees, satisfy customer obligations, pay trade creditors in full in the ordinary course and maintain its servicing platform.

The filing is an event of default under the company's prepetition Credit and Guaranty Agreement dated October 30, 2025, which automatically accelerated its obligations under that agreement. The outstanding principal balance under the agreement is $311.6 million, and enforcement is automatically stayed by the bankruptcy filing.

Alongside the petitions, the company entered into a stalking horse asset purchase agreement with a purchaser group comprising Claypool Holdings, LLC, Cardinal Finance LLC, ACM-AR Inc., ACM Finance Inc. and Cardinal Asset Holdings LLC, covering substantially all of the sellers' assets other than excluded assets, including all vehicle inventory, receivables and dealer loans, the equity of non-debtor ACM Funding, LLC and the residual certificates and servicing rights under its asset-backed securitization platform. The cash consideration is $10.0 million, plus $13.6 million in respect of owned real property, plus the value of retail vehicle inventory at closing by reference to Black Book wholesale value, plus $10.0 million in respect of new inventory, together with rollover indebtedness and the assumption of liabilities including those under the securitization documents. The purchaser must deposit $25.0 million into escrow within three business days, and its bid protections are a $9.0 million break-up fee, payable only from the proceeds of a consummated alternative transaction, and up to $1.5 million of expense reimbursement. The sale remains open to higher or better offers, including a credit bid by the company's lenders, with a bidding procedures order sought within 21 days of the petition date and a sale approval order within 35 days.

The company is seeking debtor-in-possession financing from the parties that provided its prepetition financing: new money term loan commitments of up to $60 million, drawn as up to $35 million on an interim order and up to $25 million on a final order, plus roll-up loans of up to $65 million converting part of the prepetition debt. The proposed pricing is Term SOFR, subject to a 1.00 percent floor, plus 10.50 percent for term benchmark loans, with a scheduled maturity of May 22, 2027. Under the prepackaged plan described in the filing, trade creditors would be paid in full, and $2.5 million is contemplated for holders of the common stock from the monetization of excluded assets, although the company states there can be no assurance any proceeds will be available for shareholders. Retention bonuses for employees, capped at $5.6 million in aggregate and payable only if the sale closes, include $2,000,000 for President and Chief Executive Officer Douglas Campbell, $500,000 for Chief Financial Officer Marie Persichetti and $500,000 for Chief Operating Officer Jamie Fischer. The company warned that trading in its common stock during the cases is highly speculative and that holders may experience a significant or complete loss on their investment.
