Maria Corina Machado has given Venezuela's transition a date, even if Washington has not. In her first substantive remarks since returning to the country last weekend after months in exile, the opposition leader and Nobel Peace Prize laureate said on Thursday that the United States has suggested presidential elections take place next year. Secretary of State Marco Rubio has laid out a sequence of steps for moving toward a democratic transition that makes clear that presidential elections will be held in 2027, she said, citing the prompt appointment of a renewed National Electoral Council and compliance with constitutional deadlines among the steps.

The claim is precise, and it is hers. The State Department did not immediately respond to a request for comment, and US officials' on the record statements describe institutional steps, a renewed electoral council and a new top court, without fixing a year. Machado also said she was pleased that acting President Delcy Rodriguez, who addressed the nation earlier on Thursday, had backed elections, though Rodriguez's own promise, covered in yesterday's edition, carried no date, deadline or mechanism. Two principals have now endorsed an election; only one has named when.

Machado's position in the transition is itself unsettled. She returned to Venezuela after several failed attempts this year, Washington says she has every right to be there, and she thanked President Trump and Rubio for their roles in the January capture of Nicolas Maduro. At the same time, the Trump administration has praised Rodriguez and supports negotiations between her government and opposition groups that do not include Machado's Vente Venezuela movement on overhauling the electoral council and the courts before any vote. Rubio has previously said he expects Machado to support those talks.

Her priorities, as she stated them, include dismantling the repressive apparatus and releasing political prisoners, and she said she would ensure all eligible Venezuelans can cast ballots in any future election. The test of Thursday's remarks is documentary: a published timetable, a renewed electoral council appointed on a stated schedule, or a State Department statement adopting the 2027 date. Until one of those exists, 2027 is the opposition's anchor in a negotiation, not a calendar entry.
