**Latest (Sunday 14:04 ET):** An explosion in the training area of Canadian Forces Base Wainwright at about 4 p.m. local time on Saturday set off the fire, and officials on Sunday confirmed at least four people dead and dozens injured. Twenty-two people were taken to Edmonton hospitals, three of them airlifted, with 11 in critical condition, and the Royal Alexandra Hospital declared a code orange, its disaster protocol for a high-casualty incident. Global News reported a higher toll, at least seven killed and more than 40 injured, and said the blast came during a controlled burn of unexploded ordnance that struck a gas line or well; officials have confirmed neither that toll nor that account. Defence Minister David McGuinty, who flew to the base on Sunday, said the RCMP is investigating the cause and that he could not recall an incident like this in recent memory, and Chief of the Defence Staff General Jennie Carignan called it a profoundly difficult moment for the entire Canadian Armed Forces. The fire burned about 183 hectares and was about 90 percent contained by Sunday afternoon.

A major fire broke out at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright, about 180 kilometres southeast of Edmonton, on Saturday after what Prime Minister Mark Carney, in a post on X, called a serious incident at the base, and Defence Minister David McGuinty later confirmed there had been fatalities and injuries, without giving numbers. The Municipal District of Wainwright said the fire, spreading rapidly in winds gusting to 100 kilometres an hour, had crossed the base fence and that emergency crews were focused on protecting lives, homes and other structures. Alberta Premier Danielle Smith called the fire significant and tragic in a social media post, and Saskatchewan and British Columbia offered resources and assistance.
