The US Department of the Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) issued a proposed rule on Tuesday to strengthen customer due diligence requirements, which Treasury describes as integral to efforts to combat money laundering and counter the financing of terrorism. FinCEN issued the proposal as part of Treasury's implementation of Executive Order 14406, Restoring Integrity to America's Financial System, and Secretary of the Treasury Scott Bessent said the proposed rule cuts unnecessary burden, gives institutions more flexibility to effectively combat risk, and strengthens the fight against financial crime.

The proposed rule would explicitly provide that covered financial institutions can obtain additional information about beneficial owners of a legal entity customer that may be useful in mitigating risk or addressing supervisory concerns, codify exceptive relief FinCEN issued removing the requirement to identify and verify the beneficial owners of a legal entity customer each time the customer opens a new account, and specify the information a covered financial institution must obtain about each beneficial owner. FinCEN welcomes public comments, and the comment period closes 60 days after the proposed rule's publication in the Federal Register.
