The Financial Stability Board has published its 2026 G20 implementation monitoring review, the annual stocktake of how far the group's members have put the post crisis financial reform programme into practice, timed for the Bangkok meetings.

The review's value is less in any single finding than in its timing and audience: it lands in the same week that the FSB finalised its sound practices for responsible adoption of artificial intelligence in finance, a report published here on October 15, and that finance ministers are arguing about sovereign debt and the energy shock. Implementation gaps in the non bank sector have been the review's running theme, and this year's edition keeps the pressure on the members furthest behind.

For markets the document is a slow variable. Where it names jurisdictions as lagging on agreed standards, it tends to surface later in funding costs, equivalence decisions and the conditions attached to market access.
