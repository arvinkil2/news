Glencore has picked Colin Glasenberg, a nephew of former chief executive Ivan Glasenberg, to run its coal mines, Bloomberg reported on Wednesday. Glasenberg, who runs Glencore's oil operations, is due to succeed Earl Melamed in February when Melamed retires, according to Bloomberg, which cited people familiar with the appointment. Glencore still lists Melamed as its head of industrial coal.

The appointment comes as the company tries to recover lost production at Elk Valley Resources in British Columbia. Elk Valley mined 16.1 million tonnes of steelmaking coal in the nine months to September 30, down 3.3 million tonnes, or 17 percent, from a year earlier, more than the 3.2 million tonne decline across Glencore's entire steelmaking coal business. Its plants processed less coal and recovered less saleable product as crews opened new mining areas, Glencore said in its production update. Output rose 16 percent in the September quarter from the previous quarter, and the company expects full year steelmaking coal production near the low end of its 30 million to 32 million tonne forecast.

The Canadian mines generated $843 million in adjusted earnings in the six months to June 30, 44 percent of the $1.93 billion Glencore earned on the same basis from all the coal it mined, according to the company's half year figures. Glencore bought 77 percent of Elk Valley from Teck Resources in 2024 and shelved plans to spin off the coal business after consulting shareholders.

Ivan Glasenberg ran Glencore from 2002 to 2021 and was its largest shareholder as of January 30, with 1.22 billion shares, according to the company's annual report. Separately, the future of Elk Valley's Fording River mine hinges on a proposed extension now under federal and provincial review, which would maintain capacity at 10 million tonnes a year and could add about 35 years to the mine's life.
