## Latest

Governor Sanjay Malhotra announced Wednesday that the MPC voted 6-0 to lift the repo rate to 5.50%, the first increase since February 2023, ending the pause that followed 125bp of cuts through 2025. The committee shifted its stance from neutral to "calibrated tightening", a hawkish signal that takes rate cuts off the table and leaves room for further moves. The FY2027 projections moved in opposite directions: CPI inflation raised to 5.2% from 5.0%, and real GDP growth raised to 7.1% from 6.7%, framing the hike as preemption against price pressures in a still-resilient economy. The standing deposit facility now stands at 5.25% and the marginal standing facility and Bank Rate at 5.75%.

Markets gave the decision a bearish reception. The 10-year G-sec yield hardened 5-6bp to about 7.25%, the Sensex closed down 429 points and the Nifty fell 0.76%, and the rupee slid toward a record low, with traders signaling the 25bp move left them wanting more. Kotak Mahindra AMC's fixed-income chief expects another 25bp hike in December if inflation keeps broadening, while Anand Rathi called the decision touch-and-go, fully priced in, and unlikely to disturb financial markets. Governor Malhotra framed the move as reinforcement of inflation credibility before cyclical risks become entrenched, citing elevated oil prices, a weaker rupee, tighter global conditions and weather-driven food price risk.

The Reserve Bank of India's Monetary Policy Committee meets October 5-7, with Governor Sanjay Malhotra announcing the decision Wednesday at 10:00 a.m. IST. A Reuters poll of 61 economists taken September 18-28 found 35, roughly 60%, expect a 25bp hike to 5.50% from the 5.25% level held at the past four meetings. A move would be the first increase since February 2023 (CONFIRMED via Mint reporting).

The case rests on three legs. August retail inflation accelerated to 4.82%, above the RBI's medium-term target. The rupee has slipped past 96 per dollar while the Fed's September hike narrowed the India-US rate differential, adding currency depreciation pressure. And Brent crude above $100, the physical Hormuz corridor is barely functioning on a convoy basis this weekend, feeds directly into India's import-price channel.

Bank of America economists have now pulled their call forward, expecting tightening to begin at the October 7 meeting rather than December, and projecting the repo rate to reach 6.25% in the first half of 2027, roughly doubling the quantum of tightening they previously expected. Domestic funding markets are already tightening ahead of the decision: INR MIFOR overnight spiked 118bp week-on-week. Tuesday's decision is the nearest major central-bank binary in global markets, a hold would jolt the 60% consensus and ease that funding squeeze.
