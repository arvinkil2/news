## Latest

The strike is on. More than 700 employees of the Centinela mine, controlled 70 percent by Antofagasta and 30 percent by Marubeni, began their walkout at 8:00am local time Wednesday after the Labor Directorate's mandatory mediation concluded without consensus on equalizing salaries and benefits across unions. The calling unions, Workers' Union of Minera Esperanza and Workers' Union District Centinela, said the mobilization will continue "orderly and peaceful" as long as necessary. The striking workers represent about 22 percent of Centinela's direct workforce, and the stoppage is the first at the mine and only the second in Antofagasta's history. Centinela produced 240,400 metric tons of copper last year.

Antofagasta said it remains willing to continue discussions reflecting both parties' interests and that it does not expect the disruption to alter its production outlook. The dispute centers on pay and benefit differences among employees doing the same jobs under different union agreements. The unions say the company rejected a proposed mechanism to eliminate the gaps without offering an alternative. The differences date partly to bargaining schedules: the company negotiated in parallel with its three unions in 2017, producing largely equivalent terms, but since 2020 the schedules have been separated, with increases taking effect at different times.

The risk is spreading. Supervisors at BHP's Escondida, the world's largest copper mine, are now in government-mediated talks aimed at averting a strike of their own, adding a second front to Chile's copper labor tensions. Benchmark three-month copper was down 0.1 percent at $14,405.50 a tonne by 0950 GMT Wednesday, and the metal has gained about 16 percent this year on tight supply.

## Earlier

Mediation between Antofagasta Minerals and two unions at its Centinela copper mine in Chile concluded after three days with no progress, Bloomberg reported, and the unions could now walk out from October 7. SP Angel's October 5 market note flagged that this would be the first strike ever at an Antofagasta operation. The unions had already rejected the company's collective contract offer, with IndexBox reporting on September 29 that 98.73 percent of members voted against it.

Centinela is one of Antofagasta's core Chilean copper assets, and the timing is awkward for supply. Copper closed October 5 at $14,340 per tonne, its highest since May, on weak US jobs data that eased pressure on the Federal Reserve. A strike at Centinela would tighten an already firm market and could lend near-term support to prices if it persists. Antofagasta has not publicly detailed contingency plans or expected production impact; no output guidance change has been announced.

The dispute is not isolated. Fastmarkets reported on October 1 that unions at Escondida (BHP), Centinela and Philex's Padcal mine had all voted for strike action, pointing to a tense Chilean bargaining season. Whether Centinela's two unions actually walk out on October 7 is the immediate catalyst to watch; a settlement would unwind the risk premium just as quickly.
