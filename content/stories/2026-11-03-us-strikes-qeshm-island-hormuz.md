**Latest:** Qatar said on Tuesday that there was still hope for a US-Iran deal despite the overnight strikes. Foreign ministry spokesperson Majed Al Ansari said mediators were exchanging proposals between Washington and Tehran in real time, and that a Qatari delegation had met Iranian officials for more than three hours on Monday before returning to Doha that night. "If there were no hope, there would be no negotiations," Al Ansari said. Qatar is working toward an interim agreement meant to build trust and open wider discussion, and Al Ansari called freedom of navigation in the strait "non-negotiable," condemning threats to shipping. Doha said it was coordinating with Pakistan and its Gulf neighbours to prevent further escalation.

Explosions struck Iran's Qeshm Island late on Monday, and US officials confirmed that American forces carried out limited strikes on the island in the Strait of Hormuz. Iran's state news agency IRNA reported several blasts in quick succession, and the semi official Fars agency reported explosions from the direction of the sea near the port city of Bandar Lengeh.

US officials described the operation as defensive. One official cited by the Associated Press said the targets were Iranian personnel using surface to air missiles against US assets, and officials said the strikes followed Iranian fire at a US aircraft on Monday. The Wall Street Journal, which first reported the attack, described it as the latest in a series of limited strikes Washington has carried out in recent weeks without public announcement. Officials stressed that the action did not mark a resumption of major combat operations, and the Pentagon referred questions to US Central Command, which had not commented.

Earlier on Monday the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps claimed it had captured two US reconnaissance drones south of Qeshm using electronic warfare and destroyed an MQ-9 drone east of the strait. Those claims come from Iranian state linked media and have not been independently confirmed.

The timing sharpens the stakes. The strikes came hours after President Trump played down a return to war with Iran, while Iranian and Qatari officials are in a third round of talks in Doha on a proposal to reopen Hormuz traffic for two days, covered in Monday's edition, and on the morning Americans vote in the midterm elections. Oil rose on the first reports. Whether Washington treats Qeshm as a closed defensive episode or the opening of the post midterm escalation window that Monday's briefing flagged now depends on Tehran's response and on whether CENTCOM puts the operation on the record.
