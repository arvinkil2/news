Intercontinental Exchange began trading precious-metals futures in London this week, introducing gold, silver, platinum and palladium contracts on its European platform. The contracts are linked to the daily auction prices ICE operates in London and range in duration from same-day settlement to six months, with clearing through ICE's London operation, which also handles Brent crude futures. Auction participation has risen from four firms to 20 over the past decade, including Jane Street, DRW, Goldman Sachs and Citi, with daily auction volumes above 424,000 ounces.

London is the world's largest physical gold hub, with more than $190 billion in over-the-counter transactions daily and about $1.4 trillion of bullion in its vaults. Previous attempts to build a London futures market failed: the London Gold Futures Market launched in 1982 closed in 1985, and the London Metal Exchange discontinued its gold contract in 2022 after five years of thin trading.

The launch lands as gold trades around $4,140 an ounce with silver near records, and as Deutsche Bank projects silver averaging $70 by mid-2027. An LBMA High Court trial over 2019 deaths at Barrick's North Mara mine opens October 7, keeping the association's responsible-sourcing regime in the spotlight. Exchange competition for bullion flows is intensifying alongside the price action: Hong Kong and Singapore made rival pitches to the bullion industry this week, per Bloomberg.
