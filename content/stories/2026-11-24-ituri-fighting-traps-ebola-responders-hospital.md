Heavy fighting near a hospital in Ituri province in the Democratic Republic of the Congo has disrupted the vital work of Ebola responders and forced emergency teams to take cover for more than 24 hours. In a post on X on Sunday, World Health Organization Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said that agency staff and Ebola responders had been forced to shelter "for their lives" in a maternity ward of the hospital.

The WHO chief added that the agency was working with partners to evacuate trapped staff safely, before calling for their "immediate safe passage and for the hospital facilities, staff and patients to be protected." Since January 1, WHO has verified more than 87 attacks on healthcare in the DRC.

The incident happened in an area close to where the Ebola outbreak was first detected. Ituri province remains the epicentre of the outbreak of Ebola disease caused by Bundibugyo virus and accounts for more than two thirds of confirmed cases. A total of eight provinces have been impacted.

The development follows a warning earlier this month by the UN's Senior Ebola Response Coordinator, Julien Harneis, that more people have been killed by Allied Democratic Forces fighters than by the Ebola virus in recent days. "The violence in tens of villages all across the south of Ituri and North Kivu (provinces) goes unnoticed," Harneis said. "People are scared; people are furious at the lack of action on this." Repeated ADF attacks and human rights abuses against civilians have been happening for more than two decades in eastern DRC. "Following up contact tracing in areas where ADF is operating is a very hazardous idea," Harneis told journalists in Geneva on November 13.

More than six months after it was first declared, the Ebola outbreak in eastern DRC continues to spread, and WHO has warned repeatedly that rising insecurity in eastern DRC is one of the main obstacles to containing the virus. According to figures released on November 20, the outbreak has resulted in 11,762 confirmed cases, including 5,775 deaths, across 68 health zones in eight provinces. Ituri remains the hardest hit province, with a cumulative total of more than 8,000 confirmed cases and 3,791 deaths, representing a case fatality rate of 47 per cent. Of the 39 new confirmed cases reported in the latest epidemiological update, 23 were recorded in Ituri.
