Norway's Hunter Group has secured control of offshore accommodation rig owner Jacktel after winning acceptances for more than 91 percent of the company's shares in a NOK 1.63 billion ($168 million) takeover. Hunter confirmed on Monday that all conditions of its NOK 6.30 per share cash offer, made through wholly owned subsidiary HG Projectco 1, had been met, clearing the way for settlement on November 25.

The offer, launched last week, initially secured commitments from shareholders controlling about 73 percent of Jacktel; Hunter extended the acceptance deadline to Monday morning to cross the required 90 percent threshold. It now plans to compulsorily acquire the remaining shares at the same price and seek delisting of Jacktel from Euronext Growth Oslo. The offer price valued Jacktel's issued share capital at about $167 million, an 8.6 percent premium to the November 18 closing price. Hunter is funding the acquisition with available cash.

The takeover gives Hunter control of Haven, a four legged, self elevating accommodation rig that can house up to 444 offshore workers and is the only harsh environment, Norwegian Continental Shelf compliant jack up of its kind. Haven is employed by Aker BP at the Valhall field in the North Sea on a firm contract running to February 2028, with options extending into June 2028.
