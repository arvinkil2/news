**Latest:** Putin has begun his meeting with Witkoff at the Konstantinovsky Palace in St Petersburg, a TASS correspondent reported on Wednesday. Presidential aide Yuri Ushakov had said earlier that the Ukrainian conflict would be the main topic, TASS said. No outcome from the meeting had been reported when this update was written, and the report of the meeting starting rests on the Russian state agency's own correspondent, graded C within this story.

**Latest:** Witkoff has arrived in St Petersburg for talks with Russian leaders and is due to meet Putin on Wednesday in the city, Al Jazeera reported. Rubio said Witkoff would share the results of the Oslo talks with Putin and see whether progress can be made, and Kushner, who joined the Oslo talks remotely because of illness, is also expected to attend the meeting with Putin remotely. Earlier reporting in this story placed the meeting in Sochi; Wednesday reports place it in St Petersburg.

**Latest:** Witkoff and Zelenskyy agreed a peace plan in Oslo on Tuesday, and Witkoff and Jared Kushner will present it to President Vladimir Putin in Sochi on Wednesday, in what Axios reported would be the first face-to-face meeting between US and Russian officials since last November, with Senator Lindsey Graham also attending. The plan has four chapters, covering territory, security arrangements, economic cooperation and future relations between the United States and Russia. On territory it follows the current contact line, with Crimea and parts of Donbas recognised as Russian in fact but not in law and a demilitarised zone along the line; on security, Ukraine would join the European Union but not NATO, with US security guarantees and a European-led coalition force after a ceasefire. Zelenskyy said after the meeting that it was the first time the Americans had come to Ukraine with a proposal rather than demands, and Støre confirmed a plan had been agreed and that no territorial concessions had been made under pressure. Moscow has not responded to the proposal in the reporting read for this update.

**Latest:** US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth denied on Tuesday that the Pentagon ordered any changes to the satellite service Ukraine uses, saying the United States has not pulled anything back, DR reported, citing Reuters. Hegseth said there are terms of service that need to be complied with and that Starlink has the option to make decisions on that basis, and a senior Pentagon official separately said there had been no change to policy on intelligence sharing or on capabilities enabled for Ukraine. His account conflicts with Musk's own statement that the military service is owned and controlled by the US government and that limits are decided by the US military and ultimately the president, and it remains unclear who ordered the speed restriction imposed on October 14.

**Latest:** The Wall Street Journal reported that Elon Musk ordered the slowdown of Starshield, the military version of Starlink that Ukraine's forces use, The Hill reported on Tuesday. The attribution conflicts with Musk's own account last week that Starshield systems are owned and controlled by the US government and that limits on speed or usage are decided by the US military and ultimately the president, and with earlier Reuters reporting, carried in this site's October 18 story, that the order came from inside the White House. Ukraine has said the cap of about 160 kilometres per hour, imposed on October 14, left some of its offensive and interceptor drones effectively unusable.

**Latest:** Zelenskyy now says US intelligence sharing has been restored. Speaking after the Oslo talks, he told reporters the advance warning of Russian missile strikes "works now", the BBC and the Guardian reported, after warnings were seemingly suspended last week. Washington has not confirmed a halt or a resumption: Secretary of State Marco Rubio rejected Kyiv's withholding claim as scandalous and said nothing had been denied to Ukraine intentionally, so the change rests on Zelenskyy's account. Earlier in the day, the meeting had taken place with his quoted remarks about Ukraine's grievances with Washington rather than a peace text. He criticised the temporary US waiver of sanctions on Russian diesel exports as funding the Russian war effort, asked for an energy ceasefire and for Patriot interceptor missiles if Ukraine is to hold off attacks on Russian energy sites, and said requests for missiles are answered with restrictions on the Starlink satellite service used by Ukraine's military, alongside gaps in advance warning of Russian missile attacks. Washington denies deliberately withholding intelligence sharing or satellite support, and Elon Musk said the military Starshield service is controlled by the US government. SABC News reported no outcome from the Oslo session.

US special envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner will take part in meetings on Ukraine in Oslo on Tuesday, joining talks between Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, Finnish President Alexander Stubb and Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre, Norway's government said. Witkoff will attend in person and Kushner will participate remotely, and Støre said Ukraine had requested the envoys' participation, with the aim to put diplomatic efforts on a more constructive path. AFP reported that Axios, citing an unnamed US official, said Witkoff would then travel to Russia, where he hopes to meet President Vladimir Putin and present ideas, a report the White House had not confirmed when AFP asked.
