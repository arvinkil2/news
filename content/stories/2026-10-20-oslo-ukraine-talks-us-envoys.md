**Latest:** Zelenskyy now says US intelligence sharing has been restored. Speaking after the Oslo talks, he told reporters the sharing "works now", the BBC reported, while the Guardian reported him saying the United States had stopped sharing intelligence about incoming Russian strikes earlier on Tuesday and that Witkoff had promised it would resume the next day. Washington has not confirmed either that sharing was halted or that it has resumed, so the change rests on Zelenskyy's account alone. Earlier in the day, the meeting had taken place with his quoted remarks about Ukraine's grievances with Washington rather than a peace text. He criticised the temporary US waiver of sanctions on Russian diesel exports as funding the Russian war effort, asked for an energy ceasefire and for Patriot interceptor missiles if Ukraine is to hold off attacks on Russian energy sites, and said requests for missiles are answered with restrictions on the Starlink satellite service used by Ukraine's military, alongside gaps in advance warning of Russian missile attacks. Washington denies deliberately withholding intelligence sharing or satellite support, and Elon Musk said the military Starshield service is controlled by the US government. SABC News reported no outcome from the Oslo session.

US special envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner will take part in meetings on Ukraine in Oslo on Tuesday, joining talks between Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, Finnish President Alexander Stubb and Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre, Norway's government said. Witkoff will attend in person and Kushner will participate remotely, and Støre said Ukraine had requested the envoys' participation, with the aim to put diplomatic efforts on a more constructive path. AFP reported that Axios, citing an unnamed US official, said Witkoff would then travel to Russia, where he hopes to meet President Vladimir Putin and present ideas, a report the White House had not confirmed when AFP asked.
