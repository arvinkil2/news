**Latest (Saturday, October 10):** Al Jazeera's confirmation added the exact margin: Abdi-Wali took 12,292 votes to Polanski's 8,928, a gap of just over 3,300. The result landed as a relief for Prime Minister Andy Burnham, installed by lawmakers fearful that Starmer's unpopularity was opening the way for Reform UK, with Labour appearing to have won back left-wing voters it had lost to the Greens earlier this year. That win-back raises fresh questions about Polanski's leadership: after conceding, he said he would stay on as leader and chalked the result up to "Andy Burnham's honeymoon," while congratulating Abdi-Wali and noting one in three voters backed the Greens. The Greens now hold five parliamentary seats, up from one in 2023, a reminder that the left-flank pressure is not going away. For context on the scale of the swing, Starmer won this seat with 49 percent in the 2024 general election, with pro-Palestinian independent Andrew Feinstein second on 19 percent.

Labour held the Holborn and St Pancras seat, which Keir Starmer stood down from, in the by-election on October 8. Sagal Abdi-Wali, who arrived in Britain as a child refugee in the 1990s after fleeing war in Somalia and now leads the local council, was declared the winner in the early hours of Friday with 45 percent of the vote, against a third for Green leader Zack Polanski.

The contest became a two-horse race and an early test of Prime Minister Andy Burnham's popularity. Labour has held the seat continuously since its creation in 1983, and the Greens have enjoyed a membership surge since Polanski became leader in September last year on a leftward, pro-Palestinian platform.

Polanski, who is Jewish, was barred from Israel earlier in October after his party backed a motion defining Zionism as racism, a row that framed the campaign. He told Sky News he would stay on as party leader despite the loss. A Green spokesperson called the result a "huge swing in one of Labour's safest seats" and a message that voters want change on climate, rents and "justice for Palestinians." For Labour, holding the seat is a relief; for the Greens, running second in a Labour stronghold is the headline they will carry into the next electoral test.
