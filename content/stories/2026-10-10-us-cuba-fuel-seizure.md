US authorities seized 90 fuel shipments destined for Cuba worth nearly $3 million, acting against sanctions violations on fuel movements to the island, Marine Insight reported October 9. The action extends the enforcement pattern that has already hit Iranian and Russian shadow-fleet tankers this month into Caribbean fuel logistics.

The seizure is small in dollar terms but operationally significant for Cuba, which depends on imported fuel for power generation and transport and has been absorbing supply disruptions. Cuba has been drawing down fuel reserves this year, and any interception of shipments compounds the country's energy crunch. The action also signals that the administration's sanctions enforcement is broadening geographically at the same time as it is tightening on Middle East crude: from Hormuz shuttle sanctions and Iranian shadow-fleet designations to fuel routes serving Havana.

No US agency statement on the specific seizure has been independently verified; details of the enforcement action, the shipments' origin and the legal authority used remain limited to the single report. Watch for an official statement or court filing that would put the count and value on the record.
