The Reserve Bank of India issued the final Securitisation Transactions Amendment Directions, 2026 on November 23, in four parallel versions covering commercial banks, small finance banks, non-banking financial companies and all-India financial institutions. The amendments take effect on April 1, 2027 and follow draft directions published on July 27, 2026.

From that date, securitisation notes may be issued, held and subsequently transferred only in dematerialised form. The minimum ticket size is 1 crore rupees, defined as the investment of a single investor, and it applies at the time of issuance and at every subsequent transfer, not just in the primary market. Notes with amortisation structures may still be traded at amortised value if amortisation takes the holding below the floor.

Public offers of securitisation notes must comply with Regulation 21 of the SEBI (Issue and Listing of Securitised Debt Instruments and Security Receipts) Regulations, 2008. Transactions in notes issued before April 1, 2027 must also be conducted in compliance with the new dematerialisation and ticket-size requirements.

The practical effect is to keep securitisation a wholesale funding channel. A floor on secondary transfers, not only on issuance, prevents notes from being split into retail-sized pieces after issue, which is the route by which structured credit has reached small investors in other markets. Originators keep access to the investor base that already funds them; what they lose is the option of broadening it downward.
