The United States handled more than $12 billion of Venezuela's oil revenues in the first nine months of this year, according to a report prepared by the accounting firm KPMG and made public by the State Department on Thursday. Total deposits into the US Treasury custody account reached $12.26 billion between January 3 and September 30, more than $12.1 billion of it from oil, petroleum products and natural gas, with the rest from gold, minerals and fertiliser, and $12.11 billion, nearly 99 percent, was disbursed, leaving about $169 million in the account.

The arrangement was established after the Trump administration seized then President Nicolás Maduro in January, and buyers of Venezuelan oil pay into the Treasury account, from which funds are released to the interim government in Caracas. The report says the funds remain the sovereign property of Venezuela but are held pending disposition for public, governmental or diplomatic purposes as determined by Secretary of State Marco Rubio.
