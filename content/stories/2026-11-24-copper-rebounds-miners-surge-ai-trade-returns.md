Copper staged one of its biggest rallies of the year on Monday, bouncing out of the bear market it entered on Friday after Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive and set off a rush back into artificial intelligence trades. Comex copper for December delivery jumped as much as 5.5 percent to $5.787 a pound and traded at $5.762 in early New York trade, up 5 percent on the day, after earlier sinking to $5.408, its lowest since the end of March.

The contract had settled at $5.485 on Friday, down 9.9 percent on the week and 20.4 percent below the record settlement of $6.889 reached on September 9. Monday's rebound cut the deficit to about 16 percent and carried copper back above the level where it ended 2025, though the metal remained down 11 percent in November. In London, three-month copper on the London Metal Exchange fell as much as 1.7 percent to $11,948 a tonne before the Nvidia news, within $50 of a bear market of its own measured from the September peak of $14,875.

Miners moved with the metal. Freeport-McMoRan jumped 8 percent, lifting its market value to $71 billion, Southern Copper gained 6 percent to $121 billion, BHP rose 5 percent and Teck Resources about 7 percent, and together five New York listed heavyweights added more than $25 billion in the first minutes of trading. The estate's market tables put copper miners up 6.78 percent on the day, gold miners up 6.43 percent, silver miners up 6.45 percent, uranium miners up 5.9 percent and rare earth miners up 6.92 percent, with BHP up 5.09 percent and Rio Tinto up 3.27 percent.

The bounce recovered only a fraction of the damage. Eleven of the largest copper exposed miners lost a combined $127 billion in market value last week. Marex strategist Alastair Munro told Reuters the jury was still out on whether the bottom was in, noting trend following funds were still selling, and the physical squeeze in China was cooling, with the Yangshan import premium falling for a second session to $450 a tonne from last week's record $500.
