The US Food and Drug Administration on Tuesday approved Lembaz (adrabetadex) injection, the first treatment shown to help people with a severe form of Niemann-Pick disease type C live longer. Lembaz is approved to reduce the risk of death in adult and pediatric patients who weigh at least 7 kg and whose neurological symptoms began before age 6, the infantile-onset form of the disease, and it is given by a trained healthcare provider as an injection into the spinal canal every two weeks.

The agency based the approval on survival data from 36 treated patients across three clinical studies and an expanded access program: patients on Lembaz had a 55 percent reduction in the risk of death against a matched external control group, with median survival of 8.5 years against 4.9 years for controls. The drug carries a boxed warning for hearing impairment, including permanent hearing loss, and the FDA granted the approval to Mandos, LLC, a subsidiary of Beren Therapeutics, PBC.
