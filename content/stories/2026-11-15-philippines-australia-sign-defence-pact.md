The Philippines and Australia signed a defence cooperation arrangement in Manila on Sunday, witnessed by Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese, who is in the city for the ASEAN summit. Philippine Defence Secretary Gilberto Teodoro said the arrangement simplifies coordination under the two countries' Status of Visiting Forces Agreement, cutting the steps needed to run joint activities, and opens closer cooperation across maritime, air and land forces as well as disaster response.

Albanese said the agreement would increase maritime awareness, information sharing, strategic dialogue, training and interoperability, and announced additional Australian investment in maritime domain awareness, including drone technology, equipment and training. Teodoro said the closer ties would bolster the Philippines' credible deterrence posture, including in the South China Sea, where Manila and Beijing hold overlapping claims.
