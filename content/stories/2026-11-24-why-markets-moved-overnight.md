Nvidia rose 31.48 percent to 165.05 dollars, by far the largest move in the tables. The driver is the board's reinstatement of Jensen Huang as chief executive, disclosed in an 8-K and covered in the Nvidia story updated this morning; the shares are reversing last week's sharp fall in AI-related stocks rather than responding to any new demand data.

Semiconductors rose 13.63 percent to 140.22 dollars and the Nasdaq 100 rose 6.86 percent to 26,746, with the tech sector up 8.97 percent. No separate driver appears in this morning's reporting for the sector-wide move; it is the Nvidia reversal carrying the complex with it.

Natural gas rose 12.66 percent to 3.59 dollars per mmBtu. Today's European gas story reports the same cold snap on both sides of the Atlantic: European storage at 68.9 percent against an 89 percent five-year average and a fourth straight daily rise in European benchmark prices, with Henry Hub following the winter tightness higher.

Tesla rose 11.46 percent to 385.46 dollars. No clear single driver for Tesla specifically appears in this morning's reporting; the move came alongside the broader technology reversal.

Rare earth miners rose 6.92 percent to 44.97 and copper miners rose 6.78 percent to 60.15. Today's mining story explains the copper miners directly: copper rebounded out of a bear market as the AI trade returned, and the miners followed the metal. No story in this morning's reporting explains the rare earth miners' move specifically.

The cross-market read is split. Equities staged a sharp risk-on reversal led by one company's governance news, while the physical markets traded winter and surplus: Brent settled at a three-month low ahead of the November 29 OPEC+ meeting (see today's Brent story), European gas surged on cold and low storage, and US yields barely moved, with the 10-year at 4.92 percent, up 0.6 basis points.
