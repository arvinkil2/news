Natural gas was the largest mover in the tables, up 17.18 percent to 3.73 dollars per mmBtu on the front series, ahead of the December contract's expiry on NYMEX on November 25. Today's European gas story, "European gas surges as cold snap meets low storage", reports the same cold snap on both sides of the Atlantic: EU storage at 68.9 percent against a five-year average of 89 percent, a fourth straight daily rise in European benchmark prices on Tuesday, and US prices following the winter tightness higher.

Semiconductors fell 7.82 percent to 129.25 dollars and Nvidia fell 7.11 percent to 153.32 dollars, unwinding much of Monday's surge, when Nvidia rose 31.48 percent after its board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive, the event covered in today's copper story, "Copper rebounds from bear market as miners surge". No Nvidia-specific cause for Tuesday's reversal appears in today's reporting; today's tariff story, "US readies tariff hikes on China, EU and 14 other economies", reports futures extending losses as the administration prepares the new duties, and the broader tech sector fell 3.20 percent with the Nasdaq 100 down 1.84 percent.

Tanker freight rose 6.72 percent to 811.11 dollars, extending the firmness in today's "Commodities also notable" roundup, which put tanker freight up 19.05 percent on the week at Monday's close and attributed the strength to cargo demand and route disruption rather than oil prices. Today's supertanker story, "Used supertankers now worth more than newbuilds", gives the same market a physical footing: a 15-year-old VLCC valued about 25 percent above a newbuild, with Hormuz shuttle voyages stretching round trips and removing capacity.

Silver futures fell 5.40 percent to 40.81 dollars an ounce, copper fell 3.28 percent to 5.44 dollars a pound and copper miners fell 4.69 percent to 57.33 dollars. Today's metals story, "Metals give back Friday rebound as silver leads fall", frames the complex as a liquidation pattern set by positioning and rates rather than physical balances, and today's positioning story, "Net bullish copper bets fall to twenty month low", shows speculative support for copper was already at its thinnest since March 2025 before Monday's one-day rebound.

The Nikkei 225 fell 5.06 percent to 56,437 at the Tokyo close. No story in today's reporting explains the Nikkei's fall.

Corn rose 3.91 percent to 451.2 cents a bushel. Today's grains story, "Grains hold gains as USDA inspections beat expectations", covers the export inspection data behind the recent grain strength, corn inspections of 1.19 million tonnes in the week ended November 19, but no story in today's reporting explains corn's move on Tuesday specifically.

The cross-market read is a partial risk-off rotation, not a broad exit. The S&P 500 fell 0.45 percent and the Nasdaq 100 fell 1.84 percent while the Dow Jones rose 0.78 percent, Treasury yields eased, with the 10 year down 2.2 basis points to 4.90 percent, and the dollar index softened 0.47 percent to 103.83, even after consumer confidence jumped to 103.3 in November, well above the 92.5 consensus, in today's Conference Board story. Energy split along physical lines: gas rose on weather and storage while Brent fell 1.77 percent to 87.79 dollars, extending the three-month low in today's Brent story ahead of the November 29 OPEC+ meeting.
