France's health and agriculture ministries said a Shamonda virus infection was detected in a severely immunocompromised patient treated at the Hospices civils de Lyon for a serious illness, who died in early October, making it the first documented human case despite decades of circulation in animals in Africa and Asia. The ministries called it an exceptional case in a strongly immunocompromised patient, said brain complications observed before the death were very probably linked to the infection, and put the leading hypothesis as a bite from a Culicoides midge in eastern France, where the virus circulated in animals this spring and summer.

Shamonda is an orthobunyavirus transmitted to animals, mainly cattle, by Culicoides biting midges, first detected in Nigeria in the mid-1960s and reported sporadically in cattle in Africa and Japan before being identified in Europe for the first time in the summer of 2026, in cattle farms in France, Germany and Switzerland among others. The ministries said that, on current knowledge, no health risk to the general population is identified, and that clinical, biological and epidemiological investigations are continuing, with the World Health Organization and the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control notified.
