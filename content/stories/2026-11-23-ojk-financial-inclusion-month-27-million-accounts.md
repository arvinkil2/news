Indonesia's financial regulator has published the results of its 2026 Financial Inclusion Month (Bulan Inklusi Keuangan, or BIK). OJK said 27.23 million financial products and accounts were opened during the campaign, broken down as 7.21 million bank accounts, 2.07 million capital market accounts, 5.38 million insurance policies, 2.07 million financing company accounts, 4.63 million pawnshop accounts and 5.87 million fintech accounts.

The campaign ran 6,446 financial literacy and inclusion activities reaching more than 798,000 participants across Indonesia, OJK said. The results were presented by Dicky Kartikoyono, OJK's chief executive for market conduct supervision, education and consumer protection, at the BIK main event and the 2026 Financial Expo in Bandung, West Java, on October 29. FinExpo ran at Trans Studio Mall Bandung from October 29 to November 1 as part of the national GENCARKAN financial inclusion movement.

OJK said the programme prioritised communities in frontier, outermost and least developed (3T) regions and priority groups including women, students and youth, micro, small and medium enterprise owners, informal workers, farmers and fishers, persons with disabilities and rural communities. In West Java, the programme included financial education for 4,500 MSME operators, savings account openings for about 2,000 Islamic boarding school students, and an International Deaf Week event reaching about 400 participants with disabilities.

The release also ties the campaign to a new coordinating body: the National Council for Financial Well-being (DNKK), inaugurated by President Prabowo Subianto on October 22, 2026, which OJK expects to improve coordination on expanding access to formal financial services and household financial management.
