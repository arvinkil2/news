The Reserve Bank of India will conduct an open-market sale auction of government securities for Rs 25,000 crore on Tuesday, October 13, through a multi-security auction using the multiple-price method. The sale covers six securities: the 7.88 percent GS 2030, 6.10 percent GS 2031, 7.95 percent GS 2032, 7.26 percent GS 2033, 7.18 percent GS 2033 and 7.10 percent GS 2034. Bids are due between 9:30 and 10:30 am on the RBI's E-Kuber system, with results announced the same day and funds due by noon October 14.

The sale is the RBI's third tightening move in eight days. Wednesday brought the 25bp repo hike to 5.50 percent with a calibrated-tightening stance; Friday's Rs 36,000 crore bond auction cleared in full with no devolvement; and Friday evening added the emergency rupee package of a special dollar window for oil companies and a 20 percent cash reserve requirement on large rupee derivatives. The OMO sale drains liquidity on top of that: selling bonds absorbs rupees from the banking system, working against the surplus the RBI had been managing through variable-rate reverse repo operations.

Timing is the tension. The OMO sale hits on the same day India's September CPI lands, the first inflation read after the October 7 hike. Markets are watching whether the sale is a routine liquidity calibration or a signal that the RBI wants system liquidity structurally tighter to support the currency, which would make the OMO sale and the FX measures two faces of the same operation.
