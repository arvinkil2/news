**Latest, October 27:** Jordan said on Tuesday that its bombing of southern Syria on Monday evening killed more than 20 Islamic State militants, Arab News reported via Reuters. Government spokesperson Mohammad Al-Momani said on X that the attacks, which Jordan says were carried out in coordination with Damascus, refrained from targeting sites where information indicated civilians were present, and he expressed regret over the civilian casualties and said Jordan is following up with the Syrian authorities. The same Reuters report put Syria's initial official toll at seven deaths, including three children and a woman, a third figure alongside the five and eight carried below. The militant toll is Jordan's own estimate and is not independently verified, and the coordination claim remains contradicted by Damascus's account below.

Jordan said late on Monday that its fighter jets struck terrorist hideouts in southern Syria, destroying structures and vehicles and killing or wounding members of groups it links to Friday's attack on a funeral at Hamoud Church in Jordan's Karak governorate, which killed nine people. Jordan's armed forces said the strikes were carried out in coordination with Damascus, and Jordanian security forces had earlier on Monday arrested three men in connection with the church attack after a dawn shootout in Karak, Deutsche Welle reported.

Syria's account differs on both coordination and casualties. Arab News reported that Damascus said the attacks were carried out without prior coordination, and the Daraa health director, quoted by the state news agency SANA, said civilians were caught in strikes on several sites in the province's countryside. That official's toll, carried by Deutsche Welle via Reuters and in Arab News's summary, was five civilians killed, including women and children; Damascus's later account, in the Arab News report's headline and body and in ERT's reporting, put the toll at eight, including three children. Syria said it was investigating, was communicating with Jordan, and called for joint military and security coordination in border areas. ERT News also reported that Syria closed the Nasib border crossing with Jordan after the strikes; no other report read in this run carries a closure.
