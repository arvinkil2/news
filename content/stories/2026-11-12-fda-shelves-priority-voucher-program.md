The Food and Drug Administration has shelved the Commissioner's National Priority Voucher program, launched in June 2025 to cut review times for priority drugs to one to two months, according to six sources familiar with the matter and internal documents seen by Reuters. The program awarded vouchers to 22 drugs, 12 of which have been approved, but none has been awarded since Commissioner Marty Makary, who created it, left the agency in May, and the executive council of political appointees that oversaw the reviews has gone dormant; career scientists have resumed overseeing the nine drugs still in the program.

The Department of Health and Human Services disputed the account: a spokesperson said the pilot is ongoing and that the FDA is reviewing feedback from a June public hearing to refine it. Critics had attacked the program for handpicking drugs and for awards to companies, including Eli Lilly, Novo Nordisk and Merck KGaA, that had reached drug pricing deals with the White House, and a former HHS official told Reuters the initiative is quietly being phased out after its top administrator departed.
