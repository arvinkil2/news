Annual core inflation in Tokyo accelerated in November to its fastest pace in 16 months. The core consumer price index, which includes fuel prices but excludes fresh food, rose 2.9 percent from a year earlier, up from 2.6 percent in October and just below the median market forecast of 3.0 percent, data published on Friday showed. Headline inflation edged up to 2.8 percent from 2.7 percent.

Core inflation in the capital has now stayed above the Bank of Japan's 2 percent target for three consecutive months. The increase was driven partly by the end of government utility subsidies, and prices rose across a broad range of goods and services, including food, daily necessities and hotel charges. Inflation also rose for services such as public transportation and medical care. The index that strips out both fresh food and fuel, watched by the Bank of Japan as a gauge of trend inflation, eased to 2.5 percent from 2.6 percent.

Tokyo prices are treated as a leading indicator of nationwide trends, and the data will be among the factors the Bank of Japan weighs at its two-day policy meeting ending December 18. The central bank raised its policy rate to 1.25 percent in September, after a June increase, held in October, and signalled that underlying inflation has roughly reached its target and that it stands ready to keep raising rates. A majority of analysts polled by Reuters expect an increase in December and another by the end of March, with many seeing a terminal rate around 1.75 to 2.0 percent.

Short-term Japanese government bond yields edged higher on Friday as the data reinforced expectations of a move next month. Hawkish board member Naoki Tamura said on Thursday that the bank must respond swiftly to signs of an inflation overshoot, including by accelerating the pace of increases. Investors are now watching a speech by Governor Kazuo Ueda scheduled for Monday for any signal on December.
