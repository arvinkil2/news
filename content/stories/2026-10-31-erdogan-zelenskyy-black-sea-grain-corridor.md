Update, November 1: Erdogan has now formally proposed that Turkey host the next round of talks. At the Saturday Istanbul meeting he proposed that Ankara host the new trilateral round between Russia, Ukraine and the United States planned to resume in November, El Pais reported, and Zelenskyy said he is willing to meet Vladimir Putin in Turkey. Kremlin adviser Ushakov reacted coolly to wider participation in the format. Russia's ombudswoman Lantratova also announced that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to hold regular humanitarian missions at Oleshky, the town whose evacuated residents crossed to Ukrainian-held territory this weekend. El Pais is the single source for the hosting proposal and the humanitarian missions agreement.

President Recep Tayyip Erdogan hosted President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Istanbul on Saturday, and the two leaders agreed to work on a new grain corridor through the Black Sea, with Erdogan saying talks to restore safe navigation would continue. Erdogan called on Russia and Ukraine to stop targeting civilian ships, said attacks on civilian vessels are unacceptable whoever carries them out, and said Ankara has sent both sides a proposal to end the attacks that neither has acted on. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the same day in Atlanta that the United States is reviewing proposals by Turkiye and India for a limited Black Sea ceasefire to secure grain and energy shipments, and Zelenskyy said the two leaders also discussed the format of the next round of Russia, Ukraine and United States talks, planned for November in the United Arab Emirates.
