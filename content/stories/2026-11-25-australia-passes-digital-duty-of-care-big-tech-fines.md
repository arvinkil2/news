Australia's parliament has passed the Digital Duty of Care bill, with Communications Minister Anika Wells announcing on Wednesday that a service breaching its duty to protect users can be fined up to 5% of global revenue or A$109.2 million, whichever applies, a penalty Yonhap noted could exceed US$10 billion for Meta. The duty covers social media, AI chatbots, games and apps, requires platforms to protect under-18s from seriously harmful content and to assess addictive features such as infinite scrolling, and lets users switch off algorithmic recommendation feeds.

Australians harmed by a breach can also sue the platform, and the law follows a 12-month transition. US Ambassador David Bratt conveyed Washington's opposition to Wells before the announcement, arguing that a vaguely defined concept of harm plus fines of up to 5% of global revenue would pressure platforms to censor lawful expression.
