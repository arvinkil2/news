The European Commission has released 7.9 billion euros to Ukraine to support its financing needs and keep public administration functioning, the largest single disbursement made under the Ukraine Facility. The exact amount is €7,859,441,144. It is the ninth disbursement under the facility, the EU's main instrument for Ukraine's recovery, reform agenda and progress towards membership, and it takes total support under the Ukraine Plan to 40 billion euros since mid 2024, including external contributions and funds channelled through the Ukraine Support Loan. The payment includes 5.1 billion euros from that loan.

The disbursement follows the Council's conclusion that Ukraine had fulfilled sixteen reform and investment steps linked to the ninth instalment, together with outstanding steps from earlier instalments and one brought forward from the tenth; one ninth instalment step was assessed as partially fulfilled. The reforms span the judiciary, anti corruption and anti money laundering, financial markets, public asset management, energy, agri food markets, digital transformation and the green transition. Ukraine also mobilised 948 million euros in public investment in education, healthcare, housing and demining of agricultural land, of which about 451 million euros went to sub national authorities.

For the rest of 2026, 15.7 billion euros in EU support remains available to Ukraine, including just over 14 billion euros under the Ukraine Support Loan, split between 10.2 billion euros for defence industrial capacity and 3.9 billion euros in budget support, each release subject to the plan's conditions. The loan totals 90 billion euros for 2026 and 2027. Since 2022, the EU and its member states have provided 245.5 billion euros in overall support to Ukraine, including 3.8 billion euros from the proceeds of immobilised Russian assets, according to the Commission.
