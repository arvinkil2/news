Huawei has launched the Qingyun HM650, a business desktop built around its own Kirin X90 processor and an enterprise version of HarmonyOS, and says the machine is the first desktop PC in China certified as 100 percent domestically sourced, from the processor and memory to the storage and power supply. The Kirin X90 is a ten-core, 20-thread chip with integrated Maleoon graphics; the reported launch configuration pairs it with 16 GB of memory and a 512 GB solid-state drive.

The certification, issued by Zhejiang Kezheng Electronic Information Product Inspection, attests that the components are produced in China. It does not by itself establish that every component was designed by a Chinese company, and neither Huawei nor the inspection body has published the certification's scope, a caveat the South China Morning Post also noted in its launch coverage. Huawei has not published a full specification sheet, so performance comparisons with Intel or AMD machines are not yet possible from company material.

The buyer is the state. The machines are aimed at government agencies and enterprises rather than retail customers, one government agency has reportedly signed up for 600 units, and Beijing has ordered government entities to move off Windows and Intel machines toward domestic alternatives. A reported starting price of 7,199 yuan, about 1,080 dollars, and a December 1 sales start come from launch reporting; Huawei's own store listing carries no official price.

For Intel, AMD and Qualcomm the significance is structural rather than immediate. None of them can bid for the Chinese government desktop business this machine is built for, and the launch completes a Huawei stack that now runs from its own silicon through its own operating system on laptops, tablets and, with the HM650, desktops. Washington revoked the licences that let Intel and Qualcomm supply Huawei in May 2024; the HM650 is what the domestic replacement looks like two and a half years later.
