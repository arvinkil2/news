**Data as of:** Austerity package unveiled Thursday, October 1; OAT-Bund spreads October 1-2; Friday October 2 closes.
**Issued:** Sunday, October 4, 2026 (America/Toronto).
**Evidence key:** CONFIRMED (primary source), REPORTED (multiple outlets), RUMORED (unverified market reporting), ANALYTICAL (inference).

France unveiled a 43bn euro austerity package on October 1 — and bond markets sold it anyway. The 10-year OAT-Bund spread marked 130.3bp (+13.2bp day-on-day) on October 1 per market trackers, while intraday levels hit 149bp, the widest since 2012; other monitors flagged 150bp as fiscal and political risks mount (REPORTED). French 10Y OATs closed Friday around 4.90%, down just 1bp — the widening is almost entirely a France-specific risk premium, not a rates move (CONFIRMED via close levels).

The verdict from investors was blunt: a "guilty" judgment on the debt trajectory, with the package judged insufficient against France's political instability (REPORTED via Fortune). Euro area sovereign spreads broadly hit multi-year highs, with Italy also facing debt pressure (REPORTED). For the ECB, this creates an awkward two-track market — core Bunds rallying on flight-to-safety (down 6bp Friday to 3.46%) while peripheral fiscal risk reprices independently — complicating any October 29 tightening signal.

The contrast with Germany is instructive: Bunds remain the haven even as Berlin's own politics fracture (AfD at 30% in the October 3 INSA poll). But the slow-burn governance risk in Germany and the acute fiscal risk in France are converging on the same question markets will test this week: whether sovereign supply can clear at these yields without political credibility behind the fiscal math.
