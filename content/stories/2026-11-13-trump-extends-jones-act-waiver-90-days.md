President Donald Trump extended the Jones Act waiver for another 90 days on Friday, keeping foreign-flagged tankers eligible to move oil and other commodities between US ports as the war with Iran continues to drive up fuel costs. The extension continues what is already the longest waiver in the history of the 1920 law, which normally requires cargo moving between American ports to travel on US-built, US-owned and US-crewed vessels.

Foreign vessels still need approval for individual voyages under a restriction introduced in August, after earlier blanket exemptions drew opposition from the domestic maritime industry. About 300 waivers were granted under the blanket exemptions in March and May, against 48 since the switch to individual approvals, on government figures cited by Reuters. US gasoline averaged $3.91 a gallon on Thursday, against $2.98 before the Iran war began, and diesel stood at $6.10, up from $3.76, according to AAA.
