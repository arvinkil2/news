Peru's central bank, the Banco Central de Reserva del Perú, raised its benchmark interest rate by 25 basis points to 4.50 percent on Thursday, its first increase in nearly four years and a move most analysts surveyed had expected, Reuters reported. Annual inflation accelerated to 5.3 percent in October from 4.5 percent in September, driven mainly by rising food and energy costs, and twelve-month inflation expectations stayed at 3.1 percent, above the upper bound of the bank's 1 to 3 percent target range.

The bank said the increase does not necessarily signal the start of a broader tightening cycle. It expects inflation to return gradually to the target range and converge toward 2 percent as supply shocks recede, but cautioned that El Niño and geopolitical tensions in the Middle East could prolong price pressures.
