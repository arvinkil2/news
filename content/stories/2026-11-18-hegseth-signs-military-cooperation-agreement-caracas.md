US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth signed an agreement to strengthen military forces and border security with Venezuela on Wednesday, during an unannounced visit to Caracas where he met interim President Delcy Rodriguez at the Miraflores presidential palace. Speaking alongside Rodriguez in comments broadcast by state television channel VTV, Hegseth said the two countries had agreed to bolster military cooperation and that Washington looked forward to deepening its defense partnership using the expertise of the US military. Rodriguez welcomed what she called a restart of military cooperation against common threats, saying stronger training, operational equipment and access to new technologies would benefit Venezuela's armed forces, but she gave no further detail on what the collaboration will involve.

Hegseth is the latest Trump cabinet member to travel to Venezuela since US forces ousted Nicolas Maduro in January, following visits this year by the Interior and Energy secretaries that centred on the country's oil reserves and critical minerals. The visit comes while Venezuela's leadership remains unsettled: Rodriguez, a former Maduro ally, continues to act as head of state after her court-allowed term expired in July, and no date has been set for a presidential election. Hegseth was expected in Bogota later on Wednesday to deliver military equipment and discuss security cooperation with Colombia.
