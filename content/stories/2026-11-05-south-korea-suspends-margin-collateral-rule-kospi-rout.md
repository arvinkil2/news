South Korea will temporarily suspend the mandatory 140 percent collateral maintenance ratio that securities firms apply to margin lending, for three months, to prevent forced stock sales as share prices plunge, Investing.com reported, citing local media reports from an emergency financial market review meeting chaired by Finance Minister Lee Hyoung-il with officials from the Financial Services Commission, the Financial Supervisory Service and the Bank of Korea. Authorities said they would prepare further measures if volatility intensified.

The decision came as the Kospi fell as much as 8.8 percent to 4,911.28 in early Friday trading, its lowest since early February, before a 20 minute circuit breaker halted stock and derivatives trading; Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix led the losses. Japan's Nikkei 225 fell as much as 7.1 percent, putting it more than 20 percent below its June record high and into bear market territory, while Taiwan's weighted index fell 4.1 percent and Singapore's Straits Times index dropped 2.4 percent.
