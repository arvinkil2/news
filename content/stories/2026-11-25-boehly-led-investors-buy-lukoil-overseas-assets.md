Investors led by US billionaire Todd Boehly's asset management company Eldridge have struck a deal to buy Lukoil's assets outside Russia, sources confirmed to CNBC on Wednesday. The US International Development Finance Corporation and Catena Investments, based in the United Arab Emirates, are also parties to the deal, the sources said.

The assets changing hands are Lukoil's international refineries and other production assets in Bulgaria, Romania, Africa and the Middle East, together with retail petrol stations in the United States, Europe and Asia. The package would give the US-led group about 350,000 barrels per day of production. The Office of Foreign Assets Control is expected to sign off on the deal, but final approval is still needed, the sources said. CNBC gave no price, closing date or payment structure.

The agreement would let Europe buy fuel such as diesel previously produced by Lukoil through the US-led group rather than from Russia, CNBC reported.

Reuters reported on November 3 that the Boehly-led consortium was preparing a cashless securities swap for the portfolio, which was initially valued at $22 billion when the sale process began after US sanctions on Lukoil in October 2025. Under that plan, a bank acting for the consortium bought frozen Lukoil securities from about ten US and British investment funds at 30 cents on the dollar, to be exchanged for the foreign assets and held in escrow rather than transferred straight to Lukoil. More than a dozen bidders, including Exxon Mobil, Chevron and Carlyle, had pursued the assets; Carlyle dropped out last month, leaving the Boehly group in the lead. None of the parties commented to Reuters at the time.
