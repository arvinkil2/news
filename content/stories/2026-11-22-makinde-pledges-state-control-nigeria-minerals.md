Seyi Makinde, the Oyo State governor and Allied Peoples Movement presidential candidate, has promised to restructure Nigeria's mineral resources governance if elected, giving states and host communities greater control over the solid minerals in their territories. The pledge is part of the Makinde-Daura campaign's RESET Nigeria manifesto, under a "Federation that Works" programme of fiscal federalism.

Under the plan, the federal government would keep issuing mining licences within the existing constitutional framework, but state governments would be formally involved in the process, able to review applications and raise concerns on land use, environmental protection, community interests and public safety. The manifesto also proposes public registers of mining licences and beneficial owners, closer cooperation between federal and state regulators, and clearer compliance monitoring. A Federal Responsibility Review, completed in the administration's first year, would sort which functions stay federal, which run under national standards at state level, and which belong principally to states and local governments, with constitutional amendments, legislation and fiscal changes to follow.

The campaign's case is that states and communities bear the consequences of mining, including environmental damage, unsafe storage of hazardous materials, damaged roads and community conflict, while the federal government retains the licensing power and most of the benefit. The proposals are also framed as a response to illegal mining and the disputes it generates.

Jurisdiction Watch: this is a campaign pledge, not a policy change, and it is contingent on the January 16, 2027 election and on constitutional amendment, which in Nigeria requires state assembly support as well as federal legislation. Its significance for miners is directional: resource control is now a headline theme in the presidential race, in the country with West Africa's largest untapped solid minerals base, and it echoes the state assertiveness showing up in mining codes across the region this year.
