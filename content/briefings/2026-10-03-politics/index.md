## The day in brief

**1. The Iran war is entering its most dangerous economic phase.** Since the US-Israeli opening strikes of February 28, 2026 (Operation Epic Fury / Roaring Lion; Khamenei killed), the conflict has oscillated between open combat and lulls. This week: three commercial tankers were hit by projectiles in the Strait of Hormuz (Oct 1-3), an Indian sailor was killed in a Hormuz attack (Sep 23), and the US is moving thousands of troops to the region (AP, Oct 1). Trump rejected Iran's latest proposal and expects renewed bombing after the US midterms (WSJ, Sep 25); Rubio says the operation will last "weeks, not months" but expects renewed strikes. Israel is simultaneously widening into southern Beirut ("fundamentally change the security reality — from Tehran to Beirut," per IDF chief Zamir). The G7 has reportedly agreed an IEA-coordinated release of 100 million barrels (crude + diesel + refined products) from strategic reserves to cool diesel prices under Trump pressure — if confirmed, it signals allied alarm about supply, not price management. Meanwhile OPEC+ (without the UAE, which exited May 1) is expected to hold November quotas steady this weekend, but quotas have been nominal since March: Gulf producers are physically unable to fill them because Hormuz disruptions lock barrels inside the Gulf.

**2. Brazil votes tomorrow — a statistical tie for the presidency of Latin America's largest economy.** First round is October 4. Datafolha's final poll (Oct 3): Lula 42, Flávio Bolsonaro 40; AtlasIntel/Bloomberg (Sep 23-28, n=5,005): Lula 45.3 / Bolsonaro 42.2, with the runoff simulated at 47.6-47.7 — a dead heat. No candidate clears 50% of valid votes → runoff October 25. The Bolsonaro clan is running without its patriarch: Jair Bolsonaro is serving a 27-year sentence for the 2022 coup attempt. The result decides the fate of the region's largest market and its commodity/FDI posture.

**3. The US midterms in 31 days are now the pivot for everything.** November 3: Democrats lead the generic ballot by 7-8 points (El País/RCP aggregate, Sep 27); Trump's approval sits at 32-39%; prediction markets price a Democratic House near 86% but the Senate as a coin flip (Democrats need net +4 from 47 seats; the Maine, Michigan, Texas, Nebraska-independent and Ohio races are the margin). The lame-duck is already loaded: Congress only funded the government through December 11 (CR), and the Senate's October 2 compromise (71-29) attaches two weeks of DHS funding to five full-year bills — House action is still pending. Expect the outcome to directly shape Ukraine aid, Iran-war escalation authority, and tariff policy.

**4. The global rate cycle has flipped — and it is oil-driven.** The Fed hiked on September 16 to 3.75-4.00% (unanimous, first hike since July 2023), the SEP median points to ~one more hike this year (4.1% end-2026), and Warsh explicitly cited Middle East tensions. Next FOMC: October 28 (markets price 77% hold). Turkey held at 37% for a fifth time (Sep 10) but JPMorgan expects cuts to begin October 22; Nigeria eased while South Africa tightened — Africa's big central banks are diverging on domestic conditions, not the Fed's lead.

**5. Western Europe's two big economies are simultaneously politically incapacitated.** France has had no stable government since Lecornu's resignation ~September 16; Macron is hunting a third prime minister this year and the Assembly's dissolution is back on the table. In Germany, Merz's approval is 10% (Infratest-Dimap, Oct 1), the CDU fell below the 5% threshold in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania (first time in 77 years) and AfD now leads national polling at 27-28% — a level at which Germany's EU-steering capacity (Ukraine aid, fiscal reform, trade posture toward China) is degrading. The UK's Andy Burnham (PM since July 20) is under EU pressure to raise tariffs on Chinese EVs after Chinese brands took 23% of UK sales in September.

**6. Critical-minerals coercion is the week's sharpest trade weapon.** China's June 15 State Council Order No. 839 centralized rare-earth quotas and traceability; July brought dual-use shipment blocks to 14 EU firms and export-control listing of MP Materials and USA Rare Earth; a Sep 15 regulation allows exit bans for export-control violators. A key suspension of technology-transfer controls expires November 10, with no announced extension, ten days after a Trump-Xi summit expected in late October. The US counter (54-nation FORGE alliance, $30B; $12B Project Vault reserve) is the largest minerals-decoupling effort since the Cold War. Europe's own leverage play — an October deadline for "tangible results" in EU-China talks, with Sefcovic in Beijing Oct 8-9 and voluntary export restraints on Chinese EVs on the table — will test whether Brussels can extract concessions from Beijing while Washington negotiates bilaterally.

**7. Venezuela is being re-opened as a Western oil play under an unelected interim regime.** Following the US capture of Maduro (January 3 operation), acting president Delcy Rodríguez signed Trump's "largest oil deal in history" covering 65 billion barrels / 17 fields under a new Hydrocarbons Law (25-year term, 16% royalty + 34% income tax, ~$100B investment). Rodríguez promised "inclusive elections" at the UN but set no date, while Nobel laureate Machado remains exiled and blocked from returning — the US is prioritizing crude-flow stability over democratization.
